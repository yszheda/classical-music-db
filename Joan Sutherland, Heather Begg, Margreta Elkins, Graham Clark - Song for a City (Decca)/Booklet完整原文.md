## Opening Speech by Keith Michell (25 January 1975, Royal Opera House, Covent Garden)

> It is almost forty years since the fearful cyclone destroyed the town of Darwin in Australia's Northern Territory. Your Royal Highness, Your Excellencies, my Lords, Ladies and Gentlemen. It is my privilege and honour this evening to welcome you all to this concert, held at midnight on 25 January 1975 at the Royal Opera House, Covent Garden, to assist the victims of this cyclone Tracy. Darwin was a city with a population of 45,000 when this cyclone named 'Tracy' made sudden, shocking destruction of the entire city possible by the generous gift of the services of all these good people. An Australian journalist who was there wrote that houses exploded upwards, outwards and downwards, colleagues and friends, with the debris slicing through everything. Almost fifty people were killed, hundreds injured and thousands dispossessed. Now, while plans for rebuilding are formulating, Darwin, situated between Capricorn and the Equator, glitters logically under the sun in an incredible pseudo snow and tinsel of ripped roofing iron, broken glass and shattered walls, uprooted trees that gave their distressed leaves to something tougher than an autumn frost. It is the noise of Tracy that the survivors Richard Bonynge remember most vividly. The whine increasing to a constant shriek, on and on over seven hours deadening senses so that many didn't sleep for even days after, could not sleep for the sound of the wind in the now silent city where even London to be here, and it is incidentally his first official visit as patron of the Royal Opera House. And Mr. Richard Bonynge is to conduct the Royal Philharmonic Orchestra.

> Australians have known natural disasters before: flood, drought and bush fires, which Tonight is indeed, as His Excellency The High Commissioner has said, evidence of such a disaster as this. And the people in tremendous sympathy and long standing friendship that exists between Australia and Great Britain. It is something which I am sure every one here would give thought to helping at such a time. the people of Great Britain. It remains only for me now to introduce the soloists tonight who, with two exceptions, are profoundly grateful for and proud about. And all Australian or New Zealanders. Miss Heather Begg, Miss Margreta Elkins, Mr. Graham Clark you on their behalf and everyone concerned (who is an Englishman - this young tenor is making his debut at the Royal Opera House whose idea it was - she instigated the whole tonight - two weeks ago he auditioned with idea; the magnificent Royal Philharmonic Orchestra, without whom the idea could not have happened; and the Royal Opera House and Miss Joan Sutherland. Of course you, the audience, are to be thanked; and the soloists, the members of the Orchestra, the entire Royal Opera House staff and stage staff, the London Weekend Television crew, and the Decca Record Company Ltd., all of whom have donated their services tonight. Thank you.
