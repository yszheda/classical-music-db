## About Georges Enescu

Many superb musicians have come out of Romania, but George Enescu was perhaps the greatest of all. A violinist, pianist, composer, conductor and teacher, he worked for the improvement of musical education and opportunities. Enescu was the embodiment of his country's musical culture. Several of his compositions have always been popular but there are signs that in the 21st century more of them are being discovered. Unfortunately, owing to the darkness of the record companies and the artist's own lack of interest in recordings, his recorded legacy is small in comparison with his artistry. His recordings were made on 78rpm discs and he preferred to record his most famous pupil, Yehudi Menuhin, because they knew the producer's dues would sell well.

Enescu was born on 19 August 1881, in a village now named after him. He had his first violin lessons at four and at the age of seven he was able to begin playing the piano and the violin. At last he had more orthodox violin tuition from the Viennese-born pupil Eduard Caudella. In 1888 he entered the Vienna Conservatory, studying violin (with Sigmond Bachrich of the Rosé Quartet and Joseph Hellmesberger Jnr), piano, chamber music (with Joseph Hellmesberger Snr) and composition (with Robert Fuchs). In 1893 he went to Paris to study at the Paris Conservatory under Martin Marsick. In 1895 he graduated with the highest honours. At the Paris Conservatoire, Enescu continued his violin studies with Joseph White and Martin Marsick, while Mozart, Beethoven, Brahms, Goldmark and Fauré were his harmony teachers. Enescu achieved a great violin prize in 1899 and settled permanently in Paris, becoming an integral part of the French musical scene and spelling his name in the French fashion, 'Georges Enesco'.
