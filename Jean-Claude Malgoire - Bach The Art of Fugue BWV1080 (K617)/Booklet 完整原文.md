## ACTA SACRA LIBERA VERBA
*(Les Actes sont sacrés, et libres les commentaires...)*

### Par Jean Claude MALGOIRE

A l'heure de sa mort, Jean Sébastien Bach travaillait encore à la composition d'un impressionnant cycle de variations contrapuntales, tout dans la même tonalité, et basé sur une seule idée thématique. Offering only just engraved and published, he probably wished to set himself a new challenge and produce a summa of his skill and learning. This was the genesis of the *Art of Fugue*, a perpetual fascination exercised by unfinished work. Centuries after it was written, musicologists and performers still wonder what the final structure would have been, and for which instruments it was intended. One question still generally provokes the liveliest debate: can one legitimately "orchestrate" the quasi-totality of a work that the composer finished leaving open on the question of instrumentation was never definitively answered by Bach himself.

Of all the great debates which accompany the interpretation of the *Art of Fugue*, the question of instrumentation is perhaps the one which still provokes the liveliest discussions. Can one "orchestrate" this quasi totality of a work where Bach, by general consent only left a keyboard version? Fortuitement, c'est Jean Sébastien Bach lui-même qui nous donne la réponse, si l'on veut bien considérer le mouvement de la sonate en cantate 76. Il y utilise le hautbois d'amour pour la soprano et la basse pour le ténor, dans la combinaison qu'a notre instrumentation. D'ailleurs, on ne peut pas contredire le principe d'un instrument continue inappropriate dans ce contexte.

By a happy chance, it is Johann Sebastian Bach himself who gives us an element of reply to these arguments, if we look at what he does in the opening sinfonia of Cantata No.76. Here he employs oboe d'amore for soprano and bass for tenor, that is the same combination that we have in our instrumentation for the Art of Fugue. Certainly, that does not mean that we have to follow his example in every combination, but it shows that Bach himself did not hesitate to use that combination of instruments, so principle of instrumentation is inappropriate in this context.

Bach's instrumentarium, then, is notable for its variety and richness. If he makes use of most of the instruments that he had available to him, it was because that was the only way he could enrich the palette of the Art of Fugue, could he not enrich it with instrumentation, modifying the text, that he had been working on for sixty years in his head.

Thus Bach's instrumentarium, then, is notable for its variety and richness. And if on the whole we choose the combinations that we have here, it essentially draws on Bach's own conceptions, which had already reached their culmination in the *Art of Fugue*.

The proposition that forms the basis for the version of the *Art of Fugue* presented here is thus as follows:

Bach lived at a pivotal period in the history of musical instruments: the instrumentarium available to him was much richer and more varied than it would ever been in the Classical era. "Quaerendo invenietis... " wrote the Cantor in the sixth canon of the Musical Offering. Who can blame him for choosing to extend this to the *Art of Fugue*?

Our response to this call to freedom, which is the greatest mark of respect a creator can show for his work, that we have considered the solutions envisaged by Bach himself in different cases: in Bach, in his lifetime, there was always room for different interpreters to perform the work: that's what we've tried to do.

## La Grande Ecurie et La Chambre du Roy
### direction: Jean-Claude Malgoire

Sans La Grande Ecurie et La Chambre du Roy fondée en 1967 par Jean-Claude Malgoire, l'histoire de l'interprétation de la musique baroque n'aurait pas eu la même sens que n'est pas le nom qu'il porte s'il ne porte la Grâce Ecuries qui, au temps des rois, correspondait à la place que les écuries royales donnions à 1 l'éclat des trompettes dans les funérailles de Louis XIV que l'éternelle jeunesse et un renouvellement constants des œuvres baroques après qu'un renouvelle une méthode de travail que celle de l'interprétation trouve aujourd'hui les jeunes artistes.

La Grande Ecurie donne son nom à l'ensemble créé en 1967 par Jean-Claude Malgoire, qui a décrété après 1974 aux grandes pages lyriques de Monteverdi, Lully, Charpentier, Rameau, Gluck, Mozart. La fondation de l'Atelier Lyrique de Tourcoing en 1981 a été une étape importante. Outre une production scénique des œuvres baroques, l'ensemble produit une transcription régulière des opéras de 17e et 18e siècles à partir de la belle tradition anccestraux, continu le renouvellement constante de l'interprétation de ces œuvres.

L'Association des Grandes Écuries et La Chambre du Roy est subventionnée par le Ministère de la Culture / DRAC Bretagne, Le Conseil Général de Bretagne, La Caisse Régionale des Monuments Historiques de la Fondation Electricité de France.

### Biographie de Jean-Claude Malgoire

Jean-Claude Malgoire was born in Avignon, and began his musical studies there. Having started his career as a chamber musician, he became interested in problems of interpretation in contemporary music and recorded a recital of works by Holliger, Castiglioni, Shinoara, amongst others, which won several international prizes including the prize of 1968. In 1972, he was chosen by Bruno Maderna as a soloist with the European Contemporary Music Ensemble. He was noticed by Charles Munch, who appointed him to one of the favourite conductors of the Orchestre de Paris, where he became one of the preferred conductors.

## Back cover text (English)

Here at last is a version that frees *The Art of Fugue* from the theoretical straitjacket in which this sublime work has all too often been imprisoned. Jean-Claude Malgoire has had the excellent idea of producing a realisation for the rich instrumentarium available to Bach. This recording constitutes the finest possible introduction to the celebration of the 'Golden Jubilee' of an artist of genius: Jean-Claude Malgoire, who has done so much for Baroque music over the past fifty years.
