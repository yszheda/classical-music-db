## Singers Biographies (English)

### THE SINGERS

**Delmas, Jean François, bass**
was born at Lyon in 1861, and died in 1933. He studied with Olbi at the Paris Conservatory. With first prizes in singing and in opera in 1886, he made his debut the same year as Saint-Bris in *Les Huguenots* at the Opéra, where he stayed until 1927. His repertoire there included the Landgraf, Wotan, Gurnemanz, Hans Sachs and Kurwenal, Athanaël (Thaïs), a creator's role, Méphistophélès (Faust and La Damnation de Faust), Leporello, Hagen (Sigurd), Frère Laurent (Romeo et Juliette), Don Diègue (Le Cid) and numerous others.

At Monte Carlo, in 1909, he sang Wotan, in the complete Ring, in French, with Litvinne, Van Dyck, Rousselière and others. In 1912, he appeared as the Boyar Afanassie, in *Bizarne's Ivan the Terrible*. Delmas was a frequent soloist with the Orchestra of the Société des Concerts du Conservatoire. He recorded for G&T, gave Zonophone and Fonotipia. International Zonophone for G&T and are presented here.

**Héglon, Meyriane, contralto**
was born on 21 June 1867, in Brussels, and died there in 1942. She studied with Marie Sasse, Rosine Laborde, MM Barbot, Tequi and Obin. She made her debut at the Paris Opéra, November 1890, as Amneris in *Aida*. Among her roles there were Uta (Sigurd), Walküre, Dalila (Samson et Dalila), the première of *Thaïs*, with Sibyl Sanderson and J.F. Delmas. She made her debut in 1899, she sang Dalila, with Alvarez and Renaud, creation, with the same company in 1907 *Théodora* (Xavier Leroux), with Rousselière and Alvarez, the same role at the Opéra as David in *Le roi d'Ys*.

From 1894 she frequently engaged as soloist with the Orchestra of the Société des Concerts du Conservatoire, Paris. In 1901, she sang Amneris in *Aida*, with Melchissedec; Queen Elizabeth in *Amy Robsart*, with the same cast as in 1898; and the title role in *Messaline*, with Tamagno and Melchissedec and Soulacroix: a world creation. In 1904, she sang Dalila, with Alvarez and Renaud; in *Hélène*, by Saint-Saëns, with Melba Georges, that creation. In 1905 she gave the rôle of La Vougne from Cardonne, with Rousselière created by Xavier Leroux. She sang at the Théâtre de la Monnaie, Brussels, in 1894-1902.

From 1894 Mme Héglon was frequently engaged as soloist with the Orchestra of the Société des Concerts du Conservatoire, Paris. She married the composer Xavier Leroux, with Rousselière and created in 1899 the title role in *Théodora* and married Jean Périer.

**Lafitte, Léon (tenor)**
He was born at Saint-Geniès (Haute-Garonne) in 1875 and died in Paris in 1938. At the Paris Conservatory, he obtained second prize in opera and in a first access it in opéra-comique. In the same year (1898), he made his debut at *Messinger*. Among his other roles: Faust (Faust), Don Ottavio, Raoul (Les Huguenots), the Duke Radamès Rodrigue, Samson, Loge (Das Rheingold), Lohengrin, Parsifal, Roméo, Mephistopheles (Faust), Don Ottavio, Raoul (Les Huguenots). He sang regularly at La Monnaie, Brussels, from 1899 to 1914. In Monte Carlo, in 1904, he sang Pyramus et Thisbé, by Trémisot, with Pyrame et Thisbé, by the composer Xaviel Leroux.

**Simon-Girard, Juliette (soprano)**
Daughter of the soprano Caroline Simon Girard, she was born in Paris in 1859 and died at Nice in 1954. She studied comedy - not singing - at the Paris Conservatory under Régnier and received a first access in 1876. Her long, triumphant career began in 1877 at the Folies Dramatiques, where she made an impressive debut at the creation of Offenbach's *La Foire Saint-Laurent*. In the same year she sang Tambour-Major in Offenbach's *La Fille du Tambour-Major*. In 1879, she sang Saint-Laurent, with creation of Varney's *La Fille de Mme Pomme*. Among her creations she can name, creation, continued to sing until 1914. She recorded only for G&T.

### Translations of 3 Saint-Saëns selections

**Ascanio: The song of Scozzone (Nº 8, Act 2 (Libretto: Louis Gallet))**

Fiorentina! Ah! who calls me? Like a sentinel, who is there?
Nose in the air, between two street posts, it is the noble May!

The moon shows its horns, observing him in such a state, in such a state!
With wide-open eyes he coon a song after his fashion,
"Ah, beautiful Florentine, ah, bella, bella Fiorentina!
Proud gallant graybeard, sing, sing - while I laugh, knowing the pleasant surprise that awaits you at home;
Bunt run! your lady, who is fire and flame, is making the most of her time at your expense!
Is making the most of her time at your expense!
The moon shows its horns, observing him in such a state, in such a state!
"Ah! the cruel Florentins!" Cruda, cruda Fiorentina!
Ah! ah! bella Fiorentina!
Proud gallant graybeard, sing, sing - while I laugh, knowing the pleasant surprise that awaits you at home;
What that heart beat, where nothing remains after your love has been removed!

**La Solitaire (from *Mélodies persanes*, by Armand Renaud)**

O proud young man, o killer of gazelles
pale rider with the velvet gaze,
on your steed whose feet have wings
carry me toward the heaven of loves.
I have often, at night, on my terrace,
and my sobs - you do not hear them,
and my soul stretched my arms to you,
your hair the scent of the open air,
in my breast why does your cruel weapon
piercing a heart with a single glance,
O proud young man, killer of gazelles.
On your horse, with its winged feet, add my sad heart to your blood-stained booty.

**HAROLD BARNES**
