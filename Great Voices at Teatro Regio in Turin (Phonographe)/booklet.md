L'importanza di un teatro si giudica anche dal nome degli interpreti, nel nostro caso i cantanti che vi hanno agito, contribuendo a scriverne la storia. Naturalmente il Regio di Torino non fa eccezione, esibendo un'imponente schieramento canoro che comprende molti tra i nomi piu significativi succedutisi durante due secoli di storia del teatro lirico, dai "castrati" ai grandi tenori, alle famose "primedonne". Non vanno tuttavia taciute le gravi lacune che in questo campo caratterizzano la vicenda del nostro teatro: basti dire che, a parte le assenze di nomi "storici" quali Rubini e la Malibran, Duprez e Angelo Masini, il Regio é I'unico fra i grandi teatri italiani a dover registrare la contemporanea assenza del piu straordinario terzetto del '900 canoro, formato da Caruso, Titta Ruffo e Chaliapin. Cio non toglie che, nonostante questa grave lacuna, I'ultimo mezzo secolo di storia del Regio annoveri un numero di presenze qualificate, ventidue delle quali -non necessariamente tutte celeberrime, ma che di tale storia costituiscono altrettanti paragrafi - sono state raccolte in questo CD che il Teatro Regio ha voluto dedicare a se stesso. Evidentemente e ampiamente giustificato I'intento: farsi ricordare, sia a coloro che ne hanno ibene n mente I'importanza, sia agli immemori, che sono certamente il piu, fuori ma anche in Torino. Alla luce di questa finalita tali presenze sono sufficienti a offrire, grazie al fondamentale rapporto fonografico, un significativo "spaccato" di vita teatrale italiana: anzi doppiamente significativo, poiché la successione di nomi importanti, alcuni dei quali rappresentano altrettanti punti fermi nella storia della vocalita, si interseca con titoli di opere a loro volta legati da alcune scelte di repertorio indicative per capire I'ambito artistico culturale entro il quale si muoveva il nostro teatro lirico durante il cinquantennio 1880 - 1930. Cosi, ad esempio, non é un caso che la presenza di Francesco Tamagno - insostituibile in un documento dedicato al Regio (anche se in fondo egli vi canto pochissimo) - si sposi a quella, oggi dimenticata di Meyerbeer: essa dimostra infatti I'appartenenza schiettamente ottocentesca del grande tenore torinese, le cui qualita di colore e di accento, di timbro e di squillo, nonostante la primordiale tecnica d'incisione, si avvertono in codesta pagina de ll Profeta. In realta, alla fantasia popolare Tamagno evoca soprattutto Otello, personaggio che tuttavia, paradossalmente, egli non interpreto mai al Regio, lasciando questo privilegio a un altro tenore piemontese, Giovanni Battista De Negri,
interprete di classe elogiato persino dallo scorbutico autore. Otello é opera che da un lato richiama automaticamente quei musicisti cresciuti all'ombra del tardo Verdi, e dall'altro, con riferimento alla sua vocalita, anticipa I'opera "verista". Ecco dunque Catalani, con Loreley, opera oggi dimenticata al pari del suo autore, ma che allora, dopo aver visto la luce al Regio nel febbraio 1890, vi compare altre tre volte durante il successivo quarto di secolo. In due di queste occasioni protagonista é la polacca Salomea Krusceniski (italianizzazione dell'originale Kruszelincka), tecnicamente e stilisticamente ben dotata, autentica "'primadonna" dispensatrice del fascino slavo dall'alto di un'avvenenza e di un'intelligenza sofisticata quasi senza pari. Ma la Krusceniski é anche colei che aveva portato al trionfo bresciano nel maggio 1904 (poi ribadito nel gennaio 1906 al Regio) la Madama Butterfly fischiata tre mesi prima alla Scala. E' questo un riferimento che ci riporta ovviamente a uno dei vanti storici del Regio: avere avviato alla celebrita Puccini, tenendone a battesimo i primi due capolavori, Manon Lescaut e La Boheme. Puntualmente ne ritroviamo gli echi nel cimelio di Cesira Ferranti la prima Mimi, e nella sua pienezza canora di Rosetta Pampanini, aureo timbro pucciniano se mai ce n'é stato uno. Pucciniana va senza dubbio considerata anche Rosina Storchio, una delle cantanti "storiche" italiane di questo secolo, che pero ritroviamo qui nella versione, peraltro a lei decisamente congeniale, di primadonna buffa. GREAT VOICES TEATRO KEGIO TRIN

Salomea Krusceniski
It is possible to iudge the importance of a theatre by the names of the people who have performed in it - in this specific case, the singers who have played their part in writing the history of Turin's Teatro Regio. Some of the greatest singers of the last two centuries of opera history - castrati, great tenors, primedonne - have trod its boards. It is also true that many other greats have not. Legends such as Rubini, Malibran, Duprez and Angelo Masini are conspicuous by their absence, and the Regio is the only great Italian opera hause never to have been graced by the presence of the three true greats of 2Oth century opera, Caruso, Titta Ruffo and Chaliaplin. Be that as it may, over the last fifty years greats and not so-greats alike have sung at the Regio and performances by twenty two of them are included on this CD in which the theatre is fittingly celebrating its 250th anniversary. The intention is clear and well justified. The theatre wishes to be remembered both by those who acknowledge its importance and by those who do not, most likely the majority outside of Turin but also in the city itself. With this aim in mind, the singers presented on the CD provide a significant insight into Italian theatrical life. Some would say doubly significant inasmuch as each singer embodies a given period in opera history and he works presented are selected from repertoires which reflect shifts in the artistic and cultural tendencies of Italian opera in the period between 1880 and 1930.

Here his openly nineteenth century style is most apparent. Despite the dated recording technique, his colour and accentuation, timbre and shirillness shine through in this piece from ll Profeta. The popular imagination normally associates Tamagno with Othello, a character which, perhaps paradoxically, he never played at the Regio. He left the privilege to the another Piedmontese tenor, the great Giovanni Battista De Negri, who was praised by none other than cantankerous Verdi himself. Othello is an opera which automatically evokes the memory of the singers who grew up in the shadow of the later Verdi. Vocally speaking, however, it anticipates "verismo". An example of this is Catalani in Loreley, an opera which has now been forgotten


---

along with its composer, but which in the twenty five years following its premiere at the Regio in February 1890, received three more productions. On two of the three occasions is starred the technically and stylistically well endowed Polish soprano Salomea Kruszelnicka, a veritable "primadonna" with her typically Slav good looks and unique sophistication. It was she who was responsible for the triumph of Madama Butterfly in Brescia in May 1904 (and in January 1906 at the Regio itself) after the premiere had met with whistles of disapproval at La Scala three months earlier. All of which brings us to one of the Regio's proudest boasts: that of having helped Puccini on his way to celebrity by premiering his first two masterpieces, Manon Lescaut and La Boheme. And of course we find echoes of these events in the priceless curio of Cesira Ferranti, the first Mimi, and in the glorious voice of Rosetta Pampanini, a golden Puccinian timbre if ever there was one. Another Puccini specialist was Rosina Storchio, one of the truly great names in Italian singing this century. Here though we hear her in her beloved role as the "primadonna buffa". Opposite her as Malatesta in the first Don Pasquale staged at the Regio (December 1912) is that extraordinary musical reciter Giuseppe De Luca. He reveals all his versatility in what is the piece de resistance of this CD, a perfect rendering of Don Carlos, an opera he interpreted during that same 1912-13 season. It is no concidence that there are a number of baritone tracks on the CD. This was the timbre dearest to Verdi's hear: indeed three of the five tracks from Verdi feature baritones -De Luca himself, Mariano Stabile, whose masterful Falstaff cannot be praised enough and that singer of Verdi "par excellence" Carlo Galeffi, here in a passionately moving - perhaps excessively so! "Eri tu". Wagner and Rossini baritone arias are also to be heard on the CD. "Wotan's goodbye"is sung by Luigi Rossi Morelli, one of the leading Italian interpreters of Wagner, and Riccardo Stracciari also appears as an exceptional Figaro in partnership with Fernando Carpi, a graceful tenor, the forerunner of the equally skilful Dino Borgioli in Faust, a cornerstone of the French repertoire so much in vogue in Turin, and the Rossinian Giovanni Manurita . These latter two singers were, with the one and only Pertile, the true darlings of audiences at the Regio.The theatre's love affair with Rossini does not end here. Albeit somewhat dated tecnically, none other than Conchita Supervia, the only mezzosoprano on the CD,
lays claim to recognition as a precursor of the so called "Rossini Renaissance" with her rendering of the rondo from Cenerentola. The splendid "Puritan" Ezio Pinza, with De Angelis and Pasero one of the three leading Italian basses this century, is also the sole representative of this category. Along with Bellini, the "serious" side of Donizetti is also represented in the form of the final aria from Lucrezia Borgia sung by Ester Mazzoleni, without doubt one of the legends of the Regio. She is by no means the only dramatic soprano on the CD. But in a numerous group, which includes names such as Russ, Burzio, Arangi Lombardi and Cigna, xenophilia compels us to grant pride of place to Dame Eva Turner., the greatest British singer this century. At the Regio she sang mainly Wagnerian parts invariably opposite the Basque tenor with antifascist inclinations Isidoro (de) Fagoaga,

on the CD in the role of Siegfried in Gotterdammerung. Maria Caniglia, who possessed the most beautiful soprano voice prior to the Tebaldi era, also interprets Wagner on the CD. She was discovered by talent scout Baron Paolo Mazzonis di Pralafera in 1930 and returned to the Regio in 1933 as Elsa of Brabant opposite Pertile's Lohengrin. Yet the female voice we identify most with the Regio - at least until the late 4Os - was of course that of Toti Dal Monte, "la Toti", here in a touching interpretation of Donizetti' Linda. Her male equivalent was Aureliano Pertile of Padua, to whom a place of honour is also reserved on the CD. Pertile was without doubt one of the great singers at the Regio and not surprisingly he was nicknamed "il tenore del Regio". Here we hear him in all his famed versatility in an incredibly inspired, universally acclaimed Lohengrin - a fitting end to a great theatre.
