### Programme Notes by Walter Labhart

#### English Version

**George Enescu and his French Contemporaries**

Until now the important contribution of the Romanian composer, violin virtuoso, pianist, conductor and music teacher George Enescu (George Enesco, 1881-1955) to the arts of his second homeland, France, has been recognized only in passing. Together with the sculptor Constantin Brâncuși (1876-1957), the painter Irina Iteanu (Ileana, 1896-1963) and the painter Marcel Janco (Janco, 1895-1984), who both made significant contributions to the Dadaism movement, Enescu was regarded in Paris as one of the most important Romanian pillars of cultural exchange in the French capital.

Unlike his Paris-based compatriots Brâncuși and Filip Lazăr (1894-1956), who in 1928 co-founded the progressive Parisian-based artists' society "Triton", Enescu had a lasting influence on the Paris music scene. He took valuable inspiration from three contemporary Romanian pianists: Clara Haskil (1895-1960), Dinu Lipatti (1917-1950), and Victor de Sabata. Having moved to Paris in 1895, Enescu focused as a conductor mainly on the works of Claude Debussy, Paul Dukas, Gabriel Fauré, Edouard Lalo and Maurice Ravel. Claude Debussy's violin sonata was among the works included in his repertoire.

Through Maurice Ravel, Enescu became acquainted with fellow pupils at the Paris Conservatoire in the composition class of Gabriel Fauré (1845-1924). In 1922, along with Alfred Cortot and Jacques Thibaud, Enescu became one of the first members of the newly founded École Normale de Musique de Paris. His close colleagues included André Caplet, Gabriel Grovlez, Charles Koechlin, Paul Ladmirault, Raoul Laparra, Florent Schmitt, Roland-Manuel and Florent Schmitt.

Ravel's most challenging piano work, *Gaspard de la Nuit* (1908), based on the poetry of his compatriot Aloysius Bertrand, was described by the composer himself as "three Romantic caricature poems". The greatest of his virtuosity is found in *Ondine*, an uncompromising song of poignantly unostentatious simplicity. *Le Gibet* features full-blooded bell chimes depicting the macabre vision of a gibbet at sunset, with its hauntingly persistent bell ostinato. *Scarbo*, the spectral dwarf, depicts nightmarish effects with insistent repetition of obsessive outbursts, sequences of seconds chasing after one another and needle-sharp grace notes.

*La Valse* is a choreographic poem that Ravel dedicated to his own work. As he recalled in his *Autobiographical Sketch* (1928): "I conceived this work as a sort of apotheosis of the Viennese waltz, mingled with the impression of a fantastic, fatal whirling." Besides the orchestral version as a "poème chorégraphique", Ravel also arranged versions for two pianos and for piano solo.

Debussy's *Douze Études* (twelve études) were written in 1915, dedicated "à la mémoire de Frédéric Chopin". The études explore various technical challenges: No. 1 for five fingers (after Czerny), No. 2 for thirds, No. 3 for fourths, No. 4 for sixths, No. 5 for octaves, No. 6 for eight fingers, No. 7 for chromatic degrees, No. 8 for ornaments, No. 9 for repeated notes, No. 10 for opposite sonorities, No. 11 for compound arpeggios, and No. 12 for chords.

Two of Enescu's early piano works appear here: the *Vierstimmige Fuge* (Four-voice Fugue) from 1897, and the *Rhapsodie Roumaine No. 1*, originally written for orchestra in 1898. The piano transcription loses none of the original orchestral effect, based on two Romanian folk songs.

Marcel Mihalovici (1898-1985), born in Romania, moved to Paris in 1919 on the advice of George Enescu. In the French capital he received further training at the Schola Cantorum under Vincent d'Indy. His *5 Bagatelles op. 27* demonstrate his neoclassical style with influences from Romanian folk music and the Parisian "Groupe des Six".

#### German Version

**George Enescu und seine französischen Zeitgenossen**

Bisher wurde die Bedeutung des rumänischen Komponisten, Violinisten, Pianisten, Dirigenten und Musikpädagogen George Enescu (George Enesco, 1881-1955) für die Musik seiner zweiten Heimat Frankreich nur beiläufig wahrgenommen. Zusammen mit dem Bildhauer Constantin Brâncuși (1876-1957), der Malerin Irina Iteanu (1896-1963) und dem Maler Marcel Janco (1895-1984), die beide maßgeblich zur Entwicklung des Dadaismus beitrugen, wurde Enescu in Paris als eine der wichtigsten rumänischen Stützen des Kulturaustauschs in der französischen Metropole angesehen.

Im Gegensatz zu seinen ebenfalls in Paris ansässigen Landsleuten Brâncuși und Filip Lazăr (1894-1956), der 1928 Mitbegründer der progressiven Künstlergesellschaft "Triton" wurde, übte Enescu einen nachhaltigen Einfluss auf das Musikleben der Seinemetropole aus. Als prägende Vorbilder wirkten vor allem drei zeitgenössische rumänische Pianisten auf ihn: Clara Haskil (1895-1960), Dinu Lipatti (1917-1950). Schon 1895 nach Paris übersiedelt, setzte sich Enescu als Dirigent besonders für Claude Debussy, Paul Dukas, Gabriel Fauré, Edouard Lalo und Maurice Ravel ein.

Durch seinen Förderer Maurice Ravel lernte Enescu als Mitschüler in der Kompositionsklasse von Gabriel Fauré (1845-1924) am Pariser Konservatorium kennen. 1922 wurde er zusammen mit Alfred Cortot und Jacques Thibaud eines der ersten Mitglieder der neu gegründeten École Normale de Musique de Paris.

Ravels herausforderndes Klavierwerk *Gaspard de la Nuit* (1908), basierend auf der Poesie seines Landsmanns Aloysius Bertrand, wurde vom Komponisten selbst als "drei romantische Karikaturgedichte" beschrieben. *Ondine* ist ein kompromissloses Lied von ergreifender Schlichtheit. *Le Gibet* zeigt voller Klangglocken, die die makabre Vision eines Schafotts bei Sonnenuntergang darstellen. *Scarbo* der spektrale Zwerg, zeigt alptraumhafte Effekte mit beharrlicher Wiederholung.

*La Valse* ist ein choreografisches Gedicht, das Ravel seinem eigenen Werk widmete. In seiner autobiografischen Skizze (1928) erinnerte er sich: "Ich konzipierte dieses Werk als eine Art Apotheose des Wiener Walzers, vermischt mit dem Eindruck eines fantastischen, fatalen Wirbelns."

Debussys *Douze Études* (zwölf Etüden) wurden 1915 geschrieben und sind "Andenken an Frédéric Chopin" gewidmet.

Marcel Mihalovici (1898-1985), in Rumänien geboren, zog 1919 auf Rat von George Enescu nach Paris. Seine *5 Bagatellen op. 27* demonstrieren seinen neoklassizistischen Stil mit Einflüssen aus rumänischer Volksmusik und der Pariser "Groupe des Six".

### Artist Biography - Luiza Borac

Acclaimed by the international music press as a "virtuoso of astonishing brilliance" and "thoroughly poetic artist" (Dr Joachim Kaiser, *Süddeutsche Zeitung*), Luiza Borac has established herself as one of the most charismatic artists of her generation. Born in Campia Turzii, Romania, the pianist has won over critics and audiences alike, including First Prize in the Mendelssohn Competition in Berlin and the Gold medal in the International Music Competition in Italy, the silver medal at the Gina Bachauer International Piano Competition in Salt Lake City, the debut prize at Carnegie Hall, New York, the Prix du Disque, the Grand Prix and the audience prize in the International Edvard Grieg piano competition in Oslo.

Her pioneering project to record all the piano works of George Enescu won the much-sought-after BBC Music Award in 2007 as "superbly, idiomatically played, very heartily recommended" (BBC Music Magazine), while the International Record Review described it as "a benchmark for future interpretations".

Luiza Borac regularly performs in Europe and the USA. She has given concerts at such places as Walt Disney Hall in Los Angeles, the Amsterdam Concertgebouw, the Konzerthaus in Stockholm, the Schleswig-Holstein Music Festival, the Edinburgh Festival, the Oslo Music Festival and London's Queen Elizabeth Hall. She has worked with such artists as Vladimir Jurowski, Murray Perahia and Radu Lupu. Luiza Borac has been celebrated as "a dream of a pianist" (Rondo), "a worthy ambassador for a genius" (Music & Vision) and as a pianist of "subtle but absolute mastery" (BBC Music Magazine). Gramophone has commented that "Luiza Borac creates an extraordinary sense of identification with the music, a revelation".

A musician of great sensitivity with an internationally recognized career, she supports various charitable projects. Such projects include *Music in Our Schools* run by the Bachauer Foundation, the MENORAH project for the Jewish victims of the Schaumburg-Lippe in North Germany and a Romanian charity which supports orphanages and street children in the artist's native country. The UNESCO city of Alba has awarded her the honorific title of "Ambassadrice de la Cité épiscopale" and also its medal of honour in recognition of her involvement in *Polifonia*, an international project run by Rotary with the aim of eradicating polio.

Her double CD releases, featuring compositions by the legendary pianist Dinu Lipatti, forms part of her collaboration with the Academy of St Martin in the Fields and includes the first-ever recordings of several works. This release received a five-star rating from BBC Music Magazine, four stars from Gramophone and 18 stars, and was rated best CD in the bi-monthly publication *Piano News*.

In 2014 Luiza Borac obtained her doctorate research into the piano works of George Enescu at the National University of Music Bucharest with the highest distinction (summa cum laude).
