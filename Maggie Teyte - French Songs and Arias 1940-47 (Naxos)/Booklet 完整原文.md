### Page 2-3: Maggie Teyte Biography

**Great Singers • Maggie Teyte (1888-1976)**
**French Songs and Arias**

In the introduction to her autobiography *Star on the Door* (Putnam, 1938) Maggie Teyte relates an incident from her early days as a singer in the USA:

"I was given a dressing-room with a star painted on the door, which was the custom in those days. But when I saw it I said to the attendant: 'I'm not good enough to put my initials on the large mirror.'

'Twinkle, twinkle little star,
What a pity that mine stars are not scrubbed on their doors!'"

This story demonstrates Teyte's level-headed approach to her singing. Her success in America was never under any illusions about the hard work that would be needed to achieve it and so she worked. The excerpts from *The Standard Hour* recital and recordings made in London with Gerald Moore represent that aspect of her career for which she is now best remembered as an exponent of French mélodie and opera. With the exception of the last of the items recorded with Moore, Teyte sang all the roles in which she excelled. She knew well Claude Debussy's *Pelléas et Mélisande* which she had learned with the composer's highest regard for Ravel (1875-1937) and heard much from him about interpretation.

It was as Mélisande that Maggie Teyte began her career. In 1907, when still in her teens, she repeated many times. 'Learn to sing Mozart well, and you will take care of the rest' – Chausson was an early role; she sang it at a Mozart Festival in Paris when she was fourteen. This rendition of his arietta [Track 18] may lack the boyish freshness that was so highly praised in those days. But it is a worthy memory of her love for this music.

The selection from *Mignon* by Thomas (1811-1896) is a reminder that she studied the title role of *Mignon* whilst still a student at the Opéra-Comique in Paris, since in her success in Debussy's *Pelléas et Mélisande*, in the latter work she provided a worthy successor to Mary Garden, Mélisande's creator of 1902, and is remembered here by two excerpts.

The first track [1] is Geneviève's reading of Golaud's letter to Pelléas (scene 1.2), in the scene in which the lovers meet illicitly by the castle fountain. In this, rather than lingering on her own lines performed by the same singer, but sung by *deux* of her friend Dinamora's presentation of the text, maintains the emotional tension throughout this scene. In *Pelléas et Mélisande*, an enigmatic close to this unconventional love triangle. Teyte had originally been understudy for the role of Mélisande for a period, now usually assigned to a mezzo-soprano, since the soprano carries the mark of authenticity. In the songs, too, Teyte had precision of word. If there has never been anything indefinite about Debussy's music, he always had precise instructions.

Hahn is considered to have brought Hahn himself his affection for the man and his music, and she imbues the music with warmth and understanding. It was a highly-charged, a considerable charm himself. Hahn's music has precision of word and a limpid soprano when she commented on the tempo of one of his songs: "You play it quicker than I thought, monsieur, but I like it. You play it as you sing it, you will always be in style." The musical comedy *Mozart* was first performed in Paris in 1925, *Ciboulette* two years earlier.

**Maggie Teyte**

Maggie Teyte was born in Wolverhampton in 1888. She was a pupil of Jean de Reszke in Paris and made her debut in 1906, at 18. She was 16 when she changed the spelling of her name to Teyte, in order to avoid the pronunciation. Teyte achieved great success as Musette Cerfa in *La Opéra-Comique*; on her return to England she established herself as a regular artist at the Opéra-Comique and then as the first British singer to sing leading operatic roles at the Metropolitan Opera. Teyte's career included roles of Butterfly, Mimi, Mélisande and Hänsel; she was particularly celebrated in the French repertory and was one of the few singers to create new works by contemporary composers. Her association with Debussy began when she joined the Opera-Comique in 1906 and she became one of his most trusted interpreters. She appeared in many operas and concert recitals, appearing throughout Britain and making many recitals, some with the pianist Alfred Cortot as her accompanist. She made many records. Teyte's last public performance was in a concert of songs by Debussy, Fauré, Hahn and Chausson in London in 1966. She died in 1976. Teyte's final performance as Mélisande took place in London in 1956. She was made a Chevalier of the Légion d'honneur in 1957 and appointed CBE in 1958. Her autobiography *Star on the Door* (Putnam, 1938) was reprinted in 1976.

**Pierre Monteux**

Pierre Monteux was born in 1875 and raised in Paris. While a student he played the violin in the first performance of *Pelléas et Mélisande* and in 1911 was appointed conductor of Diaghilev's *Ballets Russes*, directing the premieres of Stravinsky's *Le Sacre du Printemps* and *Le Rossignol*, and Ravel's *Daphnis et Chloé*. He left France for the USA in 1917, and later, in San Francisco. In Europe he worked regularly in Amsterdam, Paris and, towards the end of his life, in London. He died in 1964.

– Paul Campion

### Pierre Monteux

Pierre Monteux was born in 1875 and raised in Paris. While a student he played the violin in the first performance of *Pelléas et Mélisande* and in 1911 was appointed conductor of Diaghilev's *Ballets Russes*, directing the premieres of Stravinsky's *Le Sacre du Printemps* and *Le Rossignol*, and Ravel's *Daphnis et Chloé*. ### Gerald Moore

Gerald Moore was born in 1899 and spent his teenage years in Canada. On returning to England in 1921 he studied the piano with Mark Hambourg and was soon active on the concert circuit and in the recording studio as an accompanist. For over forty years Moore partnered virtually all the world's greatest singers and instrumentalists in live and recorded recitals, forming notable musical associations with Fischer-Dieskau, Schwarzkopf and Janet Baker among others. In later years he spent much time lecturing and writing on the art of the accompanist. Created a CBE in 1954, Gerald Moore died in 1987.

---

### Leslie Heward

Leslie Heward was born in Yorkshire in 1897. After studying at the Royal College of Music he undertook a variety of musical work, later being appointed to important orchestral posts in South Africa. In 1927 he returned to England, soon becoming conductor of the City of Birmingham Orchestra. As well as being a fine orchestral and operatic conductor, Leslie Heward was a composer of considerable promise. He died in 1943.

### Producer's Note

Maggie Teyte's performances on American Radio were limited to an even dozen Bell Telephone Hour shows between 1945 and 1949 and to her one 1947 Standard Hour programme, reproduced on this disc (Tracks 1-2 and 18). While not up to the highest technical standards of the period, these recordings are released for their rare historical and artistic value. As far as is known, this is their first release since 1947.

The Naxos historical label aims to make available the greatest recordings in the history of recorded music, in the best and truest sound that contemporary technology can provide. To achieve this aim, Naxos has engaged a number of respected restorers who have the dedication, skill and experience to produce restorations that have set new standards in the field of historical recordings.
