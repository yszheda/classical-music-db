## Historical Note

This historical edition of *Bohème* now on CD was recorded in June, 1929 in the studios of Columbia in Milan. It was the second time the entire masterpiece by Puccini had been recorded by means of the electrical process. The very first electrical edition had appeared on the international market just the year before on initiative of Columbia's directors, with Cesare Sabajno as conductor. That historic edition, featuring singers such as Aristide Baracchi at the end of his career, Rosina Torri, a very delicate artist, who unfortunately forgotten, and Ernesto Badini, the famous popular character baritone of those days. The conductor, of course, was Carlo Sabajno, one of the first tenors in an outstanding conducting history since its very beginning.

But Columbia decided to include the most popular opera by Puccini in its catalogue; they felt they had to do more and better than the first competitors and for this they secured the presence of Rosetta Pampanini (Milan, 1896-1973), that is to say a real diva who had triumphed in the years an undisputed success in the lyric repertoire. Thirty-three years old, professionally active for nine years, Pampanini was internationally considered the most exquisite Butterfly and Toscanini had invited her to sing the role of Cio-Cio-San at La Scala in 1925, twenty-four years after the de-

vastating flop of the première. Pampanini succeeded to resuscitate this opera in the eyes of critics and enthusiasts, turned it to a town and achieved a personal, unforgettable success.

This singer was also appreciated as one of the most brilliant and talented sopranos in *Tosca*, *Zazà*, *Adriana Lecouvreur* and her success was not confined to the national scene; in Trieste, Lausanne, San Carlo in Naples, Opera in Rome, Comique in Florence, Carlo Felice in Genoa, the most important theatres especially in the hard-to-satisfy audiences of Rome and Milan. London at Covent Garden in 1924 was to win triumphs also in Barcelona, Vienna and in all the most important theatres in the world.

Such a wide approval was more than deserved: Pampanini could boast a wonderful voice and a naturalness of her art, polished, shining sounds of exception beauty that never excited the audience, however, was the splendid spontaneity of her singing, emphasized by a calm self-assurance and great art in pantomime. So Mimì came out in Pampanini with a surprising credibility, both in the light-hearted climate of the first encounter and in the following anguish, both in the end reality is revealed.

Pampanini is supported in this record by a small world of simple souls who live, rejoice and suffer, animated by a group of high-level professionals, all of them backed by a long artistic experience. Tancredi Pasero (Ascoli Piceno, 1884-1942) was quite well known in the sector and was sincerely esteemed also by Beniamino Gigli, the great Renascment of his time. Also Marini's voice was naturally endowed; besides, he studied with great diligence and he stood out both for his singular timbre, clear and shining, and his solid vocal training and the fine sense of measure and aptitude for the natural care he showed in expressing the risky difficulties of the great role that in *La Bohème* is that of Rodolfo, parts for which he was considered a hardy and especially specialist, even in a time so rich in great ours as those of the twenties.
