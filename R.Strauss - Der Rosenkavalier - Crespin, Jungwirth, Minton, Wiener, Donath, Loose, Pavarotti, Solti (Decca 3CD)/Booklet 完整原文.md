### 第 14-15 页 - HIPPO_20250108_0011.jpg

*(Article continues - "Time is a strange thing..." by Michael Kennedy)*

In all three cases, arrogance is not the right word. It was simply high self-esteem, the knowledge that he had supreme gifts.

In September 1910 — the date of *Der Rosenkavalier* was still a matter of private correspondence between Richard Strauss and Hugo von Hofmannsthal — the Dresden court had little idea of the kind of opera they had commissioned. The great Max Reinhardt was called in to save the premiere. The press was given no hint about the programme.

The two creators wrote of the characters they had created almost as if they knew them personally. The Marquise was the key to the whole work; Strauss wrote that she was the woman above all others, the figure with whom they and the audience sympathised. She is not even thirty-two years of age when she is a sad woman, a woman who has lost her old lover. There will be other lovers for her after Octavian. But she will never love again.

Hofmannsthal described Octavian in these words: 'One has to imagine a girl of about seventeen who is in love with the Marquise... this is the whole point of the story, that a young girl is the lover of a man of thirty-two, the age of Octavian.'

Two months later Hofmannsthal, at his home in Rodaun outside Vienna, sent Strauss the first scene of what was originally entitled *Ochs von Lerchenau*. The composer was highly enthusiastic: '... it has inspired me old and mellow butter! Hofmannsthal's next scene was delivered within a few days, and, as he had planned, the scene between Ochs and Faninal (with a comic underplot involving Valzacchi and Annina) followed.

### 第 16-19 页 - HIPPO_20250108_0012.jpg & HIPPO_20250108_0013.jpg

*(Article continues - "Past and Present in 'Der Rosenkavalier'")*

The first performance of *Der Rosenkavalier* took place at the Dresden Court Opera on 26 January 1911. The conductor was Ernst von Schuch. The cast included Margarethe Siems (the Marschallin), Eva von der Osten (Octavian), Minnie Nast (Sophie), and Carl Perron (Baron Ochs).

The opera was an immediate success. Within a year it was performed in over 50 cities. The Vienna premiere took place on 8 February 1911 at the Court Opera, with Marie Gutheil-Schoder as Octavian and Margarethe Siems as the Marschallin.

### 第 20-23 页 - HIPPO_20250108_0014.jpg

**Synopsis**

**Act One**

The bedroom of the Feldmarschallin Fürstin von Werdenberg. She has spent the night with her young lover, Octavian. As the curtain rises, the light of a new day is dawning. Octavian wakes and looks at the Marschallin. She is still asleep. He goes to the window and looks out. The Marschallin wakes and calls him to her. They embrace.

The Marschallin tells Octavian that she must go to church. He asks if he may come with her. She says no, but promises to send for him later. Octavian leaves. The Marschallin calls her maid to dress her.

Baron Ochs enters. He has come to ask the Marschallin to recommend a young man to carry the ceremonial rose to his betrothed, Sophie, the daughter of Faninal, a rich merchant who has just been ennobled. The Marschallin suggests her cousin Octavian. Ochs agrees.

Ochs sends a note to his servant to bring Annina, a woman with whom he has had a brief affair. Annina enters and demands money. Ochs refuses. She threatens to expose him. He throws her out.

Octavian returns, disguised as a maid. Ochs flirts with him/her, not realizing it is the same person he met earlier. The Marschallin watches with amusement. Ochs leaves.

The Marschallin and Octavian embrace. She tells him that one day he will leave her for a younger woman. He protests his undying love. She sends him away with the silver rose.

---

**Act Two**

Faninal's house. Sophie is awaiting the arrival of the Rosenkavalier. Faninal enters and tells her that the Baron Ochs will be her husband. Sophie is horrified but obeys her father.

Octavian enters, carrying the rose. Sophie and Octavian fall in love at first sight. Baron Ochs enters and begins to court Sophie, behaving with vulgar familiarity. Sophie rejects him.

Valzacchi and Annina enter with a plan to expose Ochs. They create a scene in which Ochs is compromised. Faninal is furious with Sophie.

Octavian wounds Ochs in a mock duel. Ochs calls for help. The police commissioner arrives. Ochs tries to implicate Octavian, but the commissioner is suspicious.

---

**Act Three**

A private room in an inn. Ochs has summoned Annina to meet him. Instead, Octavian appears, disguised as a widow. Ochs falls for the 'widow' immediately.

Valzacchi and Annina bring false witnesses to swear that the 'widow' is Ochs's mistress and that they have children together. The police commissioner arrives.

Faninal enters with Sophie. She refuses to marry Ochs. The Marschallin arrives. She recognizes Octavian and understands the situation. She tells Ochs that he must release Sophie.

Ochs eventually agrees. The Marschallin leaves. Octavian and Sophie are united. The Marschallin has sacrificed her own happiness for the sake of the young lovers.
