## Page 10 (scans/HIPPO_20210726_0010.jpg) - English translation of liner notes

### Anton Bruckner and his Third Symphony

by Dr. Klaus Ulrich Schuster (translated by Stewart Spencer)

Anton Bruckner's Third Symphony was composed in 1873-74, and revised in 1877. Bruckner dedicated it to Richard Wagner, calling it "my Tribute to the Master". It is one of Bruckner's most extraordinary symphonies, with its grand scale and extraordinary harmonic and melodic invention. The 1877 version performed here is the most concise and concentrated version of the work.

The arrangement for piano four hands was prepared by the composer himself. Bruckner was an excellent organist and pianist, and he often prepared four-hand arrangements of his symphonies himself. This tradition allows the work to be studied and enjoyed in the domestic sphere, and it also gives us a different perspective on Bruckner's contrapuntal and harmonic thinking, stripped of the orchestral color and focused entirely on the structure and the interplay of the themes.

## Page 11 (scans/HIPPO_20210726_0011.jpg) - English translation continued

This recording by the Trenkner-Speidel piano duo presents Bruckner's Third Symphony in this wonderful piano four-hand version, allowing us to hear the work with fresh ears and appreciate the organic structure of Bruckner's thinking. The piano duo brings clarity and transparency to Bruckner's polyphonic writing, allowing each line to speak clearly while maintaining the grandeur and intensity of the symphony.

## Page 12 (scans/HIPPO_20210726_0012.jpg) - English translation of biographies

### Piano Duo Trenkner-Speidel

Ingrid Trenkner and Wilhelm Speidel have been working together as a piano duo for many years. They have performed throughout Europe and have made several highly praised recordings of the Romantic piano four-hand repertoire. They specialize in the rediscovery of overlooked repertoire, especially the symphonic arrangements for piano four hands from the nineteenth century. Their playing combines clarity of texture with the necessary grandeur and intensity for this large-scale symphonic work.
