## Biography

**Kyra Vayne**

Kyra Vayne was born in Petrograd in 1916. In 1924, after the Revolution, she and her family moved to London. Singing in a Russian choir her talent was noted and she was encouraged to train her voice. She trained with Manlio Di Veroli and later with Mignon Nevada in London. Kyra Vayne began her musical career in the thirties singing in musical comedy and variety. Encouraged to embark on an operatic career Anatole Fistoulari engaged her for the lead in Moussorgsky's "Sorotchinsky Fair" alternating with Oda Slobodskaya in London. Opportunities were limited due to the outbreak of the second World War but further engagements on radio and singing under the baton of Richard Tauber in "Die Fledermaus" followed. Kyra had to wait until the end of the war before commencing on an international career. In the post war period engagements followed in Barcelona at the Liceo in "The Invisible City of Kitezh", Britain, Denmark, Belgium at La Monnaie in "Evgeni Onegin", Holland, Yugoslavia and Italy. In Italy Kyra Vayne had the pleasure of singing Santuzza to Gigli's Turiddu in the tenor's 40th debut anniversary performance in Recanati, his birth place. In Rome she appeared in "Tosca" with Tagliavini at the Teatro dell'Opera in 1956 as well as in "The Stone Guest" at the Florence May Festival in 1954.

During the Sixties Kyra Vayne appeared both on the concert stage and in opera in London and Scandinavia. She presented the recently discovered "Cleopâtre" by Berlioz to London audiences at this time. Her last recital was given in 1965 after which she retired permanently. Kyra Vayne has suffered the fate of many artists who did not record commercially. Her contemporaries hold affectionate memories of her exciting and stylish performances but the younger generation could not gain an impression of a voice that seemed lost to the future. Fortunately Kyra Vayne recently discovered that she owned acetates and tapes of her broadcasts and private recitals. When a CD was released by an Austrian company last year to tremendous acclaim by both critics and public her voice, as she said herself, was reborn.
