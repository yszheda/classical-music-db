## Liner Notes

### SCOPE FOR THE IMAGINATION
**Symphonic Works by Berlioz**

Hector Berlioz was not only one of the most versatile and innovative composers of his time, he was also a brilliant writer of prose. Apart from countless articles and publications, this is demonstrated most impressively by his memoirs, in which the engaging chronicler reveals – despite all the exigencies of his biographer's profession – a document, the book admittedly needs to be used with care: more than once in this invented autobiography Berlioz let his imagination embellish the cold, hard facts.

Some bare facts of his life, however, are easily verifiable: born on 11 December 1803 in La Côte-Saint-André near Grenoble, France, he was originally destined for a medical career. However, his enthusiasm for music and theatre soon prevailed. In 1826 he enrolled at the Paris Conservatoire, where he studied composition with Jean-François Lesueur and Anton Reicha. His fascination with the works of Shakespeare and the actress Harriet Smithson, whom he later married, profoundly influenced his artistic development.

*(Translation: Richard Edmond)*

---

### PIERRE MONTEUX (1875-1964)

Pierre Monteux was born in Paris, where he enrolled at the Conservatoire at the age of twelve. There he studied the violin with Jules Garcin and harmony with Émile Pessard. He began his career as a violinist in various Parisian orchestras, including the Colonne Orchestra and the Lamoureux Orchestra. In 1907, he joined the Ballets Russes of Diaghilev, where he conducted the premiere of Stravinsky's *Le Sacre du printemps* in 1913.

Monteux's career took him to major opera houses and concert halls throughout Europe and America. He served as principal conductor of the London Symphony Orchestra, the San Francisco Symphony, and the Boston Symphony Orchestra. His recordings are celebrated for their warmth, clarity, and rhythmic vitality.

*(Translation: Stefan Kanzer)*

---

### RENÉ LEIBOWITZ

René Leibowitz was the prototype of an intellectual musician. Before performing a work from the past he had always undertaken a meticulous analysis of it. He had a deep feeling of spontaneity—making no concessions to pure feeling, and liked to quote Mahler's famous remark that tradition is simply slovenliness. For him what counted in performance style—was nothing more than slaveishness.

Leibowitz was born in 1913 in Warsaw and died in 1972 in Paris. A Polish composer and conductor, he was one of the principal proponents of the Second Viennese School. He studied with Webern and Ravel. He wrote operas, chamber music, and symphonies, and was a teacher to many prominent musicians including Pierre Boulez.

*(Translation: Richard Edmond)*

---

### WESTMINSTER – AN APPRECIATION AND HISTORY

Few labels from the dawn of the LP are recalled with greater admiration and affection than Westminster Records. Created in 1949 by two New York businessmen and music lover James Grayson, Mosha Naida, assistant of New York's legendary Town Hall, and the conductor Henry Swoboda, its first releases in April 1950 established Westminster as a pioneering purveyor of LPs intended to explore new and exciting corners of the repertoire.

Westminster grew rapidly during the 1950s. Through a partnership with Britain's Nixa Records, the label brought Scherchen to London in September 1953 to record with the London Symphony Orchestra works by Berlioz and Tchaikovsky and the first complete version of Handel's *Messiah* – it was also the first informed by modern ideas of performance practice. In summer 1954, the noted conductor Artur Rodzinski joined the Westminster team, recording in Vienna and then with Beecham's famed Royal Philharmonic Orchestra, appearing on record as the Philharmonic-Symphony Orchestra of London.

*(Translation: Dennis Collins)*
