## SOPRANOS IN THE GRAND TRADITION

---

**R**ecorded music is not a century old — the "modern" era of its history begins roughly with Edison's legendary first interpretation of Rossini's *Barber of Seville* in 1889, when the first fragile metal masters were made to provide us with souvenirs of the great Opera Houses, Concert Halls, theatres and churches of Europe, North and South America.

Bellini and the first Maria Callas and Maria Zamboni's first *Giulio Cesare* in 1926 and the fabled Adelina Patti (1843-1919) were never recorded on commercial discs, but their art is documented at moments of glory by their commercially issued discs, their duets and their trios, and a few recordings of their own made with considerable success in the great opera houses of Europe and America.

The vocalism of Caruso, Farrar and Amelita Galli-Curci, and Luisa Tetrazzini, though these glittering stars are only recorded partial performances and private recordings made between 1902 and 1920, mark the perfection of the *mezzo-soprano* in 1925, when the microphone was introduced and made possible a more faithful balance for singers within a realistic orchestral setting.

This collection presents to life 16 sopranos in the grand tradition who benefited from this crucial development and whose art thrives at a point of intense dramatic and artistic heritage: a series of arias and scenes by some of the most famous and best-loved composers of all time, all recorded between 1926 and 1953, that have seldom been out of a span of a quarter of a century — some 25 years — and all in commercial release during the last quarter of a century.

---

The first three sopranos in our sequence rose to fame as the "modern" stars of Italian Opera in the period following the First World War. **Maria Zamboni** (1892-1976) was a native of Milan and had studied with the celebrated baritone Mattia Battistini. Maria Zamboni (1892-1976), in fact, was the first Italian soprano to be recorded by Columbia's Parlophone label on April 25, 1926. She recorded the aria "Signore, ascolta!" from Puccini's then-new *Turandot* in June of that year.

**Rosetta Pampanini** (1896-1972) was also a famous Puccini interpreter, and she made a celebrated recording of *Madama Butterfly*. She made a recording of *Luisa* in 1927. She was also featured in London and Chicago. These were the first complete operas that Pampanini recorded.

**Toti Dal Monte** (1893-1975) was a native of Castelfranco Veneto and studied with Rosina Storchio in Milan. Dal Monte (1893-1975) was a soprano *leggera*, a coloratura soprano with a clear silvery voice. She had sung Gilda, Violetta, Lucia, Lakmé and Amina (which she portrays here). Her complete recording of *La sonnambula* was made for HMV in 1928 with Lorenzo Molajoli, Beniamino Gigli, fût that Dal Monte. She was also a Amina. The duet had the "celebration" role on others. The total effect was a recording of a lifetime.

---

The next sopranos are also important to Gigli's three complete opera arias under the baton of Molajoli.

**Gina Cigna** (b. 1900) and **Maria Caniglia** (1903-1979) are the two principal Italian dramatic sopranos of their period. Both appeared briefly at the Met (1937-39), but World War II disrupted their international careers. Cigna sang Norma in 1942 at La Scala, the first performance of *Norma* at that house since 1906.

**Pia Tassinari** (b. 1911), though the name exists in the first complete recording of *Mefistofele* with a stellar cast under Franco Ghione, was perhaps the role most closely associated with her. Tassinari's career was limited to Italy. She was also the sister of the conductor Alberto Tassinari.

**Eleanor Steber** (1914-1990) made no introduction to audiences in Europe, but was already famous in the United States. Her recording of "Depuis le jour" was made in 1947 with the Philharmonia Orchestra under Walter Susskind. Steber was a leading soprano at the Metropolitan Opera for over 20 years.

---

The next three sopranos in our sequence rose to international fame during the period following the Second World War.

**Ljuba Welitsch** (1913-1981) inherited Toti Dal Monte's roles as well as her statue at La Scala, where she appeared in several of the same roles. Her warm timbre and exquisite technique served her well in the lead comic roles, though in the postwar years she concentrated on roles closer to her own voice, such as Rusalka, Manon, Nedda, Mimi and Eve. Her Met debut in 1949 as Salome created a sensation not in the Scala because it was, in fact, an opera several years in the making. She appeared successfully at the Metropolitan Opera, the Glyndebourne, as well. A versatile presence in both lyric and coloratura roles, she excelled as Zerbinetta in *Ariadne auf Naxos*. While in the early 1950s, she appeared as Donna Anna in *Don Giovanni* and other roles.

**Margherita Carosio** (1907-2005) inherited Toti Dal Monte's roles as well as her statue at La Scala, where she appeared in several of the same roles. Her warm timbre and exquisite technique served her well in the lead comic roles.

**Kirsten Flagstad** (1895-1962), of course, needs no introduction to audiences in Europe or the United States. Her recording of "When I am laid in earth" from Purcell's *Dido and Aeneas* was made in 1948 with the Philharmonia Orchestra under Warwick Braithwaite.

**Sena Jurinac** (1921-2011) made no introduction to audiences in Europe, but was already famous in the United States. Her recording of "Twill soon be midnight" from Tchaikovsky's *Queen of Spades* was made in 1950 with the Philharmonia Orchestra under Lawrence Collingwood.

**Elisabeth Schwarzkopf** (1915-2006) needs no introduction to audiences in Europe or the United States. Her recording of "Porgi amor" from Mozart's *Le nozze di Figaro* was made in 1950 with the Vienna Philharmonic under Herbert von Karajan.

---

The illustrious roster ends with **Joan Hammond** (1912-1996), a native of Australia and later to become one of the most famous sopranos in Australia. Her recording of "Gluck, das mir verblieb" from Korngold's *Die tote Stadt* was made in 1953 with the Philharmonia Orchestra under Walter Susskind.
