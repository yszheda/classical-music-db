## Weiss, Edward (George)
*(b New York, 6 Sept 1892; d New York, 28 Sept 1984), American Pianist*

Born into a musical German-Nordic family, he grew up in Europe and studied in Berlin with Alexander Rihm and Xaver Scharwenka, through whom he was introduced to Busoni. He began to study with Busoni in 1914, following him to the USA in 1915 and returning with him to Europe, first to Zürich and then back to Berlin at the end of World War I.

In 1921 he made his début with the Berlin PO under Busoni's baton. During his time in Berlin, in addition to giving masterclasses at the Klindworth-Scharwenka Conservatory, Weiss performed a large repertory, including the 32 Beethoven sonatas; the scale of his programmes, such as the complete Etudes and Preludes of Chopin or the entire Années de pèlerinage of Liszt in a single evening, reflected the monumental style of his mentor.

Recorded German VOX 78rpm at 1927, Circle (Busoni) & some unpublished recordings (inc. many Sibelius & Busoni works).

This record contains a selection that ranges from the music of Johann Sebastian Bach to the music of the present day. It contains music by composers both well known and not well known at all. The program of works by the incomparable Bach contain the most eloquent, and perhaps the most didactic festoons provided by Liszt certainly viewed by the most profound musician of all ages. The works of Brahms, which show the final flowering of his German Romantic period, were regarded in his time as a fine, solid composer who turned out the right sort of music in the proper country. By the time of his death he was known to students and musicians as a genius of the nineteenth century. Outside of Germany he was unknown. It was not until the middle of the nineteenth century, through the efforts of Felix Mendelssohn that a re-evaluation of Bach's music began, and it was accepted that he was one of the greatest composers and the greatest technician of all time.

Of all composers for the piano Frederic Chopin was perhaps the greatest. A musician's musician, he is also appreciated by the layman. Chopin, perhaps more than any other composer, knew the piano and its limitations. His poetry and taste are impeccable and no matter how small the work, it is usually a jewel of its kind. His ETUDE IN C-MINOR, Op. 25-12 dates from an early period. It is, as its title claims, a study. Notice the delicacy and taste displayed, also the economy.

From Chopin to Brahms is a stylistic jump. Brahms was always the most conscious of craftsmen. Nothing was ever re-leased from his work table until it had been polished countless times. Yet his works never seem over-worked, especially his lyrical pieces. It is also fresh and spontaneous. Note this in his TWO WALTZES FROM OP. 39. Also notice the richness of texture that Brahms obtains from the simplest forms. His RHAPSODY is almost a hidden quality of nobility in the music that has become a characteristic of his music, but he had a fair measure of poetry as may be heard in his RHAPSODY IN G Minor.

Carl Maria von Weber, a composer unjustly neglected in our own days, is best known for his operas, mainly Der Freischütz, the first German Romantic Opera. Of his piano music the Invitation to the Dance is almost never heard in its original form (for piano). A vivid orchestration of Weber. The PERPETUAL MOTION, Op. 24 is a trifle, but a trifle full of glitter and fun. And it does move.

To try and analyze even the shortest of Bach's keyboard works would take up more room and more time than these notes could spare. If there is any musician he will grasp the technicalities and not really need the analysis. If there is a layman he may dwell into the work would confuse him and not get into the music.

The piano music of Mendelssohn, because of its native simplicity of form, even though it is not always easy to perform, comes out favorably above some of his more grandiose Piano Songs. Without the words, from which the SPINNING SONG is drawn, it is as little series of little tone pictures.

Side 1 of these record ends with a composition by the Italian-American, Alois Solito de Solis. He began as a pianist (KCL-9010 is all series of recordings Maestro de Solis is also a distinguished composer). His CAPRICCIO ESPAGNOLIE is a fine piece of Spanish-style music dedicated to the supporters of the piano, it explores the medium of Spanish rhythm and dance style, expressing the spice and vigor of the Andalusian mood.

The Moments Musicaux of Schubert are charming examples of unpretentiousness and workmanship. The MOMENT MUSICALE No. 3 is no exception. It is a lovely work full of youthful charm and vitality.

The late Jean Sibelius was almost unknown for his piano works. His rugged northern homeland stands out like mountains in his musical output, and his tone poems are one of the world's color and atmosphere. His ROMANCE IN D-FLAT Major, Op. 24-9 we see Sibelius in a different light—quite romantic and extremely lyrical mood, the snow seems to traces of greenery.

The contemporary Norwegian Hans Sæverud is best known outside his native country for his piano music. A fine romantic writer. His countryman Grieg, whose well known music for the same is a light and graceful work. The Sæverud's Peer Gynt displays power and majesty of the Troll's Hall, the dance of the peasants, rough clods. His RONDO AMOROSO displays a considerable lyric gift and a knowledge of his art.

The final offering is the WALDESRAUSCHEN (Forest Murmurs) of Franz Liszt. This is the last offering to compare this Forest Murmurs to the fact that Liszt is famous son-in-law, Richard Wagner.

The internationally known pianist, Edward Weiss, was a pupil of the great pianist-composer Ferruccio Busoni. At the age of 19, Edward Weiss had already played with orchestra, later to be heard in many concerts in Europe. He appeared as soloist with many of the major orchestras. The Chicago Tribune in Europe. Edward Weiss is considered one of the very top masters of the piano alive today. His artistry, style and technique have won him further laurels. A recording of one of Busoni's concerts prompted the late Jean Sibelius to write him: "...You have given us a rare treat indeed, and we who have preserved Master Busoni's style principles, which were epoch-making..."
