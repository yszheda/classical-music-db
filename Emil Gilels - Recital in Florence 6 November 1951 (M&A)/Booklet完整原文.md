# Emil Gilels in Florence 1951

Emil Gilels (1916-1985) was one of the greatest pianists of the twentieth century, and a leading representative of the great Russian piano school founded by Heinrich Neuhaus at the Moscow Conservatory. Along with his friend and colleague Sviatoslav Richter, Gilels dominated the piano world in the Soviet Union during the post-war period, and his international career after the 1950s confirmed his reputation as one of the truly great masters of the keyboard.

This recording captures Gilels in a live recital given in Florence on 6 November 1951, early in his international career, just after he had been allowed to travel to the West for the first time. The program is typical of Gilels, who was always willing to combine the classic repertoire with some of his favorite encores, and this recital shows the full range of his art, from the dramatic intensity of the Beethoven "Pathétique" sonata to the poetic lyricism of Chopin and Schubert, and the brilliant virtuosity of Liszt.

Gilels was always particularly admired for his Beethoven playing, and this performance of the "Pathétique" sonata shows all the qualities that made his Beethoven playing so special: the clean articulation, the perfect sense of structure, the nobility of phrasing, and the powerful dramatic intensity. He was equally at home in the poetic world of Chopin, and his performances of the Chopin pieces here are characterized by a wonderful sense of color and rubato, and the heroic polonaise gets a performance of great power and brilliance.

The recital ends with a group of Liszt pieces, which show the other side of Gilels' art: his incredible technical command, which was always the equal of any pianist of his generation, and his ability to bring out the poetic quality of even the most virtuosic Liszt pieces. This is a fascinating historical document that captures one of the greatest pianists of the twentieth century in his prime, in a live performance that has all the spontaneity and excitement that only a live concert can give.

# Emil Gilels (1916-1985)

Emil Gilels was born in Odessa in 1916. He studied first with his father, then at the Odessa Conservatory, and later at the Moscow Conservatory with Heinrich Neuhaus. He won first prize at the All-Union Competition in 1933, and again at the International Ysaÿe Competition in Brussels in 1938, where he established his international reputation. After the war he became the leading pianist in the Soviet Union, along with Sviatoslav Richter. He began his international career in 1951, when he was allowed to travel to the West for the first time, and this recital in Florence comes from that very first tour. He quickly became a favorite with audiences throughout the world, and he continued to perform and record until his sudden death in 1985, while he was still at the height of his powers.

Gilels' playing is characterized by its extraordinary beauty of tone, its perfect technical control, and its deep musical intelligence. He had a particular affinity for the music of Beethoven, and his recordings of the complete Beethoven piano sonatas and the five piano concertos remain among the finest versions ever recorded. He was also a great chamber musician, and his recordings of the Brahms violin sonatas with David Oistrakh remain classics of the chamber music repertoire.

Music & Arts is proud to present this previously unpublished live recording from Gilels' first tour to the West, captured in excellent sound from the original broadcast tapes. It gives us a wonderful opportunity to hear one of the greatest pianists of the twentieth century in the full flush of his early maturity, in a live performance that captures all the excitement of the occasion.
