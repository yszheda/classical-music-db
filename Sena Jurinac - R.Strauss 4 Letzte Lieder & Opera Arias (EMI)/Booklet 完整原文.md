## Page 5 (Inner Booklet - English Text)

**Sena Jurinac**

Few sopranos in the era just after the last war made such an immediate and lasting impres-sion as Sena Jurinac. She possessed a voice of great beauty, warm and spontaneous, and she combined this with her natural good looks. Her singing, no other soprano in my experience has ever combined a more perfect technique and a more beautiful voice. She was a true artist in every sense of the word.

She was born on 21 October 1921 in Travnik, Yugoslavia. Her father was Croatian engineer, and it was he who decided that Sena should take the name Jurinac. She was still studying at the Zagreb Academy when in 1942, when she was barely 21, as Mimi, she made her debut at the Zagreb Opera. After only one year at the Academy, she was engaged as a permanent member of the Vienna State Opera.

She first appeared at Glyndebourne in 1947 as Pamina, and in the following year sang the Countess in *Le nozze di Figaro*. In 1950 at Glynde-bourne she sang Donna Elvira, and it was as Fiordiligi that she established herself as one of the greatest Mozart singers of her generation.

In 1951 she made her debut at the Royal Opera House, Covent Garden, as Arabella, and in 1954 appeared for the first time at La Scala, Milan. She has also appeared at the Metropolitan Opera, New York.

She is particularly associated with the roles of Mozart and Strauss, and her interpretations of the Four Last Songs of R. Strauss are considered among the finest ever recorded.

Nichts fehlte ihr, und so war sie denn auch vom ersten Tag ihrer Karriere an eine gefeierte Künstlerin. Ihre Spontaneität, ihre Natürlichkeit und ihre jugendliche Ausstrahlung gaben für sie (und ihr Publikum) immer mehr als das große Talent, das ihre Stimme ohne Zweifel gewesen wäre.

Donabella war die erste Rolle, die Sena Jurinac 1948 von Fritz Busch für Glyndebourne ange-bothen bekam, und es ist wahrscheinlich, daß sie ohne die Weisheit, die er ihr vermittelte, nie das geworden wäre, was sie dann werden sollte. Zwei Jahre später, nachdem sie bereits etwa 200 Vorstellungen in Zagreb gesungen hatte, kam sie dann endgültig nach Glyndebourne. ## Page 7 (Inner Booklet - Additional Information)

**Discographical Notes**

The recordings in this collection represent some of the finest examples of Sena Jurinac's art. The Mozart arias were recorded at Glyndebourne with Fritz Busch conducting the Glyndebourne Festival Orchestra in 1950. The Smetana and Tchaikovsky arias were recorded in London with the Philharmonia Orchestra conducted by Warwick Braithwaite and Lawrance Collingwood in 1950-1951. The Four Last Songs by R. Strauss were recorded in Stockholm with the Stockholm Philharmonic Orchestra conducted by Fritz Busch in 1951.

All recordings have been digitally remastered in 1989 by Keith Hardwick for EMI Records Ltd.

**Track-by-Track Notes**

**Mozart - Così fan tutte**
The three arias from *Così fan tutte* showcase Jurinac's mastery of Mozart's demanding coloratura writing. "Ah guarda, sorella" displays her ability to convey sisterly affection, while "Come scoglio" demonstrates her dramatic power. The extended scene "È parte!... Per pietà" shows her complete command of Mozart's style.

**Mozart - Idomeneo**
The three arias from *Idomeneo* represent some of Mozart's most challenging writing for soprano. Jurinac's interpretation combines technical brilliance with deep emotional expression.

**Smetana**
The aria from *The Bartered Bride* and the lullaby from *The Kiss* show Jurinac's versatility in Czech repertoire. Her native Slavic background brings authenticity to these performances.

**Tchaikovsky**
The arias from *Joan of Arc* and *The Queen of Spades* demonstrate Jurinac's dramatic power in Russian repertoire.

**R. Strauss - Four Last Songs**
Recorded with Fritz Busch and the Stockholm Philharmonic Orchestra, these performances are considered among the finest interpretations of this cycle. The Swedish Radio Tapes capture the essence of Jurinac's art at its peak.
