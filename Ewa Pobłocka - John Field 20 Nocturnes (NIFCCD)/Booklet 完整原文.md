## Nokturny Johna Fielda (HIPPO_20250110_0006.jpg - 波兰语文章第一部分)

**Nocturne, Notturno**, po niemiecku także *Nachtstück*, sprowadzała się do romantycznej fascynacji nocą. Mrok, wieczór, noc, symbolizowały aurę tajemniczości, pełną przeciwieństw – zarówno erotyki jak i śmierci, ciemność jako symbol zła, mocy szatana, ale i niebiańskiego, księżycowego światła. Głębia końca to czas eksploracji formy wypowiedzi w muzyce – u Chopina – także w innych dziedzinach sztuki, w poezji (*Pieśni zmierzchu* Hugo), w malarstwie (*Nokturny* Whistlera, grafiki z tzw. czarnego okresu Goi i pejzaże księżycowe Caspara Davida Friedricha). W niektórych zakonach nocna pora modlitwy zwana jest nokturnem. Szersze rozumienie tego terminu upowszechnił nurt sentymentalizmu charakteryzujący okres wczesnego romantyzmu. Nazwy nokturn używano w XVIII wieku zamiennie z serenadą, kasacją i divertimenti, tworów na różne zespoły wykonywanych na otwartym powietrzu. Takie nokturny pisali wielu kompozytorów, choćby Haydn (6 *Notturni* na zespół instrumentalny) i Mozart (*Notturno* na 4 zespoły smyczkowe i 2 rogi, KV 286). Nie zapomnijmy też o *Eine kleine Nachtmusik*. A więc nazwa i gatunek były oswojone, a w okresie przyjmowania idei romantycznych nokturn stał się formą przeniesienia pieśni lub romanse na instrument, głównie fortepian, jak *Pieśni bez słów* Mendelssohna, czy niektóre też utwory charakterystyczne utworzy – jak *Nachtstücke* Schumanna. W operze śpiewany w scenie nocy nazywano nokturnem, barkarola w *Opowieściach Hoffmanna* Offenbacha też jest rodzajem nokturnu. Nokturny jednoczęściowe pisano też do śpiewania bez akompaniamentu orkiestry, jak i nokturny dwugłosowe z akompaniamentem. W kanonie nokturnów Chopina (G-dur, op. 37 nr 2), można wskazać dwugłosową linię kantonilena, z równoległymi tercjami, sekcjami i innymi interwałami, jakby imitując duet. Field wprowadza podwójne seksty i tercje w przebiegu swojego Nokturnu nr 14. Wokalna proweniencja fortepianowego nokturnu jest niezaprzeczalna. Co więcej, zarówno nauczyciele jak i kompozytorzy-pianiści ówczesnego okresu nauczali fortepian śpiewać.

## The Nocturnes of John Field (HIPPO_20250110_0007.jpg-0015.jpg - 英语文章完整内容)

**Nocturne**, *Notturno*, in German also *Nachtstück*, amounts to a Romantic fascination with the night. Darkness, the evening and the night symbolised an air of mystery, filled with contrasts – both eroticism and also death, darkness as a symbol of evil, of Satan's power, but also of the heavenly light of the moon. The depths of the night-time were regarded by poets as a symbol of the power of creative imagination. They may be approached on both the philosophical level, inspiring expressionist forms of utterance in both music – in Chopin – and also in other arts, such as poetry (Hugo's *Les chants du crépuscule*) and painting (Whistler's *Nocturnes*, drawings from Goya's so-called "dark" period and Caspar David Friedrich's moonlit landscapes).

In some monastic orders, night-time prayer was called "nocturne". A broader understanding of the term was popularised by the current of sentimentalism that characterised the period of early Romanticism. The name "nocturne" was used during the eighteenth century in alternation with "serenada", "cassation" and "divertimento", works for various ensembles performed in the open air. Such nocturnes were written by many composers, including Haydn (6 *Notturni* for instrumental ensemble) and Mozart (*Notturno* for 4 string ensembles and 2 horns, K.286). Let us not forget about the *Eine kleine Nachtmusik*, as well. So the name and the genre were familiar, and during the period when Romantic ideas were being adopted the nocturne became a form through which a song or romance was transferred to an instrument, mainly the piano, like Mendelssohn's "songs without words" or character pieces like Schumann's *Nachtstücke*. In opera, a song in a night-time scene was called a nocturne, and Offenbach's barcarolle in *Les contes d'Hoffmann* is also a kind of nocturne. One-part nocturnes were also written to be sung without the accompaniment of an orchestra, as well as nocturnes for two parts with accompaniment. The canon of Chopin's nocturnes (the G major, Op. 37 No. 2), we can point to a two-part cantilena line, with parallel thirds, sixths and other intervals, as if imitating a duet, Field introduces double sixths and thirds in a passage in his Nocturne No. 14. The vocal provenance of the piano nocturne is irrefutable. Moreover, both teachers and composer-pianists of those times taught the piano to sing.

The Irish composer John Field (1782-1837), a pupil of Muzio Clementi during the period of early Romanticism, was a composer of concertos, a quartet, divertimentos, sonatas, variations, rondos and nocturnes. He was known throughout Europe, primarily as a teacher and a pianist, distinguished by a beautiful, intense cantabile. He spent many years in St Petersburg, where he met Maria Szymanowska. From 1822, he gave concerts to Western and Central Europe, but towards the end of his life he returned to Russia – to Moscow.

Field has a place in music history, but in contemporary concert life he plays no great role. In the music history, the world of the concertos, but above all the nocturnes. The complete nocturnes were first recorded in 1994, by Ewa Pobłocka, for which she received a special medal from the Irish government. This time, she has made her complete recording of the nocturnes on an historical instrument an Erard from 1838.

**The first three nocturnes** (Nos. 1, 2 and 3, in E flat major, C minor and A flat major, in Moderato and Allegretto tempo) are rhythmically and agogically lively. The thickened pulsation in a triple quaver metre is one of the elements that unite these three pieces. That said, the Nocturne in A flat major is distinguished by a characteristic turn motif, repeated throughout the first part of the work and in the four-bar interlude in E major. That motif also appears further into the work in the key of A major, but it is altered through variation, with the participation of figurae in both hands, and leads into the conclusion. Nocturne No. 4 in A major, *Poco Adagio*, in ¾, is longer; it has a reprise form, a rising and falling melody in the middle section and a reprise of figurae in the accompaniment. The 32 bars of the embellishment at the end of the first section, distributed among spread chords (semiquavers), bring an aura of Romantic freedom to both tonal planes.

**Nocturne No. 5 in B flat major**, *Andantino*, in a 12/8 metre, maintains a fluency to the motion of the accompaniment figures occupying the compass of a twelfth, forming a backdrop to the beautiful cantilena, which is bolstered by harmonic upper dominant relations and by a reference to the upper mediant. Both sections of the work have repeated chords in both hands, reminiscent of the opening of the middle section of Chopin's Nocturne in A flat major, Op. 37 No. 2. In the ritornello and melodic, this sheen may be seen as an example of the so-called 'French' manner of the time, filling the musical texture with accompaniments against a background of repeated notes, but there are no 'free' variations from the main motif to the upper melody. The French character of the musical texture is also revealed by the fact that in the first section, the motif is presented in the form of a sequence, rising and falling, adorned with melismas. Field prefers major keys, especially C major and E flat major. At that time, E flat major – on account of the number three (three flats) – was associated with fullness, heaven, love and even a closeness to divinity. The key of C major, meanwhile, being the only one without accidentals, was associated with innocence and simplicity. Other keys used by Field – such as F major, symbolising sauviness and simplicity, E flat major, expressing cheerful love, hope and innocence, G major, connoting delicate and unallotted motions of the heart, A major, manifesting innocent love and contentment, and E minor, reflecting anguish and joy – also define in Schubert's system an expressive network of rather mild and sentimental feelings. Only keys used once – A flat major as a key of sadness or death, and minor-mode keys (A minor, D minor) – depart from that prevailing mood, although E minor for Schubert is also expressive of naïve womanly sighs of love. The harmonic writing in Field's nocturnes is not overly complicated. It is dominated by super dominant and relative relationships, although one does encounter surprising juxtapositions of chords and keys and also delicate harmonic transitions.

**The form of Field's nocturnes is indefinite.** We have here the sequencing of various periods, most often in a variation-style manner, ABA or binary form, ABA reprise form and elements of sonata form, in which two themes are traced, albeit without being sharply contrasted. Eight- and sixteen-bar periods succeed one another, literally at times, occasionally adorned at the end with a melisma, or else they are separated from one another with an ornamental figure but do not display distinct contrasts. The dimensions of the nocturnes vary. Numbers 14, 17 and 18 are quite expansive. Nocturne No. 18, in the key of E major, *And Allegretto* tempo and ¾ metre, is entitled *Le Midi*, and it departs from the nocturne type. This is an abridged solo version, from 1822, published in a series of nocturnes, of the Divertissement sur: Quator no. 1 (pub. 1819). The sections are set next to one another without any clear articulation or a coda. The first section, Allegretto, is in a rocking rhythm, after which we hear segments in other keys: in one passage, we even have a kolędowa. Also more elaborate is Nocturne No. 17 in E major, *Lento*, in 9/8. It is a solo version, published in England in 1832, of the *Grande Pastorale* for piano and string quartet.

**The most exceptional work**, characteristic in textural terms, is perhaps the Nocturne in C major (No. 7), Moderato, in 9/8. For 10 bars, the octave g²-g³ is repeated, and the left hand joins in by presenting a progression of chords, some marked arpeggio. This combination of chords is repeated unaltered as an ostinato figure up to the end of the Nocturne. We also hear a distinctive rhythmic-melodic figure in the right hand formed from the top notes of those chords, which is also repeated throughout the whole Nocturne, in a dynamic ranging from pp to ppp. Only the harmony changes. One wonders at the remarkable consistency of these figures and their substantial unity, which might suggest the texture of a toccata or a character piece. We also find a similar figure in Nocturne No. 13 in D minor, *Lento*, in 3/4. We hear a solo version (Adagio) of the *Pastorale* from the Divertissement aux Quator op. 2, published in Russia in 1811. A uniform pulse in 12/8, in a Moderato tempo, characterises the Nocturne in E flat major (No. 11), in which the first four bars – a fourfold repeat of the same note and a presentation of the accompaniment figure – are played in the bass alone, as a solo. The lyrical character of the middle section, the key of B major, with its major scale, even with the use of octaves reinforcing the line of the melody. New phrase shaping appears, but the left-hand figures are all but free of change; only the harmonic selection of chords alters the colouring. Variants of the melody created in a natural, fluent way lead this Nocturne into a reflective-melancholy character.

**The Nocturne in D minor** (No. 13), *Lento*, in ¾, belongs to a different variety of the Fieldian nocturne. It is declamatory, filled with "sigh" motifs, without a typical rhythmic pulsation to the accompaniment; it displays mournful expression. After the second section in D major, the second thematic idea (the consequent of the theme) returns, but not in F major, as it was in the exposition, but reduced in the reprise, in accordance with the principle of sonata form, to the Tonic, to D minor.

**There follow three Nocturnes** (Nos. 14, 15 and 16), two in C major, in 6/8, and one in F major in 3/4. Which are all in Moderato and Allegretto tempo. These bring a diverse range of textures: passage-work alongside repeated chords of two notes or more, separate segments and a buoyant mood. No. 15 (with a middle section in A major) can be compared to the style of Mozart, and in the shaping and sequencing of themes, and the theme of the Nocturne No. 16 to operatic models from Rossini and Donizetti. The most elaborate is Nocturne No. 14, with an introduction, reminiscent of Chopin's e minor Prelude, distinctive drawn themes, a multitude of accompaniment figures and extended trilling (written out from the top note, in line with Classical principles), in a conclusion that gradually dies down to ppp. One should also single out the Nocturne in G major (No. 12), *Lento*, in 6/8, of an "etude" character, with harp-like courses of widely spaced (more than two octaves) chords and short motifs of melody with no clear articulation between phrases. The second section, in B major, displays similarities with the Andante spianato from Chopin's Op. 22. Here, Field transfers the slow interlude from the first movement of his Piano Concerto No. 2. Chopin based his Scherzo in B minor on a theme from the slow movement of Field's Second Piano Concerto. In the theme, one can discern not just the repeated rhythm of Beethoven's "fate motif" (three quavers and a dotted crotchet in the bass), but also a leap of a sixth (the V degree of the scale (the Dominant) to the III degree of the Tonic and a descent to the root of the Tonic, which is regarded as an idiomatic initial figure of Chopin's melodic writing. That is how he begins, for example, the Nocturne in F minor, Op. 9 No. 2. The leap of a sixth is also repeated at the start of Field's Nocturnes. In A major, Andante (No. 9), and in E minor, Adagio (No. 10), both in a quaver metre, but here between the second and third notes of the theme. This is a discovery not mentioned in the literature. This Nocturne adheres to a pp, dolce and cantabile dynamic, in a rocking rhythm with nuances of the harmony of spread chords. It displays a nostalgic trait, noticeable already in the Nocturne in C minor.

**Two Nocturnes**, in A major (No. 8), *Andante*, and in E flat major, *Andantino* (No. 9), both in 9/8 time, employ in the bass a rhythmic pulse that is comparable to Chopin's nocturnes and to his Ballade in F minor, No. 38. No. 8 is a solo version (Adagio) of the *Pastorale* from the Divertissement aux Quator op. 2, published in Russia in 1811. A uniform pulse in 12/8, in a Moderato tempo, characterises the Nocturne in E flat major (No. 11), in which the first four bars – a fourfold repeat of the same note and a presentation of the accompaniment figure – are played in the bass alone, as a solo. The lyrical character of the middle section, the key of B major, with its major scale, even with the use of octaves reinforcing the line of the melody. New phrase shaping appears, but the left-hand figures are all but free of change; only the harmonic selection of chords alters the colouring. Variants of the melody created in a natural, fluent way lead this Nocturne into a reflective-melancholy character.

*Irena Poniatowska [transl. John Comber]*

Dokonała szeregu prawykonań i premierowych nagrań utworów polskich kompozytorów współczesnych, m.in. *Koncertu fortepianowego* Andrzeja Panufnika (polskie prawykonanie, pierwsza rejestracja z London Symphony Orchestra pod dyrekcją kompozytora), *Koncertu fortepianowego* Witolda Lutosławskiego (pierwsze polskie nagranie z Wielką Orkiestrą Symfoniczną Polskiego Radia pod dyrekcją kompozytora), *Koncertu fortepianowego* Pawła Szymańskiego dedykowanego wykonawcy. W swoim dorobku fonograficznym ma niemal 50 płyt, obejmujących repertuar solowy, kameralny oraz koncertowy od baroku do współczesności, a zarejestrowanych m.in. dla Polskich Nagrań „Muza", Deutsche Grammophon, Pony Canyon, Victor JVC, Conifer Records, CD Accord, BeArTon. Wiele z nich uzyskało entuzjastyczne recenzje krytyków lub prestiżowe nagrody, w tym dwa Fryderyki (lata 1998), dwa razy Złoty Medal na Międzynarodowym „Studio" (1996 i 1998) oraz Medal Jana Fielda (za nagranie kompletnu jego nokturnów). Jest także stałym gościem profesorem na Uniwersytecie Sztuk Pięknych i Muzyki Gidai w Tokio i Nagoi. ### English Version (HIPPO_20250110_0017.jpg)

**Prize winner at the International Fryderyk Chopin Piano Competition in Warsaw**, also honoured with the Polish Radio Award for the best performance of Chopin mazurkas (1980). She is a laureate of international piano competitions in Vercelli (1977), Bordeaux (1979). Ewa Pobłocka comes from a musical family, as her mother Zofia Janukowicz-Pobłocka was a professional singer. She began taking piano lessons when she was five years old. She studied at the State Higher School of Music in Gdańsk (today's Academy of Music) under Zbigniew Sliwiński and Jerzy Sukiennicki, and she graduated with honours in 1981. She continued her post-graduate studies in Hamburg under Conrad Hansen. She also had artistic consultations from Jadwiga Sukiennicka, Rudolf Kerer, Tatiana Nikolayeva and Martha Argerich. The pianist debuted on stage at the age of 17 twelve, accompanying her mother during singing recitals in Poland and abroad. She has performed in almost every European country, and also in both Americas, China, Indonesia, Vietnam, South Africa, Singapore, Korea, Japan and Australia. She played in such famous concert halls as Herkules-Saal in Munich, Musikhalłe in Hamburg, St. Petersburg Philharmonic Hall, Théâtre des Champs Elysées in Paris, Kwai Hall in Tokyo, Auditorio Nacional in Madrid, Barbican Centre and Wigmore Hall in London, Musikverein in Vienna, Mozarteum in Salzburg, Lincoln Center in New York, the Philadelphia Academy of Music, Glenn Gould Studio in Toronto and many others. Among her many chamber music projects some are especially worth mentioning, carried out as part of the Chopin Year celebrations: concerts with Dang Thai Son presenting pieces for four hands and two pianos (Mozart, Poulenc and Schubert), performances with the Philharmonia Quartett Berlin with Schumann's *Faschingsschwank aus Wien* and songs with Iwona Sobotka and Artur Ruciński. Currently, Ewa Pobłocka is part of the Multi-city ensemble (with singer Iwona Sobotka and cellist Maria Leszczyńska cello), presenting chamber repertoire composed for such forces. Ewa Pobłocka was a soloist for many world premiere performances and first recordings of the works of Polish contemporary composers, including Andrzej Panufnik, Witold Lutosławski, Paweł Szymański and Paweł Mykietyn (the two last-mentioned composers dedicated their works to her). She performed *Panufnik's Piano Concerto* in the presence of the composer during the Warsaw Autumn Festival in 1990; a year later she recorded it with the London Symphony Orchestra for the British label Conifer Classics. In 1998 she recorded that concerto again for the Polish label CD Accord, performing with the Warsaw Philharmonic Orchestra under Kazimierz Kord. On that album she also plays the concertos by Witold Lutosławski and Paweł Szymański. With Lutosławski's *Concerto* she came on stage many times together with the composer himself as conductor. Their joint performance with the Polish Radio Chamber Orchestra, Orchestra del Maggio Musicale Fiorentino, Bayerisches Rundfunkorchester, and many European radio broadcasters. ## Fortepian Erard, 1838 (HIPPO_20250110_0018.jpg, 0019.jpg)

### English Version

**Erard piano, 1838**

The instrument was made in Paris in 1838. According to the Erard company's records, it was first sold on 12 December 1838. Mr Forkel and Mr Vigvier of Bordeaux became its owners. The piano's serial number is 14214. Made of mahogany wood, the instrument has an ivory keyboard with 80 keys [CC–g⁴]. All parts are original and in excellent condition, except the felt pads and the strings, which were replaced in the Erard factory in 1922. The action, especially its repetition part, is typical of the period in which the instrument was made. The piano has a beautiful, rich tone and works perfectly well.

---

### Polska wersja

**Fortepian Erard, 1838**

Instrument został zbudowany w Paryżu w 1838 roku. Według dokumentów firmy Erard, jego pierwsza sprzedaż dokonano 12 grudnia 1838 roku, a właścicielami zostali Pan Forkel i Pan Vigvier z Bordeaux. Fortepian oznaczony jest numerem seryjnym 14214, wykonany z mahoniowego drewna, a klawiatura kości słoniowej zawiera osiemdziesiąt klawiszy [C–g⁴]. Wszystkie części instrumentu są oryginalne i zachowane w doskonałym stanie, z wyjątkiem filców i strun, które zostały wymienione w fabryce Erarda w 1922 roku. Mechanizm, zwłaszcza elementy repetycji, są typowe dla okresu powstania instrumentu. Fortepian charakteryzuje się piękną, szlachetną barwą brzmienia i jest w pełni sprawny.
