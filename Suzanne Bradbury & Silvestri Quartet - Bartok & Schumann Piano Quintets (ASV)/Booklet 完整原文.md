## 作品解说

### Béla Bartók: Piano Quintet (1904)

A piano quintet is far from the most commonly heard genre in the history of music. It is, however, of vital importance for two of its greatest masters: Robert Schumann and Béla Bartók have tackled this: Bartók with his youthful Piano Quintet of 1904.

For Bartók, this second Piano Quintet, completed in July 1904, was somewhat of a false crossing; a previous version in 1897 having failed to impress himself recognised that this was really where his musical development was at an impasse and so on graduation a year before from the Franz Liszt Academy, he turned his back on tonality – or embraced it too, indeed give the impression of a man who had never been in love and in fact fell in love on the first high fight.

In style, the influence of Dvořák had been a constant since his student days in the halls in Budapest, through that the former's enthusiasm for Hungarian themes in particular the piano quintets. The quintet opens with the First Quartet in October in Vienna. He never actually saw the work, but did conduct its premiere in 1918, included it in his emigrant's baggage to the United States.

The influences are obvious. The general romantic tone reminds us of Brahms and Liszt, above all the lyrical passage from the Second Piano Concerto in 1900. This mixture later a collaboration with Bartók of his compatriot Zoltán Kodály, whose String Quartet of 1905 impressed him greatly. The work received its first performance in 1905 at the Academy of Music and its publication, in 1911, by Rózsavölgyi & Co. Budapest, with a dedication to his teacher, István Thomán, and to the memory of his father, who had died in 1887. The work is scored for piano and string quartet (two violins, viola and cello). The opening movement is in sonata form, with a slow introduction. The second movement is a scherzo with trio. The third movement is a slow movement, and the finale is a rondo. The work lasts about 30 minutes.

### Robert Schumann: Piano Quintet in E flat, Op.44 (1842)

Schumann composed his Quintet Op.44 from September to October 1842. The first public performance was given in Leipzig, at the Gewandhaus, on 8 January 1843. The piano part was played by Clara Wieck, Schumann's future wife and already a celebrated pianist, who had been advised of the work in the autumn. The work was an immediate success and established the genre of the piano quintet.

The first movement, the most Brahmsian of the whole work, is in sonata form. The exposition is repeated. The development section is built on the first and second themes. The recapitulation leads to a coda. The second movement is a funeral march in C minor. The trio is in C major. The movement is in ternary form. The third movement is a scherzo with two trios. The finale is a fugue, with the subject introduced by the first violin.

## 艺术家介绍

### Suzanne Bradbury, piano

Suzanne Bradbury was born in California but grew up in Paris, where her artist parents settled after her American father's service in France. Her musical training in Europe under various outstanding teachers gave her a rich perspective of musical culture. She later moved to Spain and studied at the Royal Conservatory of Music in Madrid.

For many years, Suzanne Bradbury was a pupil of the legendary pianist and teacher, Rudolf Serkin, at the Curtis Institute in Philadelphia. She has won numerous awards and prizes, including the First Prize in the International Clara Haskil Competition and the Busoni Competition.

### Silvestri String Quartet

The Silvestri String Quartet was founded in 1988 and became a professional group by studying with the most important masters. They spent four years at the University of Music in Vienna where the violinist Doru Stefan Pop and violist Traian Ionescu studied with the members of the Alban Berg Quartet. The quartet has won numerous prizes and has performed throughout Europe, America and Asia.

The name of the Quartet is a tribute to the Romanian conductor and composer Constantin Silvestri, who was exiled by the Ceaușescu regime.
