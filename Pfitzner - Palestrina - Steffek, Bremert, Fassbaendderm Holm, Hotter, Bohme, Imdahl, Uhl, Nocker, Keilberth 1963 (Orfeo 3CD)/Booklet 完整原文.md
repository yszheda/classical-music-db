## BAYERISCHE STAATSOPER LIVE

"The death of live performance"; "The extinguishing of spontaneous creativity"; "The suppression of the fleeting moment": these and similar questions have been levelled at a sound recording industry that is nearly fifty years old? How much argument has been wasted – and also justified! – on the question of live music theatre against the feared onslaught of the record companies and the respected preservation of the status quo of "A" and "B" policies! The fact that man's ability to express and impress again and again through sound, and to share this expression with the world, has become so widespread that it borders on the bizarre. Added to this is the possibility of capturing a fleeting evening for posterity without any loss of time, and of having it always at one's disposal. This has also broken down the inhibitions of many in society who might have wrongly assumed that "high" Art was reserved for a select few.

Nonetheless, there are still those who would prefer to see our artistic heritage enshrined in a glass case rather than allowing it to live and breathe in the here and now. The Bayerische Staatsoper is one of the few that has taken the initiative to make its productions available to the public in the form of live recordings. Every opera production is a prototype – each individual bespoke act of creative interpretation resulting in a live experience we share. Here are some of those moments prepared for you by the Bayerische Staatsoper – the company that brought you the first complete Ring Cycle in stereo!

**Peter Jonas**

## GENIUS IN THE DARK

... and alongside world history gossips, gentlemen and uninitiated with blood, the common theory of philosophy, science and the arts.

Schopenhauer's thesis of the philosophy of Schopenhauer's and his opera is interwoven with the philosopher's ideas. It would indeed be said that the idea introduced the plot, the distinction between the world as will and representation as manifested in symbols. In retrospect, although this theorem Pfitzner selected as motto seems questionable, indeed refuted by the catastrophes of our century. If Palestrina is seldom to be found on our stages today, the musical world is cutting itself off from Pfitzner's masterpiece, indeed to a great extent from his entire oeuvre. The composer is not entirely blameless for this. With fateful consequences for the reception of his works, he threw himself into the „spiritual free-for-all" created by a man of the people's will. Thomas Mann already recognized the ambivalence after a few meetings in 1917: „A one-sided, wonderfully ambivalent man, I believe, who for all his love for the redeeming intellect, undoubtedly carries much of the evil world of will of his Act 2 of Palestrina within himself."

In the Palestrina year of 1917 Pfitzner threw down the gauntlet to his composing contemporaries with the essay „Futuristengefahr" („The danger of futurism"), in which he praised the „idea" as the seed of every work of art, as the inspirational blessing. In the just completed Palestrina he succeeded in the seemingly impossible: to reshape the creative process with all its unpredictable and untrainable creative process. That moment when the appearances of the dead masters disappear again and angel tongues begin to speak is among the most wonderful to be encountered in music. That idea, what with the treatise, only superficially directed against Busoni's ideas about progress in music, Pfitzner contributed to the politicization of music, a little later he pursued the controversy extremely vigorously with the pamphlet Das neue Ästhetik der musikalischen Impotenz (The new aesthetic of musical impotence). ## SYNOPSIS

### Premier acte

Dans sa maison près de l'église Santa Maria Maggiore à Rome, au centre de la musique, le compositeur Giovanni Pierluigi Palestrina donne des leçons à son disciple de dix-sept ans, Silla. Bien que celui-ci ait appris à composer dans le style sévère, il aspire en secret à l'art nouvellement éclos en Florence. Silla a composé un morceau dans le nouveau style et est heureux de le montrer. Ighino est triste. Ighino est triste. Ighino fait de son mieux pour consoler son père, qui n'a plus rien créé depuis la mort de sa mère Lukrezia. Silla essaie d'égayer son ami. Pendant ce temps, le cardinal Borromée entre dans la salle. Il reproche à Palestrina de ne plus rien créer depuis quinze ans. Palestrina répond que le but de l'artiste n'a jamais été de servir l'Église. Borromée est venu de Trente spécialement pour commander une nouvelle messe à Palestrina. Il le menace: s'il n'écrit pas la messe, il sera accusé d'hérésie et brûlé. Palestrina refuse. Borromée invoque les puissances célestes pour lui envoyer un messager qui écrira la messe à sa place. Silla avoue son amour à Palestrina, qui la repousse. L'apparition de Lukrezia, sa femme décédée, lui prédit qu'il composera la messe. Palestrina s'évanouit.

Les pères du concile se rassemblent et aussitôt les tensions éclatent: entre les évêques d'Italie et les envoyés de l'Empereur, surtout entre l'envoyé de l'Empereur, le comte espagnol Luna, et les représentants du Pape.

### CD 3

Les délibérations commencent avec une allocution du cardinal-légat Morone, qui appelle les Pères à l'unité et à des décisions rapides sur les questions en suspens – y compris celle de la musique d'église. Contre cette hâte s'élève une opposition, menée par le comte Luna, qui demande au nom de l'Empereur des délibérations plus approfondies, notamment sur les réformes liturgiques. Lorsque le cardinal allemand de Lorraine lui coupe vivement la parole, Luna propose d'inviter également les protestants à la consultation finale du Concile. Le tumulte général qui suit cette proposition est apaisé par Morone qui annonce une pause. Les Pères doivent se calmer et revenir après midi – les délibérations doivent être menées à terme à tout prix. Novagerio invite Morone et le cardinal de Lorraine dans sa maison. Dans la salle restent les serviteurs. Les Espagnols commencent à se quereller avec les Italiens et les Allemands, et l'on en vient aux mains. Madruscht donne l'ordre aux soldats de faire feu et de tuer ceux qui bafouent ainsi le sens du saint concile.

### Troisième acte

Dans la maison de Palestrina, celui-ci, épuisé par la captivité, attend en compagnie d'Ighino et des chanteurs de Santa Maria Maggiore la fin de l'exécution de la messe à Saint-Pierre. Ighino, inquiet du sort de son père, a livré à l'un de celui-ci la partition de la messe aux soldats de Borromée. Des voix retentissent derrière la scène et acclament Palestrina comme „sauveur de la musique". Le pape Pie IV, en personne entre dans la maison de Palestrina pour le remercier et renouveler sa nomination à la tête de la chapelle papale. Borromée, repentant, demande pardon à son ami. Ighino se réjouit du bonheur de son père. Même le fait que Silla soit parti pour Florence ne parvient pas à troubler leur joie. Ighino veut faire la fête avec les siens. Palestrina reste seul avec son chantre: „Faconne-moi, Seigneur, moi qui suis la dernière proie de l'un de tes mille animaux – et je serai heureux et rempli de paix."

## SYNOPSIS (English)

### Act 1

In his house near the church of Santa Maria Maggiore in Rome, the center of the Catholic world, the composer Giovanni Pierluigi Palestrina also teaches the seventeen-year-old Silla. Although instructed in the tradition of strict counterpoint, Silla has inwardly turned to that school of composers who cultivate a new vocal style in Florence. Silla is to sing according to the new doctrine, and is happy to be allowed to sing. But Ighino is sad. He worries about his father, who has created nothing since the death of his wife Lukrezia. When Silla wants to speak with the Cardinal of Lorraine through his friend, half-saying Palestrina and Cardinal Borromeo enter the room unnoticed. Borromeo reproaches Palestrina for having written no music for the last fifteen years. Palestrina points out to the Cardinal that it has never been the goal of artists in any time to proclaim the Holy Spirit of God. Borromeo has come from the Council of Trent specifically to demand a new composition from Palestrina. He threatens him: if he does not complete the work, he will accuse him of heresy and have him burned. Palestrina refuses. In his despair, Borromeo conjures the heavenly powers to send him a messenger who will write the mass for him. Meanwhile, Silla has gone to Palestrina to confess her love to him. Palestrina rejects her. Then the spirit of his deceased wife Lukrezia appears to him. She prophesies that he will compose the mass. Palestrina faints.

The Council fathers assemble and immediately tensions become clear: between the bishops from Italy and envoys of the Emperor, above all between the Emperor's envoy, the Spanish Count Luna, and the representatives of the Pope.

### CD 3

The deliberations begin with an address by the Cardinal Legate Morone, who appeals to the Council members for unity and speedy decisions in questions still open – including that of Council members for church music. Opposition is raised to this haste, particularly on the part of Count Luna, who represents the Emperor's wish for more thorough discussion, especially on the reform of the liturgy. When the German cardinal of Lorraine violently interrupts him, Luna proposes inviting the Protestants too to the Council's final deliberation. The general tumult that follows this suggestion is ended by Morone with the announcement of a break: the members should calm themselves and appear again after midday – the deliberations must in any case be brought to an end. Novagerio invites Morone and the Cardinal of Lorraine into his house. The servants remain behind in the hall. The Spaniards begin to quarrel with the Italians and Germans, and eventually there is a violent brawl. Madruscht orders soldiers to shoot into the mob and to put to the rack those who jeer at the holy Council's way of thinking.

### Act 3

In Palestrina's house Palestrina, worn out by imprisonment, Ighino and the singers of Santa Maria Maggiore are waiting for the end of the performance of the Mass in St. Peter's. Without Palestrina's knowledge Ighino, from worry about his father's fate, had delivered the score of the Mass to Borromeo's soldiers. Offstage voices acclaim Palestrina as „saviour of music". Pope Pius IV, has himself taken into Palestrina's house to thank the composer and install him afresh as director of the Papal chapel. But Borromeo, in deepest contrition, begs Palestrina for forgiveness. Ighino is happy to see his father cheerful again: also, as Silla has meanwhile gone to Florence, joy can scarcely be clouded. Ighino wants to celebrate with the others. Palestrina remains behind, alone: „Now forge me the last stone on one of Thy thousand rings, O Lord – and I will be of good heart and at peace."

*(Translation: Lionel Salter)*
