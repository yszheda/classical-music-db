## Modest Mussorgsky (1839-1881)
### Pictures at an Exhibition

In 1874 Mussorgsky composed this suite as a memorial to his friend, the artist and architect Viktor Hartmann, who had died suddenly of an aneurysm in 1873. The composer was deeply affected by Hartmann's death, and a friend organized a memorial exhibition of Hartmann's drawings, watercolors, and architectural designs. Mussorgsky visited the exhibition and was inspired to compose this musical tribute.

The suite consists of ten pieces, representing different works by Hartmann, connected by a recurring "Promenade" theme which represents the composer walking through the exhibition. The music vividly depicts each artwork:

1. **Promenade** - The walking theme, majestic and noble
2. **The Gnome** - A grotesque nutcracker design
3. **Promenade**
4. **The Old Castle** - A medieval troubadour's song
5. **Promenade**
6. **Tuileries** - Children playing in the Paris gardens
7. **Bydlo** - A Polish oxcart
8. **Promenade**
9. **Ballet of the Unhatched Chicks** - Theater costumes for canaries
10. **Samuel Goldenberg and Schmuyle** - Two Polish Jews
11. **The Market Place at Limoges** - Women arguing in the market
12. **Catacombae. Sepulcrum Romanum** - Roman catacombs
13. **Cum mortuis in lingua mortua** - Con versis in dead language
14. **The Hut on Fowl's Legs (Baba Yaga)** - The witch's hut
15. **The Great Gate of Kiev** - Hartmann's design for a city gate

## Igor Stravinsky (1882-1971)
### Petrushka - Three Scenes from the Ballet

Stravinsky arranged these three scenes from his 1911 ballet Petrushka for solo piano in 1921. The ballet tells the story of a puppet who comes to life and suffers tragic love. These piano transcriptions are among the most demanding in the repertoire:

16. **Danse russe** (Russian Dance) - The opening scene with street dancers
17. **Chez Petrouchka** (At Petrushka's) - The puppet's lonely room
18. **La semaine grasse** (The Shrovetide Fair) - The carnival finale

## Mily Balakirev (1837-1910)
### Islamey - Oriental Fantasy

Composed in 1869, Islamey is one of the most technically demanding piano pieces ever written. Balakirev drew on Caucasian folk themes he collected during his travels. The piece features two contrasting themes - a wild, rhythmic dance and a lyrical, oriental melody - building to an increasingly frenzied conclusion.

## About the Performer

**Alfred Brendel** is one of the most distinguished pianists of his generation. His interpretations span the entire piano repertoire from Bach to contemporary works. He is particularly renowned for his Mozart, Beethoven, Schubert, and Liszt interpretations. This recording from December 1972 demonstrates his mastery of Russian repertoire.
