## Sergei Rachmaninov (1873-1943)
### Concerto for Piano and Orchestra No.3 in D minor, Op.30

**Performed by:**
- Dimitris Sgouros, piano
- Berliner Philharmoniker
- Yuri Simonov, conductor

---

### Liner Notes by Max Harrison, 1984

**I: Allegro ma non tanto** [16'18"]
**II: Intermezzo (Adagio)** [10'30"]
**III: Finale (Alla breve)** [13'24"]

First suggested by a one-bar string introduction, the main theme of the slow movement is heard complete on the oboe. It is extended orchestrally with a sad loveliness typical of the composer's early style, being answered by the notable rhetoric of the piano's remarkable entry. The strategy is one in which the opening orchestral passage suggests certain of the theme's potentialities, these throwing into relief the rather different ones uncovered by the piano. A buoyant waltz forms the contrasting middle section, decorated by the soloist with figurations of a delightful capriciousness, while its theme, an ingenious modification of the first movement's first subject, rounds in full harmony before the wood instruments which bring back the Adagio's initial music. Again the movement appears to be fading away when there is another surprise—a short, violent cadenza leading into the finale.

This makes an apt beginning to a closing movement of surpassing brilliance. In terms of Rachmaninov's idiom as it was at the time, this systematically yet with irresistible impetuosity explores the full potentialities of piano and orchestra. The finale's first subject exploits the hectic circumstances to provide effective contrast, but this is supplied by a virtuosically independent central section of four-minute variations (scherzando), immobilised until the coda, where the solo part is placed high on the keyboard, and the orchestra's development section is based on the finale's own themes resurface, though stated in a series of quite new forms. The excitement increases to a point as near frenzy as Rachmaninov ever expressed in his music, and there is an overwhelming coda.

### Dimitris Sgouros

Dimitris Sgouros was born in Athens on August 30th 1969. He began his piano studies at the age of five, and in 1977 gained a scholarship to the Athens Conservatoire, where he studied with the noted pianist and teacher Maria Hierogloy-Sgoura, graduating in June 1982 with a Professor's Diploma and Performer's Diploma, winning a First Prize and Gold Medal. In the summer of 1982 he studied piano with Dr. Stewart Gordon at the University of Maryland and in 1983 piano with Professor Misha Donskoy and composition with Professor Timothy Baxter at the Royal Academy of Music in London, where he took his Performer's Licentiate. In July he gave his first recital in the USA at the Newport Music Festival, and during which he made his first appearance with an orchestra.

Dimitris Sgouros made his North American debut in April 1982, performing with the New York Symphony Orchestra at Carnegie Hall, New York, with Mstislav Rostropovich and the National Symphony Orchestra in Washington, and in July he gave his first recital in the USA at the Newport Music Festival.

He made his British debut at the Royal Festival Hall, London, in March 1983 in Rachmaninov's Piano Concerto No.3 with Rostropovich and the London Philharmonic Orchestra. In November he made his UK recital debut, also at the Royal Festival Hall, and his 1983-84 appearances also included concerts at the Vienna Philharmonic Hall; his engagements in 1984 are his first tour of Europe and his first tour of the USA.

Dimitris Sgouros has an extensive repertoire which now extends to over 45 concertos. His talent has been recognised by many eminent musicians, among them Mstislav Rostropovich, Claudio Abbado, Charles Dutoit and the late Artur Rubinstein, who declared that Sgouros was the best pianist he had ever heard.

### Yuri Simonov

Yuri Simonov was born in Saratov, USSR, and studied at the Leningrad Conservatory with Rabinovich. He was assistant to Mravinsky with the Leningrad Philharmonic Orchestra and in 1963 made his conducting debut at the Leningrad Conservatory with Dargomyzhsky's The Stone Guest. In 1966 he won the National Conductors' Competition in Rome. In 1969 he made his debut at the Bolshoi Opera, Moscow, with Aida. The following year he was appointed Chief Conductor of the Bolshoi Theatre (a position he still holds). He has also appeared as guest conductor with opera companies in Budapest, Leipzig and Prague and regularly conducts the leading Soviet orchestras. He has taken the Leningrad and Moscow orchestras, together with the Bulgarian Radio Symphony Orchestra, on concert tours of Western Europe and Japan. In 1976 he was appointed People's Artist of the Republic of Russia and in 1981 People's Artist of the USSR.

In 1980, he founded the Bolshoi Theatre Chamber Orchestra, consisting of twenty young string players from the Bolshoi orchestra, and as such their Musical Director he has toured with them throughout the USSR and Yugoslavia. His involvement with young performers in the West has included three concerts with the European Community Youth Orchestra and master classes with the orchestra of the Guildhall School of Music and Drama in London.

Yuri Simonov made his debut with Western opera company in 1982, conducting Eugene Onegin at the Royal Opera House, Covent Garden. That same year he made his UK concert debut in three concerts with the London Symphony Orchestra, taking over at short notice from Claudio Abbado. In January 1984 he undertook a tour of West Germany with the London Symphony Orchestra, and in March 1984 he appeared with the same orchestra in a series of seven concerts of Tchaikovsky cycle with the LSO at the Barbican Centre in London.
