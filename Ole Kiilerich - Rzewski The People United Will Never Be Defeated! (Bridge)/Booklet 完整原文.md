### Notes by the Composer

**THE PEOPLE UNITED** is a series of 6 cycles, each of which consists of 6 stages, in which different musical relationships appear in order: 1) Simple events 2) Rhythms 3) Melodies 4) Counterpoints 5) Harmonies 6) Combinations of all these. Each of the larger cycles develops a character suggested by the individual stage to which it corresponds, so that the third cycle is lyrical, the fourth tends toward conflict, the fifth toward simultaneity (the fifth is also the freest), and the sixth recapitulates, in such a way that the first stage is a summary of all the preceding first stages, the second a summary of the second stages, and so on. Two songs, aside from the theme itself, appear at various points: the Italian revolutionary song "Bandiera Rossa", in reference to the Italian people who in the seventies opened their doors to so many refugees from Chilean fascism, and Hans Eisler's 1932 antifascist "Solidaritätslied", a reminder that parallels to present threats exist in the past and that it is important to learn from them. The extended length of the composition may be an allusion to the idea that the unification of the people is a long story and that nothing worth winning is acquired without effort.

## 内页 - 演奏家说明

*Note by Ole Kiilerich*

The first time I ever heard **THE PEOPLE UNITED WILL NEVER BE DEFEATED!** I knew by instinct that it would follow me for the rest of my career. I had this odd feeling that the piece was written just for me to fulfill my conception of musical expression and I felt an urge to instantly dig deeply into this overwhelming piece of art right away, and so I did. Playing and performing "The People United" over the years has been one of the greatest experiences in my musical life. Performing it live, by heart, is a physical struggle and a mental outburst that reaches out to listeners way off the beaten track of classical music. And this giant piece is so well written for the piano, that when practicing it for concert, the pianist can skip the usual etudes and scales. It massages all the muscles in both hands and brain.

Working one's way through the piece in concert performance, the overall structure of many different ideas, genres and compositional methods comes together to form a unity. In the end it becomes strikingly clear and leaves me at the cadenza (before the rebirth of the theme) with a stunned feeling of having played through the entire music history of keyboard music. One hears echoes of Bach, Brahms, Rachmaninov, Sjostakovich, Reich, Stockhausen, Boulez and many others, combined with various forms of jazz, folk and popular music. Thus, I am always inspired to the max before letting the imagination flow freely in the cadenza. And the feeling of recognition and hope when hearing and playing the first bars of the original theme again at the end of this long voyage is overwhelming.

Frederic Rzewski's genre-free and all-embracing marathon piece for solo piano is an ode to socialism and the struggle of the people. I am deeply fascinated by the power and honesty of this piece. It reminds us of a time when ideologies were allowed to be spoken out loud with grand words and honest devotion, rooted in a strong belief in a better world. The lack of compromise intrigues me in these times, where all we hear about is bench marking, consumption, loans and crisis. I believe that this marvelous piece shall stand for years and years to come because it dares to speak directly to our hearts!

## 内页 - 演奏家简介

**Ole Bartholin Kiilerich** is a highly regarded Danish pianist and improviser, active as soloist, and performer of chamber music. He has a great interest in contemporary music and is a passionate communicator who has worked deliberately to give new music greater exposure.

Mr. Kiilerich was a postgraduate student at the Carl Nielsen Academy of Music in Odense, Denmark, studying with Amie Oland, Erik Kaltoft and Amalie Malling. He continued his studies in New York and Boston, with Ursula Oppens, Stephen Drury and Seymour Lipkin. He is the recipient of numerous scholarships and grants, and has participated in many master classes and courses in and around Europe with renowned teachers including Volker Banfield, Jiri Hlinka, and Andrzej Dutkiewicz.
