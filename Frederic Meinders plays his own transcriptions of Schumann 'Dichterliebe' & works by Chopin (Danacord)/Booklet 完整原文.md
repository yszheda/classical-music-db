### SCHUMANN
**Dichterliebe, Op.48**
**3 Romances for Oboe and Piano, Op.94**
***Von fremden Ländern und Menschen*** *(Kinderszenen, Op.15/1), for the left hand*
***Wehmut***, *Op.39/9*

1840 was a significant year in Robert Schumann's career the *Liederjahr*. Focusing on concentrating on works for solo piano he switched to writing works for voice and piano, producing the astonishing songs in that period. His passion for Clara Wieck was echoed in his eight – the poems which he set reflect his emotions – and the marriage on 12 September. *Dichterliebe* is, arguably the finest of his song cycles composed in 1840. Completed that May, it originally contained twenty songs but, having had the cycle turned down by two publishers, Schumann decided to leave out four. All sixteen are settings of poems by Heinrich Heine (1797-1856). Their wide range of emotions, moving between tenderness, despair, fervour and anger, are miraculously reflected in the thirty-year-old composer's newfound bliss of writing for the voice.

Frédéric Meinders is the first pianist to attempt to render *Dichterliebe* into a convincing vehicle for the piano. That honour goes to **Theodor Kirchner (1823-1903)**, a minor but accomplished German composer, friend of Mendelssohn and Schumann, who also produced a large number of transcriptions for piano and piano duet. Though they frequently arrive at the same technical solutions, German composer Theodor Kirchner and Meinders remain faithful to Schumann with few elaborations of their own. Meinders intends to see how the two transcribers differ in the way they handle the material. For example, on No.7, '*Ich grolle nicht*' (No.7) Kirchner transfers the melody to the tenor (as does Joachim Raff) preferring a version in which Schumann prefers to keep to the original pitch – after all, though frequently associated with the famous baritone Julius Stockhausen and first sung by the great soprano **Wilhelmine Schröder-Devrient (1804-1860)**. The second song (No.2) '*Aus meinen Tränen*' the performer denies demi-semiquaver accompaniment in both hands, compared to Schumann's original part for the left hand as Schumann's original part for the left hand is retained, with the right hand merely playing the melody) while Meinders ingeniously manages the 32nd notes in both hands as well as bringing out the top line.

The 3 Romances for Oboe and Piano, published in 1849, are an example of several chamber works composed by Schumann composed in the 1840s. They are also designated to be played on the violin or clarinet, but it is as oboe pieces that they have retained their popularity. Clara Schumann, incidentally, had already composed 3 Romances for Piano (Op.11) in 1840, dedicating them to her husband. Meinders' transcription of Schumann's Op.94, No.1/3 here is not enjoyed as a companion piece.

***Wehmut*** ('Sadness') comes from *Liederkreis*, Op.39, Schumann's setting of twelve (originally thirteen) poems by Joseph von Eichendorff (1788-1857) composed in May 1840 and published in 1850. The fourth Schumann-Meinders item of this program is not a setting – but the tenth and most famous of the set, the melody almost cries out to be sung – but the first of the *Kinderszenen*, that much beloved collection of thirteen short piano pieces depicting scenes from childhood. It was written in 1838. Meinders' transcription is for the left hand alone in the key of D flat major (Schumann's original is in G major).

Godowsky's celebrated 53 Studies on Chopin's Etudes Opp. 10 and 25 invent new variations, juxtapositions, combinations, firm metamorphoses and other techniques; 22 of them are for the left hand alone. ## About Frédéric Meinders

The Dutch pianist and composer **Frédéric Meinders** was born in 1940 and received his first piano lessons at the age of five from his parents. He later studied with Jan de Man at the Royal Conservatory in The Hague where he was awarded the Prix d'excellence. Shortly after completing his studies with the renowned Nikita Magaloff in Geneva, he won the 1964 National Competition for Young Musicians. Four years later he won first prize at the International Viana da Motta Competition in Oslo, leading to concerts all over Europe, North and South America and the Far East.

Frédéric Meinders has appeared as soloist with leading European orchestras including the Concertgebouw, Vienna Symphony and Munich Chamber Orchestra under conductors such as Sir Colin Davis, Bernard Haitink and David Zinman. He has also been active as a chamber musician and performed with Arleen Auger, Janos Starker, Nelson Freire and Hermann Prey.

Frédéric Meinders has composed over 100 original works for the piano and for a wide variety of other instruments. His total of more than 700 works as composer and transcribers includes 150 for the left hand alone.
