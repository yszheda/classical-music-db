## 内页说明

### Dr. Harold Wayne 前言

Dr. Harold Wayne writes: I am especially privileged to add a further note to these so expertly written by my good and learned friend, John Freestone.

Relative rarity of early vocal records is my usual theme. However in the case of this particular CD it is not possible to comment thus as so few examples exist, indeed I am confident that quite a number of these few Berliner examples are unique. So I have had to proceed without access to information, for the entire set of Russian and Polish examples to two Berliner colleagues: Frank and Stefan Mogk, to whom I am extremely grateful. The older Berliner Gramophone recordings were made at various recording sessions in St. Petersburg, Moscow, Warsaw and Budapest. I am indebted to Henry Gage of London whose collection was purchased by Romilly Phillips and "Pop" Russell. The younger brother returned in 1917 and his records were disposed of to James Dennis of Ipswich. The Aranyi titles were in the Roland Tecmire collection and the Martapoura belonged to R. de Bry of Paris.

### 关于 7 英寸 Berliner 唱片的历史说明

The very first 7" discs were made in the U.S.A. in 1894 to a patent of Emil Berliner. But little of musical interest appeared for several years and these discs were made in very small quantities and are now probably the rarest collectable records. Early in 1897 G&T started to make 7" Berliner records were made by many famous European and American artists also by such celebrities as Marie Tempest, Selma Kurz, Marie Gutheil-Schoder, Feodor Chaliapin and Nellie Melba. The Harold Wayne Collection has given this compact disc all existing copies are of the very greatest rarity. In many cases there are probably only one or two known copies by artists who ventured into the studios with some trepidation, wondering whether the new invention would be of any commercial value whatsoever.

It is difficult for us, brought up in the era of the compact disc, to imagine the state of mind that must have existed at that time. A quality of sound which we can now achieve using modern techniques from twenty-year-old recordings would have been undreamt of in those days. After 1905 no further 7" recordings were made by the Gramophone and Typewriter Companies since the 10" and 12" records were more popular and of two and a half minutes than on this disc is issued under this label number.

### David Karlovich Juzhin (1868-1922)

David Karlovich Juzhin was born in Moscow of Greek parents and for a time was destined for the Church. He was found to have a very beautiful voice and was trained by professor Fania but he was very unhappy there and later studied privately, making his début at the Tiflis Opera in 1900 and becoming a member of the Bolshoi Theatre in Moscow from 1902 until 1909. He left there after a dispute with the management but returned in 1911. For a time after 1917 he worked with private opera companies and later became a very popular concert singer. Juzhin's career started recording in 1901 and he sang on some duets with his wife. These recordings made him one of the most famous Russian tenors of his day. He made many gramophone recordings in 1901 "G" & T in "Di quella pira" (sung in Russian), but this was the highest note he could manage comfortably. His first record at the Metropolitan Opera New York was in "La Traviata". He had appeared in the U.S.A. in 1894 to a patent of Bell Telephone, but little is known of his musical achievement for several years until the advent of the first records. He was a good friend of the famous tenor Leonid Sobinov.

### Nicolai G. Slavsky

Nicolai G. Slavsky is an artist about whom little is known. He sang a glory song and two light operatic items with a pleasing voice and great charm. He apparently confined his activities to operetta and he also made recordings of songs and duets with A. Krutikov.

### L.N. Bragina

L.N. Bragina sang mostly in St. Petersburg but details of her career are not known. Her recordings show a voice of considerable power and range.

### Andrej Labinsky (1874-?)

Andrej Labinsky was born in Warsaw and studied at the Warsaw Conservatory. He made his début at the Warsaw Opera in 1895 and sang there until 1920. He was particularly associated with the operas of Moniuszko and created many roles in Polish operas. His recordings are among the most important documents of Polish singing from the early 20th century.

### Maria Aleksandrovna Emskaya

Maria Aleksandrovna Emskaya studied in Kiev and sang there from 1900. She sang in opera in Russia and also made concert tours. She had a lyric soprano voice of fine quality and recorded for Pathé and G&T.

### Gavriil Morskoj

Gavriil Morskoj recorded this little disc in Kiev. He was a tenor of the old Italian school and his high notes were particularly brilliant. His recordings are extremely rare.

### M.A. Max

M.A. Max was a bass-baritone who sang at the Bolshoi Theatre in Moscow. His recordings are among the rarest of all Berliner records.

### Aleksandr M. Davydov (1872-1932)

Aleksandr M. Davydov was born in 1872 and made his début in Tiflis in 1893. He sang at the Bolshoi Theatre in Moscow from 1895 until 1910. He moved in 1896 where he first sang for the Mamontov private opera company. He sang in "Sadko", "The Legend of the Invisible City of Kitezh" and remained there until 1917. He made his début in 1909 in Paris singing the role of Gremin in "Eugene Onegin" at the Theatre du Châtelet. He was one of the creators of Russian opera there in 1913. He recorded from 1900 onwards for G&T, Pathé, Zonophone and other companies. He died in Paris in 1932.

### I.V. Tartakov (1871-1948)

The Latvian baritone Ivan (Yan) Tartakov (1871-1948) studied singing with professor Emilie Everardi at the Conservatory in St. Petersburg. He made his debut at the Mariinsky Theatre in 1897 and sang there until 1917. In 1920 he became a professor of singing at the St. Petersburg Conservatory and remained there until his death in 1948. He was considered an outstanding artist with a fine high baritone voice and particularly excelled in the roles in Rubinstein's "Demon" as "Mathieu" in Meyerbeer's "L'Africaine", "Figaro" in Rossini's "Il Barbiere di Siviglia". He recorded for Berliner, G&T and Pathé and for the two examples on this disc he coupled an aria from "Carmen" with "The statue of Tsarskoe Selo" than in the "Toreador Song" which did not suit his stylistry.

### Janina Korolewicz-Waydowa (1875-1955)

Janina Korolewicz-Waydowa was born in Warsaw and studied singing at the Warsaw Conservatory. She made her début at the Warsaw Opera in 1895 and sang there until 1920. She was particularly associated with the operas of Moniuszko and created many roles in Polish operas. Her recordings are among the most important documents of Polish singing from the early 20th century.

### Stanisław Bogucki (1875-1937)

Stanisław Bogucki was born in Warsaw and studied there and in Milan. He made his début at the Warsaw Opera in 1897 and sang there until 1930. He was particularly associated with the role of Moniuszko's "Halka" and created many roles in Polish operas.

### K. Czosnowska (1884-1958)

K. Czosnowska was born on 11th November 1884 in Ruda Guzowska near Warsaw and died in Warsaw on 31st May 1958. She studied singing with the dramatic actress in 1892 and in 1896 was a great success in "La belle Hélène" in Warsaw. She made guest appearances in St. Petersburg, Moscow and Odessa. She was a popular singer in operetta and also appeared in "Faust", "Les Huguenots", "Manon", "Mignon" and "Lakmé".

### Adamo Didur (1874-1946)

Adamo Didur was born at Wola Słokowa in Galicia on the 24th December 1874. He studied singing with Francesco Lamperti and with Van Huffel. He had training in Milan with Feito Marchesi. He made an early début in La Scala in 1896. After this he was engaged at the Rio de Janeiro and Buenos Aires. From 1898-1906 he sang at the Warsaw Opera House. In 1905 he sang in France, England, Spain and North and South America and in 1908 he joined the Metropolitan Opera New York where he sang until 1932. He was particularly associated with the roles of Boris Godunov and Mephistopheles.

### Julius Bochnicek (1871-1931)

Julius Bochnicek was born in Prague on the 17th April 1871 and originally studied piano and organ at the Prague Conservatory. He started his career as a conductor and sang only as a part-time amateur. He made his début for the theatre in the same town and this achieved considerable success especially in the tenor roles of Smetana. He made his début at the Theatre with Frenzer Krugsa, making his début in 1900. He sang for a year or so in Prague, Budapest and other cities of the Austro-Hungarian Empire after which he accepted an engagement in Budapest for four seasons from 1902 to 1906. He then moved to Hamburg where voice problems ended his vocal career and subsequently he was engaged as a producer in Prague at the German Theatre until 1918. His recordings are extremely rare and are highly prized by collectors as they include collections of Czech and Slovak folk songs which were very popular and his operetta "The Love of Mahratta" was given in Prague in 1920 with great success, and it was there that he died on the 17th May 1931.

It is surprising that so little can be found of him in the usual sources of singers' biographies and he is not mentioned in any of the standard reference books dealing with the period. This is true not only for Aranyi as a concert singer but certainly the voice as recorded suggests an artist with a considerable following.

### Jean Martapoura

Jean Martapoura was a French baritone who sang at the Paris Opera. He made his début in 1895 and sang until 1920. His recordings are extremely rare.

### Catherine Mastio

Catherine Mastio was a French soprano who sang at the Paris Opera. She made her début in 1895 and sang until 1920. Her recordings are extremely rare.

### Desider Aranyi (1871-1926)

Desider Aranyi was born in Budapest on the 12th May 1871 and recorded between 1900 and 1905 at the Neues Deutsches Theater already in leading roles. He took part in the first performance there of "Die Meistersinger" in Siegmund in "Die Walküre", and in festivals, performances including "Lohengrin" in the principal tenor roles. He sang in "Faust", "Les Huguenots", "Don Giovanni", "Fidelio", "Carmen", "Il Trovatore", "La Gioconda", "Faust", "Manon Lescaut", "Cavalleria Rusticana" and in the world premiere at the Neues Deutsches Theater in Prague in 1901. He also performed with great success in "La Bohème", "Madama Butterfly", "Tosca", "Aida", "Die Walküre" and in many other Wagner and Verdi particities. The two examples included on this Compact Disc show him to be the possessor of a very beautiful voice and even at the end of the aria from "Cavalleria Rusticana" he is inclined to be too emotional in his farewell to his mother.

### Rosalia Chalia (1876-1953)

Rosalia Chalia was born in Baku on the 24th October 1876 and made her début at the opera in Moscow in 1896. She sang in opera and gave recitals at the opera in Moscow City for 14 years from 1900. From 1900 to 1904 she made some visits to the U.S.A. where she appeared with success. Her very early records suggest a good quality mezzo-soprano voice. She is also mentioned in some sources as "R. Chalia" and as "Mrs. E. H. G. L."

### Nikolaj Seversky (1876-1955)

Nikolaj Seversky was a Russian baritone who sang at the Mariinsky Theatre in St. Petersburg. He made his début in 1895 and sang until 1920. He was particularly associated with the roles of Tchaikovsky and Rimsky-Korsakov.
