## Page 8-9 (English introduction)

■ On March 16, 1938, at the National Theatre opera in Prague the evening performance was about to begin. After the introductory suggestive tones full of unrest the curtain went up and a very unusual production followed.

It was the first time that the music was played in public, at the premiere of an opera by a Czech composer who had been living abroad for a number of years. The author, Bohuslav Martinů, was sitting with his French wife, Charlotte, in the dark of the auditorium, excited more than ever before. For him, he was offering the public a full-length opera, the orchestral component of which was of a fully-conceived concept, and it was also the first work in which he treated an untraditional theme of a surrealistic tone, far removed from what was usually to be seen at an opera.

Next to the Martinů sat a man of restless temperament, always overflowing with ideas and witty remarks, and full of kindness. He was Georges Neveux, a playwright well-known in Prague because some of his plays had already been staged here. Martinů had written the libretto for his new opera Julietta or a Dream-book based on his play. The play itself had already been staged in Paris within a relatively very short time after its Paris premiere in 1930.

Years later, Georges Neveux (1900-1963) remembered the occasion: "I will never forget my first meeting with Martinů. I therefore expected a celebrity but was surprised to see in front of me a man of modestly silent, which I had never encountered before. He was tall, slim, his look and his voice was had a charming Czech accent.

"I think that the feeling of sympathy I experienced was reciprocal. The sensitiveness discernible behind his apparent calm, his peculiar charm and his simplicity – all originated in his homeland."

Martinů wrote a letter to Neveux in June 1936: "I have actually read your play Juliette or a Dream-book again and without actually knowing how I put the first act to music." Both the first-night audience and the critics in Prague's daily press highly praised the work. The situation was very favourable then to the acceptance of the new work which, contained some provocative elements, and the production of which was not without risk. Martinů not only put Juliette to music, he dealt with it in a literary way, in an introduction to the piano reduction which contained some interesting ideas. "The world is present only at a given moment, which is replaced by the next, and everything is rushing forth into a void. It is in fact a psychological problem: what am I, what are you, what is truth? who has preserved the ability to retain memories, and to act and think logically, in line with his convictions. "The whole play is a desperate effort in search of something stable, and can remain in this world without memory and without time forever..." Putting the theme to music quite unexpectedly accentuated a new feature: Michel's longing for Julietta, the constant search for her lost song, the love for the constantly escaping girl of unpredictable reactions, self-willed – all this can be given an intensity of expression which only Bohuslav Martinů could do. Martinů started thinking about it in 1947, he thought about an introduction to the piano reduction. In 1953, he finished his most famous symphony – Fantaisies symphoniques – and had this to remark: "It is a small fancy of mine, that it is a small fancy..."

C'est pourquoi une pareille collaboration unique entre l'auteur et un chef tchèque, Jaroslav Krombholc, qui s'est donné une peine infinie pour préparer cette première enregistrement complète en langue tchèque sous sa direction, c'est pourquoi cette parution compte parmi les meilleures éditions de la musique tchèque.

Ivo Židek a gagné sa renommée internationale dans la seconde moitié des années 50, il a commencé sa coopération avec l'Opéra d'État de Berlin, il a été invité par cet État comme invité régulièrement, et dans la Dvořák's *New World* symphony, c'est vrai qu'il a la école tchèque de Dvořák, expressionnisme de Janáček, le cycle de lied de Schubert.

Maria Tauberová était engagée sur le plateau du Théâtre national de Prague depuis 1936, une excellente artiste colorature très tôt elle a fait un nom dans son métier, elle chante bien des rôles célèbres : Susanna dans *Les Noces de Figaro*, Constanze dans *L'Enlèvement au Sérail*, Pamina dans *La Flûte enchantée*, Emília Marty in *Jenůfa* de Janáček et d'autres. Ici elle donne toute son art à Juliette, c'est une interprétation de toute une vie, préparée depuis que Martinů lui a donné ce rôle, en 1936, la première eut lieu le 16 juin 1936. A cette époque, la scène excellente était préparée par Jindřich Honzl, direction musicale Václav Talich.
