## 内页说明 by Harold C. Schonberg (编辑)

Prodigies come and go, though it is a safe statement to say that no great instrumentalist in musical history came to fulfillment without having started as a child prodigy. Genius shows up early. But even among prodigies Josef Hofmann was exceptional, ranking with Mozart, Mendelssohn and Saint-Saëns. He was born in Cracow on January 20, 1876, and was playing in public at the age of six. At nine he played the Beethoven C major Piano Concerto with the Philadelphia Orchestra. At the age of ten he made his American debut with a concert in the Metropolitan Opera House. Qualified observers all but went out of their minds; it was not so much that the "wonderful boy," as he was called, could play the notes and play them with exquisite finish. It was that he displayed a mature approach to music, shaping his phrases like an experienced artist.

Thanks to a grant from the philanthropist Alfred Corning Clark, the boy was removed from the concert stage and sent to Germany, where he worked with Moszkowski and Anton Rubinstein. On March 4, 1894, he entered concert life again, playing the Rubinstein D minor Concerto with the composer on the podium. From that point he was a touring pianist, and so he was almost to the end of his life. He operated out of America, becoming a United States citizen in 1926. On Feb. 16, 1957, he died in Los Angeles, at the age of eighty-one.

Hofmann made a long series of acoustic recordings for American Columbia, starting 1911. Then, around 1920, he recorded for Brunswick. He never made an electrical recording — officially, at any rate. In the middle 1930s, he made some test pressings for EMI in England and Victor and Columbia in America, but would not let them be released commercially. A few copies are still in existence. There also are off-the-air recordings, taken from Philadelphia Orchestra radio appearances and even from a concert in Calmar Hall at the Curtis Institute. Hofmann was director of Curtis from 1926 to 1938. Occasionally there are rumors of this or that Hofmann treasure, in the hands of a private collector or in the vault of a conservatory somewhere.

This series of Hofmann recordings being issued by VAI Audio in association with IPRA brings alive again the playing of one of the most colossal pianists in history, a pianist with a tremendous and original personality, a pianist with grand manners, a pianist who seemed to combine a sense of style with a good measure of classicism, a pianist whose technical finesse remains the ne plus ultra of the art, a pianist who will serve for all time as the exemplar of musical elegance coupled to fire and brilliance.
