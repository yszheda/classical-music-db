## QUARTET FOR THE END OF TIME

**GIL SHAHAM, VIOLIN · PAUL MEYER, CLARINET**

## Preface by Olivier Messiaen

And I saw another mighty angel come down from heaven clothed with a cloud: and a rainbow was upon his head, and his face was as it were the sun, and his feet as pillars of fire. [...] And he set his right foot upon the sea, and his left foot on the earth. [...] And the angel which I saw stand upon the sea and upon the earth lifted up his hand to heaven, and sware by him that liveth for ever and ever: [...] That there should be time no longer! But in the days of the voice of the seventh angel, when he shall begin to sound, the mystery of God should be finished.

*Revelation of St John the Divine 10:1-7*

My *Quatuor pour la fin du temps* was conceived and written during my captivity and received its first performance at Stalag VIII A on 15 January 1941. The performers were Jean Le Boulaire (violin), Henri Akoka (clarinet), Étienne Pasquier (cello) and myself at the piano. It was directly inspired by this quotation from the Book of Revelation. Its musical language is essentially immaterial, spiritual, Catholic. Modes which achieve a kind of tonal ubiquity, melodically and harmonically, here draw the listener towards eternity in space or the infinite. Special rhythms, beyond metre, contribute powerfully in dismissing the temporal (all this remains no more than a tentative, stammering attempt), when one thinks of the overwhelming grandeur of the subject.

This Quartet is in eight movements. Why? Seven is the perfect number, the six days of Creation, sanctified by the Divine Sabbath; the seven of this rest is prolonged into eternity and becomes the eight of everlasting light, of eternal peace.

### 1. Liturgie de cristal (Liturgy of crystal)
Between three and four in the morning, the awakening of birds: a solo blackbird or night-ingale improvises, surrounded by a shimmer of sound, by a halo of trills lost very high in the trees. Transpose this on to a religious plane and you have the harmonious silence of heaven.

### 2. Vocalise, pour l'Ange qui annonce la fin du temps (Vocalise for the Angel who announces the end of Time)
The first and third sections (very short) evoke the power of the mighty Angel, crowned with a rainbow and clothed by a cloud; this sets one foot upon the sea and one foot upon the earth. In the middle section – these are the impalpable harmonies of heaven. On the piano, gentle cascades of blue-orange chords, garlanded with their distant carillon (the quasi-harmonics chiming of the violin and cello): the quasi-harmonics of the violin and cello imitate the gong tempered by a distant peal of thunder. At the end, certain phrases ascending towards the extreme high register is the accent of a man towards his God, of the Child of God towards his Father, of the deified Being towards Paradise.

### 3. Abîme des oiseaux (Abyss of the birds)
Clarinet solo. The abyss is Time, with its sorrows and its weariness. The birds are the opposite of time: their desire is our desire for light, for stars, for rainbows and joyful songs!

### 4. Intermède (Interlude)
A scherzo of more superficial character than the other movements, but linked to them none the less by melodic reminiscences.

### 5. Louange à l'Éternité de Jésus (Eulogy to the Eternity of Jesus)
Jesus is here considered as the Word. A long phrase on the cello, infinitely slow, magnifies with love and reverence the eternity of this powerful and tender Word, "the Word was in the beginning, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God."

### 6. Danse de la fureur, pour les sept trompettes (Dance of fury, for the seven trumpets)
Rhythmically the most characteristic piece of the set. The four instruments in unison are made to sound like gongs and trumpets: the last six trumpets of the Apocalypse followed by various catastrophes, the trumpet of the seventh angel announcing the consummation of the mystery of God. The use of added values, augmented or diminished rhythms, non-retrogradable rhythms. Musical themes made of granite sonorous blocks of purple flesh, of icy intoxication. Listen above all to the terrible fortissimo of the theme in augmentation and its changes in register of its different notes, towards the end of the piece.

### 7. Fouillis d'arcs-en-ciel, pour l'Ange qui annonce la fin du temps (Tumult of rainbows, for the Angel who announces the end of Time)
Certain passages from the second movement return. The mighty Angel appears, and above all the rainbow which crowns him (the rainbow: the portal of paradise, of heavenly colours and luminous vibrations). In my dreams, I have recognised chords and melodies, I know these colours and forms; then, after a momentary stage, I pass beyond reality and submit an ecstasy to a dizziness, a gyratory interlocking of rainbows. This is the circling of the blue of fire, these flows of blue-orange lava, these sudden stars: this is the tumult of rainbows!

### 8. Louange à l'Immortalité de Jésus (Eulogy to the Immortality of Jesus)
A long violin solo, acting as a pendant to the cello solo of the fifth movement. Why this towards? It is addressed more specifically to the second aspect of Jesus – the man-Jesus – resurrected immortal. The violin climbs slowly to go up at last. It is all love. Its slow ascent towards the extreme high register is the accent of a man towards his God, of the Child of God towards his Father, of the deified Being towards Paradise.

## Artist Biographies

Born in Illinois in 1971 to two scientists, raised in Israel and the USA, **GIL SHAHAM** began violin lessons with Samuel Bernstein at the age of seven and celebrates his musical debut in New York at the Juilliard School. He was only ten when he gave his first solo appearance with the Israel Philharmonic in London when he stood in for an indiscredited Itzhak Perlman. He studied with Dorothy DeLay and Sándor Végh at Juilliard and the Marlboro Festival. He now plays over 100 concerts a year with leading orchestras and conductors the world over.

Gil Shaham's exclusive Deutsche Grammophon recordings include the complete concertos of Paganini, Mendelssohn, Tchaikovsky, Bartók and Bruch, as well as violin sonatas by Bach, Brahms, Paganini, Prokofiev and Shostakovich. He has also recorded concertos by Glazunov, Barber, Vivaldi's *Four Seasons*, and made a recital with pianist André Previn.

**PAUL MEYER** was born in Mulhouse in 1965. He studied at the Paris Conservatoire and began his career as a soloist with the Orchestre Symphonique du Rhin. In 1984 he won first prize in the Geneva International Competition and in 1985 the Debut Artist of the Orchestre Philharmonique de Radio France. He appears as soloist with major orchestras including the London Philharmonic, the Philharmonia Orchestra, the Orchestre de Paris, the NHK Symphony, the Tonhalle Orchestra Zurich, and the major German radio orchestras. As a passionate chamber musician, he performs with artists such as Isaac Stern, Yefim Bronfman, Cho-Liang Lin and Jian Wang. He is a member of the Ensemble Orchestral de Paris and teaches at the Paris Conservatoire.

**JIAN WANG** was born in 1962 into a family of musicians. He moved to Australia at the age of fourteen and entered the Sydney Conservatorium. In 1979 he was awarded a celebrated documentary film from China. He studied with Robert Pikler in Sydney and a year later created a sensation playing Saint-Saëns First Cello Concerto with the Sydney Symphony Orchestra. In 1982 he won the ABC Concerto Competition and was awarded a Fulbright Scholarship to study at the Juilliard School in New York with Leonard Rose. Since then his international career has been phenomenal. In 1990 he was awarded a Grammy nomination for his recording of the Elgar Cello Concerto. He has appeared with leading orchestras including the Berlin Philharmonic, Vienna Philharmonic, and the major American orchestras. As a passionate chamber musician, he performs with artists such as Daniel Barenboim, Gidon Kremer, and Mitsuko Uchida.

**MYUNG-WHUN CHUNG** was born in Korea in 1953. He began his debut as a pianist with the Seoul Philharmonic Orchestra at the age of seven. After moving to the USA in 1968 he attended the Manhattan School of Music in New York, where he studied piano with Rudolf Serkin and composition with Nadia Boulanger. In 1974 he won first prize at the Tchaikovsky Competition in Moscow. Since then he has conducted the world's major orchestras including the Berlin Philharmonic, Vienna Philharmonic, and the New York Philharmonic. In 1989 he became Music Director of the Orchestre Philharmonique de Radio France. In 1992 he was appointed Music Director of the Deutsche Oper Berlin. He is currently Music Director of the Seoul Philharmonic Orchestra.
