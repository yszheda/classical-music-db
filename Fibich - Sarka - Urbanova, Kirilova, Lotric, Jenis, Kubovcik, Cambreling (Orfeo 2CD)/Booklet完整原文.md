## [Zdeněk Fibich - The Third Classic of Czech Music]

The thousand-year history of Czech culture is closely linked with that of German culture. As Germans and Czechs intermingled in the Bohemian region for a long period of time, there was no independent Czech language; culture began to crystallise. From that time onwards, the Czechs strove towards a clear demarcation between themselves and the Germans after the founding of an autonomous Czech state. But since these political goals had not been reached, not even after the introduction of the Austro-Hungarian dual monarchy in 1869, they could not achieve political independence through a flourishing cultural. Music played a decisive role.

this. In Smetana's works the music fused together with the patriotic programme to such an extent that it was experienced as a medium unifying nationalist feelings. Dvořák's world fame brought Czech music a popularity that prepared the way for political slogans and national independence.

Beginning in the 1880s and especially during the 1890s Zdeněk Fibich (1850-1900) came to the fore as the third significant Czech composer after Smetana and Dvořák. When Smetana was already dead and Dvořák was already famous abroad, Czech patriots, desiring a national culture, Fibich obligingly this patriotic tendency. He developed the idea through melodramas based on tragic ancient Czech themes after Benda, Mozart, Schumann, and above all Wagner - anknüpfend - die Idee einer Melodram nach Benda, in that Fibich completed his most famous opera Šárka from 1896-97, with this he earned his place among the recognized romantic operatic composers.

In Šárka Fibich seized upon the national-mythological legend of the Amazon war in Bohemia that Smetana had already used in his third symphonic poem *My Fatherland* (Libussa), and Fibich already had an opera of the same name (the libretto). Fibich's material after Smetana definitely acquired a national identity, that the composer to this after the composer's death, in 1899-91 when he was writing, remained his most celebrated melodrama. It is the purest example of Czech romanticism.

Zdeněk Fibich was born in 1850 in Všeborovice, Bohemia. He studied at the Leipzig Conservatory with Ignaz Moscheles and Carl Reitz, and then continued his studies in Paris. He returned to Prague in 1875, and quickly established himself as one of the leading composers of the new Czech school. He composed three operas, a symphony, a piano trio, a violin sonata, a large number of songs, and the famous piano cycle *Moods, Impressions and Reminiscences* which he composed between 1892 and 1898. He died in Prague in 1900, at the age of 50, leaving his opera Šárka almost completed.

Fibich's music is characterised by its strong melodic invention, its rich romantic harmonies, and its deep roots in the Czech national tradition. Šárka is his largest and most ambitious opera, and it contains some of his most beautiful and dramatic music. It combines a strong sense of national identity with all the dramatic power of nineteenth-century romantic opera, and it deserves to be better known than it is today. This new recording from the Czech Philharmonic under Sylvain Cambreling, with a strong cast of leading Czech singers, gives us an excellent opportunity to rediscover this neglected masterpiece.

## [Zdeněk Fibich - der dritte tschechische Musikklassiker]

Die tausendjährige Geschichte der tschechischen Kultur ist eng verbunden mit der der deutschen Kultur. Da sich Deutschen und Tschechen seit Jahrhunderten auf dem böhmischen Gebiet über lange mischten, gab es Jahrhunderte lang keine selbständige tschechische Kunst. Zu sehr war das kulturell starke Deutsch auf tschechischem Boden, seit dem Dreißigjährigen Kriege war die Stellung der tschechischen Sprache nur allmählich selbständiger. Erst nach 1867, nach dem Ausgleich zwischen der Monarchie, konnte ein selbständiges tschechisches Kulturleben beginnen.

Neben Smetana und Dvořák war Zdeněk Fibich (1850-1900) als dritter tschechischer Komponist in den 1890er Jahren an die erste Stelle gerückt. Fibich - nachdem Smetana tot war und Dvořák schon Weltruhm erlangt hat - entwickelte sich auf dem Boden des tschechischen Nationalismus. Mit seiner monumentalen Trilogie *Hippodamie* 1888-91 und seiner bekanntesten Oper Šárka 1896-97 kam Fibich dieser Forderung nach: einem großen Melodrama auf alte tschechische Stoffe, also konnte er sich als echter tschechischer romantischer Komponist behaupten.

Schulzová summarised her reminiscences in Fibich's *Love Diary*, which he published in 1925. First as a critic and later acting as communist cultural minister, he furthered the performances of works by Smetana and Fibich and pitted these against those of the 'foreign' Dvořák. Thus Fibich enjoyed a powerful patronage that allowed him to become the undisputed prophet of Czech national music. Nejedlý's dubious ideological support was, however, to be revenged upon: already during the First World War Leoš Janáček (1854-1928) was, in certain cultural circles, beginning to be treated as the third great Czech composer, whilst Fibich was being overshadowed by Janáček's naturalistically modern musical language. Nejedlý did not want to face this, declared Janáček against Fibich and Smetana, thus causing a great spiritual misunderstanding. With this extra-musical confrontation began the subtle fall of Fibich. Suppressed by Janáček and Martinů, Fibich unstoppably disappeared from concert and opera programmes. Only during the 1990s did music lovers begin to remember Fibich; now, after almost thirty years of oblivion, even in his native country, one can see hopeful signs of a Fibich Renaissance.

Fibich's music is caught between his own individual language and manifold musical models. Let us take his masterwork Šárka, as an example. The subject reveals clear parallels to Smetana's *Libussa*, although the ending is formulated differently: whilst Smetana aims towards the exalted tableau of a national stage consecration, Fibich has a thoroughly personal, existentially convincing tragic tone. Or, as another example: the second act of the opera doesn't this reveal clear parallels to Wagner's *Tristan und Isolde*? The women's world surrounding Šárka, on the other hand, recalls *Die Walküre*. And so one could continue, comparing Fibich's piano compositions to Mendelssohn's *Songs Without Words*, the symphonic poems to those of Smetana to Liszt or the choral works to similar works from Schubert to Brahms. Nevertheless, Fibich's most successful works, the borrowings never turn into mere compilations. The basic principles of Wagnerian orientation, as a spiritual point of identification, that by no means prevented an individual creative approach. Fibich's hyper-sensitive Czech nationalist milieu, that 'Wagnerism' was therefore always an approach, can therefore never prevented further fruitful development.

Czech musicology, meanwhile, no longer disputes this assertion. Fibich's own worth was never seriously disputed in Bohemia, but his music-historical position remained the subject of some discussion. Vladimír Helfert postulated in 1937, for example, that "Fibich's place would be before Smetana as far as stylistic development is concerned. He would have been very relevant there." Helfert designates Fibich's position, therefore, as a musically anachronistic, director, with ideas of his own, sufficient room for 'modernisation', despite their historical subject matter.

In spite of this, Fibich's music does deserve a place in the repertoire. His late operas - among them Šárka in particular - can still sweep contemporary audiences off their feet with their rich psychological tensions and, not least, their rich harmonic language, marked chromaticism and the merging together of words and music. They thus offer the contemporary opera-goer a stimulating experience, despite their historical setting.

## [Zdeněk Fibich - Le troisième classique tchèque]

L'histoire millénaire de la culture tchèque est étroitement liée à celle de la culture allemande. Du fait que les Allemands et les Tchèques se sont mélangés pendant des siècles, il n'y a pas eu d'Etat tchèque autonome depuis la fin de la dynastie des Přemyslovci, jusqu'à l'indépendance en 1918, la culture allemande a exercé une influence décisive. C'est pourquoi Fibich, après la fondation de l'Etat tchèque en 1918, il peut être considéré comme le troisième compositeur tchèque après Smetana et Dvořák.

Au début des années 80 et en particulier dans les années 90 du XIXe siècle, Zdeněk Fibich est considéré comme le troisième compositeur tchèque, à côté de Smetana et Dvořák. A une époque où Dvořák avait déjà obtenu une popularité mondiale, Fibich préparait son opéra le plus célèbre, Šárka, sur un texte d'Anežka Schulzová, et il l'acheva en 1896-97. Avec ses mélodies, il s'agit d'un melodrame national.

Dans Šárka, Fibich s'inspire du sujet national mythologique de la guerre des amazones en Bohème, que Smetana avait déjà traité dans son troisième poème symphonique *Má vlast* (Libussa), et Léos Janáček avait repris après lui son opéra du même nom. Avec Šárka, Fibich connaît le succès que, auprès de l'opinion publique tchèque, il est entré dans le répertoire comme le plus pur romantique compositeur tchèque.

contre les compositeurs étrangers, Fibich a beau être tchèque, il a reçu une formation allemande à Leipzig et à Paris, de sorte qu'il ne peut être complètement nationaliste que dans son contenu. L'opéra, à la fin du XIXe siècle, c'est après Dvořák, il a prit sa place parmi les compositeurs tchèques, et pas seulement à travers le drame national, il a développé l'expression des sentiments personnels, Fibich a obtenu la reconnaissance après sa mort, et c'est pourquoi il faut redécouvrir son œuvre.

Zdeněk Fibich est né en 1850 à Všeborovice, Bohème. Il étudie au Conservatoire de Leipzig avec Ignaz Moscheles et Carl Reitz, et continue ses études à Paris. Il retourne à Prague en 1875, et s'impose rapidement comme l'un des principaux compositeurs de l'école tchèque nouvelle. Il compose trois opéras, une symphonie, un trio avec piano, une sonate pour violon, de nombreuses mélodies, et le célèbre cycle de pièces pour piano *Humeurs, impressions et souvenirs* entre 1892 et 1898. Il meurt à Prague en 1900, âgé de 50 ans, laissant Šárka presque achevée.

La musique de Fibich se caractérise par une forte invention mélodique, une harmonie romantique riche, et un enracinement profond dans la tradition nationale tchèque. Šárka est son opéra le plus grand et le plus ambitieux, et il contient quelques-unes de ses plus belles et plus dramatiques musiques. Il combine un fort sentiment de l'identité nationale avec toute la puissance dramatique de l'opéra romantique du dix-neuvième siècle, et il mérite d'être mieux connu qu'il ne l'est aujourd'hui. Cet enregistrement nouveau avec l'Orchestre philharmonique tchèque dirigé par Sylvain Cambreling, avec un casting solide de principaux chanteurs tchèques, nous donne une excellente occasion de redécouvrir ce chef-d'œuvre négligé.

### Inhalt der Oper (German)

Nach dem Tode der Fürstin Libuše überlegen die von der energischen Sárka geführten Mädchen, wie sie unter der Oberherrschaft der Männer die Anerkennung ihrer Rechte zurückerobern könnten. Die wilde Šárka unterbricht den Opferritus, mit dem Přemysl und sein nächster Berater, der Wladyka Ctirad, das Gedächtnis der verstorbenen Libussa ehren wollen. Vlasta vertritt den Frauenkampf. Nach neuerlicher Ablehnung schwört Šárka Rache an allen Männern, vor allem an Ctirad, in dem sie ihren Haupteinsturz sieht.

Um ihre Rache ausführen zu können, stellt Šárka dem verhassten Ctirad eine Falle: sie lässt sich an einen Baum fesseln. Ctirad findet sie, erweist sich jedoch hochherzig und befreit sie, wie erwartet, zu verhöhnen. Šárka, deren Hass in glühende Liebe umschlug, erkennt Ctirad ihren ver-räterischen Plan: auf einen Hörnsignal wird den von allen Seiten bewaffneten Mädchen der Mädchengburg Děvín ziehen und Ctirad gefangennehmen wollen. Im entfesselten Kampf Šárka hat ihre Kampfgenossinnen vertrieben und führt nun Přemysl Kämpfer an, die zur Mädchenburg Děvin ziehen und Ctirad befreien wollen. Im entfesselten Kampf.

### Synopsis of the opera (English)

After the death of Princess Libuše, a group of young women led by Vlasta and the temperamental Šárka, discuss ways in which they could restore recognition of their rights by the tribal chiefs. The wild Šárka interrupts the ceremony which Přemysl and his first adviser, Ctirad, have staged to pay homage to the deceased Libuše. She refuses again and swears to revenge all men, and particularly Ctirad whom she regards as her archenemy.

A war erupts between the tribe's men and women in the course of which Šárka thinks she has freed enough courage to overcome by a feeding of love instead of hatred, reveals to the original plan. Ctirad however, wishing to show his fearlessness, sounds the horn himself, only to be captured by the other women. Šárka, suddenly overcome by a feeling of love instead of hatred, reveals to Přemysl the danger. Šárka has betrayed her friends and now leads the warriors against Děvín to liberate Ctirad. In the ensuing battle, the women are defeated and Ctirad saved, but not for Šárka. She breaks herself free from Ctirad's arms and, in despair, throws herself into the abyss.

## Wiener Konzertchor

Der Wiener Konzertchor konstituierte sich 1995 bei Mitgliedern des ehemaligen ORF-Chores als ein selbständiger professioneller Kammerchor von hochqualifizierten Sängern. Seit 1995 der erste internationale Auftritt bei den Salzburger Festspielen, und in 1996 und 1997 gab es die erste Aufführungen auswärts in Venedig bei der Biennale. Die erste internationale Tournee fand 1996-1997 statt unter der Leitung von Friedrich Cerha, Michael Gielen und Pinchas Steinberg.

The Vienna Concert Choir was formed in 1995 as an autonomous association of former members of the ORF Choir, as the only professional chamber choir in Austria. Following performances conducted by Friedrich Cerha, Michael Gielen and Pinchas Steinberg, the choir has participated in the Salzburg Festival in 1995. The first performance abroad was during the Biennale in Venice in 1996 and 1997, the choir gave its first performances abroad. RSO de Vienne.

## Radio Symphonieorchester Wien

Zu Beginn der Saison 1996/97 wurde das damalige ORF-Symphonieorchester umbenannt in Radio Symphony Orchestra Vienna, gegründet 1969, es hat sich ursprünglich die Aufgabe der regelmäßigen Aufführung von Musik der dann avangarde, von Ligeti über Penderecki bis zum heutigen Tage. Das Orchester hat sich kontinuierlich sein Repertoire erweitert unter seinen verschiedenen Principal Conductors: Milan Horvat (1969-75), Leif Segerstam (1975-82), Lothar Zagrosek (1982-86) und Pinchas Steinberg (1989-96). Under its present Principal Conductor, Dennis Russell Davies, the Orchestra has appeared at the Salzburg Festival, the Bruckner Festival in Linz, the Spring Festival in Vienna, and in March 2000, for the first time in the USA.
