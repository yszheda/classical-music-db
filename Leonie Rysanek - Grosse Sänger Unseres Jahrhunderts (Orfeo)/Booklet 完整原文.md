### Auf den Spuren von Atreus

Es wurde ihr ein „königlicher Abschied" attestiert, als sich Leonie Rysanek bei den Salzburger Festspielen am 25. August 1996 in einer Elektra-Aufführung unter Lorin Maazel nach 47 unglaublichen Jahren von der Bühne zurückzog. Die Tränen in den Augen so vieler, der Blumenregen und die endlose Begeisterung eines Publikums, das aus der ganzen Welt angereist war, galten nicht nur einer unglaublichen Aufführung, sondern waren auch ein Dank an diese Sängerin des Totalansatzes, der emotionalen Attacke und der schw elgerischen Stimmsprache, die laut Thomas Voigt in der „Opernwelt" unser Leben in Unordnung gebracht hat. Leonie Rysanek war ein Phänomen, eine „Sängerin des Herzens", eine Naturbegabung, die tatsächliche keine Nachfolge gefunden hat. Und niemand im Großen Festspielhaus, der Zeuge dieser denkwürdigen Vorstellung war, konnte damals wissen, wie nahe der Abschied für immer war…

Leonie Rysanek hat in fast fünf Jahrzehnten Operngeschichte geschrieben. Seit ihrem Debüt in Bayreuth 1951 als Sieglande zählte sie zur Welt-Elite der Sänger und war gleich begehrt als Verdi- oder Puccini-Primadonna wie als Wagner- und Strauss-Interpretin. Ihre Desdemona und Senta, Tosca und Aida, Kaiserin und Salome, Elisabeth und Küsterin sind in die Goldenen Lettern der Operngeschichte eingraviert. Zahllose CD-Aufnahmen – etwa die drei Veröffentlichungen der Strauss-Oper *Die Frau ohne Schatten* aus den Jahren 1955, 1964 und 1978 – belegen diese Karriere des Außergewöhnlichen. Und dennoch: so grandios Leonie Rysanek als Leonore oder als Milada in Dalibor, so bezwingend sie als Amelia in *Der Maskenball*, als Turandot oder Lady Macbeth war – in den Werken von Richard Strauss kamen ihre Fähigkeiten ganz besonders zum Tragen. Mein Vater hat von einer solchen Stimme geträumt, urteilte etwa der Sohn von Richard Strauss nach einer Münchner Premiere; und auch Karl Böhm, der sich selbst als „Sachwalter auf Erden" für Richard Strauss fühlte, bescheinigte der 1926 in Wien/Erdberg geborenen Sängerin eine besondere Affinität zu den stimmlichen Anforderungen dieser Partitur. rin für Strauss-Werke braucht – den typischen Höhenglanz, einen großen Atem und einen über drei Oktaven gehenden Stimmumfang – weist Leonie Rysaneks Stimme im besonderen Maße auf. Schon bei ihrem zweiten Bühnen-Engagement in Saarbrücken – nach Innsbruck 1949/50 – wurde sie in der Rolle der Arabella als Strauss-Interpretin entdeckt. Und in ihrem dritten Engagement – in München – ging es dann Schlag auf Schlag: sie sang Danae und Chrysothemis, Kaiserin, Ägyptische Helena und die Marschallin. Die ersten Jahre der Straussinterpreten wurde 1955 an der Münchner Oper mit Christel Goltz zeigen diese jubelnde, junge Leonie Rysanek. ## On the trail of Atreus (English translation)

It was unquestionably seen as a "royal farewell" for her when, after 47 incredible years, Leonie Rysanek returned to the stage at the Salzburg Festival on 25 August 1996 in a performance of *Elektra* under Lorin Maazel. The tears in the eyes of many, the cascade of flowers and the endless enthusiasm of an audience that had travelled from all over the world not only made for an unforgettable performance but were also acts of gratitude to this singer of total commitment, of emotional attack and opulent vocal splendour, who according to Thomas Voigt in "Opernwelt" has thrown our life into confusion. Leonie Rysanek was a phenomenon, a "singer of the heart", a natural gift that has found no successor. And no one in the Großer Festspielhaus who witnessed this memorable performance could know how close the farewell forever was...

Leonie Rysanek made opera history for nearly five decades. Since her debut in Bayreuth as Sieglinde in 1951 she counted among the world's elite of singers and was in demand equally as a Verdi and Puccini prima donna as a Wagner and Strauss interpreter. Her Desdemona and Senta, Tosca and Aida, Empress and Kostelnička are engraved in letters of gold in the history of opera. Countless CD recordings – for example the three issues of Strauss's opera *Die Frau ohne Schatten* from 1955, 1964 and 1978 – substantiate this. And yet: magnificent as Leonie Rysanek was as Leonore or as Milada in *Dalibor*, irresistible as Amelia in *Un ballo in maschera*, as Turandot or Lady Macbeth, in the works of Richard Strauss her abilities were quite particularly striking. My father, Richard Strauss's son, for example, after a Munich premiere, and even Karl Böhm, who confirmed himself as "champion on earth" for Richard Strauss, confirmed a special affinity for the vocal demands of the score, which a singer for Strauss works needs – the typical high brilliance, a large breath and a vocal range of over three octaves – are shown by Leonie Rysanek's voice in a special degree. Already at her second stage engagement in Saarbrücken – after Innsbruck 1949/50 – she was discovered as a Strauss interpreter in the role of Arabella. And in her third engagement – in Munich – in quick succession she then sang Danae and Chrysothemis, the Empress, Egyptian Helen and the Marschallin. The first recording in the role came in August 1953 the WDR in Cologne recorded *Elektra* under Richard Kraus. The title-role was sung then leading dramatic soprano Astrid Varnay. The two were on stage together in 1951 in Bayreuth in the Wieland-Wagner-Ring under Knappertsbusch and Karajan. Previously Leonie Rysanek had demonstrated her Strauss qualities as Arabella and Danae. Her stage debut itself then took place a few months later in London, where her Munich Intendant Rudolf Hartmann directed. The title-role was taken by Erna Schlüter – but the star of the evening was her "little sister", whom the critics acclaimed. As this success was soon repeated in Munich, Vienna and La Scala, Milan (under Dimitri Mitropoulos), it was said after her role debut in Vienna in May 1955: Never was this part so splendidly sung. The gold that lies in the Greek name Chrysothemis here flows in wonderful abundance and completely secure ability are united and are captivating.

When, at the 1955 Munich Opera Festival, *Elektra* with Christel Goltz – under the thrilling direction of Karl Böhm – was broadcast on the radio, the critics revelled anew: She generously lavished the whole splendour of her radiant soprano! The excerpts from the Chrysothemis scenes at the beginning and end of the opera bring back this "early Rysanek", who later – along with Astrid Varnay, Regina Resnik, Jean Madeira, Martha Mödl and Christa Ludwig – became one of the most distinguished advocates of Clytemnestra beside Leonie Rysanek, and also the 1955 Munich live recording with Christel Goltz shows this acclaimed, young Leonie Rysanek. If the voice of Chrysothemis is too gentle, too charming, it becomes boring was already her motto at that time: I must give colour, strength and expression.

As a partner of quite equal standing among the Atrides sisters was her Chrysothemis in the Vienna staging with Karl Böhm and Wieland Wagner, as is shown by the excerpt from the 22 December 1965 premiere with Birgit Nilsson in the title-role. Wieland changed my approach to the role: I gave the character the usual blue-eyed blonde more character and backbone, said the Chrysothemis-actress of this opera from the mid-60s was almost without exception bound up with this almost archaic, overpowering interpretation by the Nilsson-Rysanek duo. Whether Wieland Wagner or August Everding, Sellner or Renner directors – the Nilsson-Rysanek pair ensured triumphs and record-breaking applause.

[Photo: Nilsson, Böhm, Rysanek 1965]

Nilsson, Böhm, Rysanek 1965

In 1980 there was a Unitel relay from the Met with Nilsson-Rysanek (under James Levine), and in the following year Unitel likewise wanted to film *Elektra* with Karl Böhm in Götz Friedrich's production. Birgit Nilsson was "not free" for this Met relay, and Karl Böhm gained Leonie Rysanek for the part of Elektra.

The reviews of Rysanek's *Elektra* quite particularly stressed the vocal beauty, the round sonority and the visionary character of this role she made use of the expressive idiom of the dead, and in the lyrical moments she reveals the character's broken nature. At the point in time of the *Elektra* recording Leonie Rysanek had reached the zenith of her career: although 55 years old, her voice had no edge or difficulties in crescendo or diminuendo.

Since the mid-1970s Leonie Rysanek had prepared for her change of voice category. Besides Santuzza (with Placido Domingo), Kundry and Kostelnička in *Jenůfa* in particular were the beginning of the path that was to lead in 1989 to Herodias and Clytemnestra queen, in which Strauss went to the limits of tonality, offered her the magnificent opportunity of exhibiting all facets of her colourful voice and her possibilities of psychological shaping of revealing her innermost self. I have acquired my *Clytemnestra* thanks to my experiences with her daughter, she said at her debut, and thereby brought to reality Hofmannsthal's saying that the three women had been seen as a unity. She exercised clarity of words and rhythm, developed cantabile lines and soprano peaks in the mezzo-soprano role, and captivated with her trump card, her enchanting pianos. The radio live recording – 43 years after her first example in sound – documents the Salzburg performance of 25 August 1996. Here a true queen of the opera stage stepped down: even at her last stage appearance she brought about that magic of emotional bridge-building that had distinguished her for almost five decades.

Translation: Lionel Salter
