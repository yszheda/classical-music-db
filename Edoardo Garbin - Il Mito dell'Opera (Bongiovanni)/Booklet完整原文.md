# Edoardo Garbin (1884-1959)

Edoardo Garbin was born in 1884 in the Veneto region of Italy. He studied singing in Venice, and made his debut in 1903. He quickly established himself as one of the leading tenors of his generation on the Italian operatic scene. He sang at all the major opera houses of Italy, including La Scala in Milan and the Teatro Regio in Parma. His career was at its height in the first three decades of the twentieth century, and he continued to sing until the 1930s.

Garbin will always be remembered for creating the role of Rodolfo in Giacomo Puccini's *La bohème* at the world premiere in 1896 at the Teatro Regio in Turin. Puccini himself had a high opinion of Garbin's singing, and Garbin remained a close friend of the composer throughout his life. He also created other roles in new operas by Italian composers, and was particularly admired for his interpretations of the romantic Italian repertoire, especially Verdi and Puccini.

Garbin's singing represents the best of the Italian tenor tradition of the early twentieth century. He had a warm, lyric voice with a clear upper register, and his singing was characterized by intense dramatic involvement and beautiful phrasing. He was one of the last exponents of the old Italian school of singing, before the development of the heavier, more dramatic tenor sound that became common in the post-war period.

This release collects all the surviving recordings made by Garbin between 1906 and 1928. It presents a comprehensive overview of his art, covering a wide range of repertoire from bel canto to verismo. It is an invaluable document for anyone interested in the history of Italian singing, and allows us to appreciate the art of one of the greatest tenors of the golden age of Italian opera.

# The Golden Age of Italian Singing

The early decades of the twentieth century were the golden age of Italian tenor singing. A generation of singers who had been trained in the old Italian bel canto tradition were at their peak, and they left behind a legacy of recordings that allow us still today to appreciate the art of singing as it was practiced in the great Italian opera houses. Edoardo Garbin was one of the most distinguished representatives of this generation.

He created the role of Rodolfo in the premiere of *La bohème*, one of the most beloved Italian operas ever written, and he sang throughout Italy during a career that spanned more than thirty years. His recordings, collected here for the first time in complete form, allow us to hear again the art of a singer who was much admired in his day, and who deserves to be remembered as one of the finest tenors of his generation.

Bongiovanni is proud to continue its series "Il Mito dell'Opera", which is dedicated to preserving the memory of the great singers of the past by reissuing their recordings in carefully remastered editions for the modern listener. We hope that this release will help to bring the art of Edoardo Garbin to the attention of a new generation of music lovers.
