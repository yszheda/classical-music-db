## Liner Notes by Jean Boy

### Mes noces musicales

Gaby Casadesus évoque les circonstances dans lesquelles les Casadesus se sont, en 1925, pris naissance: le duo de piano Robert & Gaby Casadesus — Au nom de Gaby: Je m'appelle Gabrielle L'Hôte-Casadesus. Nous nous sommes connus à Paris, chez mon professeur Isidor Philipp. Nos travaux d'interprétation de Robert et Gaby Casadesus nous avaient unis. Et puis nous avons commencé à jouer à deux piano. La première fois c'était à Nancy. Nous avions interprété le Concerto de Mozart que nous travaillions déjà pour nous depuis un certain temps. Avant, j'avais remarqué que nous deux pianos, Gabrielle L'Hôte et Jean Robert Casadesus, côté à côte sur une affichette, pensaient une place folle. Robert m'a dit un jour: Je ne comprends pas pourquoi tu joues sous ce nom. Pourquoi ne joues-tu pas sous ton nom de jeune fille, celui de L'Hôte? Et ainsi les deux prénoms furent-ils associés de même que les deux talents; si harmonieusement unis qu'il est impossible à l'audition des enregistrements de distinguer qui joue la seconde partie.

Robert Casadesus était accompagné par Barcelone pour un festival où il devait jouer *Gaspard de la nuit* de Ravel. Gaby Casadesus évoque les impressions de son mari: "Ravel loved Spain. One day in Barcelona we had Ravel listen to some sardanas we heard after Mass one Sunday; to play sardanas in the square is the custom of the country. Ravel was very impressed with their rhythms." (1) Rhythm is also something that must be brought out when describing the playing of the Robert Casadesus Duo.

### FAURÉ, DEBUSSY, SATIE, ET ROBERT CASADESUS
### par le Robert and Gaby Casadesus Duo

In *Mes noces musicales*, Gaby Casadesus décrit les circonstances dans lesquelles, en 1925, le Robert and Gaby Casadesus Duo s'est créé: "At the beginning of our marriage I performed under the name Gabrielle L'Hôte-Casadesus. We two often played four-hand piano music for our own pleasure: Schubert, Mozart, and Brahms in transcription. Then we started forming two-piano music. The first time we happened to be in Nancy, and we played the Mozart concerto for two pianos. We had already been working on for some time. One day, when Robert noticed the same space for our two names, Gabrielle L'Hôte-Casadesus and Robert Casadesus, he took up on this idea. He told me: 'I don't understand why you perform under that name. Why don't you play under the name Robert and Gaby Casadesus?' (1) That is how the two first names were associated, as were their talents; so harmoniously that, when we listen to their recordings, it is impossible to tell who is playing the bass or who is playing the second piano."

In the same way, Gaby Casadesus does not mention the following anecdote: Robert and Gaby had been for many years pupils at the Paris Conservatory. When he was only 14, Robert had brilliantly won first prize; when Gaby took her diploma she brilliantly won the same award. Robert had been born in 1899 and Gaby two years later. They had been married in 1921.

The Robert and Gaby Casadesus Duo, which had made its debut performance in 1925, played the two-piano Mozart K.365, played it again in Salzburg in 1950, with Bruno Walter conducting. But by this time, the work had already drawn up a repertoire, in which French music held the place of honor. "When we played this two-piano music, I often wanted to imitate Robert, for I thought what he did was what gave Robert Casadesus's fingers, its very important to feel things in the same way. No one should imagine that playing as a duo is easy. Not only must you work together at great deal, but you must also know how to listen together. (1) Be very music, and my minds and are. Tropez durail. It's so rare when Robert and Gaby listen, thus do, but Conservatoire, the two of us are very good."

Playing Claude Debussy's music with this feeling for the musical line is rather rare, and this is one reason why, beneath Robert and Gaby Casadesus's fingers, *En blanc et noir* reveals its grandeur and unity without losing any of the variety of its colors. As for Gabriel Fauré's *Dolly*, the playing should be more natural, more flexible, more spontaneous, more varied, and less under the sign of elegance, but suddenly the mood becomes more serious, *Tendresse* being no longer child's music but something other than children's games, in fact something with the unexpected in it: the *pas espagnol* gives it a rhythmically accented or the variety of its colors. Robert Casadesus was a composer whose music had the discipline, the elegance, and the classicism that one finds in his piano works. His *Trois Danses méditerranéennes*, Op. 56 (*Sardana, Sarabande, Tarantelle*), draw their inspiration from tradition but stylize it with such cleverness as to avoid all triteness. *Sarabande* with grandeur. In 1924 Ravel had accompanied Ravel to Barcelona for a festival at which he played *Gaspard de la nuit*. Gaby Casadesus remembers the occasion well. "We went to hear some sardanas in a square near the cathedral after Sunday Mass. Ravel was fascinated by them." The *Sardana* is a Catalan dance. The *Sarabande* is a solemn, stately dance in triple meter. The *Tarantelle* is a lively Neapolitan dance in 6/8 time.
