## liner notes (English)

*His was the inventor's mind par excellence... Simply and ingeniously Satie pointed the way, but as soon as another musician took to the trail he had indicated, Satie would immediately change his own orientation and without hesitating open up still another path to new fields of experimentation.*

Thus his fellow-composer Maurice Ravel summed up the importance that Satie had had. He did this while on a concert tour in the U.S.A, a few years after Satie's death when he lectured on French music. But Ravel was also one of the few who recognized one of the first to play Satie's piano music in public. That was in 1911, the year Debussy first performed his orchestral versions of two of Satie's three "Gymnopédies". In both instances, Satie was not wholly appreciative. This was not because he did not admire the two more established composers. Quite the contrary. Debussy he almost worshiped. But he would have preferred them to have used their position to promote some of his newer and more experimental piano works rather than such that were more than ten years old.

But in a slightly longer perspective the effect was not insignificant. French music publishers began to interest themselves in this bizarre loner of Parisian musical life of whom one could be certain of one thing: he always carried an umbrella. The publishers were even willing to publish some of his new pieces as well. The three "Véritables préludes flasques" were written in 1912 at the behest of the publisher Demetz.

From a generalized point of view there are really only two things to note about Satie's music: he loved to mystify his audience with peculiar titles and he generally wrote his miniatures in threes. From both these points of view this selection of Satie's piano music is fully representative! It extends over three of the four decades of his composing life and even with the best will in the world it could hardly have been stretched any further. Prior to the three "Sarabandes" from 1887, inspired by Chabrier, there are only some insignificant miniatures from his unsuccessful time at the Conservatory. And after 1917 he was sufficiently established to be able to concentrate on larger forms, specially theatre music.

It is not always possible to penetrate the meaning of Satie's titles. But some of them can be explained with reasonable certainty. "Gymnopédies" refers to the ancient Spartan dances performed by naked youths. Jean Cocteau, who first met Satie in 1915 and became one of his most eager supporters, was especially fond of these pieces "whose limpid cadences evoke visions of nude boy dancers silhouetted on a Grecian urn" (Cocteau's remark). "Gnossiennes" likewise refers to ancient Greece, to the maidens of Knossos. Only the first three were published during Satie's lifetime. They build on firm thematic repetitions and, with "Bolero" in mind, it is not so difficult to understand that Ravel wrote positively of them when in 1911 he heard his friend, the eminent pianist Ricardo Viñes play them at a concert.

"Embryons desséchés" employs as subtitles the scientific names of three types of crawfish that live in the bay at St.Malo: "Holothurie", "Edriophthalma" and "Podophthalma". These names can scarcely have meant anything to the Parisian concertgoers, but one well-known melody which Satie quotes in passing is well-known in France, "Mon rocher de St.Malo".

## liner notes (French)

*Son esprit en était un d'inventeur par excellence... Satie indiquait simplement et ingénieusement le mais aussitôt qu'un autre musicien suivait ses traces, Satie changeait immédiatement sa propre orientation pour ouvrir une autre voie vers de nouveaux champs d'expériences.*

C'est ainsi que son collègue compositeur, Maurice Ravel, résuma l'importance que Satie avait eue. Ceci arriva lorsque Ravel, lors d'une tournée de concerts aux Etats-Unis quelques années après la mort de Satie, donnait des conférences sur la musique française. Mais Ravel était également l'un des rares personnalités qui reconnaissait rapidement et qui cherchèrent à promouvoir la musique de Satie. Ravel fut l'un des premiers à jouer en public la musique de piano de Satie. C'était en 1911, l'année que Debussy créa sa version orchestrale de deux des trois "Gymnopédies". Dans les deux occasions, Satie ne fut pas entièrement enthousiaste. Non, même presque sans bornes. Il est facile comprendre que Satie n'admirait pas Debussy. Satie voulait que l'on promouvait ses pièces plus récentes et plus expérimentales plutôt que celles qu'il avait écrites trente d'années plus tôt.

Vulgairement pourrait on dire déjà à cette époque: c'est Satie qui a commencé, après tout, quand il s'agit d'un modernisme français.

Sur le plan général, il n'y a en fait que deux choses à remarquer dans la musique pour piano de Satie: il aimait à mystifier son public par des titres singuliers, et il écrivait ses petites pièces trois à la fois. A ces égards, ce disque est bien représentatif! Il s'étend sur trois de ses cinq décennies productives, et même avec la meilleure volonté on ne peut l'étendre plus loin. Avant les Trois Sarabandes inspirées de Chabrier datant de 1887, il n'existe que de petites pièces sans importance datant de son temps conservatoire. Et après 1917, il était assez établi pour pouvoir se concentrer sur des formes plus grandes, spécialement pour la scène.

Il n'est pas toujours possible de pénétrer le sens des titres de Satie. Mais certains peuvent être expliqués avec certitude. «Gymnopédies» a trait à la danse des anciens spartiates, faite par des jeunes hommes nus. Jean Cocteau, que Satie a rencontré en 1915, devint un des champions les plus ardents, et il appelle particulièrement ces pièces «dont les limpides cadences évoquent des visions de garçons nus dans une urne grecque». «Gnossiennes» se réfère aussi à la Grèce antique, savoir aux jeunes filles de l'île de Knossos. Seules les trois premières mères furent publiées du vivant de Satie. Elles bâties sur répétitions thématiques certaines, et c'est facile comprendre que Ravel a pu être positivement impressionné quand en 1911 il eut entendu son ami, l'éminent pianiste Ricardo Viñes, les jouer dans un concert.

«Embryons desséchés» portent comme sous-titres les noms scientifiques de trois crustacés vivant dans la baie de St.Malo: «Holothurie», «Edriophthalma» et «Podophthalma». Ces noms peuvent à peine dire quoi que ce soit au concertiste parisien, mais l'un d'eux cite une mélodie connue: c'est la célèbre marche funèbre de Chopin qu'il met sous presse à une place en particulier...

## Roland Pöntinen biography (English, German, French)

### English
The Swedish pianist **Roland Pöntinen** made his début in 1981 with the Royal Stockholm Philharmonic Orchestra and has since then performed with major orchestras in Europe, the United States, Korea, South America, Australia and New Zealand. He has collaborated with such eminent conductors as Myung-Whun Chung, Rafael Frühbeck de Burgos, Neeme Järvi, Esa-Pekka Salonen, Leif Segerstam and Evgeny Svetlanov. Highlights of his career have included appearances with the Philharmonia Orchestra in Paris and London, and with the Los Angeles Philharmonic Orchestra at the Hollywood Bowl as well as performances at the BBC Proms where he has played both the Grieg and the Ligeti piano concertos. With his insatiable musical appetite and stupendous technique, Pöntinen has acquired a vast repertoire, ranging from Bach to Ligeti. The emphasis is on the 'golden era' of piano literature: from the 19th century with composers such as Debussy, Busoni, Szymanowski and Rachmaninov to the first half of the 20th century.

Roland Pöntinen is also a passionate chamber musician and performs regularly with musicians such as the clarinettist Martin Fröst, the violinist Ulf Wallin, the soprano Barbara Hendricks and his piano duo partner Love Derwinger. He has participated in numerous festivals including the Schleswig-Holstein Festival, Maggio Musicale Fiorentino, La Roque d'Antheron Piano Festival and the Styriarte Festival in Graz. Roland Pöntinen is also active as a composer. He is a member of the Royal Swedish Academy of Music and in 2001 received 'Litteris et Artibus' - a royal medal in recognition of eminent skills in the artistic field.
