## About Kemal Gekic

Kemal Gekic, whose name became known widely after the jury of the International Chopin Competition turned him down in 1985, is a performer bearing the stamp of interpretive originality and marked personality. His audiences find intense expression. Pianists with strong perspectives on the fascinating his enormous energy, wide dynamic range and did not change his interpretation of Chopin to conform to routine ideas. approach is valued above originality of thought and expression.

Kemal Gekic's playing so won the hearts of the audience in Warsaw that many people believed him to be destined for laurels. However, appearance in the final stage of the 1985 Competition was denied to him. Gekic believes strongly in Liszt's music as well, having performed the great Hungarian born musician's music since his debut as a professional artist.

Kemal Gekic was born in Split, Yugoslavia (today Croatia) in 1962. While still a student in 1981, he won the second prize at the Franz Liszt Competition. After a sensational performance at the Montreal International Competition, he began an international career of ever increasing distinction.

Kemal Gekic has made a wide range of recordings, including major works by Handel, Mozart, Chopin, Liszt, Rachmaninov, Scriabin and Reger. His CD of Liszt's Transcendental Etudes enjoys worldwide recognition and praise. The decade long war in Yugoslavia deeply affected his personal and artistic life. Finally he moved to Florida after one of his US tours (in 1999), to become Artist In Residence and Professor of Piano at the Florida International University, while continuing his extensive concert, recital and recording activities throughout the world.

"I have been teaching students for more than 20 years now. My way of teaching is based on what I learned performing and teaching worldwide and what I learned from my former professor and other artists I met. I don't tell students everything they have to do. Since I respect the individuality of each student I give them only the most essential information. I provide the clues and cues for them to draw as much as they can from within - from their experiences, their taste, their sense of style. My idea is to bring out the best qualities that are in them."

To respect individuality was an important component of his musical education - even as a child. Although the people from the Chopin Competition referred to his performance as "Chopin's music is both personal and, at the same time, between those extremes yet including them - seems to satisfy my audiences everywhere."

"There was an instant appeal to me since I heard his music for the first time. Liszt's music offers the pianists the happiest, most stirring moments! The more you play his music, the more you fall in love with it." It has the mysterious power to ignite the emotion of his audiences. Liszt's music is often noticed more for its transcendent piano style than for its intellectual, poetic, even speculative and even philosophical matter. Yet it is less the welter of notes on the page and more the mystical personality of Liszt the composer that inspires the pianist's deeply communicative understanding of the composer's works. Liszt's spirit seems to pervade and inhabit Gekic's intense and inspired interpretations.

Kemal Gekic once mentioned to me, "Performance is a living art form!" The aptness of the remark can be felt in every measure of the performances heard on this CD, as Liszt's music has been resurrected in the 21st century under the hands of Kemal Gekic.

## About Kemal Gekic (Biography)

Originality, strength of interpretation and perfect mastery of his instrument are some of the qualities ascribed to one of today's most formidable and exciting pianists, Kemal Gekic, whose playing has been praised worldwide by public and critics alike.

What makes him so unique is his artistic "credo" that encompasses both a knowledge of all the facts essential to reconstructing the original idea behind the works he plays and the power to bring it alive for today's audiences.

He graduated in 1982 from The Academy of Arts in Novi Sad, with the highest mark ever granted a diploma exam recital, and was immediately given a faculty appointment by the piano department. He earned his Master's degree in 1985, the same year he created a sensation at the Chopin Competition in Warsaw.

Since 1985 he has had an extensive concert activity throughout Europe, USA, Canada, Japan, South America, Africa, USSR, Middle East and Far East. His performances have been broadcast by RAI Italy, TV Portugal, TV Yugoslavia, NHK Japan, POLTEL Poland, Egyptian TV, NDR Germany, RTY USSR, Intervision, PBS USA and CBS Canada.

As a recording artist, Kemal Gekic has won accolades in Europe, America and Japan for insightful, and original views of the music of Handel, Mozart, Chopin, Scriabin and Liszt. His outstanding Naxos CD of Rossini Liszt transcriptions has won 'The Rosette' of the Penguin Guide to music, while his JVC CD of Liszt's Twelve Transcendental Etudes is generally considered to be the best recording of the set in history.

Born in Split, Croatia, Kemal Gekic lives in the United States and he is The Artist in Residence of the Florida International University. He performs worldwide.

## About Paul Freeman

Paul Freeman Born in Richmond, Virginia, studied conducting as a Fulbright Scholar at the Hochschule für Musik in Berlin. In 1987 he became the Founding Music Director of the Chicago Sinfonietta. From 1979 to 1989 he served as Music Director of the Victoria Symphony in Canada, prior to which he was Principal Guest Conductor of the Helsinki Philharmonic, Associate Conductor of the Dallas and Detroit Orchestras, and Music Director of Opera Theatre in Rochester, New York.

In January 1996, Paul Freeman became Music Director of the Czech National Symphony Orchestra in Prague. He has conducted more than 100 orchestras in 28 different countries and made more than 200 recordings.

## About the Czech National Symphony Orchestra

Ever since Czechoslovakia's bloodless Velvet Revolution of 1989, the Czech Republic has been riding a rapid wave of democratization that affects the music industry as well. Orchestras, to survive, must concern themselves with procuring foreign funds through recording contracts and overseas performances. These developments have heightened a need for higher performance standards.

In this chaotic scene, Jan Hasenohrl, an outstanding solo trumpet player, saw the chance to reshape the Czech orchestra scene and, in 1993, invited top musicians from Prague's major orchestral to form a new ensemble, the Czech National Symphony Orchestra. The orchestra gave its first concert, conducted by Vladimir Valek, in November 1993 in Dvorak Hall of the Rudolfinum.

In 1994, a national treasure of the Czech musical world, Zdenék Kosler, was named chief conductor. The orchestra's first recording was made in April 1994. Maestro Kosler died in August 1995. In January 1996, the orchestra's favorite guest conductor, Paul Freeman of the Chicago Sinfonietta, was appointed music director and chief conductor. Under Maestro Freeman's dynamic leadership, the Czech National Symphony Orchestra has experienced stunning development. He and the orchestra have made some three dozen compact discs together and have toured Italy, Ireland and Great Britain. So successful was the November 1997 tour of the United Kingdom under the batons of Paul Freeman and Libor Pesek that London's IMG Concert Management subsequently signed a five year contract to tour the Czech National Symphony Orchestra in Europe, Asia and America.

## Liszt's Works Notes

### Piano Concerto No.1 in E-flat major, S.124

Liszt began this work in 1830, worked on it on and off for many years, with composition progressing in fits and starts, and finally completed it in 1849, and it was premiered in 1852 under the baton of Hector Berlioz. He revised it again in 1853 and 1856, reaching its present form. It is in one continuous movement with interconnected sections that can be divided into four movements thematically connected, maintaining overall unity. Liszt created something new in the concerto form, first movement has an impressive opening theme that is powerful. The scherzo is in E-flat major 12/8, with muted lower strings and woodwinds creating a fantastic world.

### Totentanz (Dance of the Dead), S.126

Based on the medieval Gregorian chant "Dies irae", this is a set of variations. Liszt worked on this piece repeatedly, a sketch survives from 1839, completed in 1849. Characteristic writing has dense chromatic tension, the pieces is close to a symphonic poem with solo piano. Like the first concerto, it maintains continuity through four sections, themes connected throughout, the opening theme returns as the basis for variations, the whole work based on this theme. Starting with the theme presented beautifully by woodwinds, the piano takes the theme and then builds up through variations, the theme grows progressively through variation, it's dominated by the demonic impression, becoming a spectacular march at the end.

### Piano Concerto No.2 in A major, S.125

Liszt began the second concerto after the first, completed it in 1849, but after many revisions the final form came in 1857. Dedicated to his student, the pianist Anton Rubinstein. Like the first concerto, it is a continuous single movement work, with many changes of mood, it's a lyrical work with rich colors. This is a wonderfully rhapsodic and impressionistic work with deep impressions at every turn.

### Fantasy on Hungarian Folk Themes, S.123

Composed in 1840s, based on Hungarian folk melodies, Liszt uses characteristic Hungarian rhythmic and melodic elements, creates a rhapsodic fantasia full of national color.
