KEMAL GEKIC Originality, strength of interpretation and perfect mastery of his instrument are some of the qualities ascribed to one PHOTO:Atanas Mlazev of today's most formidable and exciting pianists, Kemal Gekic, whose playing has been praised worldwide by public and critics alike. What makes him so unique is his artistic “credo" that encompasses both a knowledge of all the facts essential to reconstructing the original idea behind the works he plays and the power to bring it alive for today's audiences. He He graduated in 1982 from The Academy of Arts in Novi Sad, with the highest mark ever granted a diploma exam recital, and was immediately given a faculty appointment by the piano department. He earned his Master's degree in 1985, the same year he created a sensation at the chopin competition in Warsaw.

Since 1985 he has had an extensive concert activity throughout Europe, USA, Canada, Japan, South America, Africa, UssR, , Middle East and Far East. His performances have been broadcast by RAI Italy, TV Portugal, TV Yugoslavia, NHK Japan, POLTEL Poland, Egyptian TV, NDR Germany, RTY USSR, Intervision, PBS USA and CBS Canada.

As a recording artist, Kemal Gekic has won accolades in Europe, America and Japan for insightful, and original views of the music of Handel, Mozart, Chopin, Scriabin and Liszt. His outstanding Naxos CD of Rossini Liszt transcriptions has won 'The Rosette' of the Penguin Guide for music, while his JVC CD of Liszt's Twelve Transcendental Etudes is generally considered to be the best recording of the set in history. Born in Split, Croatia, Kemal Gekic lives in the United States and he is The Artist in Residence of the Florida International University. He performs worldwide.
Paul Freeman Born in Richmond, Virginia, studied conducting as a Fulbright Scholar at the Hochschulefuir Musik in Berlin. In1987hebecame the Founding Music Director of the Chicago Sinfonieta. From 1979 to 1989 he served as Music Director of the Victoria Symphony in Canada, prior to which he was Principal Guest Conductor of the Helsinki Philharmonic, Associate Conductor of the Dallas and Detroit Orchestras, and Music Director of Opera Theatre in Rochester, New York. In January1996,Paul Freeman became Music Director of the Czech National Symphony Orchestra in Prague. He has conducted more than 100 orchestras in 28 different countries and made more than 200 recordings. PHOTO:Jan Vanis

The Czech National Symphony Orchestra Ever since Czechoslovakia'sbloodless Velvet Revolutionof 1989,the Czech Republichasbeen ridinga rapid waveof democratization that affects the music industry as well. Orchestras, to survive, must concern themselves with procuring foreign funds through recording contracts and overseas performances. These developments have heightened a need for higher performance standards. In this chaotic scene, Jan Hasenohrl, an outstanding solo trumpet player, saw the chance to reshape the Czech orchestra scene and, in 1993, invited top musicians from Prague's major orchestral to from a new ensemble, the Czech National Symphony Orchestra. The orchestra gave its first concert, conducted by Vladimir Valek, in November 1993 in Dvorak Hall of the Rudolfinum. In 1994, a national treasure of the Czech musical world, Zdenék Kosler, was named chief conductor. The orchestra's first recording was made in April 1994. Maestro Kosler died in August 1995. In January 1996, the orchestra's favorite guest conductor, Paul Freeman of the Chicago Sinfonietta, was appointed music director and chief conductor. Under Maestro Freeman's dynamic leadership, the Czech National Symphony Orchestra has experienced stunning development. He and the orchestra have made some three dozen compact discs together and have toured Italy, Ireland and Great Britain. So successful was the November 1997 tour of the United Kingom under the batons of Paul Freeman and Libor Pesek that London's IMG Concert Management subsequently signed a five year contract to tour the Czech National Symphony Orchestra in Europe, Asia and America.
