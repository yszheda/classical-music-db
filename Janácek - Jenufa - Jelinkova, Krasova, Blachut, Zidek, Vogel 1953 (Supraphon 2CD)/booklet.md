Nonductor Jaroslav Vogel (1894-1970) belonged to the generation which followed on -from authentic stagings of Leos Janacek's operas. As a young conductor he came into contact with Janacek's opera when the composer was still alive. Already by the end of the twenties, when Janacek died, Vogel can be considered one of the most perceptive Janacek exponents. For him the interpretation of Janacek involved understanding the artist and his surroundings and researching the genesis of the works. This meant learning to read Janacek's manuscripts and grasping a given work's structure and its evolution from initial sketches to the final score. Vogel was something of a rarity in the Czech environment in that he was a highly intellectual conductor whose erudition, analytic perceptiveness and literary ability outstripped many a trained academic's. This is at tested by his monograph on Janacek, a key work that has been translated into English and German and no doubt has prompted many foreign musicologists to study one of the greatest Czech composers of all. Jaroslav Vogel was a composer conductor, a phenomenon that was not infrequent at the beginning of this century -- he was in the company of Karel Kovarovic, Oskar Nedbal Ludvik Vitezslav Celansky, Otakar Ostrcil and Frantisek Neumann to name but a few. His study of conducting and composition (in Munich and Paris) and his wide knowledge of music helped him make a tasteful selection of the best works of late Romantism, Verismo and modernism. He tried to bridge the gap between the European tradition and modern ism -- from Verdi via Wagner, whom he particularly admired, Strauss and Puccini to music of the twentieth century. During his many years as orchestral conductor at Ostrava he introduced many new works and he is one of the few Czech conductors under whom foremost composers of the first half of the century played. In December 1932, for exam ple, Paul Hindemith played Vivaldi's Concerto for viola d'amour (arranged for viola) and his own Viola Sonata under Vogel in Ostrava; he also attended Vogel's rehearsals of the overture to his opera Neues vom Tage. In January 1933, again in Ostrava, Jaroslav Vogel conducted a program of Igor Stravinsky's music with the master himself playing the piano part to his Capriccio for piano and orchestra; the concert incidentally also featured a suite from Petrushka, Pulcinella and the Symphony of Psalms. And two years later, in January 1935, Vogel conducted Sergei Prokofiev playing his third Piano Concerto in a 10

When we consider the Czechoslovak premieres of new European music that Vogel gave -Schoenberg's Begleitungsmusik zu einer Lichtspielszene, op. 34, Bartok's Music for Strings, Percussion and Celesta, works by Stravinsky and world premieres of works by Czech composers - then he stands out as one of the most original, impressive and daring con ductors of opera and orchestral music between the 1920s and the 1960s. The first Janacek opera that Vogel came to grips with as conductor was Jenifa, which opened on 6 September 1929 at the National Moravian Silesian Theatre in Ostrava (where it had been staged once before, by Bastl, with Janacek himself in attendance at the pre miere on 1 November 1919). He conducted new productions of what is perhaps Janacek's best known operatic work in 1934 and 1938. In addition to that, Vogel conducted a 1938 production of Kata Kabanova (Katya Kabanova) at Ostrava (again after Bastl, whose production premiered on 18 January 1924 in Janacek's presence). One of Vogel's boldest acts in the propagation of Janacek's music was the staging of his last opera From the House of the Dead, at Ostrava on 12 February 1932, straight after its posthumous world premiere in Brno. The other Janacek operas he conducted before the war were The Makropoulos Case in 1935 and The Cunning Little Vixen in 1936; as to Janacek's orchestral oeuvre, Vogel also gave the first Ostrava performance of the rhap sody Taras Bulba (15.1.1929). After World War Two Jaroslav Vogel concentrated again on Janacek's operas, but this time as conductor of the National Theatre in Prague, a position he held from 1947 to 1958. A recording was made of his Prague Jenifa, and this is an invaluable record of his conception of Janacek at a time when he was a mature artist. For the National Theatre Vogel also conducted The Cunning Little Vixen and, in 1958 on the 30th anniversary of its composition, From the House of the Dead, for which Vogel reinstated Janacek's own version of the finale of the opera. Vogel's lifelong propagation of Janacek's music is best summed up by a sentence from the oration he delivered at an act of commemoration held in Hukvaldy to mark the tenth anniversary of the composer's death (1938): ".. But just as Janacek's terse speech motifs (napévky) strike a longer lasting reso-11
nance in the soul, so the whole of his not short but, as it were, volcanic life changes before our eyes into one great song of Mother Earth - one of the most glorious songs that ever has been sung..." Conductor and composer Jaroslav Vogel (1894-1970), while still at school in Plzen. studied the violin under Otakar Sevcik and composition privately with Vitezslav Novak. He continued his studies at the Academy of Music in Munich and the Schola Cantorum in Paris. Wherever he worked his tenure produced highly distinctive programming and in terpretations, whether it was at Ostrava (1919-1923, 1927-1943 and as director after World War Two until 1948), in the National Theatre in Prague (1949-1958), in Brno (State Philharmonic, 1959-1962), Plzen or during his many engagements elsewhere. Jaroslav Vogel was the author of the first substantial monograph on Leos Janacek (First published in 1958), whose music he tirelessly promoted, and he was also the first to make gramophone recordings of works by Janacek and Novak.

Soprano Stepanka Jelinkova (1911-1996) had her first engagement at Brno Opera (1936-1941) where she learnt the principal dramatic roles of her repertoire. In 1942 Vaclav Talich brought her to the National Theatre in Prague, where she stayed until 1972. Her greatest successes in Prague undoubtedly include her creation of Jenifa which ac corded with Talich's conception of the role as a lyrical one in which the chief emphasis is laid on the vocal element and cantabile performance style. In international repertoire she is best remembered for her Countess (The Marriage of Figaro), Donna Elvira and Donna Anna (Don Giovanni) and Fiordiligi (Cosi fan tutte). Her great Czech roles were Marenka (Smetana - The Bartered Bride), Karolina (Smetana - Two Widows) and the title role in Dvorak's Rusalka.

Mezzosoprano Marta Krasova (1901-1970) was a soloist at the National Theatre Prague from 1928 to 1966. She studied the violin and piano and initially considered becoming a teacher. Her interest in singing developed in her studies under Olga Valouskova and Rizena Maturova and led to engagements at Ceské Budejovice Opera and then in 1922 in 12

Bratislava, where she performed alongside such eminent guests as Slezak, Schmedes and Shalyapin. Richard Strauss proposed engaging her for the role of Oktavian (Rosenkavalier) in Vienna but she chose to take up Otakar Ostrcil's offer and joined the Prague National Opera, where she sang Amneris in Aida and Roza in Smetana's The Secret. Her extraor dinary technique enabled her to produce a coloratura soprano (Markéta) and a contralto register (the Countess in The Queen of Spades). Success in a Salzburg singing competi tion (1937) and a subsequent tour of the USA and Canada brought an invitation to work at the New York Metropolitan Opera (1939). Foreign appearances were however pre vented by the war. Her marvellous creations of Eboli, Ortrud, Konchakovna, Jezibaba and Clytemnestra were not seen outside Prague. Marriage to the composer Karel Boleslav Jirak extended her interest to contemporary music and to oratorios and concerts. The intelligence of her acting and her sense of style and order made every creation of hers an event - whether Carmen, Isabella (in Fibich's Bride of Messina), Orfeo, or Kostelnicka and Kabanicha. She appears on Supraphon complete recordings of the operas Jenifa, The Kiss (Smetana), Rusalka (Dvorak), Sarka (Fibich) and Svatopluk (Suchon).

Tenor Beno Blachut (1913-1985) spent virtually his entire professional career in the National Theatre, Prague, which he joined in 1941 after two years in Olomouc. He started out as a lyric tenor but progressed to dramatic roles in which he brought out the emo tional content of the role. In his years at the National Theatre he performed probably all the tenor roles of the Czech repertoire (Smetana's Dalibor was a role he returned to throughout his life), but he was also outstanding in Mozart, Tchaikovsky, Wagner, Beethoven and Leoncavallo. In the sixties he took on more buffo roles: Benda (Dvorak -The Jacobin), Skrivanek (Smetana - The Secret), Michalek (Smetana -.The Devil's Wall) and the immortal Mr Broucek (Janacek). He is featured on Supraphon complete record ings of Kata Kabanova and From the House of the Dead (both Janacek).

Tenor Ivo Zidek (1926) was originally interested in becoming a painter before taking up the study of music theory and then singing under Rudolf Vasek. He was only nineteen when he sang Tonik in Smetana's Two Widows at Ostrava and soon afterwards the title 13
role in Werther. After three seasons there (1945-1948) conductor Zdenék Chalabala brought him to the National Theatre in Prague where he went on to perform a wide. principally lyrical repertoire that embraced the roles of Belmont, Tamino and Don Ottavio. Jenik, Ladislav and Vitek (Smetana), as well as Benvenuto Cellini, Faust and Lohengrin From 1956 he also sang at the State Opera, Vienna, in such roles as Hoffmann, Des Grieux, Ottavio, Don Carlos and Bacchus, appeared as regular guest in Berlin, Amster dam, Stuttgart and Monte Carlo, and also sang in New York and Buenos Aires. He ex celled in Janacek roles, especially Steva - but also Laca, Gregor, Skuratov and the triple role in The Excursions of Mr Broucek. On the concert stage he was a soughtafter soloist in Stravinsky, Orff and Honegger. He was a devoted propagator of new music, including opera (Martini, Karel, Kvapil and Kaslik). On his retirement as a soloist he briefly be came director of the National Theatre (1989-1991). For Supraphon he made many suc cessful opera recordings, among them, The Bartered Bride, The Devil's Wall, Libuse (Smetana), Rusalka (Dvorak), Jenifa, The Excursions of Mr Broucek, From the House of the Dead, The Makropoulos Case (Janacek) and Juliette (Martini).

SYNOPSIS

(Before the action of the opera begins) Grandmother Buryjovka had two sons, the elder of whom inherited the mill while the younger, Tomas, worked there as an appren tice. The mill owner married a widow, Mrs Klemenova, who already had a son from her first marriage, Laca. Then Steva was born, Laca's younger brother. Grandmother Buryjovka's second son, Tomas Burya, married Jenifa, an innkeeper's daughter, and they had a daughter also named Jenifa. Tomas was widowed when Jenifa was still a child and he found a new wife in Petronila Slomkova. Daughter of the village magistrate, Petronila was a prudent and considerate woman who had loved him years before. She was a devoted stepmother (hence the Czech title Jeji pastorkyna, ‘Her Foster Daughter'); having no children of her own she lavished upon Jenifa all the love of her unfulfilled motherhood. She was unhappy in her marriage: Tomas drank, and she often had to hide 14


---

Jenifa from his cruelty. He died, but not before drinking them out of house and home. In spite of the hardship of her life Petronila tried to protect Jenifa from suffering. To make ends meet she took on the responsibility of looking after the village chapel and came to be known by all the villagers simply as ‘Kostelnicka' (meaning ‘sacristan'). Grandmother Buryjovka spoilt Steva, soon to be the new mill owner, but Laca was neglected. Jenifa, however, as a girl always felt close to Laca. In time Laca's feelings for her changed into love, which she became aware of. He was called up to serve in the army for a few years and when he returned to the village, things were not as expected. Jenifa had fallen in love with his half brother, Steva, a handsome but superficial and profligate young man, and she was hoping they would marry. Act One: Nobody realizes that the reason Jenifa wants to marry Steva is that she is carrying his child. She is anxiously waiting to see if he has been drafted. He arrives, ebullient and drunk, having escaped the draft. Kostelnicka will only consent to the mar riage if Steva can go one year without getting drunk. Laca is grateful to Kostelnicka for the respite, but fierce jealousy goads him into committing a rash act: he slashes Jenifa's face with a knife to disfigure her beauty so that Steva will lose interest in her. Act Two: Kostelnicka has been keeping Jenifa in hiding and no one knows she has given birth to a baby boy. Steva has got engaged to the wealthy magistrate's daughter Karolka and he ignores Kostelnicka's urging that he should marry Jenifa. The faithful Laca is shocked by news of Jenifa's child by Steva. Kostelnicka blurts out that the boy is dead. Thereupon, believing she is aiding Jenifa by removing the stain on her honour, she takes the child and drowns it in the stream. She tells Jenifa her boy died while she lay delirious with fever. Jenifa, in her grief, accepts marriage with Laca. Act Three: It is the wedding day and well wishers are arriving. Kostelnicka has not got over her deed; she is worn and ill at ease. There is a sudden commotion outside. Villagers rush in with dreadful news of a child found in the thawing stream. Jenifa recog nizes it as her own. Kostelnicka confesses and asks her foster daughter for forgiveness. Jenifa feels she is too wretched a creature for Laca to want to marry her now, but he refuses to abandon her; they vow to stand by each other, come what may.

Mais ce mariage ne fut pas heureux, son mari Tomas était un ivrogne et avec la petite Jenufa elles devaient souvent se cacher devant ses brutalites. Il mourut apres avoir gaspille en boisson tous leurs biens. Petronila gagnait durement sa vie mais elle ceuvrait pour que sa fille ne souffre pas de privations. Pour gagner leur vie elle s'occupait de I'église et depuis les villageois I'appellent Kostelnicka (La Marguilliere). Au moulin des deux garcon eux aussi sont devenus orphelins. La vieille Buryjovka gatait le jeune Steva, futur propriétaire tandis que Laca restait seul. Mais la petite Jenufa s'attachait fortement a ce garcon timide. Au fil des annees cette affection de Laca s'est transformée en amour. Mais Laca fut recrute pour plusieurs annees et a son retour tout est different. Jenufa est tombe amoureuse de son demi frere Steva, beau garcon superficiel et depensier, et espere I'épouser. C'est ainsi que commence I'opera: personne ne se doute que Jenufa veut épouser Steva parce qu'elle attend un enfant de lui. Kostelnicka ne veut permettre le mariage <qu'apres le delai d'un an pendant lequel Steva ne doit pas s'enivrer>. Laca lui est reconnaissant de cette decision mais sa jalousie désespéree le pousse a un acte inconsideré: il defigure Jenufa pour qu'elle perde sa beauté et que Steva la quitte. (Acte I). Jenufa s'est réfugiee dans la maison de Kostelnicka et personne ne sait qu'elle a mis au monde un garcon. Steva a donné sa parole a Karolka, la riche fille du maire et se moque des implorations de Kostelnicka. L'hesitation malheureuse de Laca en apprenant la naissance de I'enfant pousse Kostelnicka a un acte désespéré: ignorant I'amour de Jenufa pour I'enfant. humiliee dans son orgueil et pensant aider ainsi sa fille adoptive elle noie I'enfant dans la riviere et persuade Jenufa que son bebé est mort. Brisée Jenufa accepte d'épouser Laca. (Acte Il). Assaillie de remords Kostelnicka est bouleversee de ce qu'elle a fait. Au moment ou les fiances se preparent pour les noces les villageois accourent en annoncant la decouverte d'un enfant noyé. Jenufa identifie son fils. Kostelnicka doit avouer son crime et demande miséricorde a sa fille adoptive. Brisée Jenufa veut rester seule mais Laca ne la quitte pas; ils seront I'un pour I'autre un soutien dans la vie. [ aroslav Vogel (1894-1970) patri k dirigentské generaci, jez primo navazovala pri provadéni dila Leose Janacka na jeho dobové autentické interprety. Sam jako mlady umelec prisel do styku s Janackovymi operami jeste za skladatelova zivota. Jiz na prelo mu 20. a 30. let ho lze povazovat za jednoho z nejpoucenejsich janackovskych vyklada ci. Chapal interpretaci Janackova dila jako proces hlubsiho porozuméni umelecké osob nosti a jejiho okoli i jako schopnost moci a umét sledovat genezi dila do detaili. To znamenalo prirozené naucit se cist Janackiv rukopis a pochopit jeho tektoniku, vyvije nou montazi ze skici az k definitivni podobe. Vogel je v ceském tereénu ojedinélym zje vem intelektualné vyspeélého dirigenta, jaky vzdélanim, silou analytickeho postrehu i literarnimi schopnostmi predci mnohého skoleného muzikologa. To dosvédcuje jeho monografie o Janackovi, ktera se kromé ceské podoby dockala némeckého i anglickeého vydani a privedla ke studiu dila jednoho z nejvétsich ceskych skladateli nejednoho né-mecky nebo anglicky pisiciho muzikologa. Jaroslav Vogel byl soucasne komponujicim dirigentem. V ceské tradici byla na prelomu 19. a 20. stoleti tato skupina zvlaste pocetna - pripomenme alespon Karla Kovarovice, Oskara Nedbala, Ludvika Vitézslava Celanského, Otakara Ostrcila, Frantiska Neumanna. Dirigentska a kompozicni rutina (Vogel studoval v Mnichové i v Parizi) a znalost hudebni literatury vedly Vogela ve skladbe ke vkusnému vybéru toho nejlepsiho z hudby krizového romantismu, verismu i moderny. Jako dirigent usiloval o preklenuti sire mezi evropskou tradici a modernou -- tedy od Verdiho pres zvlasté uctivaneho Wagnera a Strausse i Puc ciniho az k hudbé 20. stoleti. Jako dlouholety dirigent symfonickych koncerti v Ostravé provadel radu novinek a mize se pochlubit jako jeden z mala ceskych dirigenti, ze pod jeho taktovkou ucinkovali predni skladatelé prvni poloviny 20. stoleti. Tak napir. v pro sinci 1932 hral v Ostrave za Vogelova rizeni Paul Hindemith Vivaldiho koncert pro violu d'amour (ve violové ipravé), svou sonatu pro solovou violu a vyslechl Vogelovo nastu dovani predehry k opere Neues vom Tage. V lednu 1933 dirigoval Jaroslav Vogel v Ostra ve koncert z dila Igora Stravinského, ktery pod jeho taktovkou zahral klavirni part sveho Capriccia pro klavir a orchestr. Na poradu tohoto koncertu byla i suita z Petrusky, Pulci nella a Zalmova symfonie. Schonberga Hudbu k fil mové scéné op. 34, Hudbu pro smycce, bici nastroje a celestu B. Bartoka, dalsi premiéry skladeb I. Stravinskeho nebo prva provedeni rady domacich novinek - napr. E. Z Janackovy operni tvorby Vogel nastudoval nejprve Jeji pastorkynu, a to jiz koncem 20. let v Ostrave (premiéra této v poradi jiz druheé inscenace na scéné Narodniho divadla moravskoslezského probéhla 6. 9. 1929 - prvni Bastlova inscenace méla premiéru jiz 1. 11. 1919 za Janackovy icasti), k této inscenaci pripojil nova nastudovani Janackovy nejpopularnejsi opery v letech 1934 a 1938. Pripocteme li k tomu ostravskou inscenaci Veci Makropulos z roku 1935 a Prihody lisky Bystrousky z roku 1936, mame zachycen Vogeliv operni janackovsky profil 20. a 30 let - ze symfonické tvorby budiz alespon pripomenuto, ze Vogel uvedl poprvé v Ostravé rapsodii Taras Bulba (15. 1. 1929). Po druhé svetoveé valce se Jaroslav Vogel znovu zameril na Janackovo operni dilo - tentokrat behem svého vice nez desitiletého pisobeni v praz ském Narodnim divadle (1947-1958). Tehdy také Jaroslav Vogel pouzil poprvé Janackiv autenticky zavér opery. Dirigent a skladatel Jaroslav Vogel (1894-1970) se jiz behem studii na gymnaziu v Plzni ucil hre na housle u Otakara Sevcika a kompozici soukromé u Vitézslava Novaka. Studoval také na Akademii der Tonkunst v Mnichové a na Schola Cantorum v Paiizi. Na vsech svych pisobistich se zaslouzil o vytvoreni osobitého profilu dramaturgického i interpretacniho, at jiz to bylo béhem jeho pisobeni v ostravské opere (1919-1923 äl 1927-1943, po druhé svétové valce jako séf opery do roku 1948), v Narodnim divadle v Praze (1949-1958), v Brné (Statni filharmonie 1959-1962), Plzni nebo kdekoliv jinde behem cetneho hostovani. Jaroslav Vogel je také autorem prvni velké monografie o Leosi Janackovi (poprvé vydané 1958), jehoz tvorbu cilevédome propagoval, a je realizatorem prvnich gramofonovych nahravek del Novakovych a Janackovych.

Stepanka Jelinkova (1911-1996) pisobila nejdrive u opery v Brne (1936-1941), kde si vytvorila zaklad sveho mladodramatického repertoaru. V roce 1942 ji angazoval Vaclav Talich do Narodniho divadla v Praze, kde setrvala do roku 1972. K jejim nejvetsim ispe chim po prichodu do Prahy patrila bezesporu Jenifa v souladu s Talichovym pojetim role, jako lyricke postavy, u niz je hlavni diraz polozen na vokalni slozku a jeji kantabilni prove deni. Ze svétového repertoaru zaujala jeji Hrabénka (Figarova svatba), Donna Elvira i Don na Anna (Don Giovanni) a Fiordiligi (Cosi fan tutte). Z ceského repertoaru to byla zejména Marenka (Prodana nevésta), Karolina (Dvé vdovy) a titulni role Dvorakovy Rusalky.

Marta Krasova (1901-1970), solistka opery Narodniho divadla v Praze v letech 1928-1966, studovala hru na housle a klavir a pomyslela na ucitelskou drahu. Zajem o zpév rozvinuty studiem u Olgy Valouskové a Rizeny Maturové ji vsak piivedl na scénu v Ceskych Budejovicich a jiz v roce 1922 do Bratislavy po bok hosti Slezaka, Schmede se a Saljapina. Beno Blachut (1913-1985) vénoval celou svou umeleckou drahu opere Narodniho divadla v Praze, kam nastoupil roku 1941 po dvouletém pisobeni v Olomouci. Prestoze zacinal jako lyricky tenor, propracovaval se postupne k uloham dramatickym, v nichz zdiraznoval emocionalni stranku hudebniho vyrazu. Spolupracoval na supraphonskych oper nich kompletech Kata Kabanova a Z mrtvého domu.

Starenka Buryjovka mela dva syny, starsi zdedil mlyn a mladsi Tomas ve mlyne jen mladkoval. Mlynar se ozenil s vdovou Klemenovou, ktera do rodiny Buryju privedla sveho syna Lacu z prvniho manzelstvi. Teprve pak se narodil Steva, Laciv mladsi bratr. Druhy syn starenky, Tomas Buryja, se ozenil s Jenifou, dcerou hospodskeho, a kdyz ovdovel, zistala mu mala dceruska Jenifa. Hledal pro ni matku a nasel ji v Petronile Slomkové, rychtarove dceri, moudré a rozvazné divce, ktera ho pred lety milovala. Petro nila se o Jenifku peclivé starala, a protoze vlastni déti nemela, upnula se na svou pastor kyni s veskerou laskou sveho nenaplneneho materstvi. V manzelstvi stastna nebyla, Tomas se opijel a pred jeho surovostmi se casto musela i s Jenifkou skryvat. Kdyz propil vse chen majetek, zemrel; Petronila se pak probijela zivotem velmi tezce, ale snazila se, aby dévcatko nestradalo. Aby si privydelala, prevzala starost o kaplicku, a od té doby ji lidé ve vsi nazyvali Kostelnickou. Také ve mlyné oba chlapci osireli. Starenka rozmazlovala mladsiho Stevu, budouciho majitele mlyna, a Laca zistaval sam; mala Jenifka vsak k osamélému chlapci velmi prilnula. Laciv détsky vztah se casem zacal ménit v lasku, kterou i Jenifa pozorovala. Byl vsak odveden na nékolik let na vojnu a kdyz se vratil, bylo vsechno jinak, nez oceka val.
