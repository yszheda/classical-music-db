# Donizetti: Maria di Rohan

Maria di Rohan was composed in 1843 for Vienna, where Donizetti had already scored a considerable success with his *Linda di Chamounix*. The first performance was given on 5 June that year, with the great soprano Wilhelmina Schröder-Devrient in the title role. The opera was well received, and Donizetti himself considered it one of his best works.

The story is set in Paris during the reign of Louis XIV. Maria, the wife of Count Riccardo di Rohan, has had a previous love affair with Duke Enrico di Guise. When Enrico returns from exile, the old passion is rekindled. Riccardo suspects what has happened and challenges Enrico to a duel. Enrico is killed, and Maria dies of grief.

This is one of Donizetti's most powerful late operas, with a particularly strong dramatic structure and some of his most inspired melodic invention. The title role is one of the most demanding in the Italian soprano repertoire, requiring both great vocal agility and deep dramatic intensity.

This recording, from a live broadcast in Milan in 1962, captures a performance featuring one of the leading Italian sopranos of the post-war period, Virginia Zeani, in the title role. Under the baton of Fernando Previtali, one of Italy's most experienced conductors of the Italian repertory, this performance captures all the passion and drama of Donizetti's tragic masterpiece.

Myto is proud to present this important historical recording, which documents a significant performance from one of the great sopranos of the twentieth century.

# Gaetano Donizetti (1797-1848)

Gaetano Donizetti was born in Bergamo in 1797, the son of a poor family. He studied at the Bergamo music school under the composer Simon Mayr, who recognized his exceptional talent and helped him to continue his studies at the Bologna Conservatory. By the age of 26 he had already written more than ten operas, and quickly established himself as one of the leading composers of Italian opera.

During the 1830s and 1840s he produced a remarkable series of operas, including some of his most famous works: *Anna Bolena* (1830), *L'elisir d'amore* (1832), *Lucrezia Borgia* (1833), *Lucia di Lammermoor* (1835), *Roberto Devereux* (1837), *La favorita* (1840), *Linda di Chamounix* (1842), *Don Pasquale* (1843), and *Maria di Rohan* (1843). His incredible productivity was matched by an extraordinary gift for melody and a keen sense of the theatre. He dominated Italian opera in the period between Rossini and Verdi, and his best works still hold the stage today.

In 1845 his health began to fail, and he died in 1848 in Bergamo, at the age of 51, after a long illness.

# Virginia Zeani

Virginia Zeani was born in Romania in 1926. She made her debut in 1948, and quickly established herself as one of the leading sopranos of her generation, particularly in the Italian bel canto repertory. She sang at all the major opera houses of the world, including La Scala in Milan, the Metropolitan Opera in New York, Covent Garden in London, and many others. She was particularly admired for her performances in Donizetti, Bellini and Verdi, and was known for her beautiful tone and impeccable technique. She taught for many years at the Indiana University School of Music, and continued to sing into the 1980s. She died in 2010.

# Fernando Previtali

Fernando Previtali was born in 1907 in Adria, Italy. He studied at the Milan Conservatory, and quickly became one of Italy's leading conductors. He was principal conductor of the RAI Symphony Orchestra in Milan from 1936 until 1953, and later worked with many of the major orchestras and opera houses in Italy. He was particularly known for his work in Italian opera, and conducted many important performances of the nineteenth-century Italian repertory. He died in 1985.

## Synopsis

### Act I

Enrico di Guise, who has killed a man in a duel and been forced into exile, has returned secretly to Paris. He comes to the house of Count Riccardo di Rohan, where his former lover Maria, the Count's wife, is living. Enrico and Maria meet, and their old love is reborn. Maria urges Enrico to flee, but he refuses. Riccardo returns home unexpectedly, and Enrico is forced to hide. Riccardo suspects that someone is hiding, but Maria manages to deceive him. When Riccardo has left, Enrico emerges, and he and Maria plan to flee together that night.

### Act II

At the court of Louis XIV, all is celebration. Riccardo has discovered the truth about Maria and Enrico. He confronts Maria, who admits everything. Riccardo challenges Enrico to a duel.

### Act III

The duel takes place. Enrico is mortally wounded. He is brought back to Maria's rooms. He dies in Maria's arms. Maria, overcome with grief, dies.
