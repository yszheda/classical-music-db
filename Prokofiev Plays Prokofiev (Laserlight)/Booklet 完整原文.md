## Liner Notes Essay

### SERGEI PROKOFIEV

Sergei Prokofiev was born in Sontsovka, Russia on April 23rd, 1891. His father was an agricultural manager and his mother was a highly educated woman who had studied at the St. Petersburg Conservatory. Prokofiev's earliest musical training came from his mother, who was an excellent pianist and introduced him to the works of Bach, Beethoven, Chopin, and other masters.

In his autobiography, Prokofiev wrote: "From the day I was born, I heard music. All the noises around me seemed to possess a musical quality." This early exposure to music proved to be formative.

By age five, Prokofiev was already composing, and his mother would write down his compositions since he could not yet read or write musical notation. His first composition, an "Indian Gallop," was written when he was just five years old.

At age thirteen, Prokofiev entered the St. Petersburg Conservatory, where he studied under such renowned teachers as Rimsky-Korsakov, Glazunov, and Lyadov. Despite his youth, he quickly made an impression with his extraordinary talent and innovative approach to composition.

Prokofiev's early works showed his characteristic style already emerging – a blend of classical forms with modern harmonies and rhythms. His first piano concerto (1911-12) caused a sensation at its premiere with its bold, percussive writing.

After graduating from the Conservatory in 1914, Prokofiev embarked on a career as both composer and pianist. His brilliant technique and unique interpretive style made him one of the most sought-after pianists of his generation.

The years following the Russian Revolution were tumultuous. In 1918, Prokofiev left Russia, traveling through Siberia to Japan and eventually settling in the United States. During his time abroad, he composed some of his most celebrated works, including the opera "The Love for Three Oranges."

Prokofiev eventually returned to the Soviet Union in 1936, where he continued to compose despite the political challenges of the era. His music, characterized by its melodic invention, rhythmic vitality, and harmonic innovation, remains among the most popular of the 20th century.

Prokofiev died on March 5, 1953, in Moscow, on the same day as Stalin.

## About the Artist

**Sergei Prokofiev** (1891-1953) was one of the greatest composer-pianists of the 20th century. Born in Sontsovka, Ukraine, Prokofiev entered the St. Petersburg Conservatory at the age of 13, where he studied under Rimsky-Korsakov and Glazunov. As a pianist, he was known for his brilliant technique and percussive style, which is evident in these historical recordings.

Prokofiev left Russia after the Revolution and lived in the United States and Europe before returning to the Soviet Union in 1936. His works span all genres, including seven symphonies, five piano concertos, numerous sonatas, and the famous ballets "Romeo and Juliet" and "Cinderella."

These recordings showcase Prokofiev's unique interpretation of his own works, providing invaluable insight into the composer's intended tempo, dynamics, and expressive nuances.
