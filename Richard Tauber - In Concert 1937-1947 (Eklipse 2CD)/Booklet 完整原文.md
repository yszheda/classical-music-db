## Liner Notes by Raymond Klumper-Horneman

### Richard Tauber 1891-1948

Richard Tauber was born in 1891 in the Austrian town of Linz. Initially he studied to be a conductor at the conservatory of Frankfurt. He also trained as a singer and made his debut as Tamino in *Die Zauberflöte* in 1913 at the Stadttheater in Chemnitz. From then on he established himself as a serious artist. He sang Kalaf in the premiere of Turandot at the Staatsoper of Vienna in 1926. In 1924 Tauber met Franz Lehár who was to exercise a great influence on him. The composer wrote the tenor parts in "Paganini", "Giuditta", "Schön ist die Welt", "Frederike", "Der Zarewitsch" and "Das Land des Lächelns" for Tauber. The title role in the last named operetta, that of prince Sou-Chong, had been sung more than 700 times by Tauber. The tenor's popularity was ever increasing contributed to by the many films he made in the 1920's such as "Old Chelsea" and "The Singing Dream".

In 1933 Tauber had to flee Germany as he was half Jewish. He then settled in Austria which he also had to leave in 1938 during the Anschluss. From then until his death Tauber lived in London. His last operatic appearance was as Ottavio in Don Giovanni in September 1947 at Covent Garden. This was at a time when he was already heavily burdened with the sickness which caused his untimely death in 1948.

— Raymond Klumper-Horneman
