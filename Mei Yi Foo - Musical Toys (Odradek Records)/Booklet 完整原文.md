## Liner Notes Page 2 (Artist Bio)

**Mei Yi Foo** has captivated audiences across Europe, Asia and the Americas and continues to garner reviews that praise her "unusual interior strength" together with her "personal, intelligent and unique interpretation" (La Opinion Granada).

Her rapport with musicians brings her not only to perform and direct orchestras from Europe to Asia, but also to work with prominent composers around the world.

A native of Malaysia now living in London, Mei Yi enjoys a busy schedule of playing and music-making.

### Über Musical Toys

Sofia Gubaidulina, Unsuk Chin und György Ligeti, drei herausragende Größen der Musik des 20. und 21. Jahrhunderts, haben außerordentlich viel für so gut wie jedes Medium geschrieben. Bei all den beachtlichen kompositorischen Können ist dies eine andere Seite ihrer Musik, die ich mit dieser CD beleuchten möchte, nämlich die menschlich schwerer zu fassende, persönliche Seite ihrer hoch anspruchsvollen Werke. Der Mitwirkende in der nimmt Teil an diesem ernsten Spiel, spielt es sozusagen mit, nicht ohne ein gewisses Maß an Übermut und Ironie.

## English Liner Notes by Hugh Collins Rice (Pages 4-9)

### Musical Toys

Sofia Gubaidulina, Unsuk Chin and György Ligeti, giants of the world of 20th/21st century music, wrote extensively for every possible medium. While acknowledging their compositional mastery, I also hope to illuminate a different facet found in all three works in this recording – a facet less monumental but more personal and elusive. Through my encounter with them, I have come to discover a sense of humor that binds these works collectively; once discovered, this quality presence is difficult to stifle. Like a child playing with toys, one would listen with wit and curiosity through the labyrinth of 31 tracks. The child's play here, however, is sophisticated. These composers take their musical toys seriously (all three works were, after all, written when their creators were in their mature 30s) and it is by participating in this serious game that the performer works, or more appropriately plays, with a sense of mischief and irony.

**Gubaidulina's Musical Toys** (1969) emerges from the tradition of the children's suite, as did the works of Schumann for young players like his *Album for the Young* or Bartók's *Mikrokosmos*. But rather than being didactic, Gubaidulina's work is a collection of musical character pieces that explore the sonic possibilities of the piano through a child's eyes. The pieces are short, imaginative, and filled with unexpected turns.

**Musical Toys** (1969): in one sense it is a suite of pieces which imitate the world of the young student, each movement a title redolent of children's toys, often referring to magical or fairy tale elements. As with much of her music there is a sensitivity to the world of sounds, and many of the titles refer to musical instruments and to birds.

The first piece, **Mechanical Accordion**, is just 32 seconds long but immediately establishes the playful character of the set. The accordion is suggested through the use of sustained pedaling and repetitive figuration.

**Magic Roundabout** continues the whimsical mood with its circular melodic patterns and unexpected harmonic shifts.

**The Trumpeter in the Forest** evokes a distant hunting call through repeated motifs and echo effects.

**The Magic Smith** features hammering rhythmic patterns that suggest a blacksmith at work.

**April Day** captures the capricious nature of spring weather with rapidly changing moods.

**Song of the Fisherman** uses flowing arpeggios to suggest water and the gentle motion of a boat.

**The Little Tit** is a brief, bird-like piece with quick, fluttering figures.

**A Bear Playing the Double Bass and the Black Woman** is one of the more programmatic pieces, with low, rumbling bass notes suggesting the bear and more lyrical material for the woman.

**The Woodpecker** features repeated notes and sharp attacks that mimic the bird's pecking.

**The Elk Clearing** opens out into a more spacious texture, suggesting a forest clearing.

**Sleigh with Little Bells** uses high, tinkling figures to suggest sleigh bells.

**The Echo** lives up to its name with call-and-response patterns between different registers.

**The Drummer** features persistent rhythmic patterns that suggest drumming.

**Forest Musicians** brings the set to a close with a gathering of all the previous musical ideas.

### Unsuk Chin's Études (1995-2003)

Unsuk Chin's six Études stand at the opposite extreme of this tradition from the virtuosic concert études of the 19th century. Written between 1995 and 2003, they were commissioned by the Southbank Centre and completed and revised in 2003.

The fifth piece can serve as an introduction to Chin's sound world. **Toccata** begins simply: a single finger plays a straightforward humong. But the piece rapidly develops into a complex exploration of the piano's range and capabilities.

**Grains** is somewhat different. It is in essence a study in the avoidance of the *moto perpetuo* style common in toccatas. Instead continuity is challenged by textures fragmented into isolated gestures, the most insistent being the repeated G-sharp which is virtually omnipresent. In some ways it is a highly developed form of Gubaidulina's **Woodpecker** and perhaps also a relative of some of Bartók's nocturnal soundscapes.

**Musica Ricercata** is a set of compositional studies written at a time (1951-53) when Ligeti had no hope of hearing performances of music which challenged the Soviet authorities. By the time of the first performance (1969) he was living in the west and an established figure in contemporary music.

The eleven pieces each use a different collection of notes – the first using just two pitches, the second, three and so on, till the final piece uses all 12 notes. Ligeti's desire to start from first principles can be seen easily in the first piece, where the A is heard almost entirely without the note A, a second pitch, B, appearing only at the very end.

Each piece has its own character. The third may seem only four pitches, but they are the notes of the C major and minor triads, and in contrast with the preceding piece made entirely out of semitone movement.

## Italian Liner Notes (Pages 10-15)

### Il repertorio pianistico

Il repertorio pianistico e il solo ad abbondare di composizioni musicali per bambini, che sono basate su caratteristiche tecniche dell'esecuzione e della composizione. È una tradizione che si estende dall'Album per la gioventù di Bach a composizioni per giovani pianisti come l'Album per la gioventù di Schumann o i Mikrokosmos di Bartók, per arrivare agli impegnativi studi da concerto dell'Ottocento. Ciascun pezzo di questo disco testimonia un differente aspetto della suddetta tradizione pedagogica didattica.

### Musical Toys

Sofia Gubaidulina, Unsuk Chin e György Ligeti, giganti della musica del XX e XXI secolo, hanno scritto tantissimo per ogni possibile medium. Nel riconoscere la loro maestria compositiva, spero anche di illuminare un'ultra sfaccettatura che accomuna le tre opere presenti in questo disco – una sfaccettatura meno monumentale ma più personale ed elusiva. Nel mio incontro con questi pezzi, ho scoperto quanto esso siano legati dal senso dell'umor: una volta scoperta questa caratteristica, la presenza diventa difficile da soffocare. Come un bambino con i suoi giocattoli, l'ascoltatore può attraversare il labirinto delle 31 tracce con spirito e curiosità. Si tratta tuttavia di un gioco sofisticato. Questi compositori prendono i loro giocattoli musicali sul serio (dopotutto i tre pezzi sono stati scritti quando i loro creatori avevano più di trent'anni) ed è partecipando a questo gioco serio che l'artista lavora, o meglio suona, con un senso di birichineria e ironia.

### Unsuk Chin - Sei studi per pianoforte

Gli Études di Unsuk Chin (1995-2003) si collocano all'altro estremo di questa tradizione, quello degli studi virtuosistici da concerto eseguiti prevalentemente dai compositori-pianisti dell'Ottocento. La serie è stata commissionata dalla Southbank Centre ed è stata completata e riveduta nel 2003.

Il quinto pezzo può servire da introduzione al mondo sonoro dell'autrice: Toccata inizia nel modo più semplice: un dito preme un tasto del pianoforte producendo un suono costante del "tocca", potrebbe definirsi il titolo di Toccata. Lo studio comincia in modo semplice: una singola linea, che imita apparentemente esecutore da una tastiera, con una pulsazione regolare, ma con cambiamenti ritmici e schemi di accenti irregolari. Da questo inizio la musica si dipana poi fino a diventare un flusso continuo di note.

Lo schema di apertura non si perde mai del tutto, ma allo stesso tempo lo si riguadagna completamente. Come una palla di neve, cresce sempre di più in una valanga sempre più prorompente a formare una linea di dodici note: il decorso, rim in contatto con la pulsazione di base, si piega a formare una gigantesca serie di note.
