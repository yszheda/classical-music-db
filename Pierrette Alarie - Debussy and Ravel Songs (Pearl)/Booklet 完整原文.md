## Liner Notes by Professor Stanley Henig

### PIERRETTE ALARIE

During two centuries of operatic history there have been numerous husband and wife singing partner-ships. Mario and Grisi are perhaps the most famous pair from the nineteenth century; Alagna and Gheorghiu are the obvious contemporary exemplars. The careers of Pierrette Alarie and Léopold Simoneau are synonymous with the coming of age of opera in Canada, particularly in Montreal where they performed successfully with Variétés lyriques, Les Festivals de Montréal and the Opéra Guild. The last was founded by Pauline Donaldalda, who had sung at Covent Garden before the First World War.

Whilst Alarie and Simoneau were in every sense an equal partnership and appeared very frequently together, Simoneau may be the better known today to record collectors. His career coincided with a major revival of interest in Mozart and an inevitable shortage of appropriate tenors. Alarie – with her lovely lyric soprano – faced rather stiffer competition. Even so, she enjoyed a major career in opera and on the concert stage throughout Canada and the USA. Interestingly, she gave 26 performances at the Metropolitan Opera in New York (as against only four by her husband). As befitted a French-speaking Canadian, she was hugely successful in France – particularly in Paris and at the festival in Aix-en-Provence. Alarie's first performances were with the Montreal-based Variétés lyriques – initially in operetta. She appeared in Benatzky's *White Horse Inn* shortly before her fourteenth birthday (Alarie was born in 1921), although clearly her training was far from complete. Her operatic debut was on 26 May 1943 as Barbarina in *The Marriage of Figaro*. This was also presumably her first appearance together with Simoneau who sang the equally small part of Don Curzio. The cast included Eleanor Steber and John Brownlee; Beecham conducted and it seems probable that the production was borrowed from the Metropolitan in New York. In October that same year Alarie entered the Curtis Institute in Philadelphia, one of the USA's leading conservatoires. There she took lessons from Elisabeth Schumann, who likened her new pupil's voice to that of her contemporary Lotte Schoene. Praise could hardly be higher. Indeed as with both Schumann and Schoene there is a silvery quality to Alarie's voice. During and immediately after her studies, Alarie gave a number of concerts. Her major debut as a fully fledged professional singer in a lead operatic role came in September 1945 when she appeared as Marie in *La Fille du Régiment*, opposite Simoneau's Tonio. The couple married, in real life, the following year but not before Alarie had made her Metropolitan debut as Oscar in *Un Ballo in Maschera* followed by appearances in *Contes d'Hoffman* and *Die Entführung aus dem Serail* (incredibly its première at the Met!). If opera did not always permit Alarie and Simoneau to appear together, they were soon established as an item on the concert platform – in Canada, the USA and Mexico.

Pauline Donaldalda's Opera Guild had now well established. Alarie and Simoneau first appeared with the company in May 1947 in Mozart's *The Abduction from the Seraglio* – sung in English! Later in her career Alarie would return to the company as Juliet and as Zerlina in *Don Giovanni* – by then operas were given in their original language.

In September 1949 both Alarie and Simoneau made their debuts at the Opéra Comique in Paris – Alarie as Olympia and Simoneau in *Les Contes d'Hoffman*. If Montreal was her artistic base, Paris could be considered as Alarie's second home. She appeared in *The Barber of Seville*, *Lakmé* and *Les Pêcheurs de Perles* at the Opéra Comique and *Les Fêtes d'Hébé* as well as *Les Indes Galantes* at the Paris Opera. She was also a regular performer at the Aix-en-Provence festival in the early 1950s both in opera and, with Simoneau, on the concert platform. In 1951 she made one of her very few appearances in the UK – at the Edinburgh Festival in *Don Giovanni*. This was one of the last occasions on which Fritz Busch conducted. As well as Alarie and Simoneau, the cast also included Sena Jurinac and Geraint Evans. The following year Alarie and Simoneau were together again for further performances at the opera and at Aix-en-Provence. A curious juxtaposition was witnessed in Alarie's career in 1953. The previous year the Paris Opera had revived Rameau's *Les Indes Galantes* – largely forgotten for close on two centuries. It was a great popular success with something like 240 performances over the next ten years. In 1953 Alarie sang the role of Fatima (created by Miteau the previous year) while Simoneau appeared as Damon (replacing another French Canadian tenor, Raoul Jobin). It demonstrated something of Alarie's growing versatility that she also in 1953 appeared in Honegger's *Le Roi David* at Aix-en-Provence. That same year the composer Werner Egk dedicated some new songs to her. After initial performances at Aix, Alarie went on to sing them in Munich and Baden Baden. Indeed her career was marked by increasing versatility: in concerts; Mozart, Debussy and Ravel and oratorios from Bach and Handel again to both Honegger and Egk. She demonstrated equal versatility in her operatic repertoire of some forty roles. This extended from Mozart and Donizetti through many of the great French composers of the nineteenth century, three Verdi roles – *La Traviata*, *Rigoletto*, *Un Ballo in Maschera*, to Menotti and Poulenc.

The final joint opera performance was, fittingly, in *Don Giovanni* at the Opera Guild in April 1964. However Alarie herself returned to the operatic stage in October of that year as Michaëla in a star-studded production of *Carmen* mounted by the Montreal Symphony Orchestra with Zubin Mehta conducting and Shirley Verrett in the title role. According to Jacob Siskind in the Montreal Star, 'Pierrette Alarie's Michaëla was a wonderful surprise. Miss Alarie has always been a fine musician, but I cannot remember her singing so simply, so movingly, ever before. Her third act aria was a show stopper, and rightly so. Here was the proper blend of emotion and musicianship so rarely encountered on the operatic stage'.

The concert careers of Alarie and Simoneau continued, albeit exclusively in North America with a final joint performance in November 1970 in *Messiah* – again with the Montreal Symphony Orchestra. The following year they moved to San Francisco to develop second teaching careers. Later they founded the Canada Opera Piccola in Victoria, British Columbia. With both artists well into their eighties, Alarie and Simoneau received 'Rubie' awards for outstanding achievement in the development of opera in Canada in November 2002.
