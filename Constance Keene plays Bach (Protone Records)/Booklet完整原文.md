## THE COMPOSER (HIPPO_20241219_0006.jpg)

**THE COMPOSER**

Bach's musical life combined a considerable variety of activities, several of them simultaneously. Familiar with many instruments, he also attained supreme virtuosity on the organ and the harpsichord, and within fifty years of professional labor, he occupied positions as a choirboy, orchestra violinist, violist, and harpsichordist, organist and organ-technician, composer, and teacher of music, Latin and Catechism. His last twenty-seven years were spent in Leipzig as cantor and music director of St. Thomas Church and School with which his name is inseparably linked.

Theoretically, Bach's final post should have brought him deep artistic and personal satisfaction. It never did. Almost from the moment of his arrival in 1723, he and his employers clashed. The daily arguments, drudgery, and frustration finally brought about his progressive disinterest in the church and school jobs. In his divine discontent, he withdrew more and more to express his innermost emotions, to demonstrate his musical mastery, and to implement the human hope of contemporary recognition and future fame.

This is the Bach who wrote three of the four works contained on this disc, and it is heartening to discover that each of them (unlike most of Bach's music that lay scattered and virtually forgotten for nearly a century following his death) received extraordinary acclaim and popularity.

Bach was no more than twenty when he wrote his D Minor Organ Toccata, full of elemental power, reckless runs of ascending and descending passages, and sharply dissonantal chords. A dramatic Fugue follows whose subject and development march in tight coherence to an unexpected culmination, a recapitulation of the Toccata's improvisational mood in an ending of stunning sonority.

From 1726-1731, Bach's diminishing interest in turning out church music led him to write and publish as Part I of the Clavier-Ubung six harpsichord Partitas. His first biographer, the invaluable J.N. Forkel, described them in 1802. "These works made a great noise in the musical world of Bach's time. Such excellent compositions for harpsichord had never been seen or heard before."

The Italian Concerto is among Bach's most significant and brilliant works, and it was so considered shortly after Bach published it in 1735. Even J. A. Schiebe, the Hamburg critic who was hardly a Bach enthusiast, greeted it as "A perfect model of a well designed solo concerto...which deserves emulation by all our great composers."

In Bach's full maturity, he wrote many Suites for unaccompanied violin, cello, lute and harpsichord, variously called Sonatas, Overtures, Partitas, and Suites. Why six of the Suites were labelled "French and six of them "English" remains unknown. What we do know is that the titles were appended later, and not by Bach, and that the basic movements of the French Suites (Allemande, Courante, Sarabande, and Gigue) were solidly set in the mid-seventeenth century by German composers. Later composers felt free to add extra sections. In the Fifth French Suite Bach added three, a Gavotte, Bouree, and Loure. Each Suite concludes with a Gigue, derived from the Anglo-Saxon Jig of the previous century. Bach wove his own national magic into this one with a gaiety that is almost Mediterranean.

## A final word ornamentation & THE ARTIST (HIPPO_20241219_0002.jpg)

A final word regarding ornamentation, the most typical feature of the Baroque style. At this point in musical scholarship, it would no longer be necessary to "defend" performers who assume their basic obligations toward the scores of an improvisational age. Obviously, the Bach interpreter is called upon to supplement with discretion what was skeletonized or omitted in the autographs, and it accounts for Constance Keene's discriminating embellishments.

*Abraham Chasins*

**THE ARTIST**

Constance Keene, eminent American pianist, is internationally known for her solo recitals, appearances with major orchestras and chamber music groups. In addition she has gained a world-wide reputation as coach, adjudicator and for her master classes. Her recordings have been enthusiastically acclaimed and have received many awards.
