## 关于作曲家（英文）
Zdeněk Fibich (1850-1900) stands alongside Bedřich Smetana and Antonín Dvořák as one of the three leading representatives of Czech musical nationalism in the 19th century. Born in Všebořice near České Budějovice in southern Bohemia, he received his early musical education from his mother, who was a talented pianist. He later studied in Leipzig (1865-1867), Paris (1867-1868), and Mannheim (1868-1870), absorbing a wide range of influences from the German and French musical traditions.

Fibich's output is extensive and varied, encompassing operas, symphonies, chamber music, piano works, and more than 600 songs. While his operas *Šárka* (1897) and *The Bride of Messina* (1883) were successful in his lifetime, his most enduring works today are his instrumental compositions, particularly his chamber music and piano pieces.

The Piano Quartet in E minor, Op.11 was composed in 1878, early in Fibich's career. It demonstrates his mastery of classical forms while incorporating the melodic and harmonic characteristics of Czech folk music. The work is cast in three movements: a dramatic opening Allegro moderato, a beautiful set of variations on an original theme, and a vigorous concluding Allegro energico.

The Piano Quintet in D major, Op.42, composed in 1893, is one of Fibich's finest late works. Scored for the unusual combination of violin, clarinet, horn, cello and piano, it showcases his mature style, blending lyricism, dramatic intensity, and masterful handling of the distinctive instrumental colors. The four-movement structure ranges from the expansive opening Allegro non tanto, through the deeply expressive Largo, the lively Scherzo, to the triumphant Finale.

The Ensemble Villa Musica, founded in 1968, is one of Germany's leading chamber music ensembles, renowned for their performances of both standard repertoire and lesser-known works from the 19th and 20th centuries. Their recordings for MDG have won numerous international awards for their exceptional musical quality and outstanding sound engineering.
