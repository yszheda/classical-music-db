### Back Cover

At the turn of the last century, Vienna was one of the most remarkable cities on earth. It witnessed unprecedented artistic and intellectual expression against the backdrop of traditionalism resolutely resistant to change. It was in this climate that Gustav Mahler began his directorship at the Royal and Imperial Court Opera in 1897. His reign lasted a mere ten years yet with his matchless, intrepid energy he managed to regenerate an entire artistic workforce and create the glory days of the Vienna Opera.

Marston is proud to present a centenary tribute to the amazing ten-year directorship of Gustav Mahler in Vienna. Rare, and in some cases unique, early recordings of opera and lieder highlight the vocal artistry of more than fifty Court Opera stars, including Irene Abendroth, Lola Beeth, Theodor Bertram, Hermine Bosetti, Mme. Charles Cahier, Leopold Demuth, Ottolie Fellwock, Wilhelm Hesch, Berta Kiurina, Selma Kurz, Richard Mayr, Frances Saville, Erik Schmedes, Leo Slezak, Johannes Sembach, and Edyth Walker. These fascinating documents in sound, nearly all recorded during Mahler's years in Vienna and many available here in re-issue for the first time, will also showcase important Court Opera premiers and revivals, among them Hans Pfitzner's *Rose vom Liebesgarten*, Bizet's *Djamileh*, Goldmark's *Konigin von Saba*, Wolf-Ferrari's *Le Donne Curiose*, Wagner's *Tristan und Isolde* and *Lohengrin*, and Tchaikovsky's *Eugene Onegin*. This comprehensive three-CD set from Marston also features an essay on Mahler and his work at the Court Opera by renowned scholar Stephen E. Hefling and informative thumbnail biographies of each artist represented.

### Singer Biographies

**Irene ABENDROTH** [vi] (14 July 1872 – 1 September 1932) was born in Lemberg (Lvov) Galicia of German parents. She studied with Francesco Lamperti and Italo Campanini in Milan, and continued her studies in Vienna. She made her stage debut in 1889 at the Court Opera as Amina in *La Sonnambula* but stayed only one year. She returned in the 1894-1899 season singing mainly the coloratura repertoire: the Queen of the Night, Zerlina, Gilda, and Norina. She sang in the local premieres of *Rigoletto* and *Giovanni* and *The Bartered Bride* as Sosninka and Hanselina. She later appeared in the theaters as a guest artist at the Court Opera in 1900. She sang Margarethe, Rosina in *Il Barbiere*, and Tosca in the German premiere of that opera.

She left Dresden in 1909 and thereafter sang in concerts and taught in Vienna. Her 1902 records for G&T are her only known discs, though rumors suggest others by Leopold Demuth, all recorded for G&T in 1902.

**Anna von BAHR-MILDENBURG** [v] (29 November 1872 – 27 January 1947) studied with Rosa Papier and made her debut in Hamburg as Brunnhilde in the *Nibelungen* under Mahler. He wanted her to go with him to Vienna but that was not available until 1898. She was one of the greatest dramatic sopranos of her time, preparing most of the Wagner roles with Mahler, and sang at most major German stages, as well as Covent Garden, until 1927. She was a leading teacher in her later years, the most important of her many successful pupils being Lauritz Melchior. She made two recordings for G&T but rejected them and only one survives in three copies.

**Hans BREUER** [vi] (27 April 1868 – 11 October 1929) made his Bayreuth debut in small roles in 1894 but then sang there regularly there from 1896 to 1914. He was soon heard at the Metropolitan Opera and Covent Garden and came to Vienna in 1900, remaining there until his death. Apart from Mime and the three-Sachs minor roles in *Die Meistersinger*, he sang many secondary roles during the Mahler period. His records, for G&T and Zonophone, are few in number.

**Sara CAHIER** [cont] (6 January 1870 – 15 April 1951), better known as Mme. Charles Cahier, was actually born Sara Jane Walker in Nashville, Tennessee. Her teachers included Jean de Reszke, Victor Capoul, and Amalie Joachim. After her debut in 1904 she joined the Hofoper in 1907 and remained until 1911. Under Mahler, she sang Amneris, Carmen, Dalila, Fidès, Ortrud, and Santuzza. She was also famous for her interpretation of Mahler's works, creating the alto version of *Das Lied von der Erde*. Among her later pupils were Marianne Anderson, Gina Cigna, and Rose Bampton. Her few recordings appear on G&T, Odéon, HMV, and Lyrophon. Her sole Uriel-phon disc (1910) contains the "Urlicht" movement from Mahler's Resurrection Symphony and "Ich bin der Welt abhanden gekommen."

**Leopold DEMUTH** [bar] (22 November 1861 – 4 March 1910) was born in Leopold Polzovsky in the Moravian capital Brno, studied with Carl Goldbeck in Vienna, and came to the Hofoper in 1898, remaining there until his sudden death during a concert. He was highly praised for the beauty and size of his voice and the ease of his production. He was least in the standard Verdi roles but also sang all the Wagner baritone parts, his basso cantata Erwin Stein wrote: "vocal splendor had often to make up for his lack of acting ability." Mahler's favorite bass roles were world premieres: Goldmark's *Ein Wintermärchen* and Hans Pfitzner's *Der arme Heinrich*. His most important of these is *Pifitzer's* (title role), Karpfka in *Floh*, Schnabe in D'Albert's *Floh*, and Tin Lukan in Weber's *Wald*. Der Corregidor. He recorded for Berliner, G&T, and Gramophone.

**Andreas DIPPEL** [ti] (20 November 1866 – 12 May 1932) made his debut in 1887 in Bremen and two years later joined the Metropolitan Opera, where he sang regularly until 1910. He became a member of the Hofoper ensemble in 1893 and remained there until 1898. During the Mahler era he sang 27 leading and smaller roles, including Marcello in the local premiere of Leoncavallo's *Bohème*. Over his career he sang a total of 150 roles, from Don Ottavio to the full spectrum of Wagner parts. Dippel made 68 Edison cylinders and appears on several intact Mapleson cylinders, which were recorded live at the Metropolitan Opera. He also recorded for the Victor Company, but these discs were not published.

**Elise ELIZZA** [s] (6 January 1870 – 3 June 1920) was born Elisabeth Lettner-groschen in Vienna. Marrying her first singing teacher Adolf Jentsch, Elizza also appeared under the name of Elise Jentsch. After three years as a soubrette, Elizza furthered her studies with Amalie Materna and made her Hofoper debut in 1895. She remained with the company until 1919 but retired from the stage in 1912. A most versatile artist, Elizza possessed an enormous variety of roles including: The Queen of the Night (*Die Zauberflöte*), Blondchen, Zerlina, (*Enführung*), and Brünnhilde (*Siegfried*). Apart from smaller Wagner roles, she was most often heard as Marguerite de Valois (*Les Huguenots*) and Philine in *Mignon*. In many works she sang more than one role, e.g., Adèle and Rosalinde in *Die Fledermaus*, Nedda and the title role in *La Navarraise*, and title roles in *Raimund* and *Gretel*. She recorded for various labels, including G&T, Gramophone, Odéon, Favre, and Pathé.

**Benedikt FELIX** (28 September 1869 – 2 March 1912) first sang in small houses but after retraining with Johannes Ress, tenor, he joined the Hofoper, where he remained until shortly before his death. With the exception of Beckmesser in *Meistersinger* and Johann in the world premiere of Massenet's Werther, Felix primarily sang the smaller Wagner roles, which he did countless times. He made discs for G&T and at least three Pathé cylinders.

**Ottilie FELLWOCK** [cont] (1877-?) studied with Pauline Lucca despite opposition from her family. Her singing of Ortrud in Lucca's private theater was praised by the critic Eduard Hanslick. As was the custom, Fellwock appeared as a guest before Mahler engaged her during the 1898-1899 season. Her nine roles included minor parts in the premieres of Bernstein's *Doktor Faust* and Siegfried Wagner's *Der Bärenhäuter*. She later sang major roles in Graz, Prague, and other, smaller houses until 1911, after which little there is no further trace of her. She made four rare discs in Prague for G&T.
