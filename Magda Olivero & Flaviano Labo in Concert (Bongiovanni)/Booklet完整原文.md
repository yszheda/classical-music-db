## 内页介绍 (意大利文)
### MAGDA OLIVERO O LA SUBLIMAZIONE DEL VERISMO
Oggi il termine 'verismo' è spesso usato a sproposito. Non passa occasione senza che, da pulpiti non sempre qualificati, cantanti dalla tecnica quantomeno dubbia siano tacciati dell'epiteto di 'veristi'. È giunto il momento, però, di chiederci se effettivamente il metodo di giudicare le voci di questi soloni sia quello giusto e se una volatina, un trillo od agilità impeccabili siano sufficienti a rimpiazzare una bella voce, un temperamento esplosivo o doti interpretative trascinanti. Intendiamoci, non è certo questa la sede, né il momento più appropriato per tentare una difesa dei lasciti della nostra Giovine Scuola, come, a suo tempo, l'aveva battezzata Camille Bellaigue. Ma, fermo restando che il pubblico (giudice supremo dei fatti teatrali) è ancora desideroso di questi soggetti, mi sembrava giusto puntualizzare che, in sede di giudizio critico, non si può prescindere dalla cifra stilistica dell'opera interpretata. Ossia, detto in parole povere, nessuno canta male in assoluto, ma in funzione di quanto richiesto dal contesto storico, stilistico ed estetico in cui il compositore ebbe ad operare. È ovvio che quanto detto poc'anzi sia una generalizzazione ed, in quanto tale, soggetta a più di una critica. Ma è altrettanto ovvio che non si possa giudicare Leoncavallo con il metro di Rossini... E, poi, fermiamoci un istante sugli interpreti. Su quei cantanti, cioè, che furono attivi nell'epoca d'oro della Giovine Scuola, che ne conobbero i principali musicisti, che ne seguirono le direttive ed il credo artistico. Non credo si possano sottovalutare uguali come quelle di Eugenia Burzio, Angelica Pandolfini, Carmen Melis, Gilda Dalla Rizza, Claudia Muzio, Emma Carelli, Rosina Storchio e tante altre che solo lo spazio mi impedisce di enumerare. Magda Olivero fa parte di questa schiera di elette ed, anzi, ne rappresenta, per certi versi, la sintesi e la sublimazione ad un tempo. Nata a Saluzzo il 25 marzo 1910, Maria Maddalena Olivero, in arte Magda, sin dall'esordio all'Eiar (la Rai di allora) il 2 dicembre 1932 in un piccolo ruolo nei Misteri dolorosi di Nino Cattozzo mostrò una particolare predilezione proprio per questo repertorio.

Certo, nell'arco di una carriera eccezionale sia sotto il profilo artistico, sia dal punto di vista della longevità vocale (la Olivero, in concerto, è attiva ancor oggi), non le mancarono occasioni di brillare anche in altri autori ed in altri stili. Per esempio, come non ricordare la sua Maria nella Mazeppa di Ciaikowski al Maggio Musicale Fiorentino del 1954 di cui Leonardo Pinzauti scrisse che era 'veramente commuovente nel finale dell'opera'. O la Medea di Cherubini, affrontata allo State Fair Music Hall di Dallas nel 1967 e di cui John Ardoin disse che: 'ella agisce nella scena come la più superba cantanteattrice che oggi si possa ammirare'. E come non citare i suoi strettissimi rapporti con gli autori francesi: Manon e Charlotte in Werther, Marguerite nel Faust, la femme ne La voix humaine e Soeur Marie ne Les dialogues des carmélites furono creazioni, da parte della Olivero, di figure intense e drammaticamente coinvolgenti. Ed infine Verdi, con quella portentosa grande scena ed aria di Violetta incisa nel 1939 che fece scrivere a Celletti: 'La Olivero (voce non bellissima e un po' tremula, ma tecnica e sensibilità portentose) giunse da sola nell'«Allegro' ad imporsi un ritmo toscaniniano o quasi: e questa è una delle ragioni della stupefacente riuscita dell'incisione'. Repertorio, come si può vedere, in ogni caso vasto e disparato cui si debbono aggiungere, per dovere di cronaca, anche alcuni ruoli 'di composizione' come la Contessa nella Dama di picche o la Kostelnicka nella Jenůfa, personaggi generalmente affidati ad artiste dalla forte personalità ma declinanti e che invece la Olivero affrontò ancora nel pieno possesso dei suoi mezzi. Ma, indipendentemente dai trionfi suscitati dal soprano piemontese in queste opere, è certo che l'addetto ai lavori, l'intenditore ed il semplice appassionato vedano in Magda Olivero una sorta di vestale del verismo. Una delle custodi dei segreti della Giovine Scuola. Ed avrebbero ragione, poiché nel nostro secolo nessuno seppe offrire immagini più fulgide e più centrali delle eroine di Cilea, Mascagni, Puccini, Alfano o Giordano. Adriana, Fedora, Iris, Minnie, Mimì, Manon, Katiausha rivivevano, grazie all'arte di Magda Olivero, con accenti ed inflessioni dalle quali traspariva la loro umanità, la loro verità. Non erano più, come tante, troppe volte, semplici immagini teatrali, ma vibravano ed agivano come creature reali, vere. Ed allora torniamo al discorso iniziale: abbiamo detto che Magda Olivero rappresenta la sintesi e, nello stesso tempo, la sublimazione del fenomeno verista. Perché? È la stessa Olivero a spiegarne il motivo palesando il suo credo artistico in numerose interviste da lei concesse (in particolare quella a Jean Abel apparsa in Le Provençal qui a pochi giorni prima del concerto oggetto di questa registrazione). 'Verismo significa soltanto maggiore attenzione alla dimensione teatrale'. È chiaro che questa risposta, o piuttosto che questa regola, debba essere applicata ad ogni performance (e come abbiamo visto, Olivero era un esperto in questo), ma it is just as clear that this suits the verist repertory perfectly.

And the striking thing about Olivero's art is the extreme naturalness, the smoothness with which she handles characters which other artists sometimes ruined, in their search for 'reality', a too realistic effect (Verism does not mean uncontrolled realism). I mean that what in other singers' performances seemed to be false, extemporary exaggeration and the result of an obvious division between performer and character, was a genuine, linear act with Olivero. We know that this is not the case, and that in art, sincerity is the result of very hard, meditated study. But, listening to Olivero and watching her performing on stage, one had the impression of participating, in the context of an authentic drama, in the development of the character of a woman in flesh and blood. But we also said that Olivero represents the sublimation of Verism, so at this point the discussion involves a technical matter. The image which verist sopranos normally have is that of an ample but somewhat unbalanced voice with good colour, combined with extraordinary energy and warmth. Even if Olivero's voice is not immediately striking for the beauty of its timbre (with a fast vibrato not to everyone's taste), it is obvious that these gifts can be found in her singing. But that's not all: as well as these qualities, Olivero was also gifted with really effective psychological introspection. This was due to a highly experienced phonation and polished technique which ensured her a variety of colours and a practically infinite expressive kaleidoscope. This is the substantial difference between Olivero and the other verist singers, and the only name which I would spontaneously put on the same level would be Claudia Muzio, rather than Esther Mazzoleni, as Giacomo Lauri-Volpi does in Voci parallele.

Let's finally discuss the concert in question, which was at that time transmitted by the French state radio and therefore has exceptional sound quality. I'll not waste much time on its contents: everybody can judge their value themselves, but I feel that some observations are necessary. After forty years of career, Olivero's voice was still very powerful and free from weakness in 1973. Her performance of the two arias from Adriana Lecouvreur are magnificent and have little or nothing to envy the old 78's recorded with Cetra in 1939 and 1940 respectively, or the numerous live versions (particularly the Naples' one) released on various occasions. The same is the case as far as the 'Ebben ne andrò lontano' is concerned, which I find even better than the complete Wally recorded in Bergamo the year before. The Puccini pieces are marvellous, particularly 'Vissi d'arte', which is more introspective than the 1940 Cetra 78, and 'Spunta l'aurora pallida' from Boito's Mefistofele absolutely exceptional. This is a piece which Olivero sometimes performed in her concerts, and one already known version was taken from an Amsterdam performance in 1963 and released several times. In my opinion it is the most interesting piece on this CD: an authentic lesson of abandon and the meaning of phrasing with feeling. But, having given Olivero her dues, let's also pause for an instant on the rest of this CD, as there is no lack of surprises. In Piacenza singer Flaviano Labò, for example, we find the solidity and power of tenors of old - a race which seems to have vanished from today's stages. In 'Cielo e mar', 'O tu che in seno agli angeli' and 'O paradiso', there is a proud nobility which the various Pavarotti, Domingo, Carreras and the likes are alas only able to simulate - and, if I may say so, not even very well. Then listen to the passionate ardour of 'Amor ti vieta', or the breathtaking ending of 'Recondita armonia', and you'll agree with me that if Labò (who passed away a few years ago) was still alive today, he would definitely be one of our top singers. As for the Tosca recorded in Tokyo in 1973, as well as emphasizing once again Flaviano Labò's capabilities, it shows the extreme short-sightedness of the major recording companies, unable to understand the value of Raina Kabaivanska's talent.

CARLO CURAMI

### MAGDA OLIVERO OR THE SUBLIMATION OF VERISM
Today the term 'Verism' is often used erroneously. On numerous occasions, from unqualified pulpits, singers with extremely dubious technique are accused of being 'verists'. The time has come however to ask ourselves if in fact the method of judging the voices of these 'Solons' is right and if a fast scale, a trill or impeccable agility are sufficient to replace a beautiful voice, explosive temperament and exciting performing ability. Let's make it clear that this is certainly not the most suitable place or time to try to defend the legacy of our Young School, as Camille Bellaigue christened it in her day, but since the public (supreme judge of theatrical events) still wants these individuals. I thought it should be specified that when it comes to critical judgement, the stylistic quality of the opera being performed cannot be left out. In other words, nobody sings absolutely badly, but according to the requirements of the historic, stylistic and aesthetic context in which the composer worked. Obviously this is a generalization, and as such is open to criticism, but it is just as obvious that Leoncavallo cannot be judged with the same yardstick as Rossini...

Now let's for a moment consider the singers: those who performed during the golden age of the Young School, knew its top musicians and followed their instructions and artistic credo. I don't believe it's possible to underrate singers like Eugenia Burzio, Angelica Pandolfini, Carmen Melis, Gilda Dalla Rizza, Claudia Muzio, Emma Carelli, Rosina Storchio and too many others for me to name here. Magda Olivero is part of this select band, and indeed, for certain aspects, is both its synthesis and its sublimation. Born in Saluzzo on May 25 1910, from her beginnings on the EIAR (Italy's broadcasting corporation of that period) on December 2 1932 in a small role in Nino Cattozzo's Misteri dolorosi, Maria Maddalena Olivero showed a particular preference for precisely this repertory.

During a career which was exceptional from both an artistic and vocal longevity point of view (for example, Olivero still performs in concerts to this day), she had plenty of opportunities to distinguish herself with other composers and styles as well. For example, how can we forget her Maria in Tchaikovsky's Mazeppa at Florence's 1954 Maggio Musicale, of which Leonardo Pinzauti wrote that she was 'really moving in the opera's finale'. Or Cherubini's Medea, performed at the Dallas State Fair Music Hall in 1967, of which John Ardoin said '... she performs on stage like the most superb singer/actress to be heard today'. And how could we avoid mentioning her extremely close ties with French composers: Manon, Charlotte in Werther, Marguerite in Faust, the woman in La Voix Humaine and Sister Marie in Les dialogues des Carmélites were Olivero creations of intense, dramatically enthralling characters. And lastly Verdi, with that phenomenal last scene and aria by Violetta recorded in 1939 which convinced Celletti to write: 'In the 'Allegro', Olivero (a slightly trembling, not particularly beautiful voice, but extraordinary technique and sensitivity) managed to set herself a rhythm which was Toscanini-like or almost, and this is one of the reasons for the recording's astonishing success.' As can be seen, her repertory was vast and varied and to tell the full story, some lesser roles must be added to it, such as the Countess in Dama di Picche or Kostelnicka in Jenůfa, parts normally entrusted to artists with strong personalities but who were on the wane, but which Olivero on the other hand performed while still in possession of all her ability. But apart from the triumph achieved by the Piedmontese soprano in these operas, insiders, connoisseurs and normal fans saw in Magda Olivero a sort of vestal of Verism, one of the guardians of the secrets of the Young School. And they were right, since nobody else this century has been able to offer more fluid, true performances of the heroines of Cilea, Mascagni, Puccini, Alfano or Giordano. Adriana, Fedora, Iris, Minnie, Mimì, Manon and Katyusha were brought back to life, thanks to Magda Olivero's ability, with accents and inflections from which their humanity and truth shone through. They were no longer as on too many other occasions, mere theatrical images, but they vibrated and reacted like real, true people. So let's return to the initial topic: we said that Magda Olivero represents the synthesis and at the same time the sublimation of the verist phenomenon. Why? Olivero herself explains the reason for it herself, disclosing her artistic credo in numerous interviews which she has given (in particular that by Jean Abel published in Le Provençal a few days before the concert recorded here). 'Verism means just greater attention to the theatrical dimension'. It is clear that this reply, or rather must, be applied to every performance (and as we have seen, Olivero was an expert in this), but it is just as clear that this suits the verist repertory perfectly.

And the striking thing about Olivero's art is the extreme naturalness, the smoothness with which she handles characters which other artists sometimes ruined, in their search for 'reality', a too realistic effect (Verism does not mean uncontrolled realism).
