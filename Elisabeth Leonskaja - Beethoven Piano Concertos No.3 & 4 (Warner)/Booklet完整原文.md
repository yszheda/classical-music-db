# Beethoven: Piano Concertos Nos. 3 & 4

Elisabeth Leonskaja continues her series of Beethoven piano concertos with this recording of the Third and Fourth Piano Concertos, pairing the dramatic C minor Concerto with the lyrical G major Concerto. Beethoven's five piano concertos stand at the centre of the repertoire, and they represent the composer's development from the classical world of Mozart and Haydn to the full-blown romantic individualism of his later years.

The Third Piano Concerto in C minor, Op. 37, was composed between 1800 and 1803, and it represents a huge step forward in Beethoven's development as a composer. It is far more ambitious in scale than the first two concertos, with a much more dramatic approach to the interaction between piano and orchestra, and a depth of expression that points forward to the great works of Beethoven's middle period. The C minor key gives the work a dark, tragic character that we associate with some of Beethoven's greatest works, and the writing for the piano is much more virtuosic and demanding than in the earlier concertos.

The Fourth Piano Concerto in G major, Op. 58, followed just a few years later, composed between 1805 and 1806. It is a very different work from the Third, much more lyrical and intimate in character. Beethoven broke new ground here with the very opening, which begins with the piano playing the main theme alone, before the orchestra enters. The slow movement is one of Beethoven's most inspired inspirations, with its gentle dialogue between the piano and orchestra, where the aggressive chords of the orchestra are answered by the soft pleading of the piano, before the music moves directly into the joyful finale.

Elisabeth Leonskaja is one of the world's most distinguished interpreters of the German Romantic repertoire, and she has been performing Beethoven's piano concertos around the world for many years. This series of recordings with Marek Janowski and the Deutsches Symphonie-Orchester Berlin presents her mature insights into these masterpieces, combining structural clarity with deep musical expression.

# Elisabeth Leonskaja

Elisabeth Leonskaja was born in Soviet Union in 1946, and studied at the Moscow Conservatory with Jacob Milstein. She won the Enescu Competition in Bucharest at the age of 18, and the Queen Elisabeth Competition in Brussels in 1968. She emigrated to the West in 1978, and quickly established an international career. She has lived in Vienna since 1990, and she tours extensively throughout the world, performing with all the major orchestras and at all the leading festivals.

She is particularly admired for her interpretations of the classics of the German repertoire, especially Beethoven, Schubert and Brahms, and her recordings have won many awards. Her approach to music is characterized by deep intellectual understanding of the structure, combined with a passionate commitment to communication with the audience. She is one of the great piano artists of our time, and her performances of Beethoven have been particularly praised by critics and audiences alike.

# Marek Janowski

Marek Janowski was born in Warsaw in 1939, and studied conducting in Vienna. He has held principal conductor positions with many major orchestras, including the Orchestre Philharmonique de Radio France, the Orchestra of the Opéra de Lyon, the Deutsche Symphonie-Orchester Berlin, and the Rundfunk-Sinfonieorchester Berlin. He is particularly known for his authoritative interpretations of the German Romantic repertoire, and his recordings of the complete symphonies of Bruckner and Wagner have been widely acclaimed.

# Deutsches Symphonie-Orchester Berlin

The Deutsches Symphonie-Orchester Berlin was founded in 1946, and it has established itself as one of the leading orchestras in Germany. It has worked with many of the greatest conductors of the post-war period, and it performs a wide range of repertoire from the classical period to the present day. It is based in the Philharmonie Berlin, one of the great concert halls of the world.

Beethoven's piano concertos occupy a unique position in the repertoire. They bridge the gap between the classical concerto of Mozart and the romantic concertos of the nineteenth century, and each one shows Beethoven's steady development as a composer, from the classical elegance of the first two concertos to the grand romantic scale of the Fifth Emperor Concerto. The Third and Fourth Concertos, composed between 1800 and 1806, show Beethoven at the height of his middle period, fully in command of his powers and exploring new territory in terms of form, expression and the relationship between piano and orchestra.

Elisabeth Leonskaja has been performing these works for many years, and her interpretations are the product of a lifetime of study and performance. She brings to these masterworks a deep understanding of their structure and a profound feeling for their emotional content, combining textual fidelity with spontaneous musical expression. This recording presents these great concertos in the hands of one of the greatest pianists of our time, partnered by one of Germany's leading orchestras under one of the most experienced conductors of the German repertoire.
