## LE PIANO FRANÇAIS

### Par Denis Herlin

A France, et tout particulièrement à Paris, fut durant le milieu du XIXe siècle l'objet d'une fascination de la part des musiciens étrangers comme Chopin et Liszt trouvèrent un endroit propice pour composer et la diversité des traditions musicales françaises, un climat particulièrement riche et fertile dans lequel bon nombre d'entre eux vinrent puiser. Cette période vit la naissance de formes nouvelles, d'oeuvres de musiciens français dont la renommée et l'influence furent appréciées dans le monde entier. La création de la Société Nationale de Musique, fondée le 25 février 1871, joua un rôle déterminant dans la diffusion de ce répertoire. En effet, une maison parisienne s'ouvrit qui fut le programme de ces différentes enregistrements. L'on excède bien souvent en prétendant que la Société avait été fondée par les compositeurs français de l'époque. En vérité, pour les compositeurs français, elle était un lieu de rencontre où l'on pouvait se côtoyer des pièces à caractère intime et polyphonique comme les deux volumes de *Pièces de clavecin* et les *Pièces de violes* de Marin Marais. Ces recueils témoignent de l'esprit de Chopin comme les *Quelques danses et quadrilles* de Franck qui, lui-même, s'imprégna des œuvres de Rameau. La richesse et la diversité des mouvements picturaux et théâtraux de la France des années 1870 à 1930 est également une source abondante. Les compositions comme les *Minuits* de Ravel, les *Fêtes galantes* de Debussy et les *Préludes* de Messiaen trouvent dans la structure des œuvres elles-mêmes de riches possibilités de coloration instrumentaliste et impressionniste. Que ce soit Prélude, Choral et Fugue de César Franck ou les *Petites esquisses d'oiseaux* d'Olivier Messiaen, voici rassemblé dans cette série consacrée au piano français, le fruit d'un siècle de création.

**Denis Herlin**

### By Denis Herlin
### Translated by Stewart Spencer

By the middle of the nineteenth century, Paris in general, and France's musical capital Paris in particular, had become a magnet for foreign composers such as Chopin and Liszt, who found it a propitious place for publishing their works and introducing them to a wider public. It was an especially fertile terrain which, with formal and informal academies, salons and other effervescence of works by French composers whose reputation and originality were soon appreciated throughout the entire world. The establishment of the Société Nationale de Musique on 25 February 1871 played a crucial role in the diffusion of these works. Indeed, many of the pieces included in the present series of recordings received their first public hearings at the concerts of the Société Nationale de Musique, an opportunity for all the new works performed in this forum gave young French composers a freedom to express themselves, an opportunity they would not otherwise have enjoyed. Thus works of a Lisztian or Chopinesque character, with their wealth of textures, their two triptychs, may be found alongside pieces such as Fauré's Nocturnes, Ravel's Barcarolles - imbued with the spirit of Chabrin - and, with others of their immediate forebears such as Messiaen's *Quelques danses* and Ravel's *Menuet antique*. No less influential were the richness and diversity of the artistic and literary movements in France between 1870 and 1930. Works such as Ravel's *Miroirs*, Debussy's *Fêtes galantes* and Messiaen's *Préludes* reveal through their very structure the poetry impregnated with the colours of Symbolism and Impressionist ideas. From Franck's *Prélude, choral et fugue* to Messiaen's *Petites esquisses d'oiseaux* the present edition of works devoted to French piano music represents the fruits of a century of creative endeavour.

This peremptory phrase may perhaps explain why both Debussy and Ravel held Chabrier's piano pieces in such high regard. Above and beyond their humour and poetry, these great works deserve a place among the finest works of French keyboard music.

Chabrier was born on 18 January 1841 in Ambert, in the Puy-de-Dôme. His father, a lawyer, moved to Paris in 1847, where he completed his studies. In 1856, the family settled permanently in Paris. Chabrier entered the Ministry of the Interior in 1862, where he remained for fifteen years. This position allowed him to compose at his own pace, without having to worry about earning his living by giving lessons or organizing concerts. He was thus able to devote himself entirely to composition, while pursuing an administrative career that assured him a certain financial security.

Chabrier began composing at the age of fifteen. His first works were melodies and piano pieces. In 1862, he entered the Ministry of the Interior, where he remained until 1877. This period was marked by the composition of numerous melodies, piano pieces and two operettas: *L'Étoile* (1877) and *Une éducation manquée* (1879). In 1877, he met Lamoureux, who was to play an important role in his career. Lamoureux commissioned from him an *Ode* for chorus and orchestra, which was premiered in 1878. In 1879, he composed *Gwendoline*, his first grand opera, which was premiered in 1886.

These pieces were premiered on 9 April 1881 by Charles de Bériot, and their originality was immediately recognized. The *Capriccio* remained unfinished and was completed by Maurice Le Boucher in 1914.

Chabrier died on 11 September 1894 in Paris, at the age of 53. His premature death put an end to a promising career. He left behind him a body of piano works that remain among the most original and personal in the entire French piano literature.

Chabrier did not take to the piano like other musicians; his piano writing is not at all that of a virtuoso. No one had to push him to the piano, which a multitude of notations bear witness. He was not one to seek out effects: if he ever had recourse to the pedal, it was only to obtain a particular colour. His manner of playing the piano, his manner of handling the keyboard was of his music reflected the kind of pleasure he took from life and the intense pleasure of playing the piano. He was a gifted pianist who, like Mozart, was able to express his most intimate feelings through his playing.

Chabrier was born into a middle-class family. His father was a lawyer who had come to Paris from the Auvergne. Chabrier showed musical talent from an early age, and his parents recognized this. They gave him piano lessons, and he quickly progressed. At the age of fifteen, he began to compose. His first works were simple melodies and piano pieces, but even these showed signs of his future genius.

In 1862, Chabrier entered the Ministry of the Interior. This was a secure position that allowed him to compose without financial worries. He worked there for fifteen years, during which time he composed numerous works, including two operettas. In 1877, he met the conductor Charles Lamoureux, who became an important supporter of his music.

Chabrier's piano works are characterized by their originality, their humour, and their poetic sensitivity. He had a unique ability to capture the essence of a melody or a harmonic progression, and to transform it into something entirely new. His piano writing is often very dense, with complex harmonies and intricate rhythms. Yet despite this complexity, his music always sounds natural and spontaneous.

Chabrier's influence on later French composers cannot be overstated. Ravel and Debussy both acknowledged their debt to him, and his piano works continue to be studied and performed today. His music represents a bridge between the romanticism of the nineteenth century and the impressionism of the early twentieth century.

The present recording, performed by Pierre Barbizet and Jean Hubeau, offers a comprehensive survey of Chabrier's piano works. The performances are characterized by their technical brilliance, their poetic sensitivity, and their deep understanding of Chabrier's unique musical language.

Wenn er je von der Pedaltaste Gebrauch machte, dann nur, um eine bestimmte Farbe zu erzielen. Seine Art, das Klavier zu behandeln, seine Art, die Tastatur zu handhaben, spiegelt in seiner Musik die Art von Freude wider, die er am Leben empfand, und die tiefe Freude, die ihm das Klavierspiel bereitete.

Chabrier stammte aus einer bürgerlichen Familie. Sein Vater war Anwalt, der aus der Auvergne nach Paris gekommen war. Chabrier zeigte schon in jungen Jahren musikalisches Talent, und seine Eltern erkannten dies. Sie gaben ihm Klavierunterricht, und er machte schnell Fortschritte. Im Alter von fünfzehn Jahren begann er zu komponieren. Seine ersten Werke waren einfache Lieder und Klavierstücke, aber selbst diese zeigten Anzeichen seines zukünftigen Genies.

1862 trat Chabrier in das Innenministerium ein. Dies war eine sichere Stellung, die es ihm ermöglichte, ohne finanzielle Sorgen zu komponieren. Er arbeitete dort fünfzehn Jahre lang, während dieser Zeit komponierte er zahlreiche Werke, darunter zwei Operetten. 1877 lernte er den Dirigenten Charles Lamoureux kennen, der ein wichtiger Förderer seiner Musik wurde.

Chabriers Klavierwerke zeichnen sich durch ihre Originalität, ihren Humor und ihre poetische Empfindsamkeit aus. Er hatte eine einzigartige Fähigkeit, das Wesen einer Melodie oder einer Harmoniefolge zu erfassen und sie in etwas völlig Neues zu verwandeln. Sein Klaviersatz ist oft sehr dicht, mit komplexen Harmonien und komplizierten Rhythmen. Doch trotz dieser Komplexität klingt seine Musik immer natürlich und spontan.

Chabriers Einfluss auf spätere französische Komponisten kann nicht hoch genug eingeschätzt werden. Sowohl Ravel als auch Debussy erkannten ihre Schuld ihm gegenüber an, und seine Klavierwerke werden bis heute studiert und aufgeführt. Seine Musik stellt eine Brücke zwischen der Romantik des 19. Jahrhunderts und dem Impressionismus des frühen 20. Jahrhunderts dar.

Die vorliegende Aufnahme, gespielt von Pierre Barbizet und Jean Hubeau, bietet eine umfassende Übersicht über Chabriers Klavierwerke. Die Aufführungen zeichnen sich durch ihre technische Brillanz, ihre poetische Empfindsamkeit und ihr tiefes Verständnis von Chabriers einzigartiger musikalischer Sprache aus.

## BIOGRAPHIES OF THE ARTISTS

### Pierre Barbizet (1922-1990)

**French:**
Le pianiste et compositeur français, Jean Hubeau fit ses études musicales à Paris, où il fut l'élève de Jean Gallon, Noël Gallon, Lazare Lévy et Paul Dukas, et à Vienne où il travailla avec Félix Weingartner et Josef Krrips. Jean Hubeau fut Grand Prix de Rome de composition musicale. Pianiste concertiste depuis 1937, il donna de nombreux concerts, en soliste ou musique de chambre, et devint l'un des meilleurs solistes français en France et dans le monde. Il enregistra beaucoup pour le disque et reçut outre autres des récompenses dès 1938. Un Prix du Disque pour son premier enregistrement (Schubert avec Pierre Fournier), un deuxième Grand Prix du Disque en 1947 pour l'enregistrement du Concerto pour violon de sa composition interprété par Henry Merckel et Eugène Bigot. Il reçut le Grand Prix du Disque pour l'enregistrement de l'œuvre de la musique de chambre de Fauré en 1970, et en 1981 le prix "In Honorem" de l'Académie Charles Cros pour l'intégrale de la musique de chambre de Schumann.

Son œuvre de compositeur comporte principalement des Suites symphoniques, des Concertos pour différents instruments, des Sonates et autres pièces d'ensembles, et des Ballets représentés en Europe et en Amérique. Entre 1942 et 1957, Jean Hubeau, alors Directeur du Conservatoire de Versailles, y déploya une grande activité de chef d'orchestre. Ayant été professeur de musique de chambre au Conservatoire National Supérieur de Musique de Paris durant 25 années, il a de nombreux anciens élèves qui poursuivent une carrière brillante, dans tous les domaines du concert et du disque.

Ses enregistrements de musique française (Franck, Pierné, Saint-Saëns...) et de la musique de chambre de Schumann ont été récompensés par la critique. Son "Intégrale de la musique pour piano" de Fauré a remporté un Grand Prix du Disque.

**English:**
The French pianist and composer Jean Hubeau began his musical studies in Paris, where his teachers included Jean and Noël Gallon, Lazare Lévy and Paul Dukas. He later worked in Vienna with Felix Weingartner and Josef Krrips. He was awarded the Grand Prix de Rome for composition and began his career as a concert pianist in 1937, subsequently giving numerous concerts, both as soloist and chamber musician, and with various ensembles. He became one of France's leading soloists in France and throughout the world. He made many gramophone recordings. He received his first award in 1938. A Prix du Disque for his first recording (Schubert with Pierre Fournier), a second Grand Prix du Disque in 1947 for a recording of his own Violin Concerto, played by Henry Merckel and conducted by Eugène Bigot. In 1970 he received a further Grand Prix du Disque for his recording of Fauré's chamber music and in 1981 he was awarded the "In Honorem" prize of the Académie Charles Cros for his complete recording of Schumann's chamber music.

His work as a composer comprises, principally, Symphonic Suites, concertos for various instruments, Sonatas and other ensemble works, together with a number of ballets that have been staged in Europe and America. Between 1942 and 1957, when he was director of the Conservatoire de Versailles, Jean Hubeau was in great demand as a conductor. For twenty-five years he was professor of Chamber Music at the Conservatoire National Supérieur de Musique in Paris and many of his former pupils are now pursuing brilliant careers both in the concert hall and recording studios. Jean Hubeau's recordings of French music by Franck, Pierné, Saint-Saëns and others, as well as those of Schumann's chamber music, were universally well received. His complete recording of Fauré's complete piano works was awarded a Grand Prix du Disque.

### Pierre Barbizet

**French:**
Pierre Barbizet est né au Chili en septembre 1922. Il a fait ses études au Conservatoire de Paris et a obtenu des prix de piano, d'histoire de la musique et de musique de chambre. Très jeune, il s'est consacré entièrement à la musique et a beaucoup joué à l'étranger. Il a obtenu le Grand Prix du Concours International de Scheveringen et a été lauréat du Concours Marguerite Long-Jacques Thibaud en 1949. Pendant plusieurs années, il a formé avec le violoniste Ferras un duo exceptionnel réputant le style français de leur jeu. Ils ont gravé ensemble l'intégrale des Sonates pour violon et piano de Beethoven. Le répertoire de Pierre Barbizet s'étend de Mozart aux compositeurs contemporains. Il n'est attaché à mieux faire connaître la musique de Chabrier. C'est le musicien authentique qui a toujours placé l'élégance de sa technique au service d'une expression sensible. Pendant près de trente ans, Pierre Barbizet a été le directeur du Conservatoire de Marseille. Il est mort en Janvier 1990.

**English:**
The French pianist Pierre Barbizet was born in Arica (Chile) on 20 September 1922. He studied at the Paris Conservatoire, where he was awarded first prizes for piano, music history and chamber music. From a very early age he devoted himself entirely to music, and gave many performances outside France. He won the Grand Prix at the Scheveningen International Competition in 1948. He was also a prize winner at the Marguerite Long-Jacques Thibaud Competition in 1949. In 1963 he became director of the Marseilles Conservatoire. For several years he performed mainly as a duo with violinist Christian Ferras in a duo famed for giving a pure French style, and together they recorded the complete Beethoven sonatas for violin and piano. While Pierre Barbizet's repertoire extended from Mozart to contemporary he was especially interested in promoting the composer Chabrier, and performed his complete works for piano. He used his elegant technique above all to heighten his expression, and never let it interfere with the genuine sensitivity of his performance. He died on 18 January 1990.

Stets stellte er die Eleganz seiner Technik in den Dienst des authentischen musikalischen Ausdrucks. Pierre Barbizet ist im Januar 1990 gestorben.
