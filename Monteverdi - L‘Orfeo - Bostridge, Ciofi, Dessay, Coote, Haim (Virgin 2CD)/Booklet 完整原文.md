# THE FIRST GREAT OPERA

## An introduction by Tim Carter

On 23 February 1607, the Mantuan courtier Carlo Magnone wrote to his brother in Rome about a forthcoming work to be heard in Mantua. 'Tomorrow evening the Most Serene Lord the Prince [Ferdinando Gonzaga] has ordered that we put on a play in music on the story of Orfeo that I have composed,' he writes. 'I shall be interested to hear how it turns out, for it is done in a great hurry.' No doubt it had been rushed: given the complexities of court ceremony and artistic production in early seventeenth-century Italy, mounting a musical spectacle like *L'Orfeo* at short notice was a significant achievement.

Orfeo was referring to *Orfeo*, the first opera by Claudio Monteverdi, which did indeed have its première on 24 February (it was then repeated on 1 March) under the direction of the composer. The performance was highly successful, as we know from several contemporary accounts, and it marked a turning point in the history of opera.

*Orfeo* was originally described as a 'play in music' (*favola in musica*), which is recognisable in opera in all but name. Opera was a new genre: the idea of taking a drama and performing it to music throughout had emerged in Florence only in the 1590s. The precedent was classical, although the impulse was modern. The Florentines sought to revive the principles of Greek drama in their musical declamation, which they termed *recitar cantando* ('speak while singing'). The aim was to create a new kind of musical speech that would express the text's emotions and meanings clearly and powerfully.

The first opera to survive complete, Jacopo Peri's *Euridice*, was staged in Florence in October 1600 for the wedding of Maria de' Medici to King Henri IV of France. Orfeo's text, set by Claudio Monteverdi, is one of the entertainments performed to celebrate the wedding of Francesco Gonzaga, heir to the dukedom of Mantua, to Margherita of Savoy. The librettist of *Orfeo*, Monteverdi himself may or may not have been present, but he certainly knew the score court secretary Alessandro Striggio (1573?-1630), who published his text as Striggio knew Rimuccini's libretto. *Orfeo* contains many textual and musical references to Peri's *Euridice*, and it is clear that Monteverdi and Striggio were setting themselves up in competition with the Florentines.

The subject matter is significant. The mythological hero Orpheus could not only sing and play the lyre beautifully but, as Ovid tells us, 'with his songs he drew the forests in his train, and moved the rocks'. Striggio's libretto draws on various versions of the Orpheus myth, including that found in Ovid's *Metamorphoses*. The libretto was published in 1609, but it was not printed until 1609.

The opera demands a substantial array of instruments: a five-part string ensemble (with two to a part, a bass continuo section comprising bass viol, theorbo, harpsichord, organ, regal), three trombones (two tenor, one bass), a double bass, three recorders (two sopranino, one alto), and a positive organ. The scoring was geared to Mantuan resources, as one might expect, and is largely followed here.

It is unlikely that *Orfeo* was intended to be performed by a small cast of six or ten singers, doubling up in the solo roles, as some have suggested. The cast list is very similar to the final piece in Monteverdi's Eighth Book of madrigals of 1638, 'Questi vaghi concenti', and both works call for a similar forces. The Duke's musical establishment was large, and Monteverdi had at his disposal a wide range of vocal and instrumental resources.

# SYNOPSIS

## PROLOGUE

After three statements of the opening fanfare-like *toccata*, La Musica (Music) appears in front of the curtain. She announces that she is the goddess of music, and that her song can move even the hardest hearts. She describes the power of music to calm the winds and soothe the seas, and to awaken the spirit of love in the coldest minds.

## ACT ONE

In the fields of Thrace, Orfeo is at the centre of a joyful celebration with his beloved Euridice. The shepherds and nymphs gather to sing of their happiness. They praise the beauty of Euridice and the skill of Orfeo. But their joy is interrupted by the arrival of a Messenger, who brings the terrible news that Euridice has died from a snakebite. Orfeo is overcome with grief and resolves to descend to the underworld to bring her back.

## ACT TWO

Orfeo sets off for the underworld, accompanied by Hope. At the gates of Hades, Hope departs, for she cannot enter the realm of the dead. Orfeo is left alone before the entrance to the underworld, guarded by Charon, the ferryman of the dead. Orfeo tries to persuade Charon to take him across the river Styx, but Charon refuses. Orfeo then uses his music to charm Charon into falling asleep, and slips past him into the underworld.

## ACT THREE

In the underworld, Orfeo's singing moves the spirits of the dead to pity. Proserpina, queen of the underworld, is so moved that she begs her husband Plutone to allow Euridice to return to the world of the living. Plutone agrees, on condition that Orfeo does not look back at Euridice until they have reached the upper world. Orfeo and Euridice begin their journey back, but just before they reach the surface, Orfeo turns to look at Euridice, and she disappears forever.

## ACT FOUR

Orfeo is overcome with grief. He wanders through the fields of Thrace, singing of his loss. Echo responds to his laments. Suddenly Apollo appears and invites Orfeo to ascend with him to heaven, where they can live together among the stars. Orfeo accepts, and the opera ends with a celebration of his apotheosis.

# LE PREMIER GRAND OPÉRA

## Une introduction de Tim Carter

Le 23 février 1607, Carlo Magni, gentilhomme de la cour de Mantoue, écrivit à son frère à Rome au sujet d'une œuvre qui devait être donnée à Mantoue. «Demain soir, le Sérénissime Seigneur le Prince [Ferdinand de Gonzague] a ordonné que nous donnions une pièce en musique sur l'histoire d'Orphée que j'ai composée», écrit-il. «Je suis curieux de savoir comment cela tournera, car c'est fait dans une grande hâte.» Sans doute avait-on été pressé: compte tenu des complexités de la cérémonie de cour et de la production artistique au début du XVIIe siècle en Italie, monter un spectacle musical comme *L'Orfeo* en peu de temps était un exploit considérable.

*Orfeo* faisait référence à *Orfeo*, le premier opéra de Claudio Monteverdi, qui eut effectivement sa première le 24 février (il fut ensuite repris le 1er mars) sous la direction du compositeur. La représentation fut très réussie, comme nous le savons par plusieurs témoignages contemporains, et elle marqua un tournant dans l'histoire de l'opéra.

*Orfeo* fut initialement décrit comme une «pièce en musique» (*favola in musica*), ce qui est reconnaissable dans l'opéra à tous égards sauf le nom. L'opéra était un nouveau genre: l'idée de prendre un drame et de le jouer entièrement en musique n'avait émergé à Florence que dans les années 1590. Le précédent était classique, bien que l'impulsion fût moderne. Les Florentins cherchaient à faire revivre les principes du drame grec dans leur déclamation musicale, qu'ils appelaient *recitar cantando* («parler en chantant»). Le but était de créer une nouvelle sorte de discours musical qui exprimerait clairement et puissamment les émotions et les significations du texte.

# DIE ERSTE GROßE OPER

## Eine Einführung von Tim Carter

Am 23. Februar 1607 schrieb der Mantuaner Hofbeamte Carlo Magnone an seinen Bruder in Rom über ein bevorstehendes Werk, das in Mantua aufgeführt werden sollte. „Morgen abend hat der Durchlauchtigste Herr der Prinz [Ferdinando Gonzaga] befohlen, dass wir ein musikalisches Stück über die Geschichte von Orfeo aufführen, die ich verfasst habe", schreibt er. „Ich bin gespannt, wie es ausgeht, denn es ist in großer Eile gemacht worden." Zweifellos war es überstürzt worden: Angesichts der Komplexität der Hofzeremonien und der künstlerischen Produktion im Italien des frühen 17. Jahrhunderts war die Aufführung eines musikalischen Spektakels wie *L'Orfeo* kurzfristig eine bedeutende Leistung.

Orfeo bezog sich auf *Orfeo*, die erste Oper von Claudio Monteverdi, die tatsächlich ihre Uraufführung am 24. Februar hatte (sie wurde dann am 1. März wiederholt) unter der Leitung des Komponisten. Die Aufführung war sehr erfolgreich, wie wir aus mehreren zeitgenössischen Berichten wissen, und sie markierte einen Wendepunkt in der Geschichte der Oper.

*Orfeo* wurde ursprünglich als ein „musikalisches Stück" (*favola in musica*) bezeichnet, was in der Oper in allem außer dem Namen erkennbar ist. Die Oper war ein neues Genre: die Idee, ein Drama zu nehmen und es vollständig in Musik aufzuführen, war erst in den 1590er Jahren in Florenz entstanden. Das Vorbild war klassisch, obwohl der Impuls modern war. Die Florentiner versuchten, die Prinzipien des griechischen Dramas in ihrer musikalischen Deklamation wiederzubeleben, die sie *recitar cantando* („singend sprechen") nannten. Das Ziel war es, eine neue Art von musikalischer Rede zu schaffen, die die Emotionen und Bedeutungen des Textes klar und kraftvoll ausdrücken würde.

## LIBRETTO

### PROLOGO

**La Musica**
Dal mio Permesso amato
io la Musica son, ch'a' dolci accenti
sò far tranquillo ogni turbato core,
et hor di nobil ira, et hor d'amore
poss'infiammar le più gelate menti.

### ATTO PRIMO

**Pastore**
In questo lieto e fortunato giorno,
ch'agguaglia il Ciel alla terrena sede,
e fa beato il sol d'una beltade,
cui riverente il mondo onora e cole.

**Choro**
Vieni Imeneo, deh vieni,
e la tua face accendi,
onde s'abbruci e splenda
del nostro eroe nel petto
il glorioso affetto.

**Ninfa**
Muse honor di Parnaso,
che d'Elicona in cima
fate risonare il monte,
e di Pindo la vetta,
lasciate i monti, e qui venite a schiera.

**Choro**
Lasciate i monti, lasciate i monti,
e qui venite a schiera,
a far di canti e di suoni festa.

### ATTO SECONDO

**Orfeo**
Ecco pur ch'a voi ritorno,
care selve, e care piante,
e tu, mio fido colle,
che mi vedesti lieto,
hor mi rimiri mesto.

**Pastore**
Mira, ch'a se n'alletta
la nova sposa, e invita
a i dolci baci, e a le soavi carezze.

**Orfeo**
Vi ricorda, ò boschi ombrosi,
de' miei lunghi sospiri?
Mirate hor come è lieto
il mio cor, come è pago
il mio desio, come è felice l'alma.

### ATTO TERZO

**Speranza**
Ecco l'altra palude,
ove convien che tu per te stesso varchi.
Quinci innanzi mort' à queste rive
non s'udi voce, ò di vivente, ò d'ombra.

**Caronte**
O tu ch'innanzi mort' à queste rive
ardisci di venir, fermati, e torna
indietro, s'hai pur cara la vita.

**Orfeo**
Possente Spirto e formidabil Nume,
io son Orfeo, che l'Euridice mia
ritornar cerco al mondo.

### ATTO QUARTO

**Proserpina**
Signor, quell'infelice
che con tanta pietate
Orfeo chiede a te, è la sua Euridice.

**Plutone**
Benche severo e immutabil fato
ne stringa, e ne costringa
ad esser rei, pur vincesti, o bella
pietade.

### ATTO QUINTO

**Orfeo**
Questi i campi di Tracia,
ove soleano i miei dolci lamenti
esser uditi da le fere, e da le piante?

**Apollo**
Perch'a lo sdegno e al dolor impreda
sei, figlio mio? Deh, vieni meco in Cielo,
dove in stelle d'amor t'accenderai.
