# Francesco Cavalli
## L'Ormindo

### English Chamber Orchestra conducted by Raymond Leppard

Francesco Cavalli was the leading composer of opera in Venice in the middle of the seventeenth century, and a pupil of Claudio Monteverdi, and he dominated the operatic scene there for more than forty years. *L'Ormindo* was composed in 1644, one of his earliest operas, and it tells a romantic story set among the royal court of Egypt, with two friendly pirates and a happy ending, combining comedy and romance in a most attractive combination. It's one of Cavalli's most charming operas, full of beautiful melody and attractive characterisation.

Raymond Leppard did more than any other musician of his generation to restore the neglected operas of the seventeenth-century Venetian school return to the stage, and this recording with the English Chamber Orchestra captures one of the successful products of this work, with an excellent cast led by Jennifer Wakefield, Peter Runge and Hugues Cuenod among others. This is one of the series of Decca recordings that Leppard made of these restored operas, and it's been remastered for this new release on double CD.

## 第二页 (scans/HIPPO_20220225_0002.jpg)

### The Story of the Opera

L'Ormindo tells the story of the son of the Sultan of Babylon, who has been shipwrecked and rescued, and falls in love with Erisa, the daughter of the King of Egypt. The king approves the match, but then the pirate captain Sicle is also in love with her, and there are a series of complications and misunderstandings before everything ends happily with the two young lovers united in marriage. Along the way there are plenty of comic moments from the pirates and the various courtiers, making this an opera that combines romance and comedy in the most delightful way.

It's one of Cavalli's earliest operas, composed when he was still in his early forties, and it already shows all the melodic invention and dramatic skill that made him so successful.

## The Artists

**Raymond Leppard** (1928-2019)

English conductor and scholar, who led the revival of seventeenth-century Venetian opera in the second half of the twentieth century, and did more than anyone to restore these operas to the stage and the recording studio. His performing editions of Cavalli and Monteverdi brought these wonderful works back to life.
