## Introduction

Tagliavini and Tassinari were two of the most appealing Italian singers of their day, and through stage performances and joint recitals, such as this one, recorded in San Francisco in 1949, they reached a wide South America.

In the case of this broadcast it is not only the fine vocalism that attracts, but also the varied choice of repertory; the Flowtow, Mascagni and Cilea excerpts are fine operatic 'standards', and finely performed they are too; the Mascagni reminds us that this illustrious couple sang in Wegner together at La Scala, Milan, and made a highly-praised complete recording; but most unusual are the extracts from Chopi, an opera first staged at the Teatro Lirico, Milan, in 1901. Tagliavini and Tassinari had a strong fondness for the piece, and how winningly they spin out Chopin's melodies, adapted for the purpose by Giacomo Oreffice (1865 - 1922). So little is heard today of his twelve operas that he is all but forgotten, but for over thirty years he played a significant part in the musical life of Milan. He was a pianist, respected teacher and music critic; no matter that the plot of Chopi bears little similarity to the composer's life; this music is infectious, yet casts a perplexing spell of d[dj makes you that "was that an envoi or a nocturne that they were singing in just now...?" No matter which; let the familiar melodies delight you in their unfamiliar guise!

As for the better known items, Tagliavini's honeyed tones serve M'appari well, but he is at his most exquisite in the two Massenet extracts. The vocal velvet is never forced, always pure: what a moving revelation is the aria from Le Cid; Tagliavini brings new light and shade to this familiar piece. Surely it could not be more beautifully sung.

Tassinari's contributions are equally rewarding. Voi lo sapete is infused with all the drama that she would have brought to a stage performance, but most touching is the aria from Act I of Adriana Lecouvreur - happily we have been given this on disc today.

## Pia Tassinari

Pia Tassinari, a native of Modigliana in Italy, was born in September 1903 and after studying in Bologna and Milan, made her debut as Mimi (Puccini's *Boheme*) in 1929. Her career at La Scala began in 1932 when she sang in La Scala began in 1932 when she favored from del re, and four years later she appeared there composed La forza amorosa by Zandonai. Tassinari continued to sing at La Scala for another twenty years, by which time her vocal range could encompass several mezzo roles, while some of the parts in which she had previously enjoyed success as a soprano proved rather too testing for her.

After the second world war Tassinari sang frequently with her husband, the tenor Ferruccio Tagliavini, and they appeared together in South America and at the Met in New York (Götzfried and Tosca). ## Ferruccio Tagliavini

Ferruccio Tagliavini was born in Reggio Emilia, Italy in August 1913. His initial training was as an engineer, but he soon decided that music was to be his career; after became a student at the Parma Conservatory; after winning the Concorso Nazionale Italiano he studied with the tenor Amadeo Bassi in Florence. His operatic stage debut took place in Florence with the Teatro Comunale in 1938 through Italy, including Rome, Trieste and Palermo.

Tagliavini was soon acknowledged as one of Italy's finest tenors and in 1946 appeared for the first time in South America and in 1946 he made his debut at the Metropolitan, New York (Rodolfo again); he sang nine seasons there, specializing in lyric roles by Donizetti, Verdi and Puccini. Covent Garden first heard Tagliavini during La Scala's now legendary 1950 visit (Nemorino in L'elisir d'amore), returning in 1955/6 for performances of *Tosca*; he was last heard in London in 1959 when he sang at the Adelphi Theatre and retired from the operatic stage in 1966. By the late 1960s Tagliavini's honeyed tones had deteriorated somewhat but he continued singing in occasional concerts until 1981.

In addition to his stage work, Tagliavini enjoyed a career in films, usually with some musical content, and these brought his talents to a different but equally enthusiastic public. During his brilliant career he established himself as one of the great tenors of the twentieth century - a member of the distinguished line that links Caruso, Gigli, Schipa, Björling, di Stefano. Tagliavini died in 1995.

## Gaetano Merola

The Italian conductor Gaetano Merola was born in Naples in 1881. He worked at the Met and elsewhere in New York until, in 1923, he conducted the San Carlo Opera in New York until he founded the San Francisco Opera which, under his guidance, was to become one of America's foremost companies, and of which he was General Director for thirty years. An imaginative conductor of considerable skill, Merola died in 1953.

## Producer's Note

This broadcast was the American debut of the excerpts from Orefice's obscure four-act opera, *Chopin* (Teatro Lirico, Milan, 25th November 1901). These excerpts were undoubtedly inspired by Tassinari's and Tagliavini's performances of the opera at the Teatro Municipal, Rio de Janeiro, on 13th and 15th September 1949. Pianist Karl Kritz was the Associate Conductor of the San Francisco Opera Orchestra. The flute solo in the Chopin "Entr'acte" is not credited, but was probably played by Murray Gratzter, first flautist of the San Francisco Opera Orchestra from 1948 to 1957. Note also that Tagliavini sings the *Le Cid* aria down a half-step from score pitch.
