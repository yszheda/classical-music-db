## About Aureliano Pertile

Aureliano Pertile (1887-1952) was one of the greatest Italian tenors of the 20th century. He made his debut in 1911 and quickly established himself as a leading interpreter of Verdi and verismo repertoire. He was a regular performer at La Scala in Milan, the Metropolitan Opera in New York, and other major opera houses around the world. His recordings, though mostly made during the acoustic era and early electrical era, demonstrate a voice of remarkable beauty, power, and dramatic intensity.

These early acoustic recordings, made between 1923 and 1925, capture Pertile's voice in its youthful prime. The acoustic recording process, which required singers to project directly into a large horn without electronic amplification, placed great demands on the voice. Pertile's success in this medium is a testament to his exceptional vocal technique and natural gifts.

The recordings include arias from many of the great tenor roles in the Italian repertoire, from the bel canto of Donizetti to the verismo of Puccini and Giordano. They offer a fascinating glimpse into the artistry of one of Italy's finest tenors during the early years of his recording career.

## About Acoustic Recordings

Acoustic recording was the dominant method of sound recording before the introduction of electrical recording in 1925. In this process, sound waves were collected by a large horn and directed onto a diaphragm, which vibrated and drove a stylus that cut grooves into a rotating wax disc. There was no electronic amplification; the singer had to project directly into the horn with sufficient volume and clarity to drive the cutting mechanism.

This method placed great demands on singers, particularly in terms of projection and clarity of diction. Voices that recorded well in the acoustic era tended to have particular qualities of focus and brilliance. The resulting recordings, while limited in frequency range compared to modern recordings, often possess a remarkable presence and immediacy.

The CEDAR Sound System, used in the remastering of these recordings, represents the state of the art in digital audio restoration. It can remove noise, clicks, and other artifacts while preserving the essential character of the original performance. These remastered versions offer the best possible sound quality while maintaining the historical authenticity of these important documents of operatic history.
