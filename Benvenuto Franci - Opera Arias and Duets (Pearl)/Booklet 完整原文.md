## BENVENUTO FRANCI

Franci was one of the foremost baritones of the century, his voice a big, bold instrument, firm and refined, in a great tradition no longer with us. His recordings from the 1920s display quite remarkable presence and fidelity for the time.

## BENVENUTO FRANCI

If you visited La Scala during the 1920s, you might be lucky enough to see and hear Pertile and Franci in harness under Toscanini's baton. The tenor and baritone combination was a powerful one both vocally and dramatically – as can be heard here in the duets from *William Tell* and *Aida*. Both possessed strong, vibrant voices of the kind obviously appreciated then (later examples are Jan Peerce [GEMM CD 9297], Ramón Vinay and Giuseppe Valdengo). Both were also imposing presences on the stage. Contemporary evidence of Franci's appearances at Covent Garden as Scarpia and Don Alvaro confirms these facts.

Franci's voice was a big, bold instrument that its owner used unflinchingly to forceful effect. While capable of a firm legato and some refinement of style, as all the Verdi discs here tell us, he was not a particularly subtle artist – but, like other baritones of his generation, such as Apollo Granforte, Mario Basiola and Cesare Formichi, he had the rudiments of technique and tradition firmly etched in him, his case by the great Cotogni (who also taught Bassola), a technique and tradition carried on in recent times by Piero Cappuccilli and Giorgio Zancanaro, few others. Italy just doesn't seem to produce voices of this calibre any more, so we listen to Franci with greater awe that his contemporaries may have done.

Franci was born near Siena in 1891 and studied at the Santa Cecilia Academy in Rome. After a concert there in 1916, he was pronounced 'an excellent baritone with an admirable voice'. He made his stage debut at the Teatro Costanzi in Rome as Gianotto in Mascagni's *Lodeletta*. The early part of his career in Italy was spent essaying several unusual or new roles. Glazunov's notable Italian connoisseur of the voice Rodolfo Celletti described Franci's singing as 'exuberant' and that's how it comes across in both these pieces.

Drawbacks? At the top the tone can occasionally sound too vibrant and slightly loose focus. Then there isn't always the differentiation of character that you may find in baritones less generously endowed by nature, such as De Luca and Gobbi. But Franci's compensatory virtues are enough to place him among the forefront of the century's baritones.

**ALAN BLYTH**

### Producer's note

Many of Franci's records made for The Gramophone Company (HMV) exhibit a fidelity and presence quite remarkable for the time. However, this presence was occasionally at the expense of a suspicion of blasting. Franci was a powerful baritone so it is not surprising that microphone overload was a problem when recording him. That may well have been a factor in HMV's decision to issue several of his records in contemporary abridged form. Two of these are given here: *Una fatale* (Track 12) and the earlier version of *Gran Dio* (Track 18). The result we hear then is the best that early pick-ups could manage. In the case of *Una fatale*, the transfer is fairly successful until the last third when it loses clarity. *Gran Dio* is less good in parts and required much use of technology in the transfer process. For this CD, it was replaced soon after by a fresh-sounding version as a direct pressing (Track 7). A performance however it is somewhat different from the later version and its rarity justifies its inclusion.
