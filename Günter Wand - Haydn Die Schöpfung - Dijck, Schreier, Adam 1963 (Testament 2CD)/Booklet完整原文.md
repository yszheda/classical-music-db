### Günter Wand (1912-2002)

Günter Wand was one of the greatest German conductors of the second half of the twentieth century, particularly celebrated for his interpretations of the German classical and romantic repertoire - Haydn, Mozart, Beethoven, Schubert, Brahms, Bruckner, Sibelius. He was born in Allenstein (now Olsztyn in Poland) in 1912, studied in Cologne with Hermann Abendroth, and began his conducting career in 1930s. He was General Music Director in Cologne after the war, then chief conductor of the North German Radio Symphony Orchestra in Hamburg from 1948 until 1974, and later chief conductor of the Bamberg Symphony. He continued conducting into his late eighties, making many celebrated recordings. His interpretations are celebrated for their extraordinary clarity, structural insight, rhythmic intensity and spiritual depth. He remained active almost until his death in 2002.

This recording of Haydn's *Die Schöpfung* (The Creation) was made in 1963 for North German Radio, with the North German Radio Symphony Orchestra and a distinguished cast of soloists including Peter Schreier as Uriel, Hermann van der Dijck as Raphael/Adam, and Marga Höffgen as Gabriel/Eve. It is one of the most celebrated performances of this work from the 1960s, capturing Günter Wand's characteristic clarity and structural vision of Haydn's great oratorio. It is being issued here for the first time on CD by Testament, from the original radio tapes.

## Page 7 (HIPPO_20241219_0007.jpg)

### Joseph Haydn: Die Schöpfung

Joseph Haydn wrote *Die Schöpfung* (The Creation) between 1796 and 1798, after his two great visits to London. It's an oratorio on the subject of the creation of the world, from the Book of Genesis, with a text by Baron Gottfried van Swieten. It's one of Haydn's most beloved and popular works, one of the greatest works of choral music in the classical repertoire. The work begins with the famous representation of Chaos, depicting the void before creation, then moves through the six days of creation, culminating in the creation of Adam and Eve and the final hymn of praise to the Creator. It's a work that combines classical form with extraordinary musical inspiration, and the final chorus is one of the most magnificent in all choral literature.

Günter Wand conducting the North German Radio Symphony Orchestra and Chorus in this 1963 recording from the NDR archives, issued for the first time on CD by Testament in 2001.
