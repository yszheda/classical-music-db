## Back cover

Alexander Goldenweiser (1875-1961) was one of the most important Russian pianists and teachers of the twentieth century. His students included such legends as Sviatoslav Richter, Emil Gilels, Grigory Sokolov, and many others. But as a composer, he has been largely forgotten. This is the first volume of his complete piano music, performed by the British pianist Jonathan Powell, who has rediscovered this fascinating repertoire.

## Page 1 - Introduction by Nigel Burton

Alexander Goldenweiser was born in 1875 into a wealthy Jewish family in Kishinev, Bessarabia (now Chisinau, Moldova). He studied at the Moscow Conservatory with Nikolai Zverev and Sergei Taneyev, and graduated in 1895 winning the gold medal for piano performance. He quickly established himself as one of the leading pianists in Moscow, and began teaching at the Moscow Conservatory in 1906, where he remained until his retirement in 1950.

Goldenweiser was famously the teacher of Sviatoslav Richter, Emil Gilels, Grigory Sokolov, Nikolai Petrov, and many other leading Russian pianists. His teaching was legendary for its clarity, intelligence, and deep understanding of the composer's intentions. But his work as a composer has been completely overshadowed by his career as a performer and teacher. This recording aims to restore his piano music to the repertoire.

## Page 2 - About the Composer

Goldenweiser began composing seriously while still a student, and continued composing throughout his long life. His style is rooted in the Russian romantic tradition of Tchaikovsky, Rimsky-Korsakov and Rachmaninoff, but with a contrapuntal skill that he had learned from Taneyev. His music is characterized by rich harmonies, flowing melodies, and a deep sense of lyricism. It is squarely within the mainstream of Russian romantic piano writing, but with an individuality of voice that deserves to be better known.

This first volume of his complete piano music covers the period from 1903 to 1920, including his early major works culminating in the substantial Piano Sonata in F minor of 1906.

## Page 3 - Notes on the Works

### Improvisation (1903)

This was one of Goldenweiser's earliest published piano works, a short but atmospheric piece that shows his already confident command of the piano and his harmonic imagination. It sets the mood for what is to follow: a lyrical romantic piece with rich keyboard writing.

### Three Preludes (1904)

Three short character pieces, each exploring different moods: the first is gentle and flowing, the second is more passionate, the third is quiet and introspective. Already we can hear the influence of Rachmaninoff in the rich harmonies and lyrical melodies.

### Six Variations on a Theme by Glinka (1905)

This set of variations on a theme from Glinka's opera *A Life for the Tsar* shows Goldenweiser's contrapuntal skill learned from Taneyev. Each variation explores different aspects of the theme, from gentle lyricism to brilliant virtuosity. The variations build to a powerful climax, showing the composer's growing confidence.

---

## Page 4

### Piano Sonata in F minor (1906)

This is Goldenweiser's largest single work for piano up to this point, a substantial three-movement sonata that shows his mastery of large-scale form.

**First movement: Allegro con fuoco** - Dramatic and passionate, with a powerful main theme and a more lyrical second theme. The development section shows Goldenweiser's contrapuntal skill, and the movement builds to a dramatic climax.

**Second movement: Andante tranquillo** - A slow, lyrical movement with a beautiful flowing melody, showing the composer's gift for quiet, introspective lyricism. It is the heart of the sonata, providing a perfect contrast to the outer movements.

**Third movement: Finale - Allegro molto** - A brilliant, energetic finale that brings the sonata to a powerful conclusion. It shows the composer's virtuosic writing for the piano and brings the work to a dramatic close.

---

## Page 5

### Two Elegies (1910, 1912)

Two short elegiac pieces, written in memory of friends. The first is more flowing and melancholy, the second is darker and more intense. They show the composer's emotional depth and his ability to express deep feeling in compact forms.

### Etude in F major (1914)

A concert etude in the tradition of Chopin and Liszt, combining technical brilliance with melodic charm. It is a sparkling piece that shows the composer's understanding of the piano and his ability to write brilliant keyboard music.

### Three Lyric Pieces (1915)

Three short character pieces in the tradition of Russian lyrical piano writing:
- I. Andante melancholico - Melancholy and introspective
- II. Allegro grazioso - Light and graceful
- III. Andante cantabile - Broadly lyrical and songful

## Page 6

### Six Preludes (1920)

The last work on this disc, six short preludes that show Goldenweiser at his most mature. Each prelude is a small character piece exploring different moods and textures, from the gentle opening through more energetic middle pieces to the quiet, thoughtful closing prelude. They form a perfect conclusion to this first volume, showing the composer's mastery of the miniature form.

---

## Page 7 - Alexander Goldenweiser (1875-1961)

Goldenweiser was not only a composer and teacher, he was also a close friend and confidant of many leading Russian cultural figures, including Leo Tolstoy - he was a regular visitor to Tolstoy's home at Yasnaya Polyana, and kept a famous diary of his conversations with Tolstoy that remains an important historical document. He was also a close friend of Rachmaninoff, Scriabin, and many other leading Russian composers.

As a teacher at the Moscow Conservatory for over forty years, he taught generations of leading Russian pianists. His influence on Russian piano playing in the twentieth century cannot be overestimated. It is fitting that his piano music is now being recorded for the first time, allowing us to hear his own creative voice.

---

## Page 8 - Jonathan Powell

**Jonathan Powell** (born 1969) is a British pianist who has specialised in the Russian and Soviet piano repertoire of the nineteenth and twentieth centuries. He has given many world premiere recordings of forgotten works by Russian composers, and has won particular acclaim for his recordings of music by Nikolai Roslavets, Alexander Mosolov, and other composers of the Russian avant-garde. He has performed throughout Europe, the United States, and Japan, and is recognised as one of the leading exponents of forgotten Russian piano music.

This recording of Goldenweiser's complete piano music is the result of many years of research and preparation, and Jonathan Powell is the perfect pianist to bring this fascinating repertoire back to life.
