## SIX VERISMO SOPRANOS - SIX STUDIES IN PASSION, TECHNIQUE, AND STYLE

Opera lovers have always looked upon Italy as the birthplace of star singers. Throughout the eighteenth century this was true, but with the coming of the French Revolution and a wave of new ideas there were some curious developments. Rossini lamented that the "French screetch" had been imported into Italy, and when Adolphe Nourrit visited him in Milan in 1838, he told the great tenor that "You will see for yourself that there is no longer a talent in Italy. It is now no longer a matter of doing roulades, and the facility of a Rubini would not stand firm in a good broad aria! What is wanted is expression and declamation." At La Scala around 1900, the socialites who "paid the piper" wanted only modern operas with plenty of "expression and declamation." Singers were expected to have big and beautiful voices, strongly marked personalities, and to be able to act convincingly. (Dear reader, it is not true that acting in opera began with Maria Callas.)

The sopranos featured in this album were among the leading singers in Italy; significantly, none of them scored successes in either London or New York. Gemma Bellincioni was limited to fewer roles; where she was admired only for her acting; Amelia Pinto and Rosina Storchio went to America but made no impression in a handful of performances each. Today we are prepared to give them another chance, listening to their artistry fully developed, to hear if we attained to their place in vocal history.

### GEMMA BELLINCIONI

She was born in Monza on 17 August 1861, the daughter of the comic book Cesare Bellincioni and the contralto Carlotta Scodeller. At the age of six she appeared on Friday, 4 October 1870, at the Teatro Filodrammatico Milanese in a sketch by Alessandro Salvini: Riccobon sé and set, in which she sang a song written for her by Luigi Ricci. Gemma and her sister Saffo were taught singing by their mother. Gemma also by the soprano Luigia Piatti Bellincioni and the baritone Giovanni Corsi. (Saffo, a contralto, enjoyed a long career, singing often with her famous sister, latterly under the name Frigioni.) According to Rodolfo Celletti's researches, Gemma made her operatic debut, when not quite 15 years old, in May 1879 at the Teatro della Società Filarmonica, Naples: Il segreto della Duchessa by Giuseppe Del'Orrefice.

In 1882 she visited Vigo, Malaga, Cadiz, and Granada with the great tenor Enrico Tamberlick, singing in Rigoletto, I puritani, Faust, Othello (Rossini), Rigoletto, Ernani, Linda di Chamounix, Il barbiere di Siviglia, and others. On 30 June 1883 she came to Lisbon with a contract for what the called the "Opera nova" in Rossio: Rigoletto, Gli Ugonotti, L'Africana, and Il profeta (also Philippe in Rigoletto), which offered little scope for dramatic interpretation. Her next debut role, Isabella in Roberto il diavolo, she describes as "a pathetic, weeping Princess as damp as a November rain." In San Sebastián in 1884 she triumphed in Lucia di Lammermoor, La sonnambula, I Capuleti ed i Montecchi, and La traviata.

In 1886-1887 she sang in Barcelona in Hamlet and La traviata (with Angelo Masini), and in Rossini's Otello in Budapest. In 1889 she sang Carmen and Luceria in Ferrara, Bucharest, and returned to Barcelona to sing Lohengrin in 1891.

On-board ship for Buenos Aires, Montevideo, and La Plata in 1886 she fell in love with the great tenor Roberto Stagno (1840-1897). Flattered by the adulation of the pretty young girl who deliberately prolonged her ocean sickness to appear pale and interesting, the 45 year old man doubtless returned her love. They sang together in Roberto il diavolo, Rigoletto, Mefistofele, La Gioconda, Fanta, Il barbiere, and Lohengrin. They were together for the rest of his life, although they never married.

In 1887 they returned to La Plata and Buenos Aires and sang in Romeo et Juliette, La Juive, Les Huguenots, La Gioconda, Der fliegende Holländer and the last act of Rossini's Otello. Their daughter, Bianca Bellincioni Stagno, who enjoyed a considerable career both as an opera singer and a film actress, was born in Budapest in January 1888.

In 1890 the happy couple appeared at the Teatro Real, Madrid, in the title role of Guglielmo Ratcliff. Stagno also sang some performances of Il barbiere di Siviglia, but so did Gemma.

Eighteen hundred ninety was Bellincioni's crucial year. Eduardo Sonzogno invited her and Stagno to sing, in the Teatro Costanzi, the leading roles in the three prize-winning one-act operas from his competition. Both singers, with the conductor Leopoldo Mugnone, plunged enthusiastically into a study of the words and music of Cavalleria rusticana. The premiere took place on 17 May 1890.

Gemma sang in all of the later revivals of performances of Iris (1899) at home and abroad.

### ANGELICA PANDOLFINI

Angelica, one of two young children of the baritone Francesco Pandolfini, was born in Spoleto in 1871. She went to Paris to complete her training and then made her debut at the age of 16 at the Folies-Dramatiques in 1887. In 1888 she appeared at the Teatro Argentina, Rome, the Teatro Pagliano, Florence, the Teatro Sociale, Como, the Teatro Comunale, Bologna, and the Teatro Rossini, Pesaro. In 1889 she sang in Otello (Scala 1887) and in 1898 at the San Carlo, Naples. In 1904 she sang Madama Butterfly at Alessandria and Cairo, and in 1905 at the Teatro Dal Verme, Milano. In 1907 she sang five performances of La traviata at the Costanzi, Rome. In Madrid in 1908 she sang Mefistofele with Solomiya Krusheynska and De Lucia. The last additions to her repertoire were Gluck's Iphigénie and Cilea's Gloria, which she sang at the Politeama Genovese in 1908. Among her other roles were Sulamita in La Bataillère, Anna in Le Villi, Charlotte in Werther, Iris, and Massenet's Sapho. In 1909 she married and retired from the stage.

The five Pandolfini records are among the most highly sought after rarities because the singer herself did not like them and these were withdrawn soon after publication. Now that we can hear them all together for the first time, they seem remarkably beautiful and well recorded for their day.

### AMELIA PINTO

The life and career of Amelia Pinto are well documented thanks to the researches of Dr. Nino Insinga. She was born in Palermo on 31 January 1876, the daughter of a fencing master. In 1898 she went to the Accademia di Santa Cecilia, Rome, to study singing. She made a successful debut at La Gioconda in Brescia on 28 December 1899. On 29 December 1900 she opened the Scala season as Iolde in Wagner's first performances in Milan of Tristano e Isolda, with Giuseppe Borgatti under Toscanini conducting.

At the Teatro de Opera, Buenos Aires, in 1901 she sang with Caruso again in La Regina di Saba; she also appeared in Otello, Lohengrin, and Madame Lutino in Patrica. In 1902 Pinto made her only visit to the United States, as a member of the Mascagni Opera Company. In December 1902 she was in Lisbon for La Gioconda, Tannhäuser, Germana, L'Elisir d'amore, La Juive. In October 1903 she went to Warsaw for Cavalleria rusticana. She appeared in Madrid in L'Africaine and in La Gioconda. She returned to the Teatro Costanzi in January 1906. In May 1906 she sang in Santiago, Chile. In December 1906 came her return to Trieste. She triumphed again as Isotta at the San Carlo, Naples in December 1907.

She reappeared as Isotta at La Scala on 12 March 1914, repeating the role in Genoa in December. In January 1916 she accepted an engagement in Madrid. Soon after these performances her father and her daughter Rosa Isotta died, and Amelia Pinto never sang in the theater again.

The 1902 Milani 7 G & T reveal an important voice, rich and voluminous in tone, with splendid high notes, a solid medium register, and a properly deep-toned chest register.

### CESIRA FERRANI

Cesira Ferrani (née Zanuzio) was born on 8 May 1863 in Turin, where she studied with the soprano Maddalena Frasi. In 1887 Ferrani made her debut at the Teatro Regio, Turin, as Micaëla in Carmen. In 1892 she sang Ernani in Catania. She created the role of Mimì in Puccini's Manon Lescaut at the Teatro Regio, Turin, on 1 February 1893, and again in the world premiere of La bohème at the Teatro Regio, Turin, on 1 February 1896. She also sang in the first performance of Tosca at the Teatro Costanzi, Rome, on 14 January 1900. She died on 6 May 1943.

On records we hear a likeable, well-trained singer, her voice forward, the tone fresh, the diction limpid.

### EMMA CARELLI

This important soprano was born in Naples on 12 May 1877, the daughter of Beniamino Carelli, one of the last great singing teachers of the Neapolitan school. Having studied with her father, Emma made her debut on 17 September 1893 in Mercadante's La vestale for the inauguration of the Teatro Mercadante, Altamura. She appeared as La Tosca in Milan at the Teatro Lirico, Milan. Her first triumphs in Fedora took place in Mantua and Bologna in 1900.

In 1898 she married Victor Mocchi, a self-made millionaire and later one of the founders of the Italian Communist Party. In 1911 he persuaded his wife to undertake the direction of the Teatro Costanzi, which she did triumphantly until 1926. Other singers who owed much to her included Tito Schipa, Carlo Galeffi, Luigi Montesanto, Rosa Raisa, and Bida Sayão.

Pietro and Carelli—she had many admirers of her evident reaction to any kind of art. She famously declared: "In my life I have had three passions: Walter Mocchi in the Teatro Costanzi, and this carved car which some time or another is going to crush me"—which it did, on 17 August 1928.

### ROSINA STORCHIO

Rosina Storchio was born in Venice on 19 May 1876, and died in Milan on 24 July 1945. She studied at the Conservatorio di Santa Cecilia and later with Alberto Giovannini and Giuseppe Fatuo. She made her debut at the Teatro Dal Verme, Milan, as Micaëla in Carmen in October 1893. At La Scala she starred in the first performance of Siberia on 19 December 1903.

### GIOVANNI ZENATELLO

Zenatello was born in Verona on 22 February 1876 and died in New York on 11 February 1949. He studied with Zanonni in Verona and later with Mantelli in Milan. He made his official debut at La Scala in 1906 as Cavaradossi in Tosca.

### GIUSEPPE DE LUCA

Like most baritones of the day, Giuseppe De Luca was born into a poor family in Rome, on Christmas Day 1876, and died, loved, and indeed revered, in New York on 26 August 1950. He studied with Battistini's teacher, Venceslao Persichini, at the Conservatorio di Santa Cecilia. He made his debut on 6 November 1897 as Valentino in Faust at the Teatro Politeama, Piacenza.

### ANTONIO PINI-CORSI

Antonio Pini-Corsi was born on 12 June 1859 and died in Milan on 22 April 1918. He came from a family of singers, and studied with his uncle, Achille Corsi. He made his debut in Cenerentola in 1878 as Dandini in La Cenerentola. Among the various operas in which he sang bass or baritone parts were La traviata, Labòe, Lohengrin, Hansel, Iocantès, La marionette, Pierrot, Lucia di Lammermoor, Cavalleria rusticana, I maestri cantori di Norimberga (Beckmesser), and Werther. His unrivalled comic creations included Don Giovanni, Figaro, Le donne curiose, and Il barbiere di Siviglia.

## THE SIBERIA RECORDS
### Story of the Opera

**Act One.** Vassili Ivanoff, a young soldier, meets and falls in love with the mysterious Stephana (Soprano). After a rendezvous with her that has promised itself all through the night, he goes to visit his aunt, Nikora, at the house where she has been a maid, and discovers that Stephana is the mistress of the house. Walter, rich, the rich officer who keeps Stephana in luxury, is playing cards in the next room, but hears the sudden commotion and asks who the young man is. Stephana boldly replies that he is her lover. Both men draw their swords, and Walter's friends stand for the police; in the street below Vassili's regiment is marching off to war against the Turks, but he realizes that his military career is over.

**Act Two.** At a halting place on the route that the exiled convicts must take on their long walk to Siberia. Stephana arrives in a carriage; she has given all her riches, the wages of sin, to the poor so that she may accompany Vassili to Siberia.

**Act Three.** It is Easter Saturday; Vassili is undergoing forced labor in the mines, and is kept in the fortified camp where the prisoners live. One of them offers Stephana an escape route: they can go away through a disused well while everyone is distracted by the sacred drama that is going to be acted that night. Stephana refuses: she will not leave Vassili. Among the prisoners is Stephana's old acquaintance, Gléby. The new governor then appears: it is Walitsin. He is kind to Stephana and tries to persuade her to return to his house, but in vain. Gléby taunts Vassili with Stephana's past but when she reveals that Gléby had been her first lover and had then sold her to Walitsin; the other prisoners mock him. Stephana and Vassili are now eager to escape, but Gléby overhears their plans and betrays them to the Governor. Walitsin tries to force Stephana to leave, but in despair at the idea of being separated from Vassili she stabs herself. As she dies, a human chain of new prisoners arrives. The sad and passionate drama of Stephana is over; the great and terrible drama of Siberia goes on.

While writing Andrea Chénier in Milan, Umberto Giordano, having lost the interest and financial support of the publisher Sonzogno, was forced to deep in a funeral parlor (his may well have furnished him with melancholic atmospheric feelings enough) to prepare a libretto for setting to music Luigi Illica's Siberia. Although Giordano failed to repeat the success of Andrea Chénier and Fedora, the opera was, for a while, widely performed and even entered the repertoire of the Paris Opéra, the leading roles pleasing Luciano Maréchal and Lina Cavalieri.

To record some selections from a new opera with the original cast, accompanied by Maestro Raffaello (dell) Ponti, who had arranged the vocal score of the opera, was a daring venture for G & T and might have led to many others of a similar nature if they had only chosen a better opera! The fabulous rarity of the original records indicates that very few copies were bought then.
