## Introduction

"If he gets his act together, he'll be greater than any of us", Jussi Björling is reputed to have said about Giuseppe Di Stefano when the latter made his debut at the Metropolitan Opera in New York. Whether or not Di Stefano ever "got his act together" is a debatable point, but certainly in his youthful vocal prime he was gifted with one of the most ravishing tenor voices ever to have hailed from Italy. His vocal talent was discovered when he began to sing spontaneously after winning at cards. He received his initial vocal training from the tenor Adriano Storchio. At the age of eighteen he started tuition with Luigi Montesanto in Milan and also he attended Mariano Stabile's master classes. When the war broke out he sang in vaudeville theatres and refugee camps under the name Nino Florio. Eventually he managed to escape to Switzerland and made recordings for Radio Lausanne. After the war he continued his studies briefly with Montesanto and then made his debut as Des Grieux in *Manon* on April 20, 1946 in Teatro Municipale di Reggio Emilia, opposite Mafalda Favero. (Reggio Emilia was to be the location of another notable debut. On April 29, 1961 Luciano Pavarotti sang his first role - Rodolfo in *Bohème*; the director of that production was Mafalda Favero). In 1947 he started his official recording career and made his La Scala debut, again as Des Grieux.

"I would give all of Bach's Brandenburg Concertos for Massenet's *Manon* and would think I had vastly profited by the exchange." Thus spake Sir Thomas Beecham, the famous British conductor who for many years was the sole champion of French Opera in a country that generally deemed the genre to be far too facile. *Manon* had its premiere at the Paris Opéra Comique on January 19, 1884. The number of performances: 500 in 1905, rose to 1000 by 1919 and 1500 in 1931. Subsequently Massenet's fame faded somewhat and for a long time *Manon* was virtually the only Massenet opera that kept his name alive. Meanwhile we have experienced what amounts to a Massenet boom and can make up our own minds as to whether *Manon* is really the culmination of Massenet's career or if there were not greater operas still to come.

Mafalda Favero was born on January 1, 1903 in Portomaggiore near Ferrara. She died on September 3, 1981 in Milan. She made her debut in Parma in 1927 liu. In 1928 Toscanini engaged Mafalda Favero for La Scala in 1928 to sing Eva in *Meistersinger* opposite Marcel Journet and Aureliano Pertile. In view of the small volume of her voice, it seems strange that she immediately ventured to sing Elsa. I eventually she became one of the great prima donne of La Scala. So highly was she regarded at La Scala that after the Second World War Toscanini chose her to sing Manon Lescaut in Act III of Puccini's opera on the occasion of the reopening of the newly built auditorium in 1946. Her career at La Scala continued uninterrupted until 1949. She continued giving recitals until the mid 1950s. Between 1937 and 1939 she was a member of the Metropolitan Opera Company, making her debut as Mimi. She recorded Margherita in Boito's *Faust* for Columbia and Adriana Lecouvreur for Columbia and Adriana Lecouvreur for Columbia.
