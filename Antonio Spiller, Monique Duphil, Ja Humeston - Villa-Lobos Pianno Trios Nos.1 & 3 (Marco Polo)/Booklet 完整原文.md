## 英文乐谱说明 (English Program Notes)

### Heitor Villa-Lobos (1887-1959)
### Piano Trio No. 1 in D Minor
### Piano Trio No. 3

The Brazilian composer Heitor Villa-Lobos was born in Rio de Janeiro in 1887. By the time of his death in 1959 he had long been established as the leading composer of his native country, the varied traditions of which had become the essence of his musical inspiration. As a child, Villa-Lobos studied the cello, taught by his father, who worked at the National Library. He was later to learn to play the guitar, and these two instruments assumed considerable importance in his later work as a composer. It had been intended that he should become a doctor, but the early death of his father, and his own interest in the popular music of the streets, drew him into the world of the Choro, a form of music current in the popular culture of Rio de Janeiro.

As a young man Villa-Lobos spent a number of years travelling in Brazil, engrossed in the study of the various forms of indigenous and imported music flourishing in its many different regions. At the same time, although lacking classical training, he wrote music, although his intermittent and serious attempts at formal musical study proved fruitless. As a composer he was, in fact, largely self-taught. Nevertheless he continued to compose and, in 1915, with a programme that included his first Piano Trio and second Violin Sonata, attracted some local interest, which grew during the following years, in spite of opposition in some quarters to a mixture of official acclaim and criticism.

In 1923, in good part through the influence of the pianist Artur Rubinstein, who had been impressed by his work, Villa-Lobos received financial support for a visit to Paris, where he was to spend the greater part of the next seven years, although the period brought visits to Africa and concerts of his music in Argentina and at home in Brazil. Paris, however, brought direct contact with the mainstream of contemporary music and association with leading musicians that was of great importance to him. Here he was much influenced by Ravel and established a friendship with Varèse, a musician with ideas that seem to have little in common. His music, meanwhile, proved very successful, appealing, no doubt, in its originality and exotic vigour.

With the new decade Villa-Lobos returned to Brazil, where he struggled to introduce contemporary music from Europe against considerable difficulties. In 1932, however, in the aftermath of the national revolution of Vargas, he assumed responsibility for national music education, occupying a position specially created for him and busying himself to a considerable extent with music for massed chorus and orchestra. In 1944 he went to the National Conservatory in Rio and in 1945 set up the Brazilian Academy of Music, an association of the most distinguished musicians of the country. By this time he was in good honours at home and abroad and a general reputation as the most important Brazilian composer of his generation.

Villa-Lobos's first Piano Trio was completed in 1911, in C minor, the work opens in its declared key in rapid unison, leading to a romantic theme, shifting in tonality, a prelude to the strongly lyrical melody that dominates much of the movement, in a style that suggests Fauré. The piano introduces the slow movement, followed by the violin, the cello entering to share a second theme, as the music slips between keys and textures. The third movement starts softly, at first with plucked strings and piano together, establishing the change of key from G minor to G major, although key and mood soon change, before a repetition of the scherzo theme. The final movement opens in fugal style, violin, cello and piano in turn an A flat subject, which immediately re-appears in accelerated form, leading to a romantic violin theme. A dramatic chord is interrupted by a passage of increased intensity, references to the fugal subject and a final return of the Rondo-Presto, capped in grandiose fashion.

The third of the four Piano Trios of Villa-Lobos was completed in 1918, following the fourth string quartet, the first two symphonies and the first Prole do Bebê piano suite that was to prove so popular. The new Piano Trio is a much more demanding work than the first, of greater tonal ambiguity, but with the same extravagance of melody and lyrical imagination, and a wider exploration of possible string effects. Much of the work is dominated by a theme of Brazilian provenance, first announced by the cello. The second movement offers a contrast in its simple opening texture, the violin introducing a gentle melody in an appealing fragmentary manner. A varied version of the melody is answered by a return to the theme of the first movement, interjected by piano and violin, and later to make a more emphatic return. The movement ends with the piano playing the first theme, accompanied by the chords of the muted strings, and a passage that exploits the possibilities of chordal harmonics on the cello. There is already a sense of the return of an idea first suggested at the start of the third movement, and duly makes its reappearance, either implied or fully stated, as the movement proceeds, eerily dominated by the artificial harmonics of the violin and a passage that demands the striking of the strings with the knobby tip of the bow. A coda played più forte concludes the movement and, therefore, the trio, the cello providing another treatment of the characteristic figure of the recurrent pentatonic theme and of passages of some stringency in music of varied originality that makes greater demands on the players than the earlier trios.

Villa-Lobos combines something of the thematic and rhythmic material with which he had become familiar by way of his interest in Brazilian folk techniques with which he was to become still more closely associated in Paris.

## 演奏家介绍 (About the Artists)

### Antonio Spiller

The Argentine-born violinist Antonio Spiller started to learn the violin at the age of seven and was an early winner of several important prizes in Buenos Aires, including an award from the National Broadcasting Company. As a young soloist he appeared with the Camerata Bariloche, one of the leading South American chamber ensembles, in the United States of America and in Europe. Spiller's development as a violinist was the result of study with his father, Lipko Spiller, and with Max Rostal. His love of chamber music led him to join the Joachim-Koeckert Quartet and to appear with the Munich String Trio at international festivals that included the Mozart Festival in Würzburg, the Engadiri Festival and the Regensburg String. He has undertaken concert tours in Europe and South America and to the Far East. Antonio Spiller is concertmaster of the Bavarian Radio Symphony Orchestra.

### Monique Duphil

Monique Duphil studied in Paris at the Conservatoire National Supérieur with Marguerite Long, Jean Doyen and Joseph Calvet. At the age of fifteen she won the Premier Prix in piano and made her Paris debut in the Salle Gaveau des Concerts du Conservatoire. She graduated with the Grand Prix in chamber music and the Prix de Virtuosité with Vladimir Horbowski and Harriet Wegner. After winning prizes in four international competitions, including the Warsaw Chopin Competition, she embarked on a career that has taken her to the major countries of Europe and the United States of America as a soloist with such orchestras as the Cleveland and Philadelphia Orchestras in the United States, with the Quebec Orchestra in Canada and with well-known orchestras in Central and South America. In Europe she has appeared as soloist with the Lamoureux Orchestra in Paris and in Warsaw, Bern and Munich. In Asia she has performed with the Tokyo Metropolitan Orchestra. She has appeared in New York, Hong Kong, Singapore, and in Australia in Sydney and Adelaide, as well as in New Zealand. Conductors she has worked with include André Jouve, Markevitch, Smetáček, Wislocki, Sanderling, Maag, Duroart, Schwarz and Mata.

Monique Duphil is highly regarded as a chamber music performer and has appeared with Spiller, Endres, Szeryng, Grumiaux, Tortelier, Karl Leister, Cho-Liang Lin, the Vienna String Quartet, the Salzburg Mozarteum Trio and the Melos Quartet, and in recitals with her husband, the cellist Jay Humeston.

### Jay Humeston

Jay Humeston, principal cellist of the Hong Kong Philharmonic Orchestra, is a graduate of the Curtis Institute of Music and the New England Conservatory. He studied with Leonard Rose, Mischa Schneider and Rudolf Kolisch, and in master-classes with Pablo Casals. After making his debut with the Philadelphia Orchestra at the age of seventeen, he has gone on to appear as soloist with orchestras in North and South America, Australia, Japan, Hong Kong and South East Asia, and has collaborated in chamber music recital with Isaac Stern, Young Uck Kim, Nobuko Imai and Yo-Yo Ma. For four seasons he participated in the Marlboro Music Festival and toured America, Europe and Israel with Music from Marlboro. With a Rockefeller Grant Jay Humeston performed for two years with Lukas Foss in the New York Carnegie Hall Evenings for New Music series, and as a founding member of the Boston Musica Viva recorded for Dalos Records in Aaskrian, written for him by the Pulitzer Prize winner Joseph Swantner.

With his wife, the pianist Monique Duphil, Jay Humeston has given recitals throughout North and South America, in Europe, Africa, Korea, Japan and South East Asia. The numerous radio recordings by the duo include performances for Radio New Zealand, the Australian Broadcasting Commission and Bayerischer Rundfunk.

## 法文版本 (French Version)

### Heitor Villa-Lobos (1887-1959)
### 1er Trio pour piano et en mineur
### 3me Trio pour piano

Le compositeur brésilien Heitor Villa-Lobos est né à Rio de Janeiro en 1887. À sa mort en 1959 il est depuis longtemps considéré comme le chef de file des compositeurs de son pays natal, dont les traditions variées ont été la source de son inspiration musicale.

Enfant, Villa-Lobos étudie le violoncelle sous la conduite de son père qui travaille à la Bibliothèque Nationale. Plus tard il apprend à jouer de la guitare; ces deux instruments vont prendre une importance considérable pour son futur travail de compositeur. On l'avait destiné à une carrière de médecin, mais la mort prématurée de son père, et son intérêt pour la musique populaire des rues l'entraînent dans le monde du choro, une forme courante de la musique dans la culture populaire de Rio de Janeiro. Jeune homme, Villa-Lobos passe de nombreuses années à voyager au Brésil, tout entier absorbé par l'étude des formes variées des musiques vocales et instrumentales qui fleurissent dans les multiples et différentes régions du pays. À la même époque, malgré son manque de formation technique en musique, il tient ses essais d'écriture musicale à l'écart. Mais tout le soin qu'il porte à la recherche d'une écriture musicale plus formelle se révèle infructueuse. En tant que compositeur, il est totalement autodidacte.

Cependant, un concert de ses oeuvres donné à Rio en 1915, au programme duquel sont inscrits son premier trio pour piano et la seconde sonate pour violon, retient une partie de l'attention locale. Cet intérêt, malgré l'opposition de certains milieux, va se développer au cours des années suivantes pour finalement recevoir la qualité de reconnaissance officielle.

En 1923, en grande partie par l'entremise du pianiste Artur Rubinstein impressionné par son oeuvre, Villa-Lobos reçoit une aide financière pour se rendre à Paris, où il s'établit jusqu'en 1930. Son séjour sera interrompu par des visites en Afrique et des concerts donnés en Argentine et chez lui au Brésil.

Paris lui apporte un contact direct avec le principal courant musical contemporain et lui permet de s'associer avec des musiciens qui seront pour lui d'une grande importance. Il est fort influencé par Ravel et entretient une amitié avec Varèse, un musicien avec lequel il semble partager peu de points communs. A cette époque, sa musique reçoit un grand succès par l'émotion qu'elle laisse transparaître dans son originalité et sa vigueur exotique.

Au cours de la décennie suivante Villa-Lobos retourne au Brésil où il est chargé d'introduire la musique européenne contemporaine, et ce, malgré de considérables difficultés, et en 1932, après la Révolution Nationale de Vargas, il lui est octroyé un poste spécialement créé pour lui, lui confiant la responsabilité de l'Education Nationale de Musique. Là, il se consacre avec ardeur à la production massive de musique pour choeurs et orchestres.

En 1942 Villa-Lobos crée un Conservatoire National à Rio et en 1945 il fonde l'Académie de Musique du Brésil qui rassemble les musiciens les plus renommés du Brésil. A sa mort, lui sont accordés les honneurs de son pays et de l'étranger; et il obtient la reconnaissance unanime d'être le plus important compositeur brésilien de sa génération.

Villa-Lobos écrit son premier trio pour piano en 1911, et le troisième des quatre trios pour piano est achevé en 1918, après le quatrième Quatuor à cordes, la première des deux Symphonies et la première Suite pour piano Prole do Bebê qui devait devenir si populaire.

*French version by Cécile Barthelemy*
