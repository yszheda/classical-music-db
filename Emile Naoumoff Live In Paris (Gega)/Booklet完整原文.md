# Emile Naoumoff: Live in Paris

This is the third CD in a series of live recordings by the Bulgarian-born pianist and composer Emile Naoumoff, issued by Gega New. The recital was given in December 2003 in the beautiful Salle Gaveau in Paris, and it captures the pianist in a typically interesting and varied programme that spans the whole of the romantic piano repertoire, from Beethoven to Liszt, with an original composition by Naoumoff himself added to the mix.

Emile Naoumoff was born in Sofia in 1962. He gave his first concert at the age of five, and gave his first full recital at the age of eight. He moved to France with his family when he was still a child, and he studied with the great pianist Shura Cherkassky, who recognised his extraordinary talent and guided his development. He quickly established an international career, and he has been performing throughout the world ever since, in recital and with orchestra, and he has also composed a large number of works, including piano pieces, chamber music and orchestral works.

Naoumoff is particularly noted for his imaginative programming and for his individual approach to the romantic piano repertoire. He has a particular affinity for the great works of the nineteenth century, and he brings to them a fresh perspective that combines respect for the score with spontaneous musicianship. This live recital captures all the excitement of his live performance, and shows the full range of his artistry as both pianist and composer.

# Programme Notes

**Beethoven: Piano Sonata No. 14 "Moonlight", Op. 27 No. 2**

The "Moonlight" Sonata is one of Beethoven's most popular piano sonatas, composed in 1801 and dedicated to his pupil Giulietta Guicciardi. The famous nickname was not given by Beethoven himself, but it has stuck ever since, and the work has never lost its popularity. The famous slow first movement with its broken chord accompaniment creates a mood of quiet contemplation that is deeply romantic, and this is followed by a graceful allegretto interlude before the passionate and turbulent presto agitato finale that brings the work to its dramatic conclusion.

**Brahms: Three Intermezzi Op. 117**

The three Intermezzi Op. 117 were composed in 1892, when Brahms was at the height of his late creative powers. They are three of the most beautiful of all Brahms' late piano pieces, intimate and reflective in mood, with that wonderful combination of harmonic richness and melodic beauty that characterises Brahms' late music. Each intermezzo has its own distinct character, from the gentle melancholy of the first to the restless passion of the second and the quiet resignation of the third.

**Schubert: Wanderer-Fantasie (D. 760)**

The Wanderer Fantasy was composed in 1822, and it is one of Schubert's most ambitious works for piano. It is based on the composer's own song "Der Wanderer", and the whole work is constructed from motifs derived from the song, played without a break in four connected sections. It was a very influential work, and it influenced the development of the symphonic poem and the piano concerto in the nineteenth century. It is a work of great passion and poetic intensity, and it has always been one of the great showpieces of the romantic piano repertoire.

**Emile Naoumoff: Variations sur un thème de Schubert**

The Variations on a Schubert Theme are Naoumoff's own composition, written specially for this recital. The theme is taken from Schubert, and Naoumoff constructs a series of variations that explore the theme in many different moods and characters, showing both his continuing connection with the romantic tradition that he loves so much, and his own individual voice as a composer.

**Liszt: Rhapsodie espagnole**

The Spanish Rhapsody is one of Liszt's most brilliant piano showpieces, composed in 1863. It is based on Spanish folk melodies and dance rhythms, and it requires tremendous technical prowess from the pianist. It brings this recital to a conclusion in a great burst of virtuosity and Spanish passion.

# Emile Naoumoff

Emile Naoumoff was born in Sofia, Bulgaria in 1962. He began playing the piano at the age of three, and gave his first public concert at the age of five. He gave his first complete piano recital at the age of eight. When he was twelve he moved to Paris with his family, and he became a student of the legendary American pianist Shura Cherkassky, who recognised his exceptional talent and nurtured it. He won the first prize at the Paris Conservatoire, and quickly established an international career.

He has performed throughout the world, in all the major concert halls, and he has worked with many of the leading conductors of our time. As well as his career as a concert pianist, he is also an active composer, and he has composed a wide variety of works, including piano music, chamber music, orchestral works and vocal music. He is particularly noted for his performances of the romantic repertoire, and his recordings have been widely praised by critics.
