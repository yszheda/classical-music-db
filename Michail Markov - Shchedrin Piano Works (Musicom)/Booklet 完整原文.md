## Liner Notes (English) — Pages 2-3, 10-17

### Rodion Shchedrin

"It is important that as a composer you believe in your intuition. You have to try out new things again and again. Nothing is forbidden. Time will pass its judgement." These words from Shchedrin in an interview on the occasion of his 70th birthday concisely describe in which way the composer has never wanted to conform to any rules throughout his life.

Although the greatest part of his creative period coincided with communist oppression, Shchedrin, born in Moscow in 1932, managed to retain his musical integrity and originality.

It is exactly why both in his native country and abroad he succeeded in being considered as one of the most important composers of the generation after Dmitri Shostakovich.

Shchedrin is the creator of an extensive and varied compository oeuvre, in which besides opera, religious choral works and ballets, pianoworks occupy a prominent position.

The effective way in which he makes the piano sound, indicates that Shchedrin is an outstanding pianist himself.

At first Shchedrin was not expected to make music his profession. His father, a violinist and secretary to Shostakovich, was denied at teaching the young Rodion piano, against any repudiation whatsoever. Shchedrin admitted in an interview mentioned.

When his father was appointed as a teacher at a Moscow choral school, Rodion, who had a good voice and perfect pitch, was sent to this school. As a student he had ample opportunity to hear all prominent Russian musicians perform. Among them were Dmitri Shostakovich, Sergei Prokofiev, Aram Khachaturian, Dmitri Kabalevsky, Sviatoslav Richter and Emil Gilels. This introduction evoked the young Rodion's fascination with music and as a result he still started studying piano. He was admitted at the Moscow Conservatory as a pupil of Yakov Flier, one of the greatest Russian pianists of those days. Shchedrin also studied composition with Yuri Shaporin. In an autobiographical outline, published for the booklet going with a cd on which he plays his own work, Shchedrin writes: "Apparently I was playing well, not bad, for the strict Flier turned out to be reasonably content with the results of my work as a composer increasingly, got in the way of my career as a concert-pianist." Anyway, he kept on performing his own pianoworks up to an advanced age.

Shchedrin was highly influenced by Shostakovich. At the first performance of the "Preludes and fugues" played by the composer himself, Shchedrin was deeply impressed. Fifteen years after his introduction in this work Shchedrin himself wrote a collection of Preludes and fugues. In many of his works, the influence of Shostakovich is noticeable, particularly in his snappy humour and his sharp-edged style of composition, as well as in his preference for polyphony. Shostakovich made a great effort to promote the compositions of his younger colleague.

For instance, he saw to it that Shchedrin's Carmen Suite, denounced by the authorities, remained repertoire. He also asked Shchedrin to succeed him as chairman of the Russian Composers Union. However, a figurehead he refused as a neutral counterpart of the communist party and still wanted to go on working, one had to tread on eggs, in the cited autobiographical sketch Shchedrin writes about that period: "the dream of composers working on a non-commercial basis, that their music had to be approved by the state. 'It's a myth that there were people who rejected in everything simply falling into their lap, while others suffered under their 'love for freedom'. All composers were daunted by people who were fortunate enough to be born in a free country. Dissident composers simply did not exist in the Soviet Union! Whoever showed opposition towards the system, disappeared into jail."

### Shchedrin's Piano Works — Pages 12-15

With Shostakovich, Shchedrin shares his love for Bach's polyphony.

**Piano Sonata no 1** originated in 1962. Shchedrin dedicated this work to the famous Russian pianist Dmitri Bashkirov. The first movement (Allegro da Sonata) of the sonata in three movements was written in the classical sonata form. That is to say that the work has a structure with an exposition, consisting of two contrasting theme groups, a development in which the composer freely expands both themes, combines them and makes them interact, and a concluding recapitulation of both original themes. The first theme, which is played on the white keys only, is characterized by the continually repeated minor second (b-c) in crotchets. Just like in the classical sonata form, the second theme (at least the first part of it) is more melodious. On the whole this classical notation with a more dissonant use of the musical substance reminds one of compositions from the neoclassical period of Igor Stravinsky.

The second movement, Variazioni polifonici, is slow and has a grave atmosphere. It consists of a theme with seven variations according to the polyphonic principle, with several rounds. Without it being a question of a rigorous canon, there is a horizontal texture of the theme at all twelve notes of the chromatic scale appear side by side.

The finale is a sparkling Rondo-Toccata, full of fast semi-quavers alternating with fierce, dissonant accents, which will refer to the opening theme of the first movement. Halfway the finale literal quotations of this theme (at least the first movement and the theme of the second movement enter the toccata according to the cyclical principle.

**Basso ostinato** is Shchedrin's best-known work for piano solo. Together with the Two-part Invention, it forms the mini-cycle Two Polyphonic pieces, which was dedicated to his former teacher Yakov Flier. It is a popular piece at competitions and final piano examinations, especially in Russia. A basso ostinato is a composition in which a particular short or longer motif in the bass is repeated continuously. Also Shchedrin's composition has such a basso ostinato, however it is not repeated consistently and identically. Besides there are passages in which this bass part is silent. The composition is impressive by its compelling bass-patters and fierce motives in triplets and dotted patterns in the right hand. The Two-part Invention as to form is grafted onto Johann Sebastian Bach's inventions and as far as its theme is concerned it strongly reminds of polyphonical compositions by Shostakovich.

**Troika** was written in 1959. The humorous piece is related to the troikadance in Tchaikovsky's Four Seasons, but the idiom is much more modern of course. The relatively slow tempo of Shchedrin's Troika evokes the image of a coach pulled forward without any haste, with a drunken coachman who does not know anymore where to go on the image-less plain, but who keeps on singing imperturbably in a "limp" five-eight beat.

**An Imitation of Albeniz**: Shchedrin uses the idiom of the late-romantic Spanish composer Isaac Albeniz. The work was written in 1959 and dedicated to his wife, the world-famous ballet dancer Maya Plisetskaya and as such is a declaration of love full of Spanish passion.

The enjoyable piece — one of the most accessible of Shchedrin's pianocompositions — contains imitations of guitar sounds and elements from dance.

**Humoresque**, also from 1959, is an extraordinarily witty piece, as the title indicates. Apart from Basso Ostinato, it is Shchedrin's most played pianowork. Again we recognize Shchedrin here as an admirer of Shostakovich, though this piece is probably even closer to Sergey Prokofiev's ballet music. Anyway, it is typical Russian music. The work reminds one of some satirical fairy tale by Gogol or by the 19th century author Saltykov-Shchedrin about a comical fat general. When the latter walks the streets he thinks himself quite important, but people laugh at him behind his back.

**Toccatina-Collage** is no. 12 from the Polyphonic Notebook, a collection of 25 pieces which form a kind of encyclopedia in which the composer deals with all polyphonic forms.

Number 12, the wittiest of the collection, is a two-part invention. Typical are its sudden quotations from Bach's two-part Invention in F.

**Piano Sonata nr. 2** was composed 34 years after the first one. In 1997 the work was presented in Oslo for the first time by Yefim Bronfman. The second Sonata has a milder atmosphere than the first Sonata, even though the idiom is more modern. It is less accessible, but it has more musical depth. Also here the first movement has a sonata form. The movement in semi-quavers is continued throughout the theme without interruption, but partly due to its moderate tempo (Allegro moderato) its character is mainly lyrical. The semi-quavers serve as an accompaniment for a striking theme in the left hand. The second theme is static and almost looks like a classical minuet. The long development ends in a fierce climax, after which a much shortened recapitulation concludes the first movement. Movement 2, Sostenuto cantabile, is of a greater intimacy and richer sonority. The form is three-part (ABA). The A-part is of an improvising and preluding nature. In the B-part a brilliant melodic line is accompanied capriciously by the left hand. This part ends placidly, with a shortened and altered repetition of the A-theme. In the Finale the composer requires the fastest possible speed (Presto possibile). It is an uninterrupted, mono-phone line of semiquavers, predominantly pianissimo, with occasional violent peaks in the dynamics. The locomotion reminds one of French fingerplaying, as is to be found in works like Debussy's Pour le Piano or Ravel's Le Tombeau de Couperin. The monophony and the continuous flow of extremely fast semi-quavers further refer unmistakably to the finale of Chopin's second sonata. At the same time the speed calls up associations with the permanent haste and stress of today's world.

### The Album for the Youth — Pages 16-17

**The Album for the Youth** from 1981 is a collection of 15 short pieces for children. In imitation of Schumann and Tchaikovsky, Shchedrin assembled attractive pieces for children in it. It includes both studies in which technical problems are elaborated (e.g. playing in thirds and arpeggios) and character pieces in several styles. The style pastiche Let's play Rossini's opera (with a theme from Rossini's overture La Cenerentola) is a remarkable example of this.

**Tchastushky** is originally a "Concert for orchestra", composed by Shchedrin in 1963. It is dedicated to the director Gennadi Roshdestvenski, who led its premiere with the Moscow Symphony Orchestra at the Autumn Festival of Warsaw. The effective piece was a great success, also in the US, where Leonard Bernstein led the first performance.

Bernstein was so thrilled by the piece that he immediately commissioned a second concerto for orchestra by Shchedrin.

In 1999 Shchedrin adapted Tchastushky for piano and subtitled it "Concerto for piano solo".

The Russian title of the orchestral work is Ozornye Tchastushki, which literally means "naughty limericks". The tchastushka is often an improvised tune that is found a lot in the Ukraine and in Bilorussia. It is sung or danced, if necessary accompanied by accordion or balalaika. Shchedrin himself stated about this work: "The tchastushka always contains a lot of humour, irony and sharp satire on the status quo, its advocates and its public leaders. Even mighty and fearsome names like Marx, Lenin and Stalin have been made a fool of in tchastushki!"

Tchastushki is a rhapsody of whimsical musical limericks, or contemporary folk dances and songs, the intention of which is mainly sharp and funny. On the whole the composer sticks to the sonata-form: there is a second, slow theme, which is repeated in the recapitulation in a different key. The dynamic work has a predominantly high tempo. The movement is only sporadically interrupted by speed reductions, rests and changes of time.

Because of this, and because of the technical demands in combination with the very strong dynamics which are sometimes required, this is one of Shchedrin's technically most difficult works for piano.

**Christa Lelie**

## Liner Notes (German) — Pages 4-7

### Shchedrins Klavierwerke

Shchedrin und Schostakowitsch vereinigt die gemeinsame Liebe für Bachs Polyphonie.

**Piano Sonate Nr. 1** entstand 1962. Shchedrin hat sie dem bekannten russischen Pianisten Dmitri Bashkirov gewidmet. Der erste Satz der dreiteiligen Sonate (Allegro da sonata) ist in klassischer Sonatenform geschrieben. Das bedeutet, dass das Werk die Struktur einer Exposition besitzt, die aus zwei kontrastierenden Themengruppen besteht. Der Komponist entwickelt beide Themen, wobei er sie miteinander kombiniert, einander gegenüberstellt und mit einer Reprise der beiden ursprünglichen Themen schließt. Das erste Thema, das hauptsächlich auf weißen Tasten gespielt wird, ist gekennzeichnet durch eine fortwährend wiederkehrende kleine Sekunde (b-c) in Verbindung mit der rechten Hand.

Das zweite Thema ist mindestens in seinem ersten Teil melodischer. Insgesamt erinnert diese klassische Notation mit der recht dissonanten Verwendung der musikalischen Substanz an Kompositionen aus der neoklassizistischen Periode von Igor Strawinsky.

Der zweite Satz, Variazioni polifonici, ist langsam und hat eine grabesähnliche Atmosphäre. Er besteht aus einem Thema mit sieben Variationen nach dem polyphonen Prinzip, mit mehreren Runden. Ohne dass es sich um einen strengen Kanon handelt, erscheint in der horizontalen Textur des Themas alle zwölf Noten der chromatischen Skala nebeneinander.

Das Finale ist eine funkelnde Rondo-Toccata, voll von schnellen Sechzehnteln, die mit heftigen, dissonanten Akzenten abwechseln, die auf das Eröffnungsthema des ersten Satzes verweisen. Auf halbem Weg des Finale erscheinen wörtliche Zitate dieses Themas (zumindest das erste Thema) und das Thema des zweiten Satzes treten nach dem zyklischen Prinzip in die Toccata ein.

**Basso ostinato** ist Shchedrins bekanntestes Werk für Klavier solo. Zusammen mit der "Zweistimmigen Invention" bildet es den Mini-Zyklus "Zwei polyphone Stücke", der seinem ehemaligen Lehrer Yakov Flier gewidmet ist. Es ist ein beliebtes Stück bei Wettbewerben und finalen Klavierprüfungen, besonders in Russland. Ein Basso ostinato ist eine Komposition, in der ein bestimmtes kurzes oder längeres Motiv im Bass kontinuierlich wiederholt wird. Auch Shchedrins Basso hat einen solchen Basso ostinato, jedoch wird er nicht konsequent und identisch wiederholt. Außerdem gibt es Passagen, in denen dieser Bassteil schweigt. Die Komposition ist beeindruckend durch ihre zwingenden Bassmuster und heftigen Motive in Triolen und punktierten Rhythmen in der rechten Hand. Die "Zweistimmige Invention" ist der Form nach verwandt mit den Inventionen von Johann Sebastian Bach und erinnert thematisch an polyphone Kompositionen von Schostakowitsch.

**Troika** wurde 1959 geschrieben. Das humoristische Stück ist verwandt mit der "Troika" aus der "Winter"-Szene der "Jahreszeiten" von Tschaikowsky, klingt jedoch moderner. Das relativ langsame Tempo von Shchedrins Troika impliziert das Bild einer sich langsam bewegenden Kutsche mit einem betrunkenen Kutscher, der in der ungeheuren Weite sein Ziel verloren hat — er singt jedoch weiter im stolpernden 5/8-Takt.

**Im Stile von Albéniz** benutzt Shchedrin das Idiom des spätromantischen spanischen Komponisten Isaac Albéniz. Dieses 1959 geschriebene Werk ist seiner Frau, der weltberühmten Balletttänzerin Maya Plisetskaya gewidmet, und ist demnach eine Liebeserklärung, voller spanischer Leidenschaft. Dieses reizende Stück — eine der zugänglichsten Klavierkompositionen Shchedrins — enthält Imitationen von Gitarrenklängen und Tanzelemente.

**Humoreske**, auch von 1959, ist wie der Titel bereits vermuten lässt, ein ausgesprochen witziges Stück. Ebenso wie Basso Ostinato, ist die Humoreske ein typisches Klavierstück Shchedrins. Wieder können wir hier Shchedrin als Bewunderer von Schostakowitsch erkennen, obwohl dieses Stück eher der Ballettmusik von Sergei Prokofjew entlehnt ist. Wie denn auch sei, es ist typisch russische Musik. Dieses Werk erinnert ebenfalls an ein satirisches Märchen von Gogol oder dem russischen Schriftsteller Saltikov-Shchedrin aus dem 19. Jahrhundert über einen komischen fetten General, der, wenn er durch die Straßen geht, sich für sehr wichtig hält, aber die Leute hinter seinem Rücken über ihn lachen.

**Toccatina-Collage** ist die Nr. 12 aus dem 1972 geschriebenen "Polyphonen Notizbuch für Klavier", eine Sammlung von 25 Stücken, die eine Art von Enzyklopädie bilden, innerhalb derer vom Komponisten alle polyphonen Formen behandelt werden. Das Stück Nr. 12, das witzigste aus der Sammlung, ist eine zweiteilige Invention. Charakteristisch sind die plötzlich aufbrausenden Zitate aus Bachs Invention in F-Dur.

**Die Klaviersonate Nr. 2** wurde 34 Jahre später als die erste geschrieben. 1997 wurde dieses Werk von Yefim Bronfman in Oslo zum ersten Mal gespielt. Die zweite Sonate zeigt gegenüber der ersten eine mildere Stimmung und bedient sich einer moderneren Sprache. Sie ist nicht so zugänglich, geht jedoch musikalisch tiefer. Auch hier besitzt der erste Satz eine Sonatenform. Die Bewegung in den Sechzehnteln wird im Laufe des ganzen ersten Themas unterbrochen fortgesetzt, aber dank des gemäßigten Tempos (Allegro moderato) ist ihr Charakter überwiegend lyrisch. Die Sechzehntel dienen der Begleitung eines markanten Themas, welches von der linken Hand gespielt wird. Das zweite Thema ist statisch und besitzt beinahe etwas Mechanisches. Die lange Durchführung endet mit einem energischen Höhepunkt, wonach eine gekürzte Reprise diesen Satz abschließt.

**Satz 2, Sostenuto cantabile** ist von größter Intimität und Klangschönheit. Die Form ist dreiteilig (ABA). Der A-Teil ist improvisatorisch und großartig angelegt. Im B-Teil wird eine glänzende Melodielinie auf eine launische Weise von der linken Hand begleitet. Dieser Satz endet ruhig, mit einer kurzen Wiederholung des alterierten A-Themas. Im Finale fordert der Komponist ein möglichst schnelles Tempo (Presto possibile): hier klingt eine ununterbrochene einstimmige Linie von Sechzehnteln, größtenteils pianissimo gespielt, mit gelegentlich heftigen Ausbrüchen in der Dynamik. Die Motorik lässt an das französische Fingerspiel denken, das z.B. in Werken wie Pour le Piano von Debussy, oder Ravels Le Tombeau de Couperin vorkommt. Die Monophonie und der durchgehende Fluss der schnellen Sechzehntel-Noten verweisen eindeutig auf das Finale der Zweiten Sonate von Chopin. Gleichzeitig ruft die Geschwindigkeit Assoziationen mit der ständigen Eile und dem Stress unserer heutigen Zeit hervor.

**Das Album für die Jugend**, 1981, ist eine Sammlung von 15 kurzen Stücken für Kinder. Nach Schumann und Tschaikowsky hat auch Shchedrin hierzu attraktive Stücke hervorgebracht. Es geht sowohl um Etüden, in denen technische Probleme erarbeitet werden (z.B. Terzen und Arpeggiospiel) als auch um Charakterstücke in verschiedenen Stilen. Das Parodiestück "Lassen wir Rossinis Oper spielen" (mit einem Thema aus Rossinis Ouvertüre La Cenerentola) ist ein bemerkenswertes Vorbild dafür.

**Tschastushky** ist ursprünglich ein Konzert für Orchester, das Shchedrin 1963 komponiert hat. Es ist dem Dirigenten Gennadi Roshdestvenski gewidmet, der dieses Stück im demselben Jahr auf dem Warschauer Herbstfestival mit dem Moskauer Symphonie Orchester uraufgeführt hat. Dieses effektvolle Stück hatte auch in Amerika großen Erfolg, wo es von Leonard Bernstein aufgeführt wurde. Bernstein war von dem Stück so begeistert, dass er bei dem Komponisten sofort ein zweites Konzert für das Orchester bestellte. 1999 bearbeitete Shchedrin Tschastushky für das Klavier und nannte das Werk "Konzert für Klavier Solo".

Der russische Titel des Orchesterwerks heißt "Osornye Tschastushky", was wörtlich "freche Streiche" bedeutet. "Tschastushky" ist ein oft improvisiertes Liedchen, das häufig in der Ukraine und Weißrussland vorkommt. Es wird gesungen oder getanzt, eventuell mit Begleitung von Akkordeon oder Balalaika. Shchedrin selbst sagte über dieses Werk: "In einer Tschastushka steckt immer Humor, Ironie oder eine scharfe Satire auf den Status Quo, seine Verteidiger und Volksführer. Selbst solche mächtigen und gefürchteten Namen wie Marx, Lenin und Stalin wurden in einer Tschastushka ausgelacht."

Tschastushky ist eine Rhapsodie aus bösartigen musikalischen Scherzen oder modernen Volkstänzen und Melodien, die überwiegend spitz und witzig sind. Im Hauptteilen hält sich der Komponist an die Sonatenform: es gibt ein zweites, langsames Thema, das in der Reprise in einer anderen Tonart wiederholt wird. Dieses energische Werk hat überwiegend ein hohes Tempo. Nur sporadisch wird die Bewegung unterbrochen durch Verzögerungen, Pausen oder Taktänderungen. Hierdurch und dank der technischen Anforderungen in Kombination mit der oft geforderten sehr starken Dynamik, gehört es zu den technisch schwierigsten Klavierwerken Shchedrins.

**Christa Lelie**

## Michail Markov, Piano — Pages 18-19

**Michail Markov**

Born in Moscow in 1951, Michail Markov began studying piano at the age of 7. Three years later, he performed his first solo concert in his hometown with an orchestra in the Concert Hall of the Tchaikovsky Conservatory in Moscow.

The pianist continued his musical education with Professor Victor Merjanov at Moscow's Tchaikovsky Conservatory. At the same time, he studied with Alexandr Goldenweiser in chamber ensembles class.

In 1975 M. Markov graduated with honors at the Conservatory. These honors included mentions for concert and chamber piano, accompaniment, as well as for professorship. In the following years, Michail Markov successfully performed in the Eastern countries of the former Soviet block. In the years just before leaving Russia, he was a deputy director and piano teacher at the famous Moscow Gnessin Special School for musically gifted children.

Since 1991, the musician has been living in the Netherlands where he has continued his successful combination of teaching and musical performances. His students have often been awarded prizes in national and international piano competitions. The artist himself has given many recitals and chamber music concerts in the Netherlands, Germany, France, and Russia.

In 1997 he made a CD-recording in Paris with the works of D. Schostakovitch and F. Galpine for the French label Suoni & Colori.

Since mid-nineties he is working as professor at the piano department of the Conservatory Saxion Hogeschool in Enschede, the Netherlands. M. Markov is also the co-founder of the special Young Talents piano department of this Conservatory.

M. Markov has been invited to give Master Classes at many International Music Festivals and Summer Schools in the Netherlands, USA, Austria, Russia, Poland and Ukraine.

M. Markov is the founder and president of the Foundation "Russian Music Tradition in the Netherlands" and also the artistic leader of the International Piano Competition for Young Musicians in Enschede, the Netherlands.
