### Halina Czerny-Stefańska

Halina Czerny-Stefańska (1911-2001) was one of the most celebrated Polish pianists of the twentieth century, and one of the greatest Chopin interpreters of all time. She was born in Poland in 1911, studied with her mother Maria Czerny-Stefańska and then with Józef Turczyński at the Warsaw Conservatory. In 1937 she shared the First Prize with Lev Oborin at the International Chopin Piano Competition in Warsaw, launching her international career. She was particularly celebrated for her interpretations of Chopin, and her performances of the mazurkas were legendary throughout her career. She taught for many years at the Warsaw Academy of Music, and her students include many of today's leading Polish pianists. This recording of the complete Chopin mazurkas was made in 1976-1977 and originally released on LP by the Polish label Polskie Nagrania. It is here reissued on CD for the first time by Canyon Classics, capturing one of the great 20th-century Chopin interpretations.

### Frédéric Chopin and the Mazurkas

Frédéric Chopin (1810-1849) wrote fifty-one mazurkas over the course of his career, from his earliest years in Poland through his final years in Paris. The mazurka is a Polish folk dance in triple meter, and Chopin transformed this popular dance form into one of the most personal and distinctive forms in his entire output. The mazurkas are at the very heart of Chopin's output – they are where his connection with Polish folk music is most direct, where his harmonic language is most adventurous, where his rhythmic freedom is most evident, and where his most personal emotions find expression. They range from the most brilliant, energetic dance pieces to the most intimate, hauntingly beautiful miniatures, and they contain some of the most perfect music Chopin ever wrote.

Halina Czerny-Stefańska was one of the greatest exponents of Chopin's mazurkas in the twentieth century. She had a particular feeling for the rhythmic and melodic character of these pieces, rooted in her Polish background, and her complete recording made in the 1970s remains one of the most celebrated versions of this cycle ever recorded. It captures all the different moods and characters of these wonderful pieces, from the energetic brilliance of the early mazurkas to the profound melancholy of the late ones.

### Notes on the Mazurkas

Chopin published his mazurkas in eleven groups between 1832 and 1846:
- Op. 6 (1832) – 5 mazurkas
- Op. 7 (1832) – 5 mazurkas
- Op. 17 (1833) – 4 mazurkas
- Op. 24 (1836) – 4 mazurkas
- Op. 25 (1837) – 4 mazurkas
- Op. 30 (1837) – 4 mazurkas
- Op. 33 (1838) – 4 mazurkas
- Op. 41 (1840) – 5 mazurkas
- Op. 63 (1846) – 3 mazurkas
- Op. 67 (1845, published posthumously 1855) – 4 mazurkas
- Op. 68 (1846, published posthumously 1855) – 4 mazurkas

In addition to these, there are several mazurkas published later from manuscripts:
- Four Mazurkas Op. 67 KK IIb (published 1865)
- Mazurka in A minor KK IVb No. 4
- Mazurka in A-flat major KK IVb No. 5

This recording includes all fifty-one mazurkas complete.

### Halina Czerny-Stefańska (1911-2001)

Halina Czerny-Stefańska was born in 1911 in Warsaw, Poland. Her mother was a pianist, and she began her studies with her, then continued at the Warsaw Conservatory with Józef Turczyński. In 1937 she shared the First Prize at the International Chopin Competition in Warsaw with Lev Oborin from the Soviet Union. This victory launched her international career, and she toured throughout Europe and gave many celebrated performances. She was particularly renowned for her Chopin playing, and her interpretations were noted for their beauty of tone, rhythmic freedom and deep poetic insight. She was a professor at the Fryderyk Chopin University of Music in Warsaw for many years, and taught many generations of Polish pianists. She died in Warsaw in 2001, leaving a great legacy of recordings and performances that continue to inspire pianists and music lovers today.

This complete recording of all Chopin's mazurkas was originally made for Polskie Nagrania in 1976-1977, and it is reissued here on CD for the first time by Canyon Classics, bringing this great classic recording back into circulation.
