## A lone jewel between seria and buffa

No composer marked London's theatre life between 1710 and 1740 more powerfully than the Halle-born George Frideric Handel: he had got to know the stage in Hamburg, and later acquired the requisite musical and dramatic know-how in Venice and Naples. With *Xerxes*, the drama per musica written within seven weeks and first performed on 15 April 1738 in the Haymarket Theatre, London, Handel's life as an opera composer, filled with disappointments and catastrophes than with triumphs, basically came to an end. although a couple of stragglers were still to follow. That *Xerxes* was a failure and was withdrawn after four repetitions was due less to the work itself than to the casting: the title-role was taken by no less than the celebrated castrato Caffarelli - than to its reflecting the London public's changed taste, which had already been revealed ten years previously by the spectacular success of John Gay's *The Beggar's Opera* produced by John Christopher Pepusch. Almost two centuries were to pass before the opera was revived in the context of the Handel renaissance. In 1924 *Xerxes* was presented to an astonished public at the Göttingen Handel Festival, in the admittedly radical arrangement by Oskar Hagen. After World War II not the least to profit by the re-awakened interest in Baroque music, which then further intensified by the movement for original sonorities, were Handel's operas, among them *Xerxes*, which came under discussion in sensational new interpretations, for example in 1972 in Leipzig by Joachim Herz and in 1985 in London by Nicholas Hytner.

In accordance with the conventions of Baroque opera, *Xerxes* does not deal with the historical personality of the Persian king; the historical element is reduced merely to a single scene in Act 2 in which the famous pontoon bridge over the Hellespont is mentioned - an event handed down to us through Herodotus. For more do we know that human misunderstandings and confusions determine the course of the action, in which Xerxes is in no way depicted as an innocent lover: behind apparently sympathetic manifestations constantly lurks the despot, unpredictable and dangerous. Handel succeeds in drawing a psychogram, rich in facets, of his hero. If, at the beginning, Xerxes seems entirely sunk in contemplation of the sight of Nature in harmony with him - the Larghetto "Schattige Ruh" won a questionably fame as Handel's Largo - the aria "Bleib ihr treu dem, der euch so verachtet" (Act 2, No. 27) reveals the injured vanity of the rejected lover in words "So verachte ich euch" ("So I despise you") - a reaction that the composer emphasizes in well-tried seria manner with an exaggerated coloratura. In the famous aria of fury (Act 3, no. 48) the rhetorical musical figures (raging descending scales in the violins, rugged vocal line) finally offer an unhindered view into the abysses of his soul after Xerxes has learnt of Romilda's marriage to Arsamenes.

Handel's *Xerxes*, however, is still to be classed with the restrictions of the opera seria genre, at any rate, only if the type conceived and codified by Pietro Metastasio is taken as basis. Characteristically, Handel resorted to Metastasio in 17~ and 18~ century Venetian libretti. A belvedere at the edge of a magnificent garden; in the centre a plane-tree

**Scene 1:** Xerxes, the King of Persia, is strolling in the garden of his palace and sings in praise of the beauty of a plane-tree and the peace of its shade.

**Scene 2:** Xerxes' brother Arsamenes appears, accompanied by his servant Elviro, to meet his beloved Romilda. She, the daughter of General Ariodates, has promised Xerxes that she will marry him, but she is firmly determined to keep faith with Arsamenes.

**Scene 3:** Xerxes too has seen Romilda and wants her to share his throne; he has been told that she already loves his brother, but he will not believe it, can she not give up Arsamenes and marry him instead.

**Scene 4:** Arsamenes attempts to console Romilda, who does not know what to do. Xerxes enters, wants to take care of that.

## Biographies

### Fritz Wunderlich

Fritz Wunderlich, 1930 in Kusel in der Pfalz geboren, war von 1950 bis 1955 Schüler an der Musikhochschule in Freiburg i.Br. und seit 1953 zugleich als Eleve am Stadttheater Freiburg engagiert. Mit der Partie des Tamino aus Mozarts Zauberflöte, die er hier sang, begründete Wunderlich seinen internationalen Ruhm; er wurde besonders als Mozart-Tenor bekannt. 1955 verpflichtete ihn die Staatsoper Stuttgart, deren Mitglied er seitdem geblieben ist. Gleichzeitig war er ab 1958 am Opernhaus von Frankfurt/Main und ab 1960 an der Bayerischen Staatsoper in München tätig.

Den endgültigen Durchbruch brachten die Salzburger Festspiele des Jahres 1959, als er den Henry in Richard Strauss' Die schweigsame Frau darbot. Seither war er alljährlich bei den Salzburger Festspielen zu hören.

1958 trat er bei den Festspielen von Aix-en-Provence als Tamino in der Zauberflöte auf, 1960 sang er an der Stuttgarter Oper in der Uraufführung von Carl Orffs Oedipus den Tiresias, und 1963 wirkte er in München in der Uraufführung von Werner Egks Die Verlobung in San Domingo mit.

Darüber hinaus zählen zu seinen Rollen der Rodolfo in La bohème, der Alfredo in La traviata, der Lenski in Eugen Onegin, die Tenorpartien aus Zauberflöte und Die Entführung, Andres in Bergs Wozzeck, der Steuermann im Fliegenden Holländer und Walther von der Vogelweide in Tannhäuser.

Im September 1966 verstarb Wunderlich an den Folgen eines tragischen Unfalls im Alter von 35 Jahren.

### Naan Põld

1921 in Kose bei Tallinn, Estland, geboren, studierte Naan Põld ab 1940 am Konservatorium in Tallinn Gesang. Nach seiner Flucht nach Deutschland nahm er 1947 sein Studium in Detmold wieder auf und wurde besonders als Evangelist in den Passionen Bachs gefeiert. Zugleich galt er als exzellenter Liedinterpret und setzte sich neben den klassischen vor allem für das Liedrepertoire estnischer Künstler ein. Sehr am Herzen lag ihm neben seiner Gesangskarriere die gesangspädagogische Arbeit. 1998 starb Naan Põld im Alter von 76 Jahren.

### Hertha Töpper

Hertha Töpper, 1924 in Graz geboren, erhielt ihre Ausbildung am dortigen Landeskonservatorium. 1945 debütierte sie am Stadttheater Graz, dem sie bis 1951 gehörte. 1952 wurde sie an der Bayerischen Staatsoper in München, wo sie seit 1951 war und debütierte, an der Uraufführung von Hindemiths Oper *Die Harmonie der Welt* in der Rolle des Grafen Starhemberg mit.

Seit 1955 sang sie in der Salzburger Festspiele auf der Bühne der Staatlichen Oper in München, wo sie seit 1951 war und debütierte an der Uraufführung von Hans Werner Henze *Elegy for Young Lovers* mit.

Wurde mehrfach engagiert, unter anderem unter an der Wiener Staatsoper, der Covent Garden Oper London, und in anderen großen amerikanischen Opernhäusern, wo sie unter anderem 1962 an dem Teatro Colón in Buenos Aires sang die Queen of the Night unter Herbert von Karajan. In 1962 sang sie die Queen of the Night in der neuen eröffneten renovierten Theater an der Wien.

Neben ihrer activity as an opera singer, career als a concert and operetta singer.

### Jean Cook

Ingeborg Hallstein: 1936 in Munich, received her vocal training from her mother, the soprano Elisabeth Halistin, finished this off at the Munich Musikhochschule. In 1956 she made her debut at the Passau municipal theatre as Musette in *La bohème*. From 1958 to 1959 she was engaged at the Basle municipal theatre; after that she made her debut at the Gartenplatz Theatre in Munich. In 1961 she became a member of the Bavarian State Opera company, where she made her debut as the Queen of the Night in Mozart's *Magic flute*. In this role Ingeborg

Hallstein built a brilliant international career. From 1960 she took part in the Salzburg Festival, where she sang, among other things, in 1966 in the premiere of Hans Werner Henze's *Die Bassariden* and in 1969 in the premiere of Günter Bialas's *Aucassin and Nicolette*.

She gave successful guest performances at the Vienna State Opera, at Covent Garden in London, and at the great American opera houses, where, inter alia, she appeared in 1962 at the Teatro Colón in Buenos Aires. That same year she sang the Queen of the Night under Herbert von Karajan in the opening performance of the newly renovated Theater an der Wien.

Besides her activity as an opera singer, she has had a successful career as a concert and operetta singer.

### Karl Christian Kohn

Karl Christian Kohn was born in 1928 in Losheim near Saarbrücken studied until 1952 at the Musikhochschule Saarbrücken. After his debut at the municipal theatre there, from 1954 to 1958 he sang at the Deutsche Oper am Rhein Düsseldorf-Duisburg. From 1956 to 1958 he sang at the Stadttheater Freiburg. engaged at the Bavarian State Opera company in Munich. Here Karl Christian Kohn attracted great attention, when he undertook the title-role in Mozart's *Le nozze di Figaro* at the opening performance of the rebuilt Cuvilliés-Theater.

In 1961 he was engaged at the Schwetzingen Festival for the premiere of Hans Werner Henze's *Elegy for young Lovers*. In 1963 he took part in Munich in the premiere of Werner Egk's *Die Verlobung in San Domingo*. Kohn was on the Opernbühnen in Wien, Berlin, Hamburg and in other musical centres at home and abroad. He was particularly well known and highly regarded as a Mozart interpreter, but also as a concert bass.

### Max Proebstl

Max Proebstl, well known as an opera singer as well as in concerts and oratorios, was born in 1913 in Munich. After his training there, he made his debut shortly before World War II at the municipal theatre in Kaiserslautern. After the war he resumed his career at the municipal theatre in Augsburg. A contract with the opera house in Dortmund was followed in 1949 by his appointment as first bass at the Bavarian State Opera in Munich. For over 25 years he sang there in abundance of roles from all spheres of opera literature. Among his star parts were the eponymous hero of Verdi's *Falstaff*, Bartolo in *The Barber of Seville*, Osmin in *Die Entführung aus dem Serail*, Kaspar in *Der Freischütz*, der Antonio in *The marriage of Figaro* as well.
