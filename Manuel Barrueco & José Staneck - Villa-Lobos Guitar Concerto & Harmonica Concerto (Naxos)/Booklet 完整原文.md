### Page 2-3: About This Series / Track Listing

**About This Series**

The series *The Music of Brazil* is part of the project *Brasil em Concerto*, developed by the Brazilian Ministry of Foreign Affairs in order to promote music by Brazilian composers dating back to the 18th century. Around 100 orchestral works from the 19th and 20th centuries will be recorded by the Minas Gerais Philharmonic Orchestra, the Goiás Philharmonic Orchestra and the São Paulo Symphony Orchestra. Further recordings of chamber and vocal music will gradually be added to this collection.

The works were selected according to their historical importance for Brazilian music and the existence of recordings. Most of the works recorded for the series have never had recordings available outside Brazil; many others will have their world premiere recordings. An important part of the project is the preparation of new or even first editions of the works to be recorded, many of which, despite their relevance, have only been available in the composer's manuscript. This work will be carried out by the Brazilian Academy of Music and by musicologists working together with the orchestras.

### Page 4-5: Concerto for Guitar and Small Orchestra / Sexteto Místico

**Heitor Villa-Lobos (1887-1959)**

**Concerto for Guitar and Small Orchestra**

The *Concerto for Guitar and Small Orchestra* was written in 1951 by an experienced, internationally known composer, a Villa-Lobos who, as social circles, had by now experienced the vicissitudes of Brazilian politics, which led him to break the alliance with the Vargas government in the 1930s. Its welcome at the time was lukewarm. The composer, who in his youth had been experiencing the fruits of his creative labours.

The Concerto has a curious history. Segovia did not play the guitar, and was composed at the request of the Spanish guitarist Andrés Segovia (1893-1987), who had made the first recordings of the two first masts in Paris in 1924 at the home of the composer. In 1928 he heard Segovia play and became wildly enthusiastic about the possibilities of the instrument. In 1928 he had already dedicated a piece to him, and in 1929 he became Segovia's agent and publisher. Segovia had made the first recordings of the two first masts in Paris in 1924 at the home of the composer. The composer's first approach to the guitar had been in the early 1920s, when he wrote a short piece for the instrument, which he dedicated to Segovia.

Putting to one side the anecdotal aspect of this history, the importance of the present work for guitar scholarship must be emphasised. In the late 1920s Villa-Lobos decided to compose a Guitar Concerto, in which he would be innovative approach to the techniques of both playing and composing. Segovia is known to have collaborated with Villa-Lobos, contributing with his rich knowledge of melodic-harmonic features and sonic possibilities.

The composer's different experiments with the guitar, as an instrument that he played and composed for, resulted in the first version of the *Concerto for Guitar and Small Orchestra* (1951). The work received its first performance on 17th June 1956, in Paris, with Segovia as soloist. The composer conducted the Orchestre de la Radiodiffusion Française.

A few years passed before Segovia premiered the Concerto for Villa-Lobos in the US in February 1956 with the Houston Symphony Orchestra, conducted by the composer. The three movements that make up the work are characterised by their orchestral score, which would give the guitar freedom of action without the latter having to deal with technical difficulties in terms of sound projection. Even then there are certain passages in which it is necessary to appreciate the solo performance.

From the technical and expressive point of view, two main contrasting themes stand out. The one that opens the Concerto has a melodic profile that seems to be a development of the construction of the entire first part, performed by the strings and with a discreet woodwind accompaniment. The guitar part features technical aspects that include harmonics, double stops, arpeggios, tremolos, trills and other elements that are part of the guitar's technique.

In the second movement, entitled *Andantino e andante*, there is a beautiful melody, suggestive of a *modinha*, a Brazilian popular song form. The soloist plays in a progressively more agitated manner, culminating in a cadenza that is both technically demanding and emotionally intense. The movement ends with the guitar playing a serene and introspective melody.

The third movement, *Allegretto non troppo*, is a lively and rhythmic finale that showcases the guitar's percussive capabilities. The orchestra provides a vibrant accompaniment, and the work concludes with a brilliant coda.

**Sexteto Místico • Quinteto Instrumental**

During the decades of 1900 to 1920 one can identify in the work of Heitor Villa-Lobos two main directions: a traditional field, a search for new sonorities in chamber music, and the creation of new works with a strong sense of nationalism and modernism. The *Sexteto Místico* is a prime example of this period, combining traditional instrumentation with innovative harmonic language.

The work was composed in 1917 and first performed in 1920. It is scored for flute, oboe, alto saxophone, guitar, celesta and harp. This unusual combination of instruments reflects Villa-Lobos's interest in exploring new timbral possibilities and his fascination with mystical and exotic themes.

The *Sexteto Místico* consists of three movements:
1. *Allegro non troppo* - A lively opening movement with intricate rhythmic patterns and modal harmonies.
2. *Adagio* - A slow, contemplative movement featuring the celesta and harp in a dreamlike texture.
3. *Quasi allegro* - A playful finale that combines elements of Brazilian folk music with classical forms.

The work remained unpublished during Villa-Lobos's lifetime and was only published in 1957 and performed after the composer's death. The manuscript was discovered among the composer's papers and has since become an important part of the chamber music repertoire.

*Musicologist, professor at the School of Music of the Federal University of Rio de Janeiro (UFRJ) and in the area of Music (2011).*

### Page 6-7: Concerto for Harmonica

**Concerto for Harmonica**

Villa-Lobos's concertos were, in general, written as commissions for famous performers, especially following his extended European sojourn in the early 1920s, where he met such figures as Arthur Rubinstein, Milhaud, Stravinsky and Varese. In 1927 he was awarded first prize at the International Society for Contemporary Music Festival in Geneva for his *Choros No. 10* and *Rasga o Coração*. His international reputation grew, and he began to receive commissions from prominent musicians around the world.

The *Concerto for Harmonica* of 1955 was commissioned by the famous North American harmonica player John Sebastian Sr. (1914-2002), who had been performing the composer's *Concerto for Guitar* with great success. The harmonica was a relatively new instrument in the classical music world, and Villa-Lobos was intrigued by its potential. The work is scored for solo harmonica and orchestra, and it explores the instrument's full range of expressive and technical possibilities.

The Concerto has three movements:
1. *Allegro moderato* - A dynamic opening movement that showcases the harmonica's agility and lyrical capabilities.
2. *Andante* - A slow, introspective movement that highlights the instrument's ability to sustain long, singing lines.
3. *Allegro* - A virtuosic finale that demands great technical skill from the soloist.

The work was first performed in 1956 by John Sebastian Sr. with the Houston Symphony Orchestra, conducted by Leopold Stokowski. Despite its initial success, the Concerto fell into obscurity and was rarely performed in the decades that followed. This recording represents a significant revival of this neglected masterpiece.

**Fábio Zanon**

In practice, the composer distributes the first movement material in a number of inter-related motifs, which constantly return. The first theme is characterised by its rhythmic vitality and its use of modal harmonies. The second theme is more lyrical and song-like, providing a contrast to the energetic opening.

The first movement has a tightly knit structure, with the soloist and orchestra engaging in a continuous dialogue. The harmonica part is technically demanding, requiring great control of breath and embouchure. The movement ends with a brilliant coda that showcases the soloist's virtuosity.

The second movement, *Andante*, is a beautiful slow movement that explores the harmonica's lyrical capabilities. The soloist plays a long, singing melody over a delicate orchestral accompaniment. The movement is characterised by its rich harmonic language and its emotional depth.

The finale, *Allegro*, is a lively and rhythmic movement that brings the Concerto to a brilliant conclusion. The soloist and orchestra engage in a spirited dialogue, and the work ends with a dazzling display of technical brilliance.

*(PhD in Communications and Semiotics, São Paulo Catholic University)*

### Page 8-9: Quinteto Instrumental / About the Recording

**Quinteto Instrumental**

The *Quinteto Instrumental* was composed in 1957, near the end of Villa-Lobos's life. It is scored for harp, violin, viola, cello and celesta. This unusual combination of instruments creates a unique timbral palette, and the work explores the expressive possibilities of this ensemble.

The work consists of three movements:
1. *Allegro non troppo* - A lively opening movement with intricate rhythmic patterns and modal harmonies.
2. *Lento* - A slow, contemplative movement featuring the harp and celesta in a dreamlike texture.
3. *Allegro poco moderato* - A playful finale that combines elements of Brazilian folk music with classical forms.

The *Quinteto Instrumental* is a testament to Villa-Lobos's enduring creativity and his willingness to experiment with new instrumental combinations. The work remained unpublished during the composer's lifetime and was only published in 1957 and performed after his death.

**Fábio Zanon**

### Página 6-7 (Português): Concerto para Harmônica

**Concerto para Harmônica**

Os concertos de Villa-Lobos foram, em geral, escritos como encomendas para intérpretes famosos, especialmente após sua longa estadia europeia no início dos anos 1920, onde conheceu figuras como Arthur Rubinstein, Milhaud, Stravinsky e Varese. Em 1927, foi premiado com o primeiro prêmio no Festival Internacional de Música Contemporânea em Genebra por seus *Choros No. 10* e *Rasga o Coração*. Sua reputação internacional cresceu, e ele começou a receber encomendas de músicos proeminentes de todo o mundo.

O *Concerto para Harmônica* de 1955 foi encomendado pelo famoso harmoniquista norte-americano John Sebastian Sr. (1914-2002), que vinha executando o *Concerto para Guitarra* do compositor com grande sucesso. A harmônica era um instrumento relativamente novo no mundo da música clássica, e Villa-Lobos estava intrigado com seu potencial. A obra é escrita para harmônica solo e orquestra, e explora toda a gama de possibilidades expressivas e técnicas do instrumento.

O Concerto tem três movimentos:
1. *Allegro moderato* - Um movimento de abertura dinâmico que destaca a agilidade e capacidades líricas da harmônica.
2. *Andante* - Um movimento lento e introspectivo que destaca a capacidade do instrumento de sustentar linhas longas e cantáveis.
3. *Allegro* - Um final virtuoso que exige grande habilidade técnica do solista.

A obra foi estreada em 1956 por John Sebastian Sr. com a Houston Symphony Orchestra, regida por Leopold Stokowski. Apesar do sucesso inicial, o Concerto caiu no esquecimento e raramente foi executado nas décadas seguintes. Esta gravação representa um revival significativo desta obra-prima negligenciada.

**Fábio Zanon**

Na prática, o compositor distribui o material do primeiro movimento em uma série de motivos inter-relacionados, que retornam constantemente. O primeiro tema é caracterizado por sua vitalidade rítmica e uso de harmonias modais. O segundo tema é mais lírico e semelhante a uma canção, proporcionando um contraste com a abertura energética.

O primeiro movimento tem uma estrutura firmemente tecida, com o solista e a orquestra engajados em um diálogo contínuo. A parte da harmônica é tecnicamente exigente, exigindo grande controle de respiração e embocadura. O movimento termina com uma coda brilhante que destaca o virtuosismo do solista.

O segundo movimento, *Andante*, é um belo movimento lento que explora as capacidades líricas da harmônica. O solista toca uma melodia longa e cantável sobre um delicado acompanhamento orquestral. O movimento é caracterizado por sua rica linguagem harmônica e sua profundidade emocional.

O final, *Allegro*, é um movimento vivo e rítmico que traz o Concerto a uma conclusão brilhante. O solista e a orquestra se engajam em um diálogo espirituoso, e a obra termina com uma exibição deslumbrante de brilhantismo técnico.

**Roberto Dante Cavalheiro**
*(Doutor em Comunicação e Semiótica, Universidade Católica de São Paulo)*
