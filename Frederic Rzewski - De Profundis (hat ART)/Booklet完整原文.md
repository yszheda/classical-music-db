### FREDERIC RZEWSKI
born 1938

Frederic Rzewski was born in Westfield, Massachusetts in 1938. He studied composition with Walter Piston and Randall Thompson at Harvard University, with Roger Sessions at Princeton, and with Luigi Dallapiccola in Florence. In the 1960s he was active in New York as a new improviser and founding member of the ensemble Musica Elettronica Viva. He has been living since 1970 in Italy, where he taught for many years at the Conservatorio di Musica "Luigi Cherubini" in Florence. His compositions, which include many large-scale works for ensembles and for solo piano, are performed throughout the world. He currently lives in Brussels and teaches at the Conservatoire Royal de Musique there. As a pianist he is well-known for his performances of contemporary music, particularly his own. This is his first recording as a pianist on CD devoted entirely to his own music.

### DE PROFUNDIS (1966)

De Profundis was written in 1966 for the Swedish bassist Sven-Erik Johansson who gave the first performance at the Gulbenkian Foundation in Lisbon. It is composed for speaking pianist: the pianist speaks the text while playing. The text is taken from the "Sermo ad Clerum" ("A Sermon to the Clergy") by the 16th-century English philosopher and statesman Sir Thomas More. More was executed by Henry VIII for having refused to acknowledge the king as Supreme Head of the Church in England. The work begins with the player silently striking a low C, which is allowed to ring on. This is what the player says:

> "If a man should happen to have a heretic burned, I think he ought to be very careful what he doeth. For if there be any life the bone will kindle and start again, though all the world should hold its breath, little spark will bring it out. Every heretic that any man burneth is another spark. This fire that I speak of, this fire is the word of God. It will spread from one to another till it kindle the whole world. This fire, it will never go out till the whole world be on fire. Is there any man that can put it out? Ye are the salt of the earth. What is it to be salt? To salt: it meaneth to keep flesh from stinking. If the salt should lose his savour, what will ye then do? If the salt be once out of savour, there is nothing can salt it again. It is good for nothing but to be cast out and to be trodden under foot. Ye are the light of the world. A city that is set on an hill can not be hid. Men do not light a candle and put it under a bushel, but on a candlestick; and it giveth light unto all that are in the house. Let your light so shine before men. If the light that is in thee be darkness, how great is that darkness! Be therefore perfect, even as your Father which is in heaven is perfect. The kingdom of heaven suffereth violence, and the violent take it by force. And blessed is he that shall not be offended in me. He that hath ears to hear, let him hear. Upon this rock I will build my church. I will not cease, I will not rest, I will never stop calling, calling, calling, calling, calling, calling, calling, calling, calling, calling...

De Profundis is a work that combines piano playing with spoken text, creating an extraordinary dramatic and musical intensity. It is one of the most powerful early statements of Rzewski's political and artistic vision.

### JOHN CAGE ON RZEWSKI'S READY TO HAND

Ready to Hand was composed in 2002. John Cage wrote about it in 1987 (originally for the exhibition of Rzewski's etudes prepared for a exhibition in Germany):

"Frederic Rzewski's piano etudes, he told me, were each written to be played in any order, each one making use of a different rhythmic pattern. Their multiplicity allows each performance to be different, as he does when he plays them, for the order is different each time. This indeterminacy is what I enjoy, it is what we both enjoy, coming from the different backgrounds we have, we still agree about this: multiplicity. Each etude is different from the others. This multiplicity given to the audience changes each time into a different piece. I am grateful that that's what it does. It leads me to write this note to thank him. I'm always happy when he and I are together. When we meet, we talk about gardening."

### LISABELLA'S DREAM (1979)

"Lisabella's Dream is a lullaby, it's a dream. It's music that I imagine being heard half-asleep at the edge of sleep, a kind of timeless gentle floating."

Lisabella is the composer's daughter. She was born in 1966, this piece was written in 1979 when she was thirteen. It is a gentle, meditative work that shows another side of Rzewski's compositional personality.

### KEEP IT UP (1997)

"Keep It Up was written for a dance by the Swiss choreographer Jean Renilhac. I had been using this chord that I found myself liking very much, a C major chord with an added half step in the middle. So I just kept repeating it. It just went on and on, like an obsession. I thought it would never end. But it did end, and the piece is over. So that's it."
