# Johann Sebastian Bach
## The Art of Fugue BWV 1080

## Diana Boyle fortepiano (after Johann Fritz 1742)

The Art of Fugue is Bach's last great masterpiece, left unfinished at his death in 1750. It is a sequence of nineteen fugues (contrapuntal studies) based on the same simple subject, exploring every possible contrapuntal treatment of the subject, from simple four-voice fugue to the most complex multiple overlapping fugues and canons. It is one of the most extraordinary monuments of contrapuntal writing that has ever been created, and it stands as a summation of Bach's lifelong art of counterpoint.

This recording presents the complete work on a fortepiano after the eighteenth-century Viennese instrument maker Johann Fritz, using an instrument that reproduces the clear contrapuntal lines that Bach would have expected, giving a different perspective from the modern piano or organ. Diana Boyle is one of the most distinguished British fortepianists working today, and she plays with a clarity that allows every contrapuntal line to speak with perfect clarity.

### Bach and The Art of Fugue

Bach composed the Art of Fugue over the last years of his life, and it was published posthumously in 1751 after his death. It explores all the possible ways of treating a single subject in counterpoint, showing the incredible mastery that Bach had achieved over his career. It is not just an academic exercise: it is a deeply expressive work that shows Bach at his most contemplative, and it has an extraordinary spiritual depth that has fascinated listeners ever since.

The question of the final unfinished fugue has fascinated commentators ever since. Bach left it incomplete at his death, and many have argued that it was intended to remain that way, as a musical symbol of his life and work ending before it was complete. This recording follows the original order of the posthumous first edition, including the four canons that are usually grouped with the work (BWV 1086 and BWV 1087).

## 第三页 (scans/HIPPO_20211218_0003.jpg)

### **About the Artist**

**Diana Boyle**

Diana Boyle studied with one of the great twentieth-century Bach keyboard players, Helmut Walcha, and she has specialised in performance on period keyboard instruments for many years. She has performed and recorded Bach's complete keyboard works on historic instruments, and she is recognised as one of the leading authorities on Bach performance on eighteenth-century instruments. This is her second recording for Divine Art, following her recording of the Well-Tempered Clavier Book 2 which was warmly welcomed by the critics.

### **The Instrument**

The instrument used for this recording is a modern copy of an original fortepiano made by Johann Fritz in Vienna in 1742. The original instrument is now in the Museu de la Música in Barcelona, and the copy was made by Mahomet Kanjanyan, who has become expert in copying these early nineteenth-century Viennese fortepianos. The instrument has a clear, transparent tone across the whole register, which allows the different contrapuntal lines to emerge with perfect clarity, exactly what is needed for The Art of Fugue.
