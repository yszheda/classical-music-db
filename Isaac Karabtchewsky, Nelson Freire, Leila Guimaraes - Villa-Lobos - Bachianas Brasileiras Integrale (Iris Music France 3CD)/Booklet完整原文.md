## Heitor Villa-Lobos (1887-1959)

Heitor Villa-Lobos is the most important Brazilian composer of the 20th century, and one of the most significant composers from Latin America. His **Bachianas Brasileiras** (Brazilian Bach-style Pieces), composed between 1930 and 1945, are among his most famous and beloved works.

The cycle of nine Bachianas Brasileiras represents Villa-Lobos's unique synthesis: the contrapuntal discipline of Johann Sebastian Bach is fused with the rhythmic and melodic character of Brazilian folk music. Villa-Lobos wrote: "I feel myself a disciple of Bach, the same way the Brazilian people, in their natural artistic formation, are also disciples of this great genius."

Each Bachianas is structured in the Baroque dance suite manner, typically with a Prelude, a contrapuntal movement (Fugue), a melodic Aria, and a rhythmic Toccata or Dance finale. Each movement also carries a Brazilian subtitle indicating the folk genre it evokes.

## About the Bachianas Brasileiras

The concept behind the *Bachianas Brasileiras* is one of the most original in 20th-century music: to fuse the formal and contrapuntal traditions of Bach with the vibrant folk music of Brazil. Villa-Lobos did not seek to imitate Bach, but rather to follow his example in organizing popular Brazilian materials according to the structural principles of Baroque music.

Each work in the series is conceived for different instrumentation:
- **No. 1** for large orchestra
- **No. 2** for large orchestra
- **No. 3** for orchestra with piano
- **No. 4** for piano solo
- **No. 5** for soprano and orchestra
- **No. 6** for orchestra
- **No. 7** for large orchestra
- **No. 8** for orchestra with piano
- **No. 9** for orchestra

The Brazilian subtitles to each movement reveal the folk origins:
- **Embolada**: a type of improvised poetic contest from Northeast Brazil
- **Modinha**: an old Brazilian lyrical song genre
- **Conversa**: "conversation" – the playful dialogue of fugal voices
- **Ponteio**: a type of Brazilian guitar improvisation
- **O Canto da Nossa Terra**: "the song of our land"
- **Lembrança do Sertão**: "memory of the backlands"
- **O Trenzinho do Caipira**: "the little train of the country bumpkin" – one of Villa-Lobos's most famous orchestral showpieces, depicting a train journey through the Brazilian countryside
- **Devaneio**: "reverie"
- **Pica-Pau**: "woodpecker"
- **Miudinho**: a small lively dance
- **Cantilena**: lyrical song
- **Martelo**: "hammer" – a rhythmic dance with strong accents
- **Quadrilha caipira**: country quadrille dance
- **Desafio**: "challenge" – another reference to improvised poetic contests
- **Catira batida**: a lively percussive dance from Minas Gerais

## Artists

### Isaac Karabtchewsky (Conductor)

Isaac Karabtchewsky (1914-1996) was one of Brazil's most distinguished conductors, a leading figure in Brazilian musical life for decades. He did much to promote Brazilian music, particularly the complete works of Heitor Villa-Lobos.

### Nelson Freire (Piano)

Nelson Freire (1944-2021) was one of the most celebrated Brazilian pianists of the late 20th century, internationally renowned for his poetic playing and brilliant technique. He was particularly noted for his interpretations of Brazilian nationalist composers including Villa-Lobos.

### Leila Guimaraes (Soprano)

Brazilian soprano Leila Guimaraes was a leading singer of her generation in Brazil, particularly active in concert and orchestral repertoire.
