### CZECH PIANO MUSIC
OF THE 20TH CENTURY

The present recording is a continuation of Supraphon's long-standing focus on the work of Czech composers and the art of outstanding Czech interpreters. The recording features a successful young Czech pianist, Ivo Kahánek, performing three key works by three major figures of 20th-century Czech music – Leoš Janáček, Miloslav Kabeláč and Bohuslav Martinů. Each of them has a distinctive place in the development of modern Czech music, reflecting different stylistic and aesthetic tendencies.

## Page 4

### LEOŠ JANÁČEK
### Piano Sonata "1.X.1905 FROM THE STREET"

Leoš Janáček composed his Piano Sonata in October 1905 in response to a tragic event in Brno: the death of a young worker, František Pavlík, who was killed by Austrian police during a demonstration for a Czech university in Moravia. The original title of the composition was *1.X.1905*, and the subtitle "From the Street" suggests the inspiration came from the political events that shook the city. The sonata originally had three movements, but Janáček destroyed the third movement, and only two movements have survived: "The Beginning" (Prelude) and "Death" (Epilogue). The work is a powerful musical commentary on the tragic event, combining dramatic intensity with deeply personal emotion. It is one of the masterpieces of early 20th-century Czech piano music.

### THREE FUGUES

Janáček composed his three fugues over a longer period of time: the first two (G minor and A minor) originated in 1911, while the third (A major) was completed only in 1921. They represent Janáček's encounter with the Baroque contrapuntal tradition, which he reworked in his own individual harmonic and rhythmic language. The fugues show the composer's remarkable mastery of counterpoint within the context of his modern musical language.

### MILOSLAV KABELÁC
### EIGHT PRELUDES OP. 30

Miloslav Kabeláč was one of the leading figures of the post-war generation of Czech composers, whose music developed the legacy of 20th-century modernism. He composed his Eight Preludes Op. 30 between 1940 and 1941, during the early period of the Nazi occupation. This cycle is one of the most important works of Czech piano music from the mid-20th century. Each of the eight preludes has a distinct expressive character and specific formal conception: from the rhythmic drive of *Preludio Ostinato*, through the contemplative atmosphere of *Preludio Meditativo* and the dreamlike *Preludio Sognante*, the solemn *Preludio Corale*, the nocturnal mystery of *Preludio Notturno*, the flying lightness of *Preludio Volante*, the cantabile *Preludio Arioso*, to the dramatic temperament of the concluding *Preludio Impetuoso*. The cycle demonstrates Kabeláč's distinctive approach to piano writing and his ability to create a convincing large-scale form.

### BOHUSLAV MARTINŮ
### PIANO SONATA H. 350

Bohuslav Martinů composed his Piano Sonata in 1954, during his American exile, when he was living in New York. It is one of three piano sonatas that Martinů wrote during his years in the United States, and it represents the composer's mature late style. The three-movement work combines the classical clarity of structure with Martinů's characteristic melodic invention and harmonic imagination. The first movement (*Poco Allegro*) is driven by rhythmic energy, the middle movement (*Moderato*) is more contemplative in character, while the final movement (*Adagio – Poco Allegro*) concludes the work with a combination of lyrical meditation and rhythmic drive. The sonata is one of the most important contributions to 20th-century piano literature by Martinů.

### IVO KAHÁNEK

Ivo Kahánek (born 1979) ranks among the most prominent representatives of the younger generation of Czech pianists. He studied at the Prague Conservatory and the Academy of Performing Arts in Prague with professors Ivan Klánský and Blanka Bohdanová. He is a laureate of several international piano competitions (e.g., the Beethoven Piano Competition in Vienna, the Prague Spring Competition). He has performed with all leading Czech symphony orchestras, collaborated with many prominent conductors (including Jiří Bělohlávek, Vladimír Válek, and many others), and regularly appears at major Czech music festivals. His repertoire spans from the Classical and Romantic periods to 20th-century and contemporary music, with a particular focus on Czech piano music. He has made several recordings for Supraphon, including recordings of works by Antonín Dvořák, Josef Suk, and other Czech composers.
