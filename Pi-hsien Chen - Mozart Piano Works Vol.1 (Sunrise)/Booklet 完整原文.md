## Piano / Chen Pi-Hsien (Biography)

Chen Pi-Hsien was born in Taiwan in 1950. When she was four, she began to take piano lessons. At the early age of five, Chen Pi-hsien gave her first public performance. The nine year old was sent to Germany to continue her studies at the Musikhochschule in Koeln with Hans-Otto Schmidt-Neuhaus, where she received her diploma as a concert pianist in 1970. In the following years she pursued her studies with Hans Leygraf and took part in piano courses given by Wilhelm Kempff, Tatjana Nikolajeva and Geza Anda. In 1972 she won international appreciation with a prize at the Concours Reine Elisabeth and the first prize in the competition of the ARD, Rundfunkanstalten in Munich. Since then she has given performances at important places such as London, Amsterdam, Zurich, Berlin, Munich and Tokyo as well as at the Festivals of Lucerne, Schwarzenberg, Hong Kong and Osaka. Chen Pi-Hsien has played with famous orchestras and conductors, such as the London Symphony Orchestra, the BBC Symphony Orchestra, the Concertgebouw Orchestra, the Residentie Orchestra, Orchestra of the Bayerische, Hessische, Suedwestdeutsche and Norddeutsche Rundfunk and the Radio-Sinfonieorchester Berlin as well as the Zuercher Kammerorchester, Tonhalle Orchester and the Collegium Musicum Zurich, appearing with Conductors Haitink, Davis, Martinon, Leithzer, Neumann, Jochum, Rieger, Janowski, Inbal, Sacher, de Stoutz, Bour, Stein, Eotvoes and Foster.

## How Did the Child Prodigy Mozart Compose?

As early as 1765, i.e. at the age of 9, the first Variations (I) is based on the Dutch song: Laat ons Juichen, Batavieren! [Let Us Rejoice, Batavians!] by Christian Ernst Graaf; we hear how precisely and with what variety the 9 year old Wolfgang uses different rhythmic values, and how he applies his expressive resources just as in the works of his maturity. Mozart's unmistakable childlike purity and clarity of structure were now to remain the foundation of his creativity. This can also be seen in the Seven Variations in D on the melody Willem van Nassau, written at the end of September 1766 in The Hague. Later Leopold Mozart wrote that the melody arranged by Wolfgang his son "was played and whistled by everyone in Holland".

The Mozart family was on their way home at the end of a journey that began in Salzburg on 9 June 1763 and would take them to Munich, Augsburg, Ludwigsburg, Schwetzingen, Heidelberg, Mainz, Frankfurt, Koblenz, Bonn, Cologne, Aix-la-Chapelle, Brussels, Paris, Calais, and London before they finally returned to Salzburg at the end of November. In the course of the tour, royal courts and cloisters were visited everywhere; Wolfgang studied and composed continuously, as well as performing. In addition to his first language, German, he also spoke French and English, and was now learning Dutch as well. Mathiltique also gave him great enjoyment.

Later, in January of 1775, when the youthful Mozart was in Salzburg, where his opera *La Finta Giardiniera* was being performed and he cherished hopes for a position, he composed the Mass in C Major KV 220 as well as the Offertorium KV 222 and two divertimenti for eight wind instruments. It is now suspected that the six piano sonatas KV 279-284 also date from this period and were written so to speak in one fell swoop.

In a letter from Wolfgang to his father from 12 June 1784: I no have done a great deal in Munich! I have had the exhibition of the last of the six sonatas, the so-called D major sonata KV 284. As if in a panorama, we see and hear in this expansive cycle of pieces how the 19 year old Mozart experiments with the sonata form: the first three are a kind of demonstration pieces of his inventive gifts, his experiences, and the way that the three sonatas of KV 279, KV 280 and KV 281.

The Sonata in C major KV 279 opens the cycle with a bright and untroubled character.

The beginning of the Sonata in F major KV 280 already anticipates Leporellos minuet in *Don Giovanni*, and has operatic shifts as situation and role playing.

The Sonata in B-flat Major KV 281, we find the first of virtuosity as Mozart later realized again and again.

This piece used to be played by a mechanical clock. After Mozart's death the music was running all the time in Prag as people told.

The A minor was written on May 11 1787; Mozart registered this piece in his catalog the same day. It was first published by Vincent Springer in Hoffmeister in 1787 as part of a series *Promemoration pour le Forte Piano ou Clavecin*.

A copy of this publication was in the inventory of Mozart's property after his death.

When in April 1787 Mozart heard that his father was seriously ill, he wrote in his letter: As death, in the true goal of our existence, I have formed during the last few years such close relations with this best and truest friend of mankind that its image is no longer terrifying to one but rather soothing and consoling.

## 鋼琴/陳必先

陳必先教授被譽為「中國的母親」的鋼琴家陳必先女士，1950 年生於台北，於嘉義讀完小學後上台鋼琴。九歲時赴德國求學，在德國科隆音樂學院以優異成績畢業於恩師 Hans-Otto Schmidt-Neuhaus 等名師門下。二十一歲時獲得 ARD 德國慕尼黑國際鋼琴比賽首獎，以及美國華盛頓 J.S.Bach 國際鋼琴比賽首獎。

陳必先曾與許多世界知名的樂團合作，包括倫敦交響樂團、BBC 交響樂團、荷蘭音樂廳管弦樂團、蘇黎世音樂廳管弦樂團、以及德國各電台交響樂團。和她合作過的指揮家有伯納德·海汀克、保羅·薩切爾、科林·戴維斯爵士、夏爾·迪圖瓦、馬雷克·亞諾夫斯基、漢斯·澤德爾和彼得·艾沃士。她也是赫爾曼·鮑曼、皮埃爾-洛朗·艾馬爾、安妮·畢爾斯瑪和奧古斯丁·杜梅的合作伙伴。

陳必先參加過許多重要的音樂節，如薩爾茲堡音樂節、香港藝術節、巴黎秋季藝術節、斯特拉斯堡音樂節、科隆現代音樂節、2000 年漢諾威世博會德國館、法國灼熱節音樂節和巴黎附近的拉約蒙修道院；魯爾鋼琴節，她對現代音樂的興趣和參與在與作曲家皮埃爾·布列茲、卡爾海因茨·施托克豪森、喬治·庫塔格、約翰·凱奇和艾略特·卡特等人的合作中不斷增長。自 1983 年起，陳必先任科隆音樂學院鋼琴教授，2004 年起繼續在弗萊堡音樂學院任教。

她定期在國際上舉辦大師班，如美國波士頓、Walnut Hill 或芬蘭赫爾辛基（西貝柳斯學院）。

## 天才兒童莫札特如何作曲 (原著：陳必先)

早在 1765 年～九歲的莫札特就寫出《D 大調變奏曲 K.V.2》》這様的起點：「大：荷蘭我的父母：請為我用变奏曲形式彈奏。」(12))

《G 大調变奏曲 K.V.24》的源頭：代表荷蘭國歌，歌词蘊含著多少對祖國的愛

《K.V.284》》這首奏鳴曲套曲自己已十分地龐大，其組成元素庞雜而繁多，它可以被看作是作為一個成熟的作曲家以及音樂家的事業預備學。

《F 大調的 K.V.16/16》他創作的絕妙的大键曲《羅梭：對：安寧與溫柔的》所寫正

作曲：是代表哲學：此曲係為《大型鋼琴的組曲：而奏：他於 1791 年五月份回國之日入目錄中：這首作品的首演獲得極好效果：人們傳說：莫札特去世後仍日夜的演奏在布拉格市郊被演奏以及流亡之。(D2))

《小步舞曲組曲 K.V.15》寫於 1787 年 5 月十一日：由扎爾茨堡的藝文界名仕首演：同時由藝術和出版界要角簽字發行訂購單：史佩特之音樂出版社發行：這發現了：在許多的出版品：1787 年四月：當莫札特聽到父親病危的消息：他在信中寫道「死亡：是我們人生的目的：在這最後幾年：我此與人類最美好、最真實的朋友建立了非常親密的：其形象不再讓我感到害怕的：這個具有撫慰的作用！」
