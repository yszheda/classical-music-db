### 专辑简介

本专辑由 Pearl Records 发行，收录 20 世纪最伟大长笛家马塞尔·穆瓦兹的珍贵历史录音，录音时间从 1927 年至 1935 年。这张专辑展现了穆瓦兹无与伦比的演奏艺术：无缝流畅的抒情乐句（如圣 - 桑的《天鹅》）、精湛的技巧和完美音准（如 Sechers、Reichert 和 Doppler 炫技曲）、以及高贵的简洁（如比才的《小步舞曲》和柴可夫斯基的《如歌的行板》）。

专辑包含 17 首作品，包括：
- **比才/博尔纳**：《卡门幻想曲》- 著名的炫技作品
- **圣 - 桑（塔法内尔改编）**：《天鹅》- 展现穆瓦兹如歌的音质
- **德彪西**：《绪任克斯》- 为独奏长笛创作的印象派杰作
- **多普勒**：《匈牙利田园幻想曲》Op. 26 - 长篇炫技作品（11:17）
- **巴赫**：《波罗乃兹 - 谐谑曲》- 巴洛克经典
- **塔法内尔**：《田园行板》和《小谐谑曲》- 法国长笛学派代表作
- **格鲁克**：《极乐世界之舞》（选自《奥菲欧与欧律狄刻》）- 与巴黎交响乐团合作，亨利·托马西指挥

这些录音大多为电气录音时代早期作品，音质经过 Roger Beardsley 精心修复和转录。

### 制作人注释

从留声机最早期开始，长笛家一直是最受欢迎的录音对象，无论是独奏还是为像内莉·梅尔巴女爵士和阿梅利塔·加利 - 库尔奇这样的歌唱家伴奏。在声学录音时代，乐器录音效果出奇的好，可能是因为其音域适合这一过程，声音很容易引导到录音喇叭中。

这张 CD 上的录音都是用电传录音工艺（带有麦克风和电子放大器）录制的，其保真度大大提高。话虽如此，有时工程师只是没有将足够的电平录制到唱片上，从而使表面噪音相对于音乐更大。后来的某些伦敦录音的哥伦比亚唱片在一定程度上存在这个问题。我原本想使用 1931 年 Decca 录音的柏辽兹《基督的童年》三重奏，但录音电平太低，加上 Decca 的表面噪音像往常一样糟糕，即使使用现代降噪技术，音乐也被噪音淹没了。

### 艺术家简介

**马塞尔·穆瓦兹 (Marcel Moyse, 1889-1984)**

 arguably the finest and certainly the most influential flute player-teacher of the 20th century, Marcel Moyse's priceless series of recordings provides an exemplar that very few handbooks could ever have surpassed. Whether in the seamless, lyrical phrasing of Saint-Saëns' *Le Cygne*, technical wizardry and faultless intonation of the Sechers, Reichart and Doppler showpieces, or the noble simplicity of Ravel's *Menuet* and Tchaikovsky Andante cantabile, Moyse seemingly had it all.

Marcel Joseph Moyse was born in St. Amour, Jura, on 17 May 1889. His earliest claim to fame was having played flute in an orchestra conducted by Nikolai Rimsky-Korsakov when he was just fourteen. His tutors at the Paris Conservatoire included such legendary figures as Paul Taffanel, Adolphe Hennebains, Philippe Gaubert and Lucien Capet, and in 1906 Moyse was awarded the Premier Prix. His European career might be said to have started when he was appointed solo flautist with the Padeloup orchestra in 1910, by which time he already had a burgeoning reputation as a brilliant soloist.

Three years later Moyse was appointed principal flute with the Opéra-Comique (a post he held until 1938), and toured the United States with the great Australian diva, Dame Nellie Melba. In 1918 he took up a principal flute with the Paris Conservatoire Orchestra, and the following year he joined a similar position with the Concerts Sevihan. Also during this period he played with the Ballets Russes Orchestra. By the time he was appointed Professor of Flute at the Geneva Conservatoire in 1939, and the Paris Conservatoire in 1952, (in succession to his ex-teacher, Gaubert), he was already seasoned recording artist.

In 1930 he received the first of several Grand Prix du Disque for his recording of Mozart's D major Flute Concerto, K314. In the meantime Moyse's orchestral career continued to flourish. In 1930s he appears under the most internationally renowned conductors of the age, including Otto Klemperer, Arturo Toscanini, Bruno Walter, Willem Mengelberg, Richard Strauss, Serge Koussevitzky, and with Stravinsky and Prokofiev. Albert Roussel dedicated the first solo movement of his Op. 37 *Joueurs de flûte* (1925) to Moyse, and Jacques Ibert wrote several flute works especially for him.

In 1932, Marcel had joined the Moyse Trio with his daughter-in-law violinist, Blanche Honegger Moyse, and his son Louis as pianist. The latter had (like his father before him) recently won the Premier Prix for flute in 1932, and was essentially filling in time playing in bars and restaurants, as well as working as his father's teaching assistant at the Conservatoire. Such was the impact of the new ensemble that only five years after its formation it was awarded the Grand Prix du Disque for a recording of Bach's Triosonatas, and went on to make regular tours of Europe, South America and the USA up until 1950. Meanwhile, in 1934 Marcel had been made a member of the Légion d'honneur.

Following the Second World War, in 1949 Marcel emigrated to America, and made such a profound impact that just a year later he joined with his son Louis (who had moved to the US in 1948), Rudolf Serkin, and Adolf and Hermann Busch to establish the Marlboro Music Festival. This was to become the hub of Marcel's teaching activities for the next fifty years, and attracted some of the world's finest musicians and teachers, including Pablo Casals, Alexander Schneider, Felix Galimir, Misha Schneider, and Mieczysław Horszowski. Moyse also became a regular visitor to Switzerland, where he also gave masterclasses. In 1973 he gave a series of legendary classes in Japan, and at the grand old age of 92 conducted a concert of Mozart and Dvořák in New York. He died three years later on 1 November 1984.

Among Moyse's many students were some of the leading flautists of our time, including James Galway, Michel Debost, Trevor Wye, William Bennett, Gary Wincenc, Bernard Goldberg, Robert Aitken, Julia Bogorad and Paula Robison. Fortunately, some of Moyse's teaching has survived on videotape for the illumination of future generations.

Moyse was the author of innumerable teaching publications for the flute, including two notable classics, *De la Sonorité* and *Tone Development through Interpretation*, which are still widely in use today. In addition, he composed a vast series of pedagogical flute studies, including 20 exercises et études, 24 petites études mélodiques, 101 études faciles et progressives, 25 études de virtuosité (after Czerny), 12 études de grande virtuosité (after Chopin) and 50 Variations on the Allemande from Bach's A minor flute sonata.

The Marcel Moyse Society has announced that there is to be an Archive housed at the Lincoln Center Library for the Performing Arts in New York City, and will be catalogued as part of the Music Division Collection. The collection will include rare photographs, letters to Moyse from well-known musicians such as Gaubert, Ibert and Rampal, manuscripts of unpublished study books, and audio and videotapes of lessons and master Classes.

As Moyse himself so memorably put it: 'I long for the day when I can hear the beauty of the sound comes from the generocity [sic] of the soul.'
