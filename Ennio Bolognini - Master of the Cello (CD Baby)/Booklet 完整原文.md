### Liner Notes by Lew Green

I was about 12 years old when I met Ennio Bolognini. There he was in our suburban Chicago home wearing a gray officer's uniform with all the trimmings and an air of adventure looking to me like he was ten feet tall! He was large, gracious and very talented.

Ennio Bolognini was born into a musical family in Argentina in 1893. His father, Egidio was first cellist in orchestras under Toscanini in Argentina and Europe. A close friend of the family, Arturo Toscanini became Ennio's godfather. Ennio's mother Argia, was primera singer and voice teacher.

Bolognini first studying with his father, began early on with Jose Garcia-Jacot, Pablo Casals's teacher, at the St. Cecilia Conservatory of Music in Buenos Aires. At twelve Bolognini made his debut at the Prince George Hall, Buenos Aires. At fifteen Bolognini won first prize in an international American International Contest and was awarded the Luigi Rovati cello that he played all his life. Luigi Rovati had been a pupil of Rocca who in turn had been a pupil of Stradivarius.

While still a teenager, Bolognini performed "The Swan" with Saint-Saens on piano and performed the Cello Sonata by Richard Strauss accompanied by the composer himself.

Bolognini was a musician, a linguist who spoke Hebrew, Greek, Japanese, Hungarian, Russian and fifteen Italian dialects as well as an athlete of unusual physical courage who reached astonishing levels of achievement. His interests and accomplishments, cello and language excluded, encompassed championship boxing, professional flying, cycling, automobile racing and swimming. Bolognini became Welterweight Boxing Champion of Argentina and when he moved to the United States in 1923, Bolognini became Luis Firpo's sparring partner during Firpo's preparation for the world championship fight with Jack Dempsey. Bolognini, a professional stunt flyer co-founded the American Civil Air Patrol and trained cadets to be B-29 bombers in World War II. Bolognini's life was that of a true polymath, the Renaissance man.

Known for his fiery temper and impulsive behavior, Bolognini indignantly resigned his position as principal cellist of the Chicago Symphony Orchestra when he was unjustly accused of trying to hog the spotlight because he had stepped up in the podium to assist Giscurrow, a guest conductor, overcome by stage fright. Bolognini always took his dog to the rehearsals while he was principal cellist of the Chicago Symphony Orchestra with whom he continued to perform as a guest soloist after his fiery resignation.

Bolognini lived in Las Vegas from 1951 to his death in 1979. In Las Vegas he was the founder of the Las Vegas Philharmonic Orchestra. Bolognini asked friends to sign the cello wherever he went collecting over 51 signatures including Fritz Kreisler, Heifetz, Stern, Szigeti, Libersche, Jack Dempsey, Bruno Walter, Janos Starker, Eugene Ormandy, Miklos Rozsa and many more. Bolognini's cello can be seen at the Smithsonian Institute in Washington, D.C.

Bolognini's artistry and beautiful musicianship were magnified by the extreme passion that flowed from his body through the cello to take on a life of its own as every note escaped to fill the world with joy.

*Lew Green, Xylophonia Music Company*
