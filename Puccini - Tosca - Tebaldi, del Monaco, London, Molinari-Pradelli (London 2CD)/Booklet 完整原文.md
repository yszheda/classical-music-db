## Synopsis

**Act I**
In the aftermath of the French Revolution a libertarian and republican spirit has arisen in Italy. However, with the declaration of war on France by Ferdinand IV of Naples, the climate has turned against any movement seen as liberal and considered a treason. Rome is stifling in this situation. A "Roman republic" is established with Angelotti one of its consuls. The Austrian march on Naples and the English, and an allied republic is established there. Queen Maria Carolina, having fled to Sicily with her husband, now organizes a force which, together with the British, Russians and Austrians, drives the French from Naples. Few troops then march on Rome, re-taking the city on 30 September. A secret police force comes into existence, headed by the police chief Scarpia and his most efficient informers. The action of the opera takes place during this historical period in June 1800, with Napoleon still engaged on the banks of the Po, the Austrians imprisoned in treason, but Angelotti has again been recaptured from Castel Sant'Angelo.

**Act One** The church of Sant'Andrea della Valle. It is midday and the church is deserted. Angelotti has fled there and, seeing a key laid down in the sacristy, he runs and hides himself into the family chapel where a disguise of women's clothing has been placed for him. The sacristan returns to continue work on his painting of Mary Magdalene. The sacristan, already disapproving of the free-thinking painter, is further upset by the fact that a woman who has been watching Cavaradossi's work is about to pray (in fact, Tosca, Angelotti's sister) has been the unwitting model for the painting and that the latter has given Angelotti's clothes on the chapel. He and Cavaradossi recognize each other, but are interrupted by the arrival of Tosca. A singer and Cavaradossi's mistress, and Angelotti hides again. Tosca, unreasonably jealous, accuses her lover of seeing another woman, but Cavaradossi calms her and they arrange to meet at his villa later that evening. The painting of Mary Magdalene also arouses Tosca's suspicion. Cavaradossi has given her. He then offers Angelotti the use of his villa and describes a hiding place in the old well in the garden. A cannon shot announces that the escape has been discovered and Angelotti and Cavaradossi leave at once for the latter's villa. The sacristan returns with news of Napoleon's defeat at Marengo, and the choristers rush in to explain that the celebrations will include a cantata to be sung by Floria Tosca at the Palazzo Farnese and a Te Deum in the church.

Everything changes with the news of Angelotti's escape plan, enters the church. On interrogating the sacristan, Scarpia is convinced that the painter's wife is aware of Angelotti's return; in the open chapel he finds a fan, on which is painted the coat of arms of the Attavanti family. He immediately orders the painter's arrest. In the meantime, Cavaradossi has met Angelotti and they return to explain to Cavaradossi that the most important person in the village is the chief of police. Scarpia uses the fan to make Tosca suspicious. Cavaradossi and his supposed lover as to the villa. Tosca falls into the trap. She runs off to find Cavaradossi, but is stopped by Scarpia. Sciarrone, a police agent of Scarpia, comes back from the Palazzo Farnese and reports that Angelotti is indeed hidden in the well. Scarpia, however, now believes that Cavaradossi has betrayed him and orders his arrest. Tosca arrives just as Cavaradossi is being led away. Scarpia, who has seen the fan, becomes suspicious of Tosca and orders Spoletta to follow her.

**Act Two** The action takes place later that evening in Scarpia's rooms on the upper floor of Palazzo Farnese, where a large supper table has been laid. Cavaradossi is being questioned. A guard gives one of his men a letter for Tosca. Cavaradossi is led away to be tortured. Scarpia, a police agent, Spoletta, comes back from the Palazzo Farnese and reports that Angelotti is indeed hidden in the well. Scarpia, however, now believes that Cavaradossi has betrayed him and orders his arrest. Tosca arrives just as Cavaradossi is being led away. Scarpia, who has seen the fan, becomes suspicious of Tosca and orders Spoletta to follow her.

**Act Three** It is still dark on the open platform of Castel Sant'Angelo where Cavaradossi is due to be shot. At dawn there is heard a shepherd boy is heard in the distance; a cart slowly rolls by on its way to the market place. A guard leads Cavaradossi forward. The prison door opens and Tosca is allowed to enter. She explains to Cavaradossi that she has killed Scarpia and that the execution is to be a sham one. Cavaradossi is led out to be shot. When he falls, Tosca goes to raise him up and finds that Scarpia had ordered a real execution. She hears the sound of soldiers rushing on to the rampart and throws herself over to the parapet and kills herself.
