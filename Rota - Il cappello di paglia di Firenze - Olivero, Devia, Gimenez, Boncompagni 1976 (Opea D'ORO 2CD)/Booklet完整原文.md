## About the Composer: Nino Rota

**Nino Rota (1911-1979):**
Italian composer, one of the most important Italian composers of the 20th century. He is best known for his film scores, particularly for Federico Fellini's films (La Strada, La Dolce Vita, 8½, Amarcord) and Francis Ford Coppola's The Godfather. However, Rota also had an extensive output of operas, symphonic works, chamber music, and choral pieces.

*Il cappello di paglia di Firenze* (The Florentine Straw Hat) is one of his most successful operas. It was composed between 1937 and 1945, to a libretto adapted from the classic French farce *Un chapeau de paille d'Italie* by Eugène Labiche and Marc-Michel. The opera is a brilliant example of Rota's skill in musical comedy, combining wit, elegance, and memorable melodic invention.

## Synopsis

**Place:** Paris
**Time:** 1870

**Act I:**
It's the wedding day of Elena and Clemente. Elena's father, Minardi, has just bought a new straw hat from Florence for his daughter. As the wedding preparations are underway, the situation quickly becomes complicated...

The opera is a classic French farce full of mistaken identities, frantic chases, and witty misunderstandings. The plot revolves around a straw hat that gets accidentally eaten by a horse, leading to a series of chaotic complications as the wedding party desperately tries to find a replacement before the bride notices.

## Principal Artists

### Magda Olivero (Elena)
Magda Olivero (1910-2014) was one of the most celebrated Italian sopranos of the 20th century, particularly renowned for her performances in verismo and contemporary Italian operas. Her dramatic intensity, impeccable diction, and beautiful tonal palette made her one of the most sought-after singing actresses of her generation. She enjoyed a remarkably long career, performing into her 80s.

### Nino Sanzogno (Conductor)
Nino Sanzogno (1918-2005) was an eminent Italian conductor, closely associated with RAI Italian Radio and Television. He was a champion of 20th century Italian opera and conducted many world premiere performances. His recordings are known for their clarity and idiomatic understanding of Italian style.
