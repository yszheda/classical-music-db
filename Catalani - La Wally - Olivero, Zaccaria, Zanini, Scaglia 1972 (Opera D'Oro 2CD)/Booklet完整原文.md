## Synopsis

A bear he has killed. He gets into a fight with Stromminger, his old nemesis, and they are only separated by Stromminger's daughter Wally, a willful child of nature. Afraid that Wally has a crush on Hagenbach, Stromminger orders her to marry Gellner, which she refuses. Stromminger threatens to disown her, and after singing her sad song of farewell ("Ebben? Ne andrò lontana") Wally leaves for the mountains with Walter, her long-time platonic friend.

*Act I.* (as above)

*Act II.* One year later, Stromminger has died and Wally has returned to the village to claim her inheritance. Rejected again in his courtship, Gellner taunts Wally with the suggestion that Hagenbach is going to marry the pretty Afra, keeper of a tavern where Hagenbach has been spending time (though only to drown his yearning for Wally). During a village dance Hagenbach wins a kiss from Wally, but when she realizes from his friends' laughter that he had made a wager on her, she becomes infuriated and hints that Gellner should kill him.

*Act III.* Having heard from a drunken old soldier that Hagenbach is hanging around the village after hours, Gellner finds him one dark evening and pushes him off a cliff. Wally, meanwhile, has mitigated her anger and is horrified by Gellner's boasting. In front of the whole village she descends into the tangled ravine and brings the unconscious Hagenbach back alive. Fearing her feelings are now hopelessly compromised, Wally tearfully cedes both Hagenbach and all her worldly possessions to Afra.

*Act IV.* Wally has retreated to her mountain hut with the minstrel Walter, who one day takes his leave, wishing to return to the village for Christmas celebrations. As Wally contemplates suicide she hears Hagenbach calling to her; unable to forget her noble self-sacrifice, he has come to profess his love. As heavy snow begins to fall, the pair sing a passionate duet. They try to make their way down the mountain, but Hagenbach slips and is killed in an avalanche. Wally, mad with grief, hurls herself into the abyss to join him in death.

Bill Parker
