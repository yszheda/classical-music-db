# Transcriptions for Two Pianos

This exciting collection brings together some of the most imaginative twentieth-century arrangements for two pianos from the nineteenth-century vocal and orchestral repertoire. All the arrangers are themselves composers of distinction, and the result is a programme that shows off the qualities of modern piano writing when played by two distinguished contemporary pianists like Jean-Efflam Bavouzet and François-Frédéric Guy, demonstrating the range that different pianists bring to these colourful and imaginative transcriptions to life.

---

## Page 2

### Gioachino Rossini (1792-1868) arranged by Luigi Dallapiccola (1904-1975): *Petite Messe solennelle* – Six Transcriptions (1945)

Rossini wrote his *Petite Messe solennelle* for twelve singers, two pianos and harmonium in 1863, late in his life, after he had retired from writing opera. It is a work that combines his characteristic wit and his religious faith, and it has remained one of his most beloved works. Luigi Dallapiccola, one of the most distinguished Italian composers of the twentieth century, arranged six of the most pianistic numbers from the mass for two pianos, giving them new life in this arrangement, which was completed in 1945. The six pieces capture different moods, from the gentle opening to the brilliant closing.

### Franz Schubert (1797-1828) arranged by Anton Webern (1883-1945): Six German Dances D 145

Webern arranged six Schubert German dances for two pianos in 1931, early in his mature career. Webern's touch is recognizable from the first bar, with his crystalline textures and delicate scoring, bringing out the harmonic beauty of Schubert's original dances. The result is a magical combination of Schubert's melodic invention and Webern's modernist precision.

## Page 3

### Hector Berlioz (1803-1869) arranged by Gregor Piatigorsky (completed by Vladimir Mensah): *L'Ile heureuse* from *Béatrice et Bénédict*

Berlioz's opera *Béatrice et Bénédict* contains this beautiful orchestral interlude, "The Happy Island", which is a gentle, atmospheric piece that works beautifully for two pianos, with its flowing melody and Berlioz's characteristic harmonic colour. The arrangement by the great cellist Gregor Piatigorsky, completed by Vladimir Mensah, preserves all the beauty of the original orchestral.

### Modest Mussorgsky (1839-1881) arranged by Nikolai Rimsky-Korsakov (1844-1908): *A Night on the Bare Mountain*

This is one of the most famous of Russian orchestral showpieces, originally written for piano, then orchestrated by Rimsky-Korsakov. The transcription here is by Rimsky-Korsakov himself for two pianos, and it preserves all the wild barbaric energy of the original, with its pounding rhythms and brilliant orchestral writing that transfers perfectly to the two keyboards. It makes an extraordinary effect on two pianos, with all the excitement completely undimmed.

---

## Page 4

### Igor Stravinsky (1882-1971) arranged by the composer: Three Movements from *Petrushka*

Stravinsky himself made this transcription of three movements from his great ballet *Petrushka* for piano duo, and it is one of the most famous and effective transcriptions ever made. The three movements capture all the brilliance and rhythmic energy of the original ballet, from the opening Russian dance, through the beautiful and moving melancholy of the French street music, to the final brilliant carnival chaos. It is a spectacular showpiece for two pianos, and has remained a favourite of duo pianists ever since it was published.

### Gioachino Rossini (arr. Ottorino Respighi (1879-1936): *La Boutique fantasque* – Fairy Kisses and Can-Can

Respighi arranged the final two tableaux from Rossini's *La Boutique fantasque*, based on original piano pieces by Rossini, for piano duo. The result is brilliant, colourful and full of charm, ending with the famous Can-Can which fills the hall with joy and brilliance. It makes a perfect conclusion to this varied and exciting recital.

## Page 5 - Artists

### Jean-Efflam Bavouzet

Jean-Efflam Bavouzet was born in 1962 in Niort, France. He studied at the Paris Conservatoire, and won first prize at the Concours Géza Anda in 1986. He has since enjoyed a distinguished international career, and has recorded a number of highly acclaimed discs for Chandos, including the complete Ravel piano works which have won many awards. He is recognised as one of the leading French pianists of his generation.

### François-Frédéric Guy

François-Frédéric Guy was born in 1964 in Versailles, France. He studied with Germaine Devèze and then with Christian Ivaldi at the Paris Conservatoire, and he quickly established himself as one of the most imaginative and dynamic pianists on the French scene, appearing with all the leading orchestras and at many festivals. He has a particular reputation for twentieth-century repertoire and contemporary music, and has recorded many acclaimed discs. He works regularly as a duo with Jean-Efflam Bavouzet and this is their second duo recording for Chandos.
