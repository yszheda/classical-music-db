## 作曲家介绍
Sergei Lyapunov (1859-1924) was a Russian composer, pianist and conductor, a prominent member of the "New Russian School" inspired by the nationalist ideals of Mily Balakirev. Born in Yaroslavl, he studied at the Moscow Conservatory before moving to St. Petersburg, where he became part of the circle of composers surrounding Balakirev.

Deeply influenced by Franz Liszt, whom he greatly admired, Lyapunov composed his 12 *Études d'exécution transcendante* between 1897 and 1905 as a deliberate continuation and completion of Liszt's own set of 12 Transcendental Études. Where Liszt's cycle covers all the major and minor keys from C major through B minor, Lyapunov's cycle continues from B minor through to B major, creating a complete cycle of all 24 keys when combined with Liszt's set.

Like Liszt's études, Lyapunov's works are not merely technical exercises, but highly evocative character pieces that draw heavily on Russian folk music, poetic imagery and natural phenomena. The titles evoke a wide range of scenes and moods, from the gentle *Berceuse* (Lullaby) and peaceful *Idylle*, to the dramatic *Tempête* (Storm) and the majestic concluding *Élégie en mémoire de François Liszt*, a profound tribute to his great predecessor.

Long overshadowed by more famous Russian contemporaries such as Rachmaninoff and Scriabin, Lyapunov's Transcendental Études are only now beginning to receive the recognition they deserve as important contributions to the late Romantic piano repertoire.

Florian Noack, born in 1990 in Belgium, is one of the most promising pianists of his generation. A prizewinner at numerous international competitions, he has performed extensively across Europe and beyond, earning particular acclaim for his interpretations of the Russian repertoire and lesser-known Romantic works.
