## Synopsis

### Act One

**A hill near Nagasaki**

On a hill near Nagasaki, United States Navy Lieutenant B. F. Pinkerton is being shown the little house on the hill which he has leased and is about to occupy with his Japanese wife. Goro, the marriage broker who arranged the match, finds Pinkerton enjoying the ingenious contrivances of the building. When she finds a quiet moment, Butterfly tells Pinkerton that since the death of her father she has had to support herself and her mother as a geisha. She has secretly renounced her ancestors' faith in order to embrace Christianity.

Suzuki and two other servants enter and are introduced to the new master of the house. The Imperial Commissioner arrives and the marriage ceremony takes place. After a final word of caution to Pinkerton, Sharpless, the American Consul, leaves with the Japanese officials. The gay festivities are suddenly interrupted when Butterfly's uncle, the Bonze, rushes in shouting wildly. A priest, he has discovered her renunciation of the faith of her fathers and has come to denounce her. He insists that all her relatives renounce her forever. When Pinkerton, annoyed, threatens him with physical assault, the Bonze and all the guests quickly leave. Butterfly is left weeping bitterly. Pinkerton gently consoles her, and in the thought of his love, she is happy once again.

Their conversation is interrupted by the arrival of Butterfly and her girl friends. After everyone is introduced, Sharpless asks Pinkerton about the marriage: "for nine hundred and ninety-nine years, but subject to monthly renewal!" Sharpless warns that the girl may not regard her vows as lightly as Pinkerton does, that all of her relatives have renounced her forever. But the lieutenant laughs at his friend's fears and proposes a toast to America and the American girl who will someday be his "real" wife. Night falls over the garden and a long passionate love duet is sung by the couple.

### Act Two

**Inside Butterfly's House**

Three years have passed and Pinkerton has left Nagasaki, promising that he will return when robins nest again. Butterfly insists that this does not apply in America, that he will definitely return. Suzuki is praying before an image of Buddha. She implores the gods to help her poor mistress who still waits in confident expectation for her husband's return. Sharpless manages to tell Butterfly of the letter he has received from Pinkerton. He starts to read; every phrase is interpreted by Butterfly as some happy assurance that her husband will return. Finally the Consul makes her realize the true meaning of the letter. She recoils as if struck. After a moment she recovers, rushes into the other room and returns with her baby. As soon as Pinkerton knows of his son, he will return to them. Deeply moved, Sharpless leaves, fearful for the future.

Sharpless and Goro arrive. The Consul has been unable to break the news gently to Butterfly of Pinkerton's remarriage to an American girl. Butterfly insists that it cannot be true, she has perfect faith that her husband will come back. A cannon shot booms in the harbour. Seizing a telescope, Butterfly discovers that it is Pinkerton's ship entering the harbour. Her happiness knows no bounds. She was right! Her husband is returning to her! Without losing any time, she orders Suzuki to help her decorate the house with blossoms from the garden. Then as night approaches, she puts on her wedding gown and with her son and Suzuki, waits by the door for her husband's arrival.

### Act Three

**Inside Butterfly's House**

The long night of waiting has passed. The grey light of dawn finds Butterfly still standing, watching, waiting. The sunshine finally awakens Suzuki and the baby, who have been asleep. Butterfly persuades Suzuki to take the baby and go and rest. Before long Butterfly orders Suzuki to close the curtains and draw them back, then bids the sobbing maid join the child in the garden. When Suzuki realizes who the strange woman is, she breaks down in despair. Out of consideration for her mistress, however, she agrees reluctantly to help break the news. Pinkerton is already in the room with Sharpless when Butterfly comes in with the child. She can barely stand when the truth begins to dawn upon her. He sees Kate, Pinkerton's new American wife, and collapses at his feet. She dies as Pinkerton is heard calling her name.
