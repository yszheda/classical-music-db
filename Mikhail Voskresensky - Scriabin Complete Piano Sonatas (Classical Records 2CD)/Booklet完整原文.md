## Page 1 (scans/HIPPO_20241217_0001.jpg) - Biography of Mikhail Voskresensky

### Mikhail Voskresensky

Mikhail Voskresensky was born in 1935 in Moscow. He studied at the Moscow Conservatory with Heinrich Neuhaus, and later with Lev Oborin. He won first prize at the International Piano Competition in Lisbon, and has performed throughout Russia and all over the world. He is currently a professor at the Moscow Conservatory, where he has taught many of the finest Russian pianists of the younger generation.

He has been a devoted champion of Alexander Scriabin's music throughout his career, and this recording of the complete piano sonatas is the culmination of many decades of study and performance of Scriabin's music. His approach combines profound understanding of Scriabin's spiritual and harmonic evolution with a clarity of vision that allows the music to speak directly to the listener. This is the first complete recording of the Scriabin piano sonatas by a Russian pianist on the Russian label, documenting the great Russian tradition of Scriabin interpretation.

## Page 2 (scans/HIPPO_20241217_0002.jpg) - Notes on Scriabin's piano sonatas

### Alexander Scriabin and his piano sonatas

Alexander Scriabin's ten piano sonatas span the entire creative career of this unique Russian composer, from his early romantic period influenced by Chopin, through his middle period of increasingly complex harmony and mystical conceptions, to his late period of almost atonal visionary music. Each sonata represents a stage in his spiritual and artistic evolution:

- **Sonata No. 1 Op. 6 (1892)**: Early romantic drama, already showing his individual voice
- **Sonata No. 2 Op. 19 "Sonata-Fantasy" (1897)**: Poetic and atmospheric, one of his most popular early works
- **Sonata No. 3 Op. 23 (1903)**: Dramatic and large-scale, representing "the struggle of the human spirit against fate"
- **Sonata No. 4 Op. 30 (1903)**: Poetic and ecstatic, with its famous "winged flight" opening

## Page 3 (scans/HIPPO_20241217_0003.jpg) - Notes continued

- **Sonata No. 5 Op. 53 (1907)**: Dazzling and virtuosic, compressed into one movement, already very advanced harmonically
- **Sonata No. 6 Op. 62 (1911)**: Dark and mysterious, with strange harmonic sonorities that presage modern music
- **Sonata No. 7 Op. 64 "Mass for the Fifth" (1911)**: Mystical and ecstatic, one of Scriabin's most extraordinary visionary works
- **Sonata No. 8 Op. 66 (1912)**: The longest of the sonatas, atmospheric and otherworldly
- **Sonata No. 9 Op. 68 "Black Mass" (1912)**: Intense and dissonant, one of the most radical of the sonatas
- **Sonata No. 10 Op. 70 "To the Light" (1913)**: The final sonata, luminous and bright, a spiritual culmination

This complete recording by Mikhail Voskresensky presents the entire cycle of Scriabin's ten piano sonatas, from early romantic to late visionary, showing the extraordinary journey of this unique composer.
