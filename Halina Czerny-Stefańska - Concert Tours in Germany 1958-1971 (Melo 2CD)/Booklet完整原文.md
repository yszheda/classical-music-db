### Halina Czerny-Stefańska (1911-2001)

Halina Czerny-Stefańska was one of the most celebrated Polish pianists of the twentieth century and one of the greatest interpreters of Chopin of all time. Born in Warsaw in 1911, she studied first with her mother pianist Maria Czerny-Stefańska, then with Józef Turczyński at the Warsaw Conservatory. In 1937 she shared the First Prize at the International Chopin Piano Competition in Warsaw with Lev Oborin, which launched her international career. She was particularly admired for her Chopin playing, which was characterized by a beautiful tone, natural rhythmic freedom and deep poetic insight. She taught for many years at the Fryderyk Chopin University of Music in Warsaw, and her students include many of today's leading Polish pianists.

This double CD presents previously unpublished live recordings from Halina Czerny-Stefańska's concert tours in Germany in 1958, 1968 and 1971, compiled from German radio archives. It includes a wide selection of her Chopin interpretations, plus Brahms and a piece by Scriabin, recorded live in concert, capturing her playing in live performance in these spontaneous, inspired readings. These recordings are being issued here for the first time commercially, preserving documented her art in live performance from the peak of her career.

### About these recordings

These recordings come from the archives of the Norddeutscher Rundfunk (NDR) in Hamburg, where Halina Czerny-Stefańska gave concerts during her tours of Germany in 1958, 1968 and 1971. The recordings were made for radio broadcast, and they have been preserved in the archive ever since. This is the first time they have been released commercially on CD. They capture her live performances from different stages of her career, showing the evolution of her interpretations over more than a decade. The sound has been carefully restored and remastered from the original tapes.

### Halina Czerny-Stefańska on Chopin

Halina Czerny-Stefańska always had a special connection with the music of Chopin. As the co-winner of the first prize at the Chopin Competition in 1937, she was recognized throughout her life as one of the supreme masters of Chopin's piano music. Her interpretations were always characterized by a deep respect for the text, a natural sense of rhythm and rubato that came from her Polish roots, and a poetic warmth that was entirely her own. These live recordings show her Chopin playing at its finest, spontaneous and inspired in a concert setting.
