### Page 2-3 (Russian text, English section follows)

В музыкальном искусстве ХХ века, полном трагизма, рефлексии, часто – эмоциональной и интеллектуальной отстраненности, искусство Арама Хачатуряна (1903–1978) – «остров радости». В его музыке – поразительный неиссякаемый оптимизм. Яркие краски и контрасты вызывают непреложные ассоциации из мира изобразительного искусства. Едва ли не лучший живописный образ – в словах композитора, начиная Бориса Асафьева: в середине 1930-х он называл Хачатуряна «Рубенсом русских» его музыку был окрещен как искусство «высокого Возрождения».

Хачатурян – практически первый композитор Востока, ставший классиком на Западе. At the base of his art lies Armenian folklore (the tumultuous, impulsive or, on the other hand, endless-ly plaintive improvisations of the folk singers, the ashugs) as well as the 19th and 20th century European tradition (the "Eastern" pages of the "Mighty Five," especially Borodin and Rimsky-Korsakov) and the novel ideas of Prokofiev and Ravel in the sphere of musical language and orchestration). The endless monody of the East and the finite, symmetrical and, of course, the polyphonic musical form of the West present themselves as the principles of musical organization, in many ways, antithetical to each other, which had combined together in Khachaturian's musical legacy.

Aram Khachaturian was born on June 6, 1903 in Tbilisi (Tbilisi). A son of a bookbinder (who later became the owner of a bookbinding workshop), he learned musical notation only at the age of 19. Prior to that time, his musical experience had been the accumulation of musical impressions, predominantly within the realm of folklore, as well as spontaneous attempts at their realization. Khachaturian was permeated like a sponge with the many-voiced and many-language folklore of the Caucasus, which was later to become one of the foundations as well as the inexhaustible source of his musical creativity.

Upon arriving in Moscow in 1922 Khachaturian became a student of the Gnessins' Musical Secondary School (which was later to become the Gnessins' Musical College), first as a cello student and later (from 1925) as a composition student under the tutelage of Mikhail Gnessin. From 1929 to 1934 Khachaturian had been a student of Moscow Conservatory, studying with Gnessin during the first year and, subsequently, with Nikolai Myaskovsky.

One of the greatest musical teachers in Russia, along with Sergei Taneyev, Myaskovsky directed his pupils not on his own composition by cultivating their mental outlook and "placing" compositional technique "into their hands". Hence, the creative aspirations of his pupils differed greatly: such composers as Shebalin, Golubev and Kabalevsky could very well be classified as traditionalists, whereas Mosolov and Prokofiev – as avant-gardists. Khachaturian, who within the time-frame of a few years had turned into one of the best Soviet composers, presents himself as, possibly, one of the best examples of Myaskovsky's educational genius.

The instrumental and symphonic gear in Khachaturian's music had been determined back in his years at the Conservatory. At the same time, the instrumental sphere included both solo instrumen-

The turn of the 1930's and the 1940's coincides with the beginning of the theatrical trend in the composer's legacy; his first ballet "Happiness" appears (written in 1939; in its second, considerably revised version of 1941, it has received the title "Gayanè"), as well as music to dramatic theatrical plays, among which especially notable is the incidental music to the play "The Masquerade" by Mikhail Lermontov (staged by the Vakhtangov Theater in 1941). The "Waltz" from the incidental music to the "Masquerade" along with the "Saber Dance" from the ballet "Masquerade" ballet "Spartacus" are among some of the greatest hits in Khachaturian's music as well as of classical 20th century music in general.

During the years of the Great Patriotic War of 1941-1945 Khachaturian's music had obtained new colors. His most important composition of the war period, the Second Symphony, otherwise known as the *Symphony with a Bell*, presents itself as a high tragedy, inspired by the suffering and the heroic struggle of the Soviet people against German Nazism. However, in this work too, a humorous trend, characteristic of Khachaturian's music, has made itself perceptible.

After the successes of the previous years, at the end of the 1940's a dramatic turning-point happened in Khachaturian's career: he was part of a group of Soviet composers, accused of "formalism" and "anti-populism" in the ruefully famous Degree of the Central Committee of the Communist Party, made in 1948. His Third Symphony from 1947 had been severely criticized. It was during these years particularly that the peak of the composer's activities within the sphere of incidental music (for films and dramatic theatrical plays).

Since the beginning of the 1950's Khachaturian had not only been a public figure but also a teacher and a conductor. Among Khachaturian's pupils at Moscow Conservatory and the Gnessins' Russian Academy of Music there have subsequently become famous many authors: Andrei Eshpai (who prior to 1950 had been a pupil of Myaskovsky), Edgar Hovhannisyan, Eduard Khagagortyan, Mikael Tariverdiev, Kirill Volkov, Tolib Shakhidi, Alexei Rybnikov, Mark Minkov and Bogdan Trotsyuk. Khachaturian had conducted numerous performances of his compositions with the major Soviet and Western orchestras.

The "dark period" in Khachaturian's career had finished along with the end of Stalin's era in 1953. Already in the following year Khachaturian had completed one of his most noteworthy compositions – the ballet "Spartacus". Despite the awarding of the Lenin Award, granted to him in 1959 – Khachaturian had repeatedly made numerous revisions in the ballet throughout the 1960's. Out of the three scenic versions made for the Bolshoi Theatre made, respectively, in 1958, 1963 and 1968) the last one is the most well-known, which had been carried out by the ballet-master Yuri Grigorovich (whose choreography was utilized at the premiere of this final version, conducted by Gennady Rozhdestvensky, incorporating scenery by Simon Virsaladze), – it was also conferred with the Lenin Award in 1970.

To a certain degree, the last two decades of the composer's work present themselves as a return to his sources. A trilogy of Concerto-Rhapsodies for his favorite instruments, the violin, cello and the piano (respectively, in 1961, 1963 and 1968). Likewise, three solo sonatas were written at that time – for violin, cello and viola.

Aram Khachaturian passed away in Moscow on May 1, 1978, a little over a month prior to his seventy-fifth birthday.

The composer was buried in the Pantheon of Armenian Cultural Workers in Yerevan.

In Khachaturian's first masterpiece, the **Concerto for Piano and Orchestra in D flat major**, composed in 1936, his own personal style is organically intertwined with the groundwork of the genre's history. A priority for the composer was the romantic model of the piano concerto. The key, the sound image and the textural aspect of the concerto's initial theme are close to the theme of the introduction of Tchaikovsky's First Piano Concerto, whereas the general principles of the dialogue between the piano and the orchestra are similar to Rachmaninov's Piano Concertos. However, there are two more composers contemporary to Khachaturian, whose influence should be especially noted. The concerto's overall construction (the initial theme being repeated in the Coda of the final, third movement), as well as the character of the piano part in general (graphical qualities, as well as the accentuation of percussive sounds) demonstrate a similarity between Khachaturian's Concerto and Prokofiev's First Piano Concerto (composed in the same key). In particular, the themes of the primary theme groups of both concertos are very close to each other. Let us observe that from the time of the Conservatory when Myaskovsky's composition class, Prokofiev's music had remained for Khachaturian a strongest impression and creative impulse in the sphere of contemporary music. Another contemporary composer, who had influenced first of all Khachaturian's harmonic language and orchestral style was Maurice Ravel.

In one movement, what is needed the composer describes: "I sincerely wanted to compose for the thirtieth anniversary of the October Revolution something large, solemn and extraordinary. I sought for means for expressing my grandiose ideas. (...) I wanted to write such a composition, in which the listener would perceive my unwritten program. I wanted to express in this composition a feeling of happiness and pride of the Soviet people for their great (...) Motherland. However, as it seems, I was mistaken in my choice of technical means, and could not duly observe a sense of measure." This is how Khachaturian spoke in January 1948, at the conference of the Communist Party about his *Third Symphony (Symphony-Poem)* in C major for orchestra, 15 trumpets and organ. In the choice of the unusual ensemble, most likely, the composer had felt the influence of Prokofiev: his "Ode to the End of the War" was written for symphony orchestra, eight harps and four pianos.

The premiere of the work took place in Leningrad on December 13, 1947, with the Leningrad Philharmonic Orchestra; the Moscow premiere took place on December 25 of that year, but this time with the participation of the USSR State Symphony Orchestra. In both concerts the organ part was performed by Isay Braudo, and the conductor was Yevgeny Mravinsky. Both premieres were very successful, which is why the subsequent devastating astating condemnation, carried out only a month later by the Party activists, was even more unexpected.

A contemporary view of the Third Symphony should most likely be a two-sided one: on one hand Khachaturian was not able to adjust the "outward" form of a "jubilee composition" with the "inward," psychological form, while, on the other hand, in the Symphony one could discern a search on the part of the composer for a new musical language and new contrasts of sound. This is especially noticeable in the juxtaposition of the dazzling fanfare motive of the trumpets with a tumultuous organ improvisation and subsequently, in the sharply dissonant combination of these sound layers.

The one-movement Symphony is essentially in Sonata form; the theme of its first theme group is presented by a trumpet fanfare; the theme of the subsidiary theme group (likewise in C major) is "relatively Eastern," reminiscent of either an Eastern or a Spanish theme. In the coda, the subsidiary theme presents itself as a grandiose dance in a brilliant orchestral "attire". A continuous accumulation of orchestral effects increases the "Spanish" associations (for instance, with Ravel's "Bolero"), as well as similarities with certain fragments of Debussy's and De Falla's music.

### Page 10-11 (Kirill Kondrashin biography - Russian text, English translation on following pages)

Кирилл Петрович Кондрашин (1914–1981) родился в семье оркестровых музыкантов. Провел в детстве многие часы на репетициях, он уже в 14 лет принял зрелое решение стать дирижером. В 1932–1936 гг. он учился в Московской консерватории у Б.Я. Хайкина. С 1931 г. Кирилл Кондрашин начинал дирижировать сначала в Театре юного зрителя, а с 1934 г. в Музыкальном театре им. В.И. Немировича-Данченко. Так началась первая творческая биография К.П. Кондрашина, когда он был оперным дирижером по преимуществу, время от времени выступал с симфоническими программами. В 1936 г. он становится дирижером Малого оперного театра в Ленинграде, в 1943 г. – Большого театра в Москве.

Решительный по всем, что касается вопросов искусства, К.П. Кондрашин предпринял неординарный шаг: неудовлетворительный практикой подготовки оперных спектаклей, в 1956 г. он ушел из главного театра Советского Союза и на несколько лет остался дирижером без постоянного оркестра, работая с разными коллективами. Последнее выступление К.П. Кондрашина в театре состоялось в 1958 г. в Чикаго, когда он дирижировал оперой Дж. Пуччини «Мадам Баттерфляй» с Ренатой Те-бальди и Джузеппе ди Стефано в главных ролях.

В 1960 г. Кондрашин встал во главе оркестра Московской государственной филармонии, которым руководил до 1975 г. Это был новый коллектив, созданный в 1951 г. и в начале носивший имя Московского молодежного оркестра. В сотрудничестве с оркестром проявлялась кропотливой репетиционной работы. Следует вспомнить, что с этим оркестром К.П. Кондрашинным были впервые исполнены многие новейшие произведения Александра Шостаковича: Четвертая (1961) и Тринадцатая (1962) симфонии.

Едва ли не главное препятствие, затруднявшее работу дирижера и в конце концов заставившее его покинуть оркестр, – «текучая» кадров: пройдя школу Кондрашина, музыканты уходили в более престижные (в финансовом и карьерном отношении) коллективы. В 1975 году Кондрашин покинул пост главного дирижера, а в декабре 1978 г. не вернулся из зарубежной поездки и остался в Амстердаме. Там он занял место главного приглашенного дирижера знаменитого Концертгебау-оркестра, художественным руководителем которого в то время был Бернард Хайтинк, высоко ценил творческое сотрудничество и личное общение с К.П. Кондрашиным. Перед дирижером открывались новые перспективы: он согласился возглавить оркестр Баварского радио, собирался вернуться к работе в опере и дирижировать в Ла Скала. Но эти планы не осуществились. 7 марта 1981 года К.П. Кондрашин внезапно умер после концерта, в котором дирижировал Первой симфонии Малера.

**Леонид Петров**

During the late 1950s, Kondrashin became well known as a symphony conductor owing to many tours and performances with famous soloists - S. Richter, E. Gilels, D. Oistrakh, V. Cliburn. In 1960, he became director of Moscow State Philharmonic Orchestra, which he managed until 1975. This was a new team created in 1951 and initially named Moscow Youth Orchestra. It was with this particular orchestra that Kondrashin first performed Shostakovich's Fourth (1961) and Thirteenth (1962) Symphonies.

Kondrashin's progress towards achieving his objectives was strongly hindered by a factor that was the key reason for his leaving the orchestra: musicians trained by Kondrashin accepted invitations to other, better paid orchestras. In 1975, Kondrashin had to resign and in December 1978 he never came back from a tour and stayed in Amsterdam where he took the position of Principal Guest Conductor of the famous Concertgebouw Orchestra. Its artistic director at the time was Bernard Haitink who thought highly of his co-operation and personal contact with Kondrashin. New opportunities were available to the conductor; he agreed to become Director of Bavarian Radio Orchestra, intended to revert to the opera and conduct in La Scala. But these plans were never carried out. On 7 March 1981, Kondrashin suddenly died after a concert where he conducted Mahler's First Symphony.
