## Page 3 (English notes)

"I do not consider myself a composer in the usual sense of the word. Each composer, being a master of his craft, (...) decides the style of the composition beforehand, determines the future theoretical aims, thinks over, invents and tries out on the keyboard the appropriate themes. In my case, the process is not such. I understand quite well the verses of Alexander Blok:

> I wait for a new, previously inconceivable bell ring
> To frighten away my utter boredom.
> I wait for my own sort of inspiration". – Samuil Feinberg had said to his friend, composer Anatoly Alexandrov. The "previously inconceivable bell ring" could be hear up to now in the music of Feinberg, as well as his piano performances, which are simultaneously original and inspired, frequently occurring truly following some kind of ingenuous inspiration.

Feinberg is first of all known as one of the most outstanding representatives of the Russian school of piano playing. The attraction and the unique intellectual might of his piano playing – now this could be said in all truth – has outlived the musician. Feinberg's art lives on both in the numerous gramophone recordings, produced for the most part between 1938 and 1961, and in the pianist's direct pupils. However, Feinberg's own musical compositions are forgotten, for the most part.

Nonetheless, it must be remembered that in the 1920's Feinberg's music enjoyed as much success as his performances at the piano. He was the first musician to perform in tours abroad as a representative of the Russian post-revolutionary milieu of composers – this was back in 1923. During those years Feinberg's name as a composer was inseparable from such names as Myaskovsky, Shebalin, Alexandrov and Goedicke. One of his piano sonatas (the Sixth), and also a cycle of piano Preludes, have been published by the major Austrian music press, the Universal Edition. Neither indulging in the extremities of avant-garde trends in the 1920's, nor adjusting himself to the standard "populist" trends in the later era of Socialist Realism (being an ingenuous performer, Feinberg could have very well permitted himself to engage in the latter), the composer remained true to his calling and up to the end of his life had preserved his interesting and original style, which had transformed itself throughout the years.

The first compositional endeavors by Feinberg pertain to his years of piano studies with Alexander B. Goldenweiser, and little is known about them. The young Feinberg learned harmony and composition with Nikolai Zhilyayev, a pupil of Sergei Taneyev. Unlike his teacher, Zhilyayev had been one of the innumerable "Scriabinists" of Moscow, and Feinberg had found a like-minded person in him. Scriabin had served for the young composer as a sort of primary impulse towards the formation of his own personal compositional style. The initial Scriabinistic "impulse" was, in Feinberg's case, considerably weighed down by an exquisite polyphonic approach. Due to the logic of contrapuntal development, the harmonies do not soar upwards in inspired, Scriabin-like manner, but rather bounce back resiliently, remaining bound to their sturdy, solid foundation on the ground (just like the elaborate texture of Bach's Chorale Preludes ends up being bound the melodies of the chorales). This creates an entirely new type of expression, which is closer rather to such composers as, for instance, Myaskovsky. This is especially apparent in the piano sonatas from

As is well-known, Feinberg performed the entire "Well-Tempered Clavier" by Bach in the course of three concerts as early as 1914, and had subsequently repeated his cycle in performances numerous times. Fortunately, on the seventy-second year of his life he recorded it. however, the result of his other titanic labor – on the cycle of all 32 of Beethoven's piano sonatas – so to speak, has sunk into oblivion. The first time he performed this cycle in the year of his fiftieth anniversary, during the 1940/41 season and had performed it numerous times since then. Out of the 32 sonatas, only five turned out to be recorded on tape. Listening to the Eleventh Piano Sonata, included on this compact disc, along with Feinberg's own music, one begins to understand better the nature of his pianistic interpretations, which would very likely be utterly different, had Feinberg not thought of himself as a composer.

The phenomenon of a pianist-composer as a reasoning co-author of the great, classical composers, presents itself, unfortunately, as dwindling and, possibly, irrevocably disappearing in our time of a total standardization and specialization in performance. All the more valuable becomes to us the memory of that, which was present among us only recently – literally in the memory of two generations, and all with greater care we have a duty to preserve it and to study thoroughly what has been preserved of it for us.

## Page 5 (Russian notes)

«Я не считаю себя композитором в обырном смысле слова. Каждый композитор – мастер своего дела – (...) заранее решает стиль вещи, определяет будущие теоретические установки, обдумывает, придумывает, подбирает на клавиатуре подходящие темы. У меня дело обстоит не так. Я вполне понимаю строчки Блока:

> - Жду, чтоб спутнул мою скуку смертельную
> Новый, доселе немыслимый звон.
> Я жду своего рода наития...» – говорил Самуил Фейнберг своему другу, композитору Анатолию Александрову. «Доселе немыслимый звон» слышится и сейчас в музыке Фейнберга, и в его пианистических трактовках – одновременно оригинальных и вдохновенных, часто сложившихся действительно по какому-то гениальному наитию.

Фейнберг известен в первую очередь как выдающийся представитель русской фортепианной школы. Обаяние и неповторимая интеллектуальная мощь его пианизма – сейчас это уже точно можно сказать - пережили музыканта. Искусство Фейнберга живет и во многих грамзаписях, произведенных в основной массе в 1938-1961 гг., и в непосредственных учениках пианиста. Однако композиторское творчество Фейнберга теперь почти забыто.

А ведь в 1920-е гг. композиторские работы Фейнберга пользовалось не меньшим успехах, чем его пианистическое искусство. Он был первым музыкантом, выступившим на зарубежных гастролях в качестве репрезентанта русского пореволюционного композиторского сообщества, - еще в 1923 году. Имя Фейнберга – композитора в те годы было неотделимо от имен Мясковского, Шебалина, Александрова, Гедике. Одна из его фортепианных сонат (б-я), а также цикл прелюдий были изданы крупнейшим австрийским издательством Universal-Edition. Не примыкая ни к крайностям авангардизма в 20-е, не пристраивался к общему "народному" хоря в более позднюю эпоху социалистического реализма (будучи гениальным исполнителем, Фейнберг мог себе это позволил), композитор остался верен себе и до конца дней сохранил интересный и оригинальный стиль, трансформировавшийся с годами.

Первые композиторские опыты Фейнберга относятся еще к годам учебы в фортепианном классе А. Б. Гольденвейзера, и о них нам мало что известно. Молодой Фейнберг занимался гармонией и композицией с Николаем Жиляевым, учеником Сергея Танеева. В отличие от своего учителя, Жиляев был одним из многочисленных московских "скрябинистов", и Фейнберг нашел в нем своего единомышленника. Скрябин послужил для молодого композитора неким первоначальным импульсом к формированию своего, особого композиторского почерка. Первоначальный, скрябинский "порыв" у Фейнберга изначально оказался отягощен довольно изощренной полифонией. В силу логики полифонического развития гармонии уже больше не метят призмы, по-скрябински, - взвысь, - а, скорее, "пружинят", оставаясь по прочном, земном основании (как затейливая фактура баховских хоральных прелюдий оказывается привязана к мелодии хорала). Это создает совершенно другой тип экспрессии, более близкий, например, к Мясковскому. Особенно это заметно в сонатах 1920-х гг. – Шестой и Седьмой, в Первом фортепианном концерте. А в средние и поздние годы – ведь Фейнберг сочинял музыку до конца дней – об уменьчении Скрябиным напоминают едва заметные люлисы. Например, Двенадцатая фортепианная соната и Скрипичная соната, написанные уже в 1950-х – 1960-х гг. – музыка, заметно отличающиеся от того, что именуется "осколом большого стиля". Их угловатость, "некомфортность», очевидные странности стиля в сочетании с философской умудренностью содержания свидетельствуют о том, что автор пытался до конца своих дней искать нечто новое в выразительной области.

Необычайно экспрессивный стиль Фейнберга начал упрощаться, однако, уже в середине 1930-х гг. В этом отразилась общая тенденция советской музыки того времени. Соната № 2, сочиненная в 1936 году, характерна для этой эпохи стилистического перелома. На смену масштабному, драматическому развитию, пышной фактуре, приходит уменьчение классической экономностью выразительных средств, камерные высказывания, появляется кантилена, отдаленно сопоставима с той, что мы слышим у другого композитора, также любимого Фейнбергом – Николая Метнера. Соната имела большой успех на концерте, где Фейнберг, помимо собственных сочинений, представлял музыку своего друга, Аи. Александрова, 19 ноября 1938 года, а вскоре после этого была записана на грампластинку.

Второй фортепианный концерт в четырех движениях, который был композирован в 1945 году, likewise to a certain degree carries in itself traces of Feinberg's fascination with the music of Medtner. At the same time, once again, it reflects the general tendency towards a large-scale form, pomp, grandeur of expressive means, so characteristic of Soviet music of the last years of World War II and the first years after the war. This is what Feinberg's pupil Victor Bunin, a great connoisseur of teacher's musical legacy, writes about Second Piano Concerto:

*"From the very first sounds of the rhythmically energetic primary dotted formula in the timpani and the resilient syncopations a mood of vivacity and cheerful impatience is established. In the pizzicato bass line leaps, in the pungent eighth notes of the accompanying voices (...) a huge reserve of young forceful energy. In the first movement Feinberg utilizes a traditional, classical formal structure with a traditional double exposition. In its first statement, carried out by the orchestra, in addition to the main theme, there are also two additional themes present: a song-like, canonical transition theme and a light, elegant conclusory theme. (...) The subsidiary theme appears only in the exposition of the solo piano. Flowing out of the transition theme in an organic fashion, it acquires a special type of warm and affectionate character. The development section, (...) rather small in its proportions, is entirely based on the concerto's invocatory formula. Its repeated occurrences in the forms of interchange alternately in the orchestra and the piano part gradually come to a dramatic intensification and the main climax of the main movement, which coincides with the beginning of the recapitulation. The recapitulation is more compact in its size compared with the exposition. Once again, the invocatory horn-call of the main dotted phrase is sounded out in the

Following the tradition of the four-movement sonata and symphonic cycle, Feinberg wrote the third movement in the genre of a Scherzo. The Finale begins with a statement of a graceful dance theme in the solo piano. The overall elated, joyous mood is complemented by humorous mimicking interplay of short phrases between the piano and orchestra. The triplet repetitions in the manner of a toccata (...) make the second theme related to a tarantella. Towards the exposition the swift carnival-like whirlwinds gradually subside, and (...) slowly, with the prospect of completely subsiding the first theme is sounded out pensively – first in the oboe, then in the horn. Subsequently, in the next episode the new theme, as if repeating the beginning of the main theme, which had just sounded out prior to that, later undergoes a totally different type of development. Starting as an improvisation, this episode, gradually extending and broadening the intonations of meditation, present in the themes, steadily gathers a greater amount of emotional content and finally leads to a powerful climax, which alters the atmosphere of festivity of the finale and compels the listener to ponder upon the eternal questions of existence. An abundance of light, cheerfulness, energy and carnival-like qualities becomes completely counterbalanced by a wise perspective of life and an ambiguous understanding of it."

Soon after the triumphal success of the composition, Feinberg, just as the other musicians of his circle, was accused of "formalism". The party ideologues in the musical sphere could not be gratified even by such a "softened" style of Feinberg (compared to the expressionistic style of the 1920's), which, moreover, had obtained features of ostentation and pomp. It is partially for this reason that there are not too many performances by Feinberg of his own music: the two compositions, included in this compact disc, make up practically the entire legacy of Feinberg's recordings of his music, which are available to us (with the exception of another variant of a recording of two movements from this same Concerto, made from the broadcast during an anniversary concert in 1960). There are other disappointing "lacunae" in other chapters of Feinberg's discography.

In his middle and late-period style – since, after all, Feinberg had composed music up to the end of his life – the traces of the composer's fascination with Scriabin could be perceived only by scarcely discernible nuances. Feinberg's intensively expressive style, however, had started to turn to a new simplicity already in the middle of the 1930's. This was reflected by the overall tendency in Soviet music at that time. Suite No.2, which was composed in 1936, is very exemplary of that time period of stylistic turning point. The large-scale dramatic development and the splendidly elaborate musical textures were supplanted by a turn to a classical thrift of expressive means and a chamber-like mode of utterance, appearance of cantilena, remotely commensurable with that, which we could hear in the music of another composer, whom Feinberg admired – Nikolai Medtner. The Suite had an enormous amount of success at a concert, where Feinberg, in addition to performing his own compositions, had presented the music of his friend, Anatoly Alexandrov, which happened on November 19, 1938, and shortly afterwards was recorded.

Как известно, Фейнберг еще в 1914 году исполнил в трех концертах весь «Хорошо темперированный клавир» Баха, и многократно повторил этот цикл. К счастью, на семидесят втором году жизни он записал его. Но результат другой его титанической работы – над циклом из всех 32 сонат Бетховена – можно сказать, канул в Лету. Впервые он сыграл этот цикл в год своего пятидесятилетнего юбилея, в сезоне 1940/41 гг, и с тех пор многократно повторял. Из 32 сонат записями на пленку оказались при этом всего пять. Слушая Одиннадцатую сонату Бетховена, помещенную на этом диске наряду с музыкой самого Фейнберга, начинаешь понимать лучше природу его пианистических трактовок, которые, возможно были бы совсем другими, не мысля себя Фейнберг композитором.

Пианист/композитор как мыслящий соавтор великих, классических авторов – изменение, увы, уменьшающее в наше время тотализации и специализации в исполнительстве. Тем ценнее память о том, что было среди нас совсем недавно – буквально на памяти двух поколений, и с тем большей бережностью мы должны хранитъ ее и изучать то, что осталось.

**Федор Софронов**
