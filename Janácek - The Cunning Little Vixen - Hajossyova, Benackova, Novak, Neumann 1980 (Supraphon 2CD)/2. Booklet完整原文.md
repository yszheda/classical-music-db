## 专辑介绍（英文）
### THE CUNNING LITTLE VIXEN
The Adventures of the Cunning Little Vixen first appeared in the form of serialized humorous sketches by the artist and huntsman Stanislav Lolek, accompanied by the text of the author Rudolf Těsnohlídek, featured from April 1920 in the afternoon edition of Lidové noviny (Popular Daily) newspaper. At first Těsnohlídek was not particularly enthusiastic about the assignment, a fact apparently due to its having been more or less decreed by the paper's editor-in-chief, Arnošt Heinrich. "This is a book I hated", he would later reminisce. "I destroyed the first chapter right away and went to listen to the wood workers' talk. And then everything went smoothly."

Still, in fact the serial's unexpected success surprised Těsnohlídek. Incidentally, among the many aficionados of the newspaper's light reading supplement was Marie Stejskalová, the Janáčeks' housekeeper - and it was her laughs which first drew Leoš Janáček's attention to the story. Janáček's immediate impulse to turn the serial into an opera may have been viewed with some reserve; no wonder then that the timid Těsnohlídek took the news of the composer's interest in "Bystrouška" for a joke of his fellow editors. Only later did he come to understand how well his story served Janáček for a musical apotheosis of Nature.

On May 13, 1921 Lidové noviny carried an interview with Janáček, dealing with his new opera. He then remarked that for the Vixen's adventures to acquire the necessary dramatic charge certain modifications would have to be made on the original story, with the plot developing from a funny start, through a serious middle part, to a funny ending. "... At Luhačovice last summer I pondered the whole thing, now I am carrying it within me, I'm working", Janáček told his interviewer.

The underlying roots of Janáček's interest in this subject matter can be traced down to his diaries, containing affectionate records of birds' voices and monitoring the lives of several generations of blackbirds nesting in his own garden, as well as of other animals. He loved the forest and was an ardent admirer of nature, and of the beauty and fullness of life.

Thus he transposed the Vixen's story into a new dramatic form, by no means depriving it of the qualities of Těsnohlídek's sprightly account of the fates of his protagonists. The plot of the opera is more compact than its literary model: Janáček picked ten chapters out of the total 23 written by Těsnohlídek, changed the ordering of individual episodes and their extent, and considerably reduced the number of dramatis personae. Using his instinctive sense of dramatic action, he "invented" analogies of human and animal characters, like Těsnohlídek assigning to the latter a catalogue of archetypal human virtues and vices. The most important alteration concerned the fate of the main protagonist, the vixen Bystrouška, who does appear in the final act as the mother of a large family but soon thereafter perishes at the hands of a poacher, Harašta's shot destroying Bystrouška's family bliss (an episode absent from Těsnohlídek's story) constitutes a powerful dramatic turn reaching a cathartic point only with the Forester's meeting — symbolic, as it were — with young Bystrouška and the little frog, a moment raising awareness of the miracle of life's indestructible capacity for renewal.

As a rule, Janáček was not used to working on a single piece at a time: "... one thing being already sharply defined in the conscious part of the mind, and another one, existing still in the former's shade, as he himself explained. Similarly, concurrently with composing The Adventures of the Cunning Little Vixen (1921—1923) he produced The Wandering Madman chorus and his first string quartet, inspired by Tolstoy's Kreutzer Sonata, and sketched the first pages of the operas The Makropoulos Case and of the Danube Symphony.

This wide scope of activity notwithstanding, he tackled the Těsnohlídek story with enormous enthusiasm and affection — indeed, he welcomed in "Bystrouška" a personification of the forest and the melancholy of the autumn of life, with unpretentious warmth he evoked his love for nature, yielding one of his freshest works.

The opera's first — widely acclaimed — production was mounted in Brno, on November 6, 1924, the year of Janáček's seventieth birthday, by the conductor František Neumann, director Ota Zítek and stage designer Eduard Milén.
