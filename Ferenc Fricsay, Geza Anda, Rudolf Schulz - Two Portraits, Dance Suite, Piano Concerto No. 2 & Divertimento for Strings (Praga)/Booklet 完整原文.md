## FERENC FRICSAY'S MEMORIAL

Ferenc Fricsay was born in Budapest in 1914 and studied music under Bálázs Zoltán, Kodály, von Dohnányi, and von Weingartner. He received his first diploma at the Budapest Academy of Music at the age of 15, for violin, piano, viola, cello, trombone, percussion, composition and conducting. Since 1933 he worked as *répétiteur* and conductor at age 19, substituting for his father on the podium of the Young Musicians' Club of Szeged.

In 1936, at the age of 22, he succeeded his father as conductor of the Young Musicians' Club. Upon graduating in 1935, Fricsay became chorus master and director of the chorus of the Budapest Opera then, from 1938 to 1943, he was music director of the Szeged Philharmonic Orchestra in the third largest city in Hungary; he also served as director of its military band from 1938 to 1944. In 1942 he was court-martialed by the government on Miklós Horthy's orders because of his Jewish musicians, and for having helped Jewish musicians. Fricsay himself learned to speak reliable French by means of records.

After the Nazis occupied Hungary in 1944, the chief editor of the *Szegedi Napló* advised Fricsay to flee the city and he went to Budapest to marry his wife; he had left his wife and three children stranded at his parents' home in Budapest. In 1945, soon after the liberation of the city by the Red Army, Fricsay was asked by the Budapest Philharmonic Orchestra to become principal conductor. At the request of the Geza Anda, a pupil of Kodály, he gave numerous concerts in Vienna, Budapest and in the Vienna Volksoper and in the Salzburg Festival. In late 1948 he made a guest conducting appearance at the opera in Goerlitz, and from January/July 1949 to 1950 he became principal conductor of the RIAS Orchestra in Berlin. The enthusiastic reception of Fricsay's work on this international stage led to his being appointed Chief Conductor of the Berlin RIAS Symphonic Orchestra and Conductor of the German Opera Berlin, positions from which he held from 1949-1952, performing then in the Théâtre des Champs-Élysées in Paris.

Fricsay made his debut with the Israel Philharmonic Orchestra in 1951 and in 1954, he gave his first concert in Germany as music director of the Bavarian State Opera in Munich. In 1956 he became chief conductor of the RIAS Symphonic Orchestra, the Deutsches Oper Berlin and the Berlin Philharmonic Orchestra. He was also General Music Director of the Munich Civic Opera, a position he held until 1958. Fricsay gave his last concert as music director of the Bavarian State Opera in October/November 1958, in December 1958 in Beethoven's 9th Symphony at the Theater an der Wien, and finally succumbed to stomach cancer on 20 February 1963, at the age of 48 in Basel, Switzerland. Fricsay was known for his interpretations of the music of Mozart and Beethoven as well as Bartók, Stravinsky, Hindemith and Kodály. He died in 1963 in Basel, Switzerland.

## TWO PORTRAITS, Op.5

Bartók dedicated this work to the violinist Stefi Geyer, a young musician. The Two Portraits were not finalized in February 1911 in Budapest but on 14 April 1911. Bartók gave the manuscript for the first of the two (*Idée*) at least until 1911 and probably later. The second portrait, *Une grotesque*, was composed in 1907-08, and first performed on 8 March 1907, in a concert given at the Academy of Music by Bartók, Emil Szoldán and Aladár Hársanyi. The manuscript of *Une grotesque* was only published in 1956 in the series of the Complete Works.

---

## DANCE SUITE (1923)

Bartók composed the Dance Suite for the 50th anniversary of the unification of Buda and Pest, the city was to organize a great festival. He chose five pieces from his own works. The figures of Hungarian music, Bartók, Kodály and Dohnányi were invited. The Dance Suite was first performed on 19 November 1923, at the concert given in the Theatre Royal.

## PIANO CONCERTO No.2

Bartók composed this concerto between 1930 and 1931. He gave the first performance on 23 January 1933, at the same time, the work was given under the baton of its creator on 3 February 1933 in Budapest. The work was well received. In 1936, it was given under the baton of its creator on 3 February 1933 in Budapest. The work was well received and it is with Geza Anda that Fricsay gave the work on 25 May 1952 at the Théâtre des Champs-Élysées.

## DIVERTIMENTO

The Divertimento for string orchestra was written in 1939. It was commissioned by Paul Sacher for the Basel Chamber Orchestra. Bartók composed this work during the summer of 1939 at Saanen, in the chalet owned by W.J. Reichert. This work was given for the first performance on 11th June, 1940 in Basel. ### HOMMAGE À FERENC FRICSAY

Le chef d'orchestre **Ferenc Fricsay** (Szeged en Autriche-Hongrie) le 31 juillet 1914 – Bâle Suisse), le 20 février 1963) montre très jeune des aptitudes pour la musique. Il entre à l'Académie Franz Liszt de Budapest où il étudie et où sont professeurs Bela Bartók, Zoltán Kodály et Leo Weiner. Il débute à 19 ans comme chef de l'Opéra de Szeged. Puis après, dès 1935, il remplace son père à la tête d'un orchestre similaire. À cette époque déjà, il fait preuve de grandes dispositions. Il dirige dès 1936 l'orchestre philharmonique de Szeged et de 1938 à 1943, il est directeur musical de l'Opéra de Budapest. En 1942, il est condamné par un tribunal militaire pour avoir aidé des musiciens juifs. Après la guerre, il s'installe à Berlin où il dirige l'Orchestre RIAS de 1949 à 1952. Pendant cette période, il se produit dans les plus grandes salles de concert européennes. Il meurt en 1963 à l'âge de 48 ans.

### FRICSAY ET LA FRANCE

Afin de perpétuer le souvenir d'un musicien qui l'avait séduit, Lutz von Pahlen créait en novembre 1974 la Ferenc-Fricsay-Gesellschaft et les nombreux amis et admirateurs de ce grand chef lui rendent un vibrant hommage. Elle fait précéder sa réédition par Geza Anda (1921-1976), pianiste hongrois d'origine tchèque et Salomon Miklos (né en 1932), violoniste. Ils sont tous deux formés à l'Académie Franz Liszt de Budapest par Kodály et par Ferenc Fricsay. En 1952, Fricsay dirige le 2e Concerto pour piano de Bartók avec Geza Anda en soliste. Paris découvre l'importance du 2e Concerto pour piano et la signification profonde du Divertimento de 1939.
