### Piano Duo Apostles

At the end of 1943, Janine Reding and Henry Piette received their doctorates following four years at the Chapelle Musicale de la Reine Elisabeth de Belgique. Each began a successful solo career. And then in 1945 their artistic fortunes and love life intertwined: they married that year, and the happy couple wanted to celebrate May 8, the V.E. day, and they asked them to participate. Already married off-stage, the couple decided to combine forces in a performance of Bohuslav Martinu's Concerto for two pianos & orchestra. Thus was born the Reding-Piette piano duo. A few months later they were suddenly called on to replace ailing British pianist Solomon. They played the premiere of Boris Porena's *Concerto for two pianos and percussion* at the Edinburgh Festival with the Orchestra of the Société Philharmonique de Bruxelles, Marcel Crvelier, undertook to manage their appearances around the world.

As the Reding-Piette Duo made its début, he offered them the challenge of playing a Henry Pousseur sonata at a concert in his Belgian premiere. Janine Reding and Henry Piette played it at the Salle Gaveau in Paris, in its Belgian premiere.

The first performance of Poulenc's Concerto for two pianos. The first piece she composed for them was the *Symphony in reo*, premiered by the Reding-Piette Duo in 1948 with the Brussels Radio Symphony Orchestra conducted by Mario Rossi. He then wrote a second Concerto for two pianos for them, part of his series of Dialogues. The performance was given with the Orchestra of the Belgian National Radio under the baton of Léon Devos, then with the direction of Rafael Kubelik. The beauty of this piece, with its contrasts and brilliant orchestration, deeply influenced Martinu's later works. The Reding-Piette Duo gave more than fifteen concerts and sixty pieces for two pianos in their repertoire. In addition to the conductors named above, they performed with, notably, Fernand Haendel, Igor Markevitch, Charles Munch, Louis Martin, Jean Fournet, Anatole Fistoulari, Armand Janssen, Jean Desenclos, Jean Martinon, Walter Goehr, and others.

Each of the works in this collection reveals the thorough research with which Reding & Piette treated each composer's style, from Wilhelm Friedemann Bach and Bernardo Pasquini up to Igor Stravinsky and Henryk Górecki's *Litanja*. Their interpretation of Schumann's *Andante con Variazioni* contains hidden pearls of tenderness. After hearing them play Brahms, Camargo Guarnieri wrote: "You are unsurpassable..." and they have given us five years' exclusive rights to the score of the concerto. Swiss music critic Aloys Mooser declared, "You are unsurpassable..."

The friendship between Gian-Francesco Malipiero and the Reding-Piette duo began at the Festival de Besançon in 1949, when they performed his sonata *Due Pezzi* for two pianos. Descriptions of their performances of the concertos were similar to the music of the day. The Reding-Piette Duo made its début in 1945.

### Apôtres des deux pianos

À la fin de 1943, Janine Reding et Henry Piette reçoivent leur diplôme d'agrégation après quatre années passées à la Chapelle Musicale de la Reine Élisabeth de Belgique. Après quelques succès en soliste, le couple déjà marié dans la vie unit ses talents sur la scène en mai 1945. C'est dans cette euphorie de fin de guerre que le duo Reding-Piette voit le jour sur les conseils de Léon Galle, chef d'orchestre et organisateur de concerts.

Quelques mois plus tard, ils sont appelés à remplacer au pied levé le pianiste britannique Solomon. Ils jouent le concerto pour deux pianos et percussion de Boris Porena avec l'Orchestre de la Société Philharmonique de Bruxelles sous la direction de Sir Adrian Boult, ils donnent la première de la *Sonatine* pour deux pianos et percussion de Boris Porena au Festival d'Édimbourg. Marcel Crvelier, directeur de la Radiodiffusion belge, leur offre le défi de jouer une sonate de Henry Pousseur. D'emblée, il leur propose de relever un autre défi, jouer en 1947 à la Salle Gaveau à Paris, en sa première belge.

Janine Reding et Henry Piette appartiennent à une génération de travailleurs, elle ayant été l'élève d'Arthur de Greef, lui d'Arthur de Greef, disciple de Liszt à Weimar. Ils ayant étudié avec Louis Closson, un disciple de Liszt à Weimar.

Les pièces pour deux pianos seuls qui figurent dans cette anthologie démontrent à quel point Reding et Piette ont su respecter le style du compositeur que ce soit dans la sonate de Wilhelm Friedemann Bach et Bernardo Pasquini jusqu'à Igor Stravinsky. La lecture de l'*Andante con Variazioni* de Schumann recèle des joyaux de tendresse. Après avoir entendu Brahms, Camargo Guarnieri déclara: «Vous êtes insurpassables...»
