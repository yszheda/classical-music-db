## A great evening by the Seine

The most important thing this evening was
played on water, not on land. We have never
yet seen Puccini's tragedy "Il Tabarro",
which takes place on a barge on the Seine,
in so well-shaped and at the same time so
convincingly authentic production. Regie,
Bühne and a company from which Fischer-
Dieskau did not stand out toweringly, as he
often does otherwise, but was only one of
truly matched singer-actors who natu-
rally worked up to a well-nigh perfect per-
formance, wrote the well-known critic
Helmut Schmidt-Garre on 10 December
1973 in his review in the *Münchner Merk-
ur* of the première.

That view of the performance is not sur-
prising, since Wolfgang Sawallisch as
conductor and Günther Rennert as produ-
cer were responsible for making Puccini's
one-acter the chief concern - the only time,
incidentally, except for Verdi's
*Falstaff*, that the two artistic directors of
the Bavarian State Opera together under-
took such a work.

Wolfgang Sawallisch was General Music
Director from 1969 to 1982; from 1982 to
1992 he additionally held the post of Chief
Conductor at the Vienna State Opera. Günther
Rennert headed the Bavarian State Opera from
1967 to 1976 as Intendant.

The decision to perform all three parts of
Puccini's *Trittico* could not be taken, as so

frequently in the performance history of
the cycle of one-acters, first performed in
1918 at the New York Met. Because of its
all too affecting content and its widely ex-
tensive lyricism, *Suor Angelica* is fre-
quently omitted in countries north of the
Alps, as how Günther Rennert described
his dislike of the sugary story about a
nun's indiscretion, too greatly dominated
by the 19th-century Italian operatic tradition.

Rennert and Sawallisch restricted them-
selves to the jealousy drama *Il Tabarro*
and the brilliant opera buffa *Gianni
Schicchi*. Puccini himself had planned to
bring the first part of his *Trittico*, which
had already been completed in 1916, to
its performance separately in Rome in the
winter of 1916/17, together with *La
Vill* (1884). This plan could not be reali-
zed only because Titta Ruffo, the barito-
ne foreseen for the role of Michele, was
called up on military service. Later, how-
ever, Puccini protested vehemently
against the increasing tendency to dis-
pense with *Suor Angelica*, which was de-
nigrated as sentimentally tearful.

At the new production in Munich in 1973,
at Günther Sawallisch's urging the German
translation was decided upon, although at
the Bavarian State Opera at the beginning
of the '70s the majority of all operas had
long since been performed in their original
language. After Rennert's death in 1982,
Wolfgang Sawallisch brought out the
Puccini double bill in a new production in
the original Italian.

Not only in outward form did Puccini turn
away from the late Romanticism, which
with *Il Tabarro* the musical intro-
duction, the opening scene, a typical
mixture of atmospheric idyll and exact
depiction of a dismal poor quarter of
Paris. From small and very small motives
he created new sound combinations; the

stimuli of his contemporaries Stravinsky,
Schoenberg and Debussy clearly to be
heard, without Puccini betraying his own
tonal idiom.

## Synopsis

The peniche de Marcel (Michele) is aus
dem Hafen an die Seine festgemacht. Geor-
gette, la femme de Marcel, appor-
te un bouquet à un camarade qui vient
d'un d'eux, le jeune Henri (Luigi). Une
femme nommée «la Frette», parce qu'elle
ressemble à un furet, arrive à bord; elle
veut chercher son mari, «la Taupe». Les
deux vieux amis doivent partir mainte-
nant et repartent chez eux. C'est le soir,
Georgette rêve à la maison et promet de ris-
quer une allumette sur le pont du bateau
que son mari, Marcel, se sera endor-
mi. Ce sera le signal pour Henri de la re-
joindre. Henri s'éloigne et descend à ter-
re. Alors que peu à peu le soir tombe,
Marcel s'approche de Georgette et lui
rappelle les heureux heureux de leur ména-
ge. À cette époque, leur enfant vivant en-
core et il les cachait tous deux d'un geste
tendre. Marcel demande à Georgette de
Georgette écarte tout souvenir du passé et
reste chez elle. Lorsqu'il allume sa
pipe, Henri prend la flamme de l'allumet-
te pour le signe convenu avec Georgette et
monte sur le pont. Marcel l'aide à saut et
la barge, le force à avouer son amour pour
sa femme. Dans la lutte, l'étrangle et le
arrive, il la retrouve le mort de son man-
teau. Georgette est apaisée par le com-
portement de son mari et cherche à le dis-
traire par sa coquetterie. Il l'invite alors à
venir avec lui à la cale où il trouve d'une
geste brusque. Emporté par sa colère,
Marcel précipite Georgette épouvantée
sur le cadavre de son amant.
