## About this Recording

This 1967 recording of Mussorgsky's *Boris Godunov* (in Rimsky-Korsakov's revision) remains one of the most celebrated recordings of this Russian opera, led by the great Bulgarian bass Boris Christoff in the title role, triplecasting as Boris, the monk Pimen, and the vagabond Varlaam. André Cluytens conducts the Paris Conservatoire Orchestra and the Sofia National Opera Chorus, with a cast that includes Evelyn Lear as Marina Mnishek, John Lanigan as Shuisky, and Dimitr Ouzounov as the False Dimitry. This was the classic stereo recording of the work for many decades, remastered in 2002 at Abbey Road Studios for EMI's "Great Recordings of the Century" series.

Modest Mussorgsky (1839-1881) composed his great opera *Boris Godunov* based on Pushkin's drama and Karamzin's history, telling the story of Tsar Boris Godunov who came to the throne after murdering the rightful heir, haunted by his crime and eventually dying in agony as the False Dimitry approaches Moscow. This is one of the greatest Russian operas, a masterpiece of musical drama and psychological characterization.

The version performed here is the standard revision and orchestration by Nikolai Rimsky-Korsakov (1844-1908), which was the version most commonly performed and recorded throughout the 20th century.
