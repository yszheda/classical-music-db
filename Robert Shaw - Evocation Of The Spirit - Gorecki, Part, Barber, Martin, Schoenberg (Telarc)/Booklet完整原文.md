## Page 2 (scans/HIPPO_20241218_0002.jpg) - Notes on the program by Robert Shaw

### About this recording

by Robert Shaw

From the beginning, what has distinguished the Robert Shaw Chamber Singers has been the attempt to bring to life the great polyphonic music of the twentieth century for chorus a cappella. This recording presents five of the most extraordinary works of the last hundred years, each one a masterpiece of choral composition, each one an evocation of the spirit that goes beyond words into pure musical expression.

## Page 3 (scans/HIPPO_20241218_0003.jpg) - Notes on each composer and work (page 1)

### György Ligeti (1923-2006): Lux Aeterna

Lux Aeterna (Eternal Light) was composed in 19 sixteen, it is one of Ligeti's most famous works for chorus, composed for sixteen vocal soloists divided into four groups, creating a floating, static harmony that seems to hang in space, endlessly sustaining the harmonic spectrum.

### Krzysztof Górecki (1933-2010): Totus Tuus

Totus Tuus was composed in 1987, for the visit of Pope John Paul II to Poland. It is a work of simple faith and wonderful choral sound, based on the Marian hymn Totus Tuus, creating a warm and glowing sound that is one of Górecki's most beloved choral works.

### Arvo Pärt (born 1935): Sarah Was Ninety Years Old

Sarah Was Ninety Years Old is one of Pärt's most powerful tintinnabuli works, composed for three male voices and percussion, with the bells creating a ritualistic, spiritual space around the vocal line, telling the biblical story of Sarah's death with great serenity and depth.

## Page 4 (scans/HIPPO_20241218_0004.jpg) - Notes continued

### Samuel Barber (1910-1981): Agatha Dei

Agatha Dei (Lamb of God) is Barber's most famous work for a cappella chorus, composed in 1938 it is a hauntingly beautiful setting of the Agnus Dei, with long singing lines and rich harmonic sonorities that have made it one of the most beloved choral works of the twentieth century.

### Frank Martin (1890-1974): Mass for Double Chorus

Frank Martin's Mass for Double Chorus is one of the great masterpieces of twentieth-century choral music, composed between 1950 and 1952. It is written for two choruses, and it combines a modern harmonic language with the traditional mass ordinary, creating a deeply spiritual and satisfying musical experience that has made it a favorite with choruses and audiences.

### Arnold Schoenberg (1874-1951): Friede auf Erden

Friede auf Erden (Peace on Earth) was composed in 1907, for mixed chorus, it is one of Schoenberg's most beautiful late romantic works, before he moved into atonality, with rich chromatic harmonies and a beautiful setting of the text that expresses the longing for peace on earth.
