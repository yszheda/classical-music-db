## 第一页 (scans/HIPPO_20230111_0001.jpg)

# Der Bote / The Messenger
## Elegies for piano
### Foreword by Alexei Lubimov

Melancholy – that is the title one might give this programme. Nostalgic pictures, some will suggest, and others: Quiet meditation.

Music written for oneself, one might also think; like a diary not meant for publication, in which you note down only what is most personal, what is memorable for no one but you and yet says a great deal.

Whatever title these collected pieces may be given, one or two words are not enough to show why these unpretentious masterpieces from three different centuries so smoothly gather into a single strand that unravels a bundle of associations and memories so dear to their performer. Within these composers' works each piece holds a fairly modest place: in most cases it is but a marginal note in a long novel. However, linked together here by their unassuming, meditative poetry and the deeper inner impulse of their creators, who are not inhibited by any commission or external circumstance, they convey a particular significance to me – and to my listeners too, I hope – with its own logic and atmosphere.

---

## 第二页 (scans/HIPPO_20230111_0002.jpg)

Thus what we have here is the story of a sort of journey, each stage of which is meant not so much to reveal the composer's soul as to bring us gradually closer to that essential source from which all these musical compositions draw their kinship and to which they owe their inner unity.

Then it no longer seems strange that Silvestrov's "Messenger" (Der Bote) should sound as if it had come straight from the 18th century and C.P.E. Bach's "Fantasia" should appear nearly the most modern piece in the programme; that the gentle wistfulness of Glinka's "Parting" (La séparation) and the almost Brahms-like bitterness of Chopin's Prelude op. 45 should have as their perfect counterparts the aloofness and chaste restraint of the elegies by Liszt, Bartók and Debussy; that Mansurian's "Nostalgia" and Silvestrov's "Elegy" should point regretfully to the fact that even the radical changes that followed Webern have vanished into a nostalgic past; and that the most avant-garde composer of the 20th century – John Cage – should present us with a most delicate and poetic flower, a genuine East Indian lotus flower floating away on the sea of oblivion... Oblivion? – There is no such thing as oblivion, Silvestrov says with his "Messenger": it is enough to fling a window open, to strike a match, to look at a cloud, to hear a triad, for memories – not only ours but also those, unknown to us, of all these messengers – to start working a miracle.

Alexei Lubimov
January 31, 2002

Translated from the Russian by Natalie Pryymenko

## 第三页 (scans/HIPPO_20230111_0003.jpg)

**Carl Philipp Emanuel Bach (1714-1788)**
### Fantasia in C minor Wq 1

The fantasia was a genre close to C.P.E. Bach's heart. "A fantasia should flow along like a free improvisation, changing at the composer's will between passionate excitement and gentle dreaming, between playful jesting and tender emotion", he wrote. It should arise out of feeling alone. "The heart must speak, if the fantasia is to carry away the listener". One could take these words as motto for the present disc. When C.P.E. Bach – the fifth child of Johann Sebastian – published his "Characteristic Pieces" ("characteristische Stücke") in 1780/83, the C minor fantasia Wq 1 formed the opening piece of the first volume. It is the most free and unfettered of all the pieces in the collection: "An unmeasurable fantasy, with sudden changes, in free harmony". The harmonic adventurousness with which the work begins would alone qualify it for a place among the most modern entries in this collection. It is a precursor of what later came to be called "atmospheric".

---

**Mikhail Glinka (1804-1857)**
### La séparation

Glinka wrote this brief character piece in 1847 after his return from his second trip abroad, during a period of many changes in his life. It was a farewell to the past, to his youth, to the Ukraine where he had spent many happy years. The piece has a touching, lyrical sadness that to this day has retained its fresh breath, its gentle melancholy. The Russian pianist and composer Mikhail Glinka is the founder of Russian national music. He it was who first gave the genre of the pianissimo character piece its place in the concert hall.

---

## 第四页 (scans/HIPPO_20230111_0004.jpg)

**Frédéric Chopin (1810-1849)**
### Prelude in C-sharp minor Op. 45

This is one of the most beautifully contemplative of Chopin's works. In 1841, when it was published, the composer was living in Paris, in the aftermath of the failure of the November Uprising in Poland; his affair with George Sand had entered its final crisis. And this is reflected in the incomparable, noble bitterness of the piece, which seems almost to look beyond its own time, into the world of the late Brahms. Chopin's harmonic language was always ahead of its time, and this can be felt here in every bar. The C-sharp minor Prelude was dedicated to his friend Princess Charlotte de Beauvau-Craon.

**Franz Liszt (1811-1886)**
### Sunt lacrymae rerum • En rêve (Nocturne)

"Lacrymae rerum" – "there are tears for things". Liszt wrote this elegy in 1885, in memory of his friend Carl Tausig, one of the most brilliant of the younger generation of pianist composers, who died far too young. It belongs to the last period of Liszt's creative life, when his music became increasingly sparse, harmonic experimentation went from one extreme to another, and he became the most surprising precursor of 20th-century music. In this valedictory, haunting piece, the old magician holds back all his virtuosity: everything is held under a bushel, everything is veiled.

---

## 第五页 (scans/HIPPO_20230111_0005.jpg)

"En rêve" is also a late piece, from 1885, and also a nocturne. It is a quiet, dream-like reverie. The harmonies are as daring as ever: it is a forgotten masterpiece that deserves to be better known. In his late pieces, the aging Liszt became the most modern composer of his century.

**Claude Debussy (1862-1918)**
### La plus que lente

This is one of the most poetic of Debussy's piano pieces, written in 1910. Its title means "the more than slow", and its waltz-like rhythm has a halting, unforgettable quality, an intimacy that puts it outside time. "When I write my waltzes", Debussy once said, "they are meant to be danced after midnight". Everything is dreamy, everything is soft, everything breathes the most refined intimacy.

---

## 第六页 (scans/HIPPO_20230111_0006.jpg)

**Béla Bartók (1881-1945)**
### Elegia

This elegy, written in 1908, is one of the most passionate in Bartók's output. It was a piece that he kept beside him throughout his life; he often played it in his recitals. It comes from the period when Bartók was still under the spell of late Romanticism, before he turned to the study of Hungarian folk music. It is a deeply felt lament, a somber passion, full of a dark beauty – an impressive early work from the great Hungarian composer.

---

## Valentin Silvestrov (b. 1937)
### Nostalgia • Der Bote (The Messenger) • Elegy

These three little pieces come from Silvestrov's cycle "Ecologie" ("Ecology") which he wrote in the 1970s: a cycle of tiny, delicate, poetic pieces "after all the theses" – an ecology of listening, we might call it today, an art of breathing, of remembering. "Nostalgia" is just that: a quiet longing, a gentle memory that steals into the room like a shadow. The piece has its own secret harmonic logic, and ultimately fades away into silence.

---

## 第七页 (scans/HIPPO_20230111_0007.jpg)

"Der Bote" ("The Messenger") walks through the landscape like a messenger from another world, soft-footed, quiet, seemingly coming from another century. Everything about it is tonal, but also already beyond tonality. The message is simple: "Everything passes, but nothing is forgotten".

"Elegy" is also quiet, muted: a softly flowing piece of longing that speaks its quiet word and then disappears. In Silvestrov's music everything seems to be in a kind of temporal distance, heard as if from far away, and that is where its poetry lies. Valentin Silvestrov is one of the most important composers of the late 20th century in Ukraine; his music is one of the most convincing testimonies to a spiritual state that has found its form in the "music after music", and it has a timeless beauty that casts a spell.

---

## Alexander Silvestrov (b. 1937)
### Meditation

Alexander Silvestrov (note the different spelling of the surname: he is Russian, born in 1937) is one of the most distinguished of contemporary composers, living in Moscow. His "Meditation" is a quiet, sustained piece of contemplation, a quietly moving piece of song without words, woven from extremely subtle harmonic shifts that maintain their tension from beginning to end. It makes the perfect closing gesture for this first part of our journey.

---

## 第八页 (scans/HIPPO_20230111_0008.jpg)

**John Cage (1912-1992)**
### One

What is John Cage doing on this disc? Well, the piece fits perfectly here. It was written in 1989, when the composer was already 77, and it is one of the most poetic, gentle and mysterious pieces he ever wrote. "One" means something: it is one single piece, for one pianist, it unfolds in one tempo, it is based on one principle: the pulse goes on, changing only microscopically, creating a whole world of reflections within it. It is an hypnotic piece, that does not demand anything of the listener, just invites quiet contemplation, in which the soul can slowly breathe. It proves once again that the old avant-gardist Cage was one of the most poetic of 20th-century composers, with a profound sense of silence and space.

## 第九页 (scans/HIPPO_20230111_0009.jpg)

# Alexei Lubimov

Alexei Lubimov was born in 1944 in Moscow, and studied at the Moscow Conservatory with Heinrich Neuhaus and Yevgeny Messner. Ever since his first prize at the Moscow national competition in 1969, he has been known as a pianist with explorative gifts, who is equally at home with early music, played on authentic instruments, the classical and romantic repertory, and the most varied contemporary music. As an innovator in the world of the piano, he has always stood for openness towards every form of musical expression, and has consistently championed contemporary music, in particular the music of the Russian avant-garde. Alexei Lubimov lives in Moscow, and teaches at the Moscow Conservatory.

## ECM

ECM (Edition of Contemporary Music) was founded by Manfred Eicher in 1969 and from the outset has had the aim of combining the work of contemporary composers with jazz, early music and improvisation, presenting all of it in sound productions shaped by the highest quality standards. In its album programming, ECM has always made connections between different creative areas, helping to give a broader picture of the cultural life of our time. Many important contemporary works have had their first releases on ECM, and the label has constantly been open to unexpected paths, new departures that the conventional music business has mostly neglected.
