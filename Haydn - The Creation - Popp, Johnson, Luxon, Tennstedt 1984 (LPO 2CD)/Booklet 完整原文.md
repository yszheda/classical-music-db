### HAYDN'S CREATION: THE BACKGROUND

By 1790, when Prince Nicholas Esterházy died, Haydn had spent nearly thirty years at Eisenstadt. He had reached his ninety-second symphony and the Op. 64 quartets, and had composed countless works for the Prince's insatiable appetite for baryton trios - in all there are no fewer than 126. Now aged fifty-eight, he was given a pension and, while maintaining his allegiance to Prince Nicholas II (for whom he composed a series of Masses), spent more time in Vienna and made a few trips abroad. In 1791 he made his first visit to London and was given an honorary doctorate at Oxford University. It was during his second visit in 1794-95 that Salomon gave him the libretto of *The Creation*. The text derives from Milton's *Paradise Lost* and from the biblical story. It is said to have been originally intended for Handel. On his return to Vienna, Haydn set to work on the score, with the text adapted by Gottfried van Swieten.

Baron van Swieten was a man of culture, a diplomat and Court Librarian of Vienna, former Ambassador to the Court of Frederick the Great. He was described by one of his contemporaries as "not so much a friend of Haydn's and Mozart's but very much a patron - and with a very high opinion of himself."

Though *The Creation* has been acclaimed by posterity, the situation has changed since the 1790s. As one contemporary put it: "Why does no one know his great masses, famous in the whole of Europe for twenty years?" Haydn's first oratorio, *Il ritorno di Tobia* (1775), had not been successful, and most music lovers in Vienna would not have encountered his operas. Indeed, for all intents and purposes, Haydn's operas did not emerge from the obscurity of the Hungarian plains until the 1950s.

As a result, Haydn was never more aware of the artistic challenge of a large-scale oratorio. He summed up his approach in these oft-quoted words: "I was never so pious as when I was composing *The Creation*. Daily I fell on my knees and asked God that He grant me the strength to finish the work." It was a successful conclusion.

**Performers**

**LUCIA POPP** (Soprano - Gabriel) was born in 1939 in Slovakia and studied at the Bratislava Conservatory. She made her debut at the Slovak National Theatre in 1960 and subsequently appeared at the Vienna State Opera, Bavarian State Opera, and Covent Garden. She was particularly renowned for her Mozart roles and her beautiful coloratura technique.

**ANTHONY ROLFE JOHNSON** (Tenor - Uriel) studied at the Guildhall School of Music and Drama and with Peter Pears. He made his operatic debut in 1973 and has since appeared with all major British opera companies and at the Metropolitan Opera, La Scala, and the Salzburg Festival. He is particularly noted for his interpretations of Mozart, Britten, and Baroque repertoire.

**BENJAMIN LUXON** (Bass-Baritone - Raphael) was born in 1937 and studied at the Royal College of Music. He made his debut at Covent Garden in 1960 and has since appeared with opera companies throughout the world. He is particularly noted for his interpretations of British music and his recital performances.

**KLAUS TENNSTEDT** (Conductor) was born in 1926 in Germany and studied at the Leipzig Conservatory. He served as principal conductor of the London Philharmonic Orchestra from 1979 to 1987 and was particularly renowned for his interpretations of Mahler, Bruckner, and the German Romantic repertoire.

**LONDON PHILHARMONIC CHOIR** was founded in 1946 and has performed with all the major British orchestras. The choir has a repertoire ranging from Renaissance polyphony to contemporary works.

**LONDON PHILHARMONIC ORCHESTRA** was founded in 1932 by Sir Thomas Beecham and has been one of Britain's leading orchestras ever since. The orchestra has performed under the direction of many of the world's greatest conductors.
