# Johann Sebastian Bach
## Mass in B minor (Messe in h-moll) BWV 232

## Herbert von Karajan conducts Vienna Philharmonic, Vienna Singverein, December 1950

The Mass in B minor is one of Bach's supreme achievements, the last great work he completed, and one of the monuments of Western music. Bach compiled the work in its final form in the last years of his life, drawing on earlier music he had composed over the course of more than thirty years, bringing it together into a complete setting of the complete Ordinary of the Mass. It is a work that brings together everything that Bach had learned about composition over the course of his entire career, a summation of his art that stands as one of the greatest works ever written.

Bach never heard the work performed complete in his lifetime; it was not performed complete until after his death, and like so much of his music it fell into neglect after his death before being revived in the nineteenth century as part of the general Bach revival. Since then it has been recognized as one of Bach's greatest works, and it holds a unique place in the repertoire as the greatest setting of the Mass Ordinary ever written, a work that explores the full range of human emotion and spiritual experience.

This recording was made at a concert performance in Vienna in December 1950, and it brings together one of the most extraordinary casts ever assembled for the work. It includes two of the greatest singers of the twentieth century, Kirsten Flagstad and Kathleen Ferrier, both at the height of their powers, together with Elisabeth Schwarzkopf, Elisabeth Ludwig, Julius Patzak, Otto Edelmann, Alfred Poell and Paul Schöffler, all of them among the greatest singers of their generation.

## 第二页 (scans/HIPPO_20220419_0002.jpg)

Kathleen Ferrier's career was already approaching its end at the time of this performance—she was already suffering from the cancer that would take her life only three years later—but her singing here has the same noble, spacious, expressive quality that always characterised her singing at its best. Her performance of the "Crucifixus" is one of the most moving you will ever hear, and it is preserved here for posterity in this live recording from the peak of her powers.

Herbert von Karajan conducts a performance that is wonderfully sustained and dramatic, bringing out all the drama and all the spiritual depth of this great score. The Vienna Philharmonic Orchestra plays with all the virtuosity and warm tone that are its trademark, and the Vienna Singverein Chorus sings with great commitment and precision. This is one of the most important recordings of this great work from the middle of the twentieth century, and it preserves a very special performance with one of the most extraordinary casts ever assembled. It is issued here for the first time on CD, taken from the original broadcast tapes and professionally remastered for this release.

## The Artists

**Herbert von Karajan** (1908-1989)

One of the greatest and most influential conductors of the twentieth century, long associated with the Berlin Philharmonic and the Vienna Philharmonic. He was equally at home in the Baroque repertoire and the Romantic repertoire, and this recording shows his deep understanding of Bach's great Mass.

**Kirsten Flagstad** (1895-1962)

The great Norwegian soprano, universally recognised as the greatest Wagnerian soprano of the twentieth century. She was also a distinguished singer of song and oratorio, and her performance of the soprano solos in this recording shows the same grandeur of scale and beauty of tone that made her so famous in Wagner.

**Kathleen Ferrier** (1912-1953)

The great English contralto, one of the most beloved singers of the twentieth century. Her voice was remarkable for its rich, warm, expressive quality, and she moved audiences everywhere with her sincerity and directness. This recording of the Crucifixus and the Agnus Dei is one of the last she ever made, and it preserves her noble and moving interpretation for posterity. She died only three years after this performance, at the age of 41.

**Elisabeth Schwarzkopf** (1915-2006)

The great German soprano, famous for her beautiful voice and her impeccable style in opera and Lieder. She was one of the greatest Strauss singers of the twentieth century, but she was also a distinguished Bach singer, and her Laudamus te is one of the most beautiful you will ever hear.

## 第六页 (scans/HIPPO_20220419_0006.jpg)

**Elisabeth Ludwig** (1920-2007)

Austrian mezzo-soprano/contralto, long-time member of the Vienna State Opera, admired throughout the world for her intelligent musicianship and expressive performances in opera and Lieder. Her performance of the Et in Spiritum sanctum is one of the highlights of this recording.

**Julius Patzak** (1898-1986)

Austrian tenor, one of the most distinguished tenors of his generation, famous for his intelligent and expressive performances in Bach and Wagner. His Benedictus on this recording is wonderfully warm and expressive.

**Otto Edelmann** (1917-2003)

Austrian bass, long-time member of the Vienna State Opera, he sang all the great bass roles from Mozart to Wagner, and his Quoniam tu solus sanctus has a noble gravity that is perfectly suited to this great music.

**Alfred Poell** (1882-1956) [correction: 1882-1956]

Austrian bass, distinguished member of the Vienna State Opera, he sang many important roles in the German repertoire.

**Paul Schöffler** (1897-1977)

Austrian baritone, long-time member of the Vienna State Opera, he was one of the most respected singers of his generation.

**Vienna Philharmonic Orchestra**

One of the world's most celebrated orchestras, in 1950 it was at the peak of its powers with its characteristic warm string tone and superb ensemble playing.

**Vienna Singverein Chorus**

One of the finest Austrian choruses, long associated with the Vienna Philharmonic, they bring great commitment and precision to this great choral work.
