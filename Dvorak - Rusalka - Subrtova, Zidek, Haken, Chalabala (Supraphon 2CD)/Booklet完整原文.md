# Antonín Dvořák: Rusalka

Antonín Dvořák composed his lyric fairy-tale opera *Rusalka* between 1899 and 1900, when he was at the height of his creative powers. It was his ninth opera and the seventh to be staged. The first performance took place at the Prague National Theatre on 31 March 1901, and from the very outset it was recognised as a masterpiece. Today it is the most popular and most frequently performed of all Dvořák's operas, and it has entered the repertoire of opera houses throughout the world.

The story of the water nymph who falls in love with a human prince and sacrifices her voice for the chance to become human has all the elements of a romantic fairy tale, but Dvořák infused it with a depth of emotion and a richness of musical invention that raise it far above the level of a simple fairy story. The score is full of some of Dvořák's most beautiful and most inspired melodic inspiration, from the famous "Song to the Moon" that opens the first act to the profoundly moving final scene. The orchestration is masterly, capturing the mysterious atmosphere of the forest and the lake, and building to great climaxes of romantic passion and tragic intensity.

This recording, made in 1961 with the Czech Philharmonic Orchestra under Karel Ančerl, captures one of the great post-war productions of the opera at the Prague National Theatre, with a cast that included many of the leading Czech singers of the generation. Milada Šubrtová's Rusalka is justly celebrated as one of the finest interpretations of the role in the history of the opera, and she is partnered by Beno Blachut, one of the greatest Czech tenors of the twentieth century, as the Prince, and Eduard Haken, the leading Czech bass of his generation, as Vodník. This release preserves for posterity a landmark performance of one of the greatest Czech operas, sung in the original Czech by the greatest Czech singers of its day.

# Antonín Dvořák (1841-1904)

Antonín Dvořák was born in Nelahozeves, near Prague, in 1841, the son of a butcher and innkeeper. He studied music in Prague, and gradually made his way as a composer, earning his living as a church organist while building his reputation as a composer. His first big success came in 1878 with the Slavonic Dances, which quickly made him famous throughout Europe. In the 1890s he lived in the United States, where he was director of the National Conservatory in New York, and it was there that he composed his most famous work, the Ninth Symphony "From the New World". He returned to Bohemia in 1895, and continued to compose major works until his death in 1904.

Dvořák was one of the greatest masters of orchestration of the Romantic era, and his music is characterised by its wonderful melodic invention, its rich harmonic colouring, and its deep roots in the folk music tradition of Bohemia and Moravia. He composed in every major genre, but it is perhaps his orchestral music that is best known today, including the nine symphonies, the concertos for piano, violin and cello, the symphonic poems, and the Slavonic Dances. However, he also composed eleven operas, of which *Rusalka* is universally recognised as the masterpiece.

# Karel Ančerl (1908-1973)

Karel Ančerl was one of the greatest Czech conductors of the twentieth century. He was born in 1908, and studied composition and conducting in Prague. He became principal conductor of the Czech Philharmonic Orchestra in 1950, and held that post until 1968, when he was forced to leave the country after the Prague Spring. He died in Toronto in 1973. During his time with the Czech Philharmonic he conducted many legendary performances and recordings, and he was particularly admired for his interpretations of Czech music, especially Dvořák, Smetana and Janáček. His recordings with the Czech Philharmonic are still regarded as among the finest versions of these works ever made.

# Milada Šubrtová (1924-2011)

Milada Šubrtová was one of the leading Czech sopranos of the post-war period. She was born in 1924, and made her debut at the Prague National Theatre in 1948. She quickly became one of the principal sopranos of the company, where she sang all the major lyric and spinto roles, including Puccini, Verdi, Wagner and of course the Czech repertoire. She was particularly celebrated for her portrayal of the title role in Dvořák's *Rusalka*, which she sang many times during her career, and her interpretation became legendary in the Czech tradition.

## Synopsis

### Act I

By the banks of a lake in a forest, the water nymphs play. Their father Vodník, the water spirit, is approached by Rusalka, his daughter, who tells him that she has fallen in love with a human prince who often comes to hunt in the forest. She wants to become human so that she can be with him. Vodník tells her that she is making a terrible mistake, but finally agrees that she should go to the witch Ježibaba to ask for her help.

At Ježibaba's cottage, Rusalka asks the witch to turn her into a human. Ježibaba tells her that she will become human, but she will lose her voice, and if the prince does not love her, she will be forced to return to the lake as a water spirit again, and if the prince marries another woman, she will die. Rusalka accepts all these conditions, and Ježibaba casts the spell.

The prince comes to the lake hunting, and finds Rusalka. He is captivated by her beauty, and takes her back to his castle, already falling in love with her.

### Act II

At the prince's castle, preparations are being made for the prince's wedding. The prince has been captivated by Rusalka, but a foreign princess has come to the court, and she sets out to seduce the prince. The prince is tempted, and falls under her spell. He abandons Rusalka, and declares his love for the foreign princess. Rusalka is heartbroken, and returns to the lake, where she is reunited with her father Vodník.

### Act III

Rusalka returns to the lake, and is now a water spirit again. The prince has realised his mistake: he has come to understand that he really loves only Rusalka. He comes to the lake to find her, and begs her forgiveness. He finds her, and they embrace one last time. The prince dies in Rusalka's arms, and Rusalka returns to the depths of the lake, forever alone.

## Biographies continued

**Beno Blachut (1913-1985)**

Beno Blachut was one of the greatest Czech tenors of the twentieth century. He was a principal singer at the Prague National Theatre for more than forty years, and was particularly admired in the Czech repertoire, especially his performances of the Prince in *Rusalka*, which was one of his signature roles. His beautiful, clear tenor voice and passionate interpretations made him a favourite with audiences throughout Czechoslovakia.

**Eduard Haken (1910-1996)**

Eduard Haken was the leading Czech bass of his generation. He sang at the Prague National Theatre from 1937 until 1980, and sang all the great bass roles in the repertoire, from Mozart and Wagner to the Czech classics. His portrayal of Vodník in *Rusalka* was one of his most famous roles.
