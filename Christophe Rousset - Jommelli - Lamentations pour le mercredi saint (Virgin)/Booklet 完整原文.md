## LINER NOTES / NOTES DE LIVRET / LINER NOTES

### FRENCH TEXT (Pages 8-11)

**Niccolò Jommelli: Les Lamentations du prophète Jérémie pour le Mercredi Saint**

Né en 1714 à Aversa, une petite ville située au nord de Naples, le jeune Nicolò Jommelli effectue sa formation musicale dans deux des célèbres conservatoires de Naples, le San Onofrio et la Pietà dei Turchini. Il commence son activité de compositeur de théâtre dans cette même ville en 1737, puis collabore avec les plus grandes scènes italiennes. La carrière de Jommelli peut être retracée en quatre grandes étapes : Venise, où il est maître de chœur à l'Incurable ; le lancement pour jeunes filles des Incurabili (1743-1747) ; Rome enfin, en 1748, il devient maître coadjuteur (adjoint) de la Chapelle pontificale et compose l'oratorio *Le Rose* et *Ester* de Wurtemberg le nomme, en 1754, Oberkapellmeister de sa cour, et enfin Naples, où il se retire en 1769 et n'écrira plus que pour l'église. Il compose également pour les cours de Vienne et de Lisbonne, produisant au total une vingtaine d'opere buffe et serie, de sérénades, de cantates et de pastiches. Dans le domaine de la musique religieuse, Jommelli laissait à tous les genres : oratorios, messes, psaumes, motets et pièces liturgiques variées.

Les trois Lamentationes de Jeremia sont écrites à Rome en 1755, alors que Jommelli assiste Pietro Paolo Benedetti à Saint-Pierre, à la Cappella Giulia. Au fait tant du tradition de setting and performing Lamentations, after a very productive period of favour in the Renaissance, was in decline in Italy (unlike France, where Lambert and Charpentier in the seventeenth century, and later Clérambault and Delalande in the eighteenth, were to continue the practice). There were exceptions: the tradition was maintained in Italy by such men as Durante, Leo, Porpora and Perez) and in Rome right up until the nineteenth century. While the earlier settings were written in a great variety of styles, both for soloists and choirs, a cappella or with instrumental accompaniment, the works which emerged in the late sixteenth century were to follow the development of the oratorio.

### The Lamentations Text

The Old Testament text, attributed to Jeremiah (c. 650-570 BC), laments the destruction of Jerusalem, written as an acrostic poem, divided into five chapters. These latter verses were restored both in the later Greek Bible and in the liturgical text, which is made up of nine lessons. Only the original Latin text has been preserved. The first three lessons were sung on Maundy Thursday, the next three on Good Friday, and the final three on Holy Saturday. The Hebrew letters which form the opening verses of each of the chapters are also preserved, although their musical setting varies.

### ENGLISH TEXT (Pages 12-15)

**Nicolò Jommelli: Lamentations of the Prophet Jeremiah for the Wednesday in Holy Week**

Nicolò Jommelli was born in 1714 in Aversa, a small town north of Naples, and received his musical training in two of the famous Neapolitan conservatories, the S Onofrio and the Pietà dei Turchini. It was for Naples that he first wrote in 1737, before going on to write for many of Italy's leading theatres. Jommelli's career falls into four periods: Venice (1743-1747), where he was choirmaster at the Incurabili; a veritable explosion of activity at various Roman theatres, where he was choirmaster at the Incurabili; Rome, where he was vice-maestro coadiutore (associate choirmaster) in 1748 at the Chapel of St Peter's in the Vatican and wrote for the doors of Viterbo and Lucca, then at the age of forty, in 1754, he went to serve a German prince as director of music, court and army, serenades, cantatas and pasticcios. In the field of religious music, Jommelli left works on every genre: oratorios, masses, motets, etc. as well as producing a number of liturgical works.

The Three Lamentations of Jeremiah were written in Rome in 1755, when Jommelli was assistant to Pietro Paolo Benedetti, at the Pope's Chapel, in the Cappella Giulia. At that time the tradition of setting and performing Lamentations, after a very productive period of favour in the Renaissance, was in decline in Italy (unlike France, where Lambert and Charpentier in the seventeenth century and Clérambault and Delalande in the eighteenth, were to continue the practice). There were exceptions: the tradition was maintained in Italy by such men as Durante, Leo, Porpora and Perez and in Rome right up until the nineteenth century. While the earlier settings were written in a great variety of styles, both for soloists and choirs, a cappella or with instrumental accompaniment, the works which emerged in the late sixteenth century were to follow the development of the oratorio.

The Old Testament text, attributed to Jeremiah (c.650-570 BC), laments the destruction of Jerusalem, written as an acrostic poem, divided into five chapters. These latter verses were restored both in the later Greek Bible and in the liturgical text, which is made up of nine lessons. Only the original Latin text has been preserved. The first three lessons were sung on Maundy Thursday, the next three on Good Friday, and the final three on Holy Saturday. The Hebrew letters which form the opening verses of each of the chapters are also preserved, although their musical setting varies.

The setting of these texts, as Jommelli's choirboy for Pietro Paolo Benedetti at the Cappella Giulia that had led him to the position of vice-maestro coadiutore (associate choirmaster), Jommelli, together with Galuppi, was unquestionably the best composer of the time, and one of those who most enchants his audiences. The German poet Schubart bears this out, saying that after 1745 Jommelli was unanimously considered the greatest composer in the world.

### GERMAN TEXT (Pages 16-17)

**Nicolo Jommelli: Die Lamentationes Jeremiae Prophetae zum Mittwoch der Karwoche**

Der 1714 in Aversa, einer Kleinstadt nördlich von Neapel, geborene Nicolo Jommelli erhielt seine Ausbildung in zwei der berühmtesten Konservatorien Neapels, dem S. Onofrio und der Pietà dei Turchini. In Neapel hatte er 1737 auch seine ersten Erfolge als Opernkomponist. Anschließend schrieb er für fast alle großen Bühnen Italiens. Die Karriere Jommellis läßt sich in vier große Abschnitte einteilen: Venedig, wo er Kapellmeister am Ospedale degli Incurabili war (1743-1747), Rom, wo er 1749 maestro coadiutore der päpstlichen Kapelle wurde, Stuttgart, wo der Herzog Karl Eugen von Württemberg ihn 1754 zu seinem Oberkapellmeister machte, und schließlich Neapel, die Stadt, in der er 1769 starb und in der er sich fast ganz auf die Kirchenmusik verlegte. Er komponierte auch für die Höfe von Wien und Lissabon und schuf ungefähr zwanzig Opern seria und opera buffa, Opernwerke, Serenaden, Kantaten und Pasticcios. Im Bereich der Kirchenmusik widmete sich Jommelli allen Gattungen: Oratorien, Messen, Psalmen, Motetten und liturgischen Stücken aller Art.

Die drei Lamentationes Jeremiae Prophetae entstanden 1755 in Rom, als Jommelli stellvertretender Leiter der Kapelle an St. Peter war. Zu dieser Zeit war die Tradition des Vertonens und Aufführens der Lamentationen, die nach einer äußerst produktiven Phase während der Renaissance ihren Höhepunkt überschritten hatte, in Italien im Niedergang begriffen (im Gegensatz zu Frankreich, wo Lully und Charpentier im 17. Jahrhundert sowie Clérambault und Delalande im 18. Jahrhundert diese Tradition weiterführten). Es gab Ausnahmen: Die Tradition wurde in Italien von Komponisten wie Durante, Leo, Porpora und Perez aufrechterhalten und in Rom bis ins 19. Jahrhundert hinein gepflegt. Während die früheren Vertonungen in einer großen Vielfalt von Stilen geschrieben wurden, sowohl für Solisten als auch für Chöre, a cappella oder mit instrumentaler Begleitung, folgten die Werke, die Ende des 16. Jahrhunderts entstanden, der Entwicklung des Oratoriums.

Der alttestamentliche Text, der dem Propheten Jeremia (ca. 650-570 v. Chr.) zugeschrieben wird, beklagt die Zerstörung Jerusalems. Das Gedicht ist als Akrostichon aufgebaut und in fünf Kapitel unterteilt. Diese letzten Verse wurden sowohl in der späteren griechischen Bibel als auch im liturgischen Text wiederhergestellt, der aus neun Lektionen besteht. Nur der ursprüngliche lateinische Text ist erhalten geblieben. Die ersten drei Lektionen wurden am Gründonnerstag gesungen, die nächsten drei am Karfreitag und die letzten drei am Karsamstag. Die hebräischen Buchstaben, die die Anfangsverse jedes Kapitels bilden, sind ebenfalls erhalten, obwohl ihre musikalische Gestaltung variiert.

## COMPLETE LATIN TEXT WITH TRANSLATIONS (Pages 18-25)

### FIRST LAMENTATION / PREMIÈRE LAMENTATION / ERSTE LESUNG

**Incipit Lamentatio Jeremiae Prophetae**
Here begins the Lamentations of the Prophet Jeremiah.

**Aleph**
Quomodo sedet sola civitas plena populo!
Facta est quasi vidua domina gentium:
princeps provinciarum facta est sub tributo.

How doth the city sit solitary, that was full of people!
how is she become as a widow!
she that was great among the nations, and princess among the provinces,
how is she become tributary!

**Beth**
Plorans ploravit in nocte, et lacrimae eius in maxillis eius:
non est qui consoletur eam ex omnibus caris eius:
omnes amici eius spreverunt eam, et facti sunt ei inimici.

She weepeth sore in the night, and her tears are on her cheeks:
among all her lovers she hath none to comfort her:
all her friends have dealt treacherously with her, they are become her enemies.

**Ghimel**
Migravit Judas propter afflictionem, et multitudinem servitutis:
habitavit inter gentes, nec invenit requiem:
omnes persecutores eius apprehenderunt eam inter angustias.

Judah is gone into captivity because of affliction, and because of great servitude:
she dwelleth among the heathen, she findeth no rest:
all her persecutors overtook her between the straits.

**Daleth**
Viae Sion lugent, eo quod non sit qui veniat ad solemnitatem:
omnes portae eius destructae, sacerdotes eius gementes,
virgines eius squalidae, et ipsa oppressa amaritudine.

The ways of Zion do mourn, because none come to the solemn assembly:
all her gates are desolate, her priests sigh,
her virgines are afflicted, and she herself is in bitterness.

**He**
Facti sunt hostes eius in capite, inimici eius locupletati sunt:
quia Dominus locutus est super eam propter multitudinem iniquitatum eius.

Her adversaries became the head, her enemies prosper;
for the Lord hath afflicted her for the multitude of her transgressions.

### SECOND LAMENTATION / SECONDE LAMENTATION / ZWEITE LESUNG

**Vau**
Et egressus est a filia Sion omnis decor eius:
facti sunt principes eius velut arietes non invenientes pascua:
et abierunt absque fortitudine ante faciem subsequentis.

And from the daughter of Zion all her majesty is departed:
her princes are become like harts that find no pasture,
and they are gone without strength before the pursuer.

**Zain**
Recordata est Jerusalem dierum afflictionis suae, et praevaricationis suae,
omnium desiderabilium suorum, quae fuerant a diebus antiquis:
cum caderet populus eius in manu hostili, et non esset qui auxiliaretur ei:
viderunt eam hostes, et deriserunt sabbata eius.

Jerusalem remembered in the days of her affliction and of her miseries
all her pleasant things that were from the days of old,
when her people fell into the hand of the adversary, and none did help her:
the adversaries saw her, they did mock at her sabbaths.

**Heth**
Peccatum peccavit Jerusalem, propterea instabilis facta est:
omnes qui glorificabant eam spreverunt eam, quia viderunt ignominiam eius:
ipsa autem gemens, et conversa est retrorsum.

Jerusalem hath grievously sinned; therefore she is become as an unclean thing:
all that honoured her despise her, because they have seen her nakedness:
yea, she sigheth, and turneth backward.

**Teth**
Sordes eius in pedibus eius, nec recordata est finis sui:
deposita est vehementer, non habens consolatorem.

Her filthiness is in her skirts; she remembered not her last end;
therefore she came down wonderfully; she hath no comforter.

**Vide, Domine, afflictionem meam, quoniam erectus est inimicus.**
Behold, O Lord, my affliction, for the enemy hath magnified himself.

### THIRD LAMENTATION / TROISIÈME LAMENTATION / DRITTE LESUNG

**Jod**
Manum suam misit hostis ad omnia desiderabilia eius:
quia vidit gentes ingressas sanctuarium suum,
de quibus praeceperas ne intrarent in ecclesiam tuam.

The adversary hath spread out his hand upon all her pleasant things:
for she hath seen that the heathen entered into her sanctuary,
concerning which thou didst command that they should not enter into thy congregation.

**Caph**
Omnis populus eius gemens, et quaerens panem:
dederunt pretiosa quaeque pro cibo ad refocillandam animam.

All her people sigh, they seek bread;
they have given their pleasant things for meat to relieve the soul.

**Lamed**
O vos omnes qui transitis per viam, attendite et videte
si est dolor sicut dolor meus, quoniam vindemiavit me,
ut locutus est Dominus, in die irae furoris sui.

Is it nothing to you, all ye that pass by? behold, and see
if there be any sorrow like unto my sorrow, which is done unto me,
wherewith the Lord hath afflicted me in the day of his fierce anger.

**Mem**
De excelso misit ignem in ossibus meis, et erudivit me:
tetendit reti pedibus meis, convertit me retrorsum:
posuit me desolatam, tota die maerore confectam.

From on high hath he sent fire into my bones, and it prevaileth against them:
he hath spread a net for my feet, he hath turned me back:
he hath made me desolate and faint all the day.

**Nun**
Vigilavit jugum iniquitatum mearum: in manu eius convolutae sunt,
et impositae collo meo: infirmata est virtus mea:
dedit me Dominus in manu, de qua non potero surgere.

The yoke of my transgressions is bound by his hand;
they are knit together, they are come up upon my neck;
he hath made my strength to fail:
the Lord hath delivered me into their hands, against whom I am not able to stand.

**36. Jerusalem convertere ad Dominum Deum tuum.**
Jerusalem, return to the Lord thy God.
