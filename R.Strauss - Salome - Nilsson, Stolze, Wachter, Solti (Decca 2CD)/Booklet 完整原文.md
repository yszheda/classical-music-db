## Synopsis

The palace of the Tetrarch, Herod Antipas. On the terrace Narraboth, the young captain of the guard, and the Page are on watch. In the banquet hall the tetrarch is feasting. A young Syrian, Narraboth, is fascinated by Salome, stepdaughter of Herod and daughter of Herodias. He cannot take his eyes off her, and the Page tries to prevent him from gazing at the princess. The sound of a great voice is heard. It comes the voice of Jokanaan (John the Baptist) who has been imprisoned for defaming the Tetrarch and his wife. As two guards explain to a Cappadocian, Herod has ordered that no one speak to the prisoner. Salome comes out on the terrace to escape her stepfather's advances and the advances of Jokanaan. Her voice rings out again, and the guards prepare to drag him down into the cistern. Salome is intrigued by Jokanaan's voice and demands to see him. When the captain of the guard refuses to let her see the prophet, she threatens to denounce him to the Tetrarch. Fearing her power, he relents and allows her to see Jokanaan.

When Salome sees Jokanaan she is immediately captivated. She praises his white skin, his raven hair, and his "mouth like a scarlet thread." Jokanaan rebuffs her advances, calling her "daughter of Babylon" and "daughter of Sodom." Salome is not discouraged. She now demands to touch his body. Jokanaan again rebuffs her, and she asks for his hair. Again he rejects her. Finally, she demands to kiss his mouth. Narraboth, who has been listening to this exchange, is overcome with jealousy and kills himself. The Page comments that he too will soon be dead. Jokanaan returns to the cistern, and Salome is left alone.
