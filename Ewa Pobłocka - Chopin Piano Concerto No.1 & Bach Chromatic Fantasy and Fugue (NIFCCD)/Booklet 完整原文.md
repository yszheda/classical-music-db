Ewa Pobłocka... o tym, o co nigdy nikt mnie nie zapytał...

Jedyne miejsce na ziemi?
Gdańsk-Oliwa, bo brzeg morza, lasy i wzgórza morenowe, widok z wykusza na Kwietnej, piaseczyste dufty.

Oprócz muzyki interesuje mnie...
Poszukiwanie odpowiedzi na temat sensu sztuki i znalezienie „obiektywnej" definicji piękna. Czym jest sztuka? Moim zdaniem, powinna wywołać emocje, wzruszać, budzić zachwyt, ale ma też sprawiać, że czujemy się piękniejsi, bo wzbogaceni pięknem.

Najpiękniejsze miejsca – podróże?
– Malta, bo słońce, piękna architektura i mimo, że to wyspa – poczucie nieograniczenia!
– Szkocja, bo surowa, podobnie Norwegia, ale cudowne krajobrazy...
– Kolumbia, bo ma w sobie energię; podobnie jak ludzie z Ameryki Południowej;
– Wenecja, bo jesienią „pełna mgieł", kościół na wyspie Torcello, a w nim posadzki;
– Chiny, bo najbardziej interesujące zetknięcie z zupełnie inną kulturą;
– Wietnam, bo to azjatycka Italia;
– Skandynawia, bo surowość natury, specjalne światło, umiejętność kreowania nastroju (świece!).

## 内页 12-13 (HIPPO_20241231_0008.jpg - 英文访谈)

Ewa Pobłocka ... answers to questions nobody has ever asked me ...

My place on Earth?
Gdańsk-Oliwa, because of the seashore, forests, and moraine hills, the view from the bay window in Kwietna Street, the sandy trails.

Apart from music, I am interested in ...
Finding an answer to the questions surrounding the sense of art, and discovering an 'objective' definition of beauty. What is art? In my opinion it should trigger our emotions, move us, inspire admiration, but also make us feel more beautiful, as we become enriched with the beauty we derive from art.

Places to travel?
Malta, sunshine, architecture and, despite it being an island, the sense of boundlessness!
Scotland, austere, like Norway, but that incredible landscape...
Columbia, full of energy, just like people from South America.
Venice, with its autumn mists, the church on the island of Torcello, and the mosaics.
China, the most fascinating encounter with a different culture.
Vietnam, the Italy of Asia.
Scandinavia, the austerity of nature, unique light, the ability to create a mood (candles!).

## 内页 14-15 (HIPPO_20241231_0009.jpg - 英文访谈续)

Kashuby, Stone Circles, and the silence and magic there.
Prezemysl and the view from Kalwaria Packowska towards Ukraine.

Dream of destinations, still to visit?
Mexico and the Shetland Islands.

Sources of inspiration?
There are moments, insubstantial but remembered, often caused by the "genius loci" of the places where I stay, play, listen and watch ...
A view from the window, a single line of a poem, or even just a few words, a gust of sea breeze, the colour of clouds at sunset.

The most important and memorable concert performances, and why?
I remember best those concerts during which I had an impression that I was actually composing the piece I was performing, and also those at which I encountered a strong feeling of communication with the audience. Those were not necessarily the events held in 'top' concert halls of the world—
from those prestige occasions I recall huge stress and the internal imperative "I must".

Obviously, there were exceptions, such as my first performance during the Warsaw Autumn with Panufnik's Concerto in 1990 at the Warsaw Philharmonic Concert Hall, when after the first part of the concert—Haydn's "Ariadne on Naxos", and the recital in Herkulessaal, Munich, where I played Bach's Variations and felt the audience listening intently, following me.

Other memorable concerts were those in Winchester (UK) when a chorus of birds suddenly broke into song while I was playing Schubert, and the fragrance of freshly mown grass drifted through opened windows. Also Białystok, the hall in the Branicki Palace with Olga Pasiecznik—we were performing Duparc's Songs, about death. The emotional tension among the public, or rather the invisible thread linking us and the listeners, was incredible!

Lately—my recital at the Gliwice Palm House, among lush greenery, in humility reaching 90 percent, playing for the audience "almost hidden in the bush"—I could feel them breathing, greedy for music.

My first Bach recital at the Warsaw Philharmonic. Performing Bach on Good Friday at the Ethnographic Museum in Pruszków, when I asked the audience not to applaud. They were only Bach and the lights went out together with the last chord. I wanted everybody to leave the concert just with the music in their souls.

The first concert under Witold Lutosławski with the Silesian Philharmonic in Katowice—playing with that concern, "don't spoil the composer's work in his presence".

Lutosławski's Concerto performed to mark the centenary of the Warsaw Philharmonic under Kazimierz Kord, when only music mattered. I could feel the audience's friendliness and the musicians' concentration.

## 内页 16-17 (HIPPO_20241231_0010.jpg - 英文访谈续)

Concerts in Saint Petersburg—a special audience there, well-educated and music-thirsty, and all those flowers received from complete strangers.

A concert in Uzhhorod (Ukraine)—in a magnificent synagogue turned into a movie theatre with exquisite acoustics. I began my recital with the hall only half filled with audience members as I played the number of listeners steadily grew (thanks to mobiles!) until the end when I was given virtual storm of applause for Bach, Mozart, Chopin and Panufnik.

The concert at Théâtre des Champs Elysées, where I played with the Silesian String Quartet (Bach, Mozart, Chopin, Panufnik).

And finally, chamber concerts with Dang Thai Son—a true music-making experience. Nothing trivial counted, not the dress, nor the piano stool. Just becoming one with the sound ... playing Mozart as Mozart, Chopin as Chopin and Schubert as Schubert.

And conductors?
There are four of whom I think with endless warmth, almost love. Each one different, each leaving his own "mark" on me.

Kazimierz Kord, with whom I have played the greatest number of concerts and at least half of my repertoire for piano with orchestra (Bach, Mozart, Chopin, Ravel, Saint-Saëns, Grieg, Panufnik, Lutosławski, Szymanowski). It was Kord who taught me 'how to perform and record with the orchestra, how to listen to music, how to evaluate myself and how to survive several weeks on a concert tour'. Immediately after one such tour of the Federal Republic of Germany, he proposed to her this concert, which stays untroubled in my memory.

My husband Stanisław Leszczyński, because he shaped my musical taste, pointed out the necessity of looking for artistic truth and widened my horizons immensely (informed historic performance, performance styles, knowledge of global discography).

Marek Moś, because he taught me to read the score and notice all details being a contemporary counterpart of the „Renaissance Man", in a sense he shaped my view on music but, as he is a profoundly thinking humanist, also on interpersonal relations.

Kazimierz Kord, because he believed in my abilities; he was always encouraging me to act.

Andrzej Chłopecki, because he opened my eyes to the world of contemporary music.

Composers whom I had the honour to work with and perform the music they wrote for me. Because they were insightful, uncompromising, had crystal-clear views on things. I was always curious to find out how they perceived reality—the same way as I did, or differently?

What is life about?
In matters that we have under control: life is about finding the right proportion between work and leisure, between what is vital and what we can let go, for a single word, the clue is in the proportions, just like in ancient art, especially architecture. To be able to separate professional passion from life, devoting time and attention to the latter.

And in matters we cannot control: we should enjoy the good that life has in store for us and find in ourselves the humility and acceptance of the outcomes of fate, not neglecting to nurture our fighting spirit. Life is wonderful and worth living, despite the tears ...

Definition of an artist?
An artist is a person who, regardless of his or her situation in life, demonstrates creative will, intuition and the ability to constantly search for new means of expression to convey thoughts and emotions.

... I do not mention my family ...
this is the question you should ask my daughters.

I dream of ...?
Long walks along the sea coast.

Being able to see a true opera or theatre performance without the addition of rubber boots, swimming pools, brothels, dirty words or provocations. It could be some Classical Greek play with a chorus and a protagonist ... But it should treat the words (or music—for instance in Mozart's operas) as the priority. The world in which the man's right to enjoy silence is observed.

The world in which the man's right to enjoy silence is observed.

## 内页 20-21 (HIPPO_20241231_0012.jpg - 艺术家传记)

[Ewa Pobłocka 与 Kazimierz Kord 照片]

Ewa Pobłocka ...

Czy to synonim dla „polskiej pianistki" nie jest jej wyzwala, ale także trudna i odpowiedzialna dziwaczna wersja „chopinizmu"? Pytanie retoryczne, które najlepiej pozostawić bez odpowiedzi. Istnieją jednak artyści, których nie wyobrażalna jest izolacja od dzieła Fryderyka Chopina, i którzy, choć pomyślnie zwiedzają rozległe terytoria muzyki, do Chopina wciąż powracają. Ewa Pobłocka jest wśród nich. Ciekawe, kiedy pierwszy raz o Chopinie pomyślała? Wywiadzie z samą sobą z tego roku, przyznała: „fakt, że zagrałam w Konkursie Chopinowskim, był decyzją podjętą już bardzo dawno temu. Już w pierwszej klasie szkoły podstawowej wiedziałam, że zagram tam". Ale Chopin to przecież tylko jeden z wielu punktów odniesienia w muzycznej konstelacji kompozytorów, którzy tworzą jej świat...

Dla polskiej pianistki Konkurs Chopinowski jest jednocześnie błogosławieństwem i przekleństwem, bo czyż polski pianista bez muzyki Chopina może w ogóle egzystować? Ewa Pobłocka w październiku 1980 roku, podczas zmagań w 10. Międzynarodowym Konkursie Pianistycznym, nie miała łatwego zadania. Na jej barki spadła odpowiedzialność podwójna. Po pierwsze była w polskiej ekipie pianistów, która zwyciężyła w tzw. „małym konkursie chopinowskim" — eliminacjach ogólnopolskich, wyłaniających krajową „drużynę" do udziału w „tym prawdziwym". Realia socjalistycznej Polski Ludowej nie ułatwiały zadania. Po drugie — jak to podał Stanisław Dąbrowski — „na swej nagrodzie Ewa Pobłocka ciężko zapracowała, nie poddając się stresowi nawet wtedy, gdy od trzeciego etapu zaczęła walczyć własnymi siłami sama broniła honoru polskiej interpretacji". Trochę mniej wysiłku, wyrzeczeń i determinacji, by nie poddać się ciężarowi oczekiwań od widowni złożonej z publiczności — Nagrody Polskiego Radia za najlepsze wykonanie muzyki Chopina na konkursie, w 1980 roku należy uznać za podwójnie wymagającą. Nagrodę „Złota Polska" otrzymała za najlepsze wykonanie koncertu — Mazurkami. W finale wykonała Koncert e-moll op. 11 z Orkiestrą Filharmonii Narodowej pod dyrekcją Kazimierza Korda.

W finale pianistka wykonała Koncert fortepianowy e-moll op. 11 z Orkiestrą Filharmonii Narodowej w październiku 1980 roku, był nim Tadeusz Strugała; 17 grudnia 1984, był nim jej ówczesny dyrektor artystyczny dyrygent tej samej orkiestry stał jej ówczesny dyrektor artystyczny — Kazimierz Kord. Dla Ewy Pobłocki stał się on jednym z najważniejszych mentorów, ale także artystycznych przyjaciół. Z Kazimierzem Kordem, jak sama mówi, „zagrałam więcej koncertów i z większej części mojego repertuaru z fortepianem i orkiestrą (Bach, Mozart, Chopin, Ravel, Saint-Saëns, Grieg, Panufnik, Lutosławski, Szymanowski). To Kord nauczył mnie „jak wykonywać i nagrywać z orkiestrą, jak słuchać muzyki i jak oceniać samą siebie i jak przetrwać kilka tygodniowe tournee". Bezpośrednio po jednym z takich tournee w Republice Federalnej Niemiec zaproponował jej ten koncert, który utrwalony jest na naszej płycie.

## 内页 22-23 (HIPPO_20241231_0013.jpg - 英文传记)

Could it be that a synonym for Polish 'pianist' is its narrower—yet treacherous—field known as 'Chopinism'? A rhetorical question, which is best left unanswered. Yet there are artists who could hardly be imagined in isolation from the work of Fryderyk Chopin and who, although successfully exploring extensive areas of music, continually return to Chopin. Ewa Pobłocka is among them. It is interesting to wonder when she first thought about Chopin. In the self-conducted interview on her website, she does not let on. In 1980, on the pages of Głos Wybrzeża, she admitted: 'the fact that I would play in the Chopin Competition was decided a very long time ago. Already in the first year at primary school I decided that I would play in the Competition'. I always remembered about that [...]. Yet Chopin is just one of the many reference points in the constellation of the composers who form her world...

For Polish pianists, the Chopin Competition is both a blessing and a curse. After all, can they possibly exist without the music of Chopin? In October 1980, when competing in the 10th International Piano Competition, Ewa Pobłocka was faced with no easy task. A dual responsibility fell on her shoulders. First, she had won the so-called 'little Chopin Competition'—the nationwide qualifying competition held to find the Polish 'team' to take part in the 'real thing'. The realities of socialist People's Poland made it impossible to compete on one's own initiative. Secondly, as Stanisław Dąbrowski put it, 'Ewa Pobłocka worked hard for her prize, not succumbing to stress even when, from the third round onwards, burdened with a great responsibility, she alone was defending the honour of Polish Chopin interpretation'. It takes great strength and determination not to yield to the weight of expectation from an audience demanding that you win.' Ewa Pobłocka emerged victorious from that extraordinary competition (in 1980, the Chopin Competition was marked by a very high standard and the now legendary 'row' over the judges). She won Fifth Prize and the prize for a Polish artist is perhaps the most important for the best performance of a Chopin work on the piano—the Annual Award of the Polish Radio for the best performance of Chopin mazurkas. In the final, she performed the Piano Concerto in E minor, Op. 11 with the Warsaw Philharmonic Orchestra. In October 1980, the WFO was conducted by Tadeusz Strugała; on 17 December 1984, it was led by its then artistic director, Kazimierz Kord. For Ewa Pobłocka, Kord became one of the key figures on her artistic pathway, the conductor with whom she has played the greatest number of concerts and at least half of my repertoire for piano with orchestra (Bach, Mozart, Chopin, Ravel, Saint-Saëns, Grieg, Panufnik, Lutosławski, Szymanowski). It was Kord who taught her 'how to perform and record with the orchestra, how to listen to music, how to evaluate myself and how to survive several weeks on a concert tour'. Immediately after one such tour of the Federal Republic of Germany, he proposed to her this concert, which stays untroubled in my memory.

## 内页 24-25 (HIPPO_20241231_0014.jpg - 英文文章续)

in that interview for Głos Wybrzeża, she confessed to Wanda Obniśka:
'The Competition had already been going for six days when I arrived in Warsaw to play. I came to the Philharmonic and played myself in with Bach.
Bach's music has such order and calmness that it attunes one quite splendidly.' So the music of Bach also within the context of the Chopin competition, and at a crucial moment, at the very beginning of a difficult, mentally onerous ordeal. Johann Sebastian Bach and his ordered, perfectly designed soundworld were for Ewa Pobłocka among the most important points of reference, and she is one of those artists who play his music in a very personal way. The reviewer for Życie Warszawy placed her Bach interpretation 'somewhere between Arrau and Richter'. Jan Weber wrote as well, that 'rarely can one hear such an original and beautiful performance of Bach by a Polish pianist. The text was ideally served—vividly, limpidly and distinctly: the polyphonic voices were marvellously led, and there was restraint in the tempo and emotions.' Those words referred to a studio album recorded for Polish Radio in 1983, on which, alongside Schumann's Sonata No. 2 in G minor, Pobłocka played Bach's Partita No. 6 in E minor, BWV 830.

Weber gave a similar appraisal of a recital in the Chamber Hall at the Warsaw Philharmonic on 5 February 1985, in which she performed the same Partita and the Chromatic Fantasy and Fugue in D minor, BWV 903. Her Bach is perfectly precise, but not mechanical; so crystal clear and ostensibly calm in details, because that is merely the surface. A great deal is going on here, but in order to persuasively convey the content of that music, hidden within the sound structures, and to speak directly to the listeners, Pobłocka deliberately and consistently controls the emotions. As a result, the climaxes—for instance in the Fugue of BWV 903 and in the opening Toccata and the closing Gigue from the Partita—are convincing in their dramatic consistency. In the Partita, there is also no lack of poetic episodes, expressive of delicacies (Allemande) or the utter suspension of time (Sarabande). There is a contrast between motoric and energetic movements (Corrente and Air), and the work is rounded off by a polyphonic Gigue, with a masterfully differentiated structure of voices and layers. It is really a unique creation in her rich output of Bach recordings.

## 内页 26-27 (HIPPO_20241231_0015.jpg - 文章续)

According to Ewa Pobłocka, an artist is someone who demonstrates 'the ability to constantly search for new means of expression to convey thoughts and emotions'. The pianist's words chime with the understanding of human nature defined by Romain Ingaron (man as a creator of values'). In the short, but fascinating, essay 'O naturze ludzkiej' [On human nature], he wrote: 'we can gain full satisfaction in life solely from the fact that we have succeeded in creating values that bear the stamp of our personality'...

## 内页 28-29 (HIPPO_20241231_0016.jpg - 文章续)

Niezwykłość artystycznej więzi Kord i Pobłocki słychać w każdym dźwięku tego nagrania, prezentowanego tutaj bez żadnych retusz. To Koncert e-moll specyficznie bezkompromisowy: prosty, jasny i przejrzysty, energiczny i liryczny, momentami szybki i zdecydowany, oddany w tempach szybkich, gdzie „słyszy" się dyrygenta. Ale też pełen manier – tam, gdzie maniery są niezbędne. W skrajnych przypadkach, gdzie wykonanie przez Kazimierza Korda, ani Ewa Pobłocka nie „zmiękcza" się na drobne – nie szukają tysiąca zbiegów szczegółów, które koniecznie trzeba wyłożyć na pierwszy plan, by „słuchać się" w dźwiękach, ale „słuchać się" w całą sytuację bytującą między dźwiękami – „słuchać" czystego obrazu muzyki. Tutaj przemawia Chopin swoją młodzieńczą naturą, prostotą, zrozumieniem i uwydatnianiem przez wykonanowanie. Kilka tekstowych niecierpliwości, niefortunności (wroocze wraże) dodaje wykonaniu rumieńców. Allegro maestoso z inwencją i niepotrzebną pomocą – w momencie Romany jest spokojna, ale nie nudna, utrzymana w odpowiednich proporcjach, ale też niesie moment (ten słynny „bell" z exkluzją magii, co jest trudne do znalezienia w innych relacjach. Czas zatrzymuje się, nastrój i barwa są tam do wyszukania. Nikt, kto nie zna tych kilkunastu sekund muzyki Chopina nie wie, jak wykonać to trzydzieści kilka tysięcy minut muzyki Chopina – nieważne czy nie należącej do jego obozu.

Na pytanie o „ulubionych kompozytorów" zadane samiej sobie w wywiadzie „auto-wywiadowczym" umieszczonym na jej stronie internetowej, Ewa Pobłocka wymieniła jedynie dwa nazwiska – i nie ma wśród nich Fryderyk Chopin — „Bach, bo każda nuta na chwałę Pana i ładowana optymizmem nawet w Trauer-Ode; Schubert, bo smutek i wzruszenie...".

## 内页 30-31 (HIPPO_20241231_0017.jpg - 封底内页)

W Particie nie brakuje zresztą epizodów poetyckich, przemawiających delikatnością (Allemande) czy zupelnym zawieszeniem czasu (Sarabande). Skontrastowane są ognistymi motorowymi i energicznymi (Corrente i Air), a całość podsumowuje polifoniczny Gigue – z mistrzowsko zróżnicowaną strukturą głosów i płaszczyzn. Kreacja właściwie unikalna wśród Bachowskich nagrań.

Według Ewy Pobłockiej artystą jest ktoś, kto posiada [...] zdolność ciągłego poszukiwania coraz to nowych źródeł wyrazu dla przekazania swoich myśli i emocji". W krótkim, ale fascynującym eseju „O naturze ludzkiej", w którym zdefiniował człowieka jako „twórcę wartości", Romain Ingaron [...] pisał: „że jedynie zadowolenie w życiu czerpać można stąd, iż udało się nam stworzyć wartości noszące piętno naszej osobowości"...

Marcin Majchrowski
