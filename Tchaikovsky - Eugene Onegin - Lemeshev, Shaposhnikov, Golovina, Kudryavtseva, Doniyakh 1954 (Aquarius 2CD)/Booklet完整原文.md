## Page 3 (scans/HIPPO_20241208_0003.jpg) - Artist Biographies (Russian)

В рамках большого Собрания записей великого русского певца С.Я. Лемешева в «Аквариусе» выходит уникальная запись спектакля «Евгений Онегин», сделанная по трансляции из Ленинградского Малого оперного театра. До сих пор эта бесценная во всех отношениях запись не была доступна широкой аудитории и выходит в свет впервые. Наряду с С.Я. Лемешевым и В.Н. Кудрявцевой, дуэтам с которой был посвящен один из дисков Собрания, в спектакле заняты выдающиеся артисты ленинградской оперной сцены.

**Обладатель редкой красоты лирического баритона Сергей Николаевич Шапошников** (1911-1973) был певцом высочайшей музыкальной культуры. Тонкое чувство стиля, совершенная дикция и безукоризненное владение словом позволили ему стать не только выдающимся оперным, но и камерным певцом. С 1935 г. Шапошников был солистом Малого оперного театра, на сцене которого он выступал почти 40 лет, исполняя ведущие партии. Помимо Онегина - Роберт в «Иоланте», Жермон, Фигаро в «Севильском цирюльнике», Андрей Волконский, Евгений Листницкий в «Тихом Доне», Олег Кошевой в «Молодой гвардии» Ю. Мейтуса, Кирилл Извеков в одноименной опере А. Чернова, Паратов в «Бесприданнице» Д. Френкеля, и др. Как концертный певец он исполнял романсы и песни П. Чайковского, А. Дворжака, А. Алябьева, А. Аренского, М. Балакирева, М. Глинки, А. Даргомыжского, С. Рахманинова, Н. Римского-Корсакова, вокальные циклы Ю. Шапорина, Ф. Шуберта, Д. Кабалевского, А. Рубинштейна, Г. Свиридова, Д. Шостаковича, Р. Шумана, В. Волошинова, русские народные песни. Многие из них известны в записях на радио и на пластинках. Долгие годы дружбы соединяли С.Н. Шапошникова и С.Я. Лемешева. В 1971 г. Сергей Яковлевич писал Шапошникову:

«Друг мой, дорогой Сереженка! Беспримерно благодарю тебя за теплое, нежное ко мне, в то же время очень серьезное и умное по отношению к нашему искусству, письмо... Я иногда слушаю тебя по радио. И всегда радуюсь, что все твои высокие намерения, твой академизм и благородство исполнения никогда тебе не изменяют. С большой радостью прочитал в письме о том, что ты сейчас идешь петь Жермона. Молодец!!! Восьмого ноября в Ленинградской филармонии должен состояться мой концерт с оркестром Федосеева. Буду очень счастлив повидаться с тобой. Сердечно обнимаю. Всегда твой Сергей Лемешев».

**Меццо-сопрано Ольга Николаевна Головина (1904-1975)** обучалась пению у Л.Г. Звягиной, пела в оперных театрах Самары (1930-1933), Свердловска (1936-1937). В 1933-1936 и после 1937 г. была солисткой Малого оперного театра. Среди оперных партий - Любаша, Далила Кармен, Лель, Любава, Марина Мнишек, Солоха. Обладала сочным голосом и драматическим темпераментом. Была исполнительницей партий Графиня (в очереди с Н.Л. Вельтер) и Полина в «Пиковой даме», поставленной в 1935 г. в МАЛЕГОТе В.Э. Мейерхольдом.

### Сюжет

**Действие первое. Картина первая.** Летний вечер. Сад в усадьбе Лариных. Юные голоса дочерей - Татьяны и Ольги - вызывают у Лариной и няни Филиппьевны воспоминания о молодости. Издали доносятся звуки протяжной песни. Это крестьяне пришли поздравить барина и принесли с собой разукрашенными цветами и лентами сноп - символ законченной жатвы. Песни крестьян привлекают внимание девушек - задумчивой, мечтательной Татьяны и беззаботной, шаловливой Ольги. В гости к Лариным приезжают их сосед Владимир Ленский, влюбленный в Ольгу восторженный юноша-поэт, и его друг Онегин, светский молодой человек, недавно прибывший из Петербурга и скучающий в деревенской глуши. Татьяна глубоко взволнована встречей с Онегиным, в нем она видит своего избранника.

Continuing our collection of recordings of the great Russian singer Sergei Lemeshev we offer this unique recording of a performance of "Eugene Onegin", which was recorded live at the Maly Opera Theatre in Leningrad. This priceless recording has never been made available to the general public and is published here for the first time. Along with Sergei Lemeshev and Vera Kudryavtseva, who have already been the focus of an earlier release, are assembled a cast of prominent artists from the Leningrad opera.

**Sergei Shaposhnikov (1911-1973)**, the possessor of a lyric-baritone voice of rare beauty was a singer of the highest musical culture. His refined sense of style, perfect diction and vocal acting allowed him to become not only an outstanding opera, but also chamber singer. Shaposhnikov was the lead baritone at the Maly Opera Theatre from 1935 - the stage upon which he performed for almost 40 years. In addition to Onegin he also performed Robert in "Iolanta", Germont, Figaro in "Il barbiere di Siviglia", Andrei Bolkonsky, Eugene Listnitsky in "Quiet Flows the Don", Oleg Koshevoy in "The Young Guard" by Yuri Meitus, Kyril Izvekov in A. Tchernov's opera of the same name, Paratov in "The Dowry" by D. Frenkel, etc. As a concert singer, he performed romances and songs by Tchaikovsky, Dvorak, Alyabev, Arensky, Balakirev, Glinka, Dargomyzhsky, Rachmaninov, Rimsky-Korsakov, song cycles by Shaporin, Schubert, Kabalevsky, Rubinstein, Sviridov, Shostakovich, Schumann, Voloshinov and Russian folk songs. Many of these are known from radio broadcasts and records. He enjoyed a long friendship with Lemeshev who wrote to him in 1971: "My friend, dear Seryozhenka! Infinite thanks for your warmth and tenderness towards me, and at the same time a great respect to your serious and intelligent writing on our art... Sometimes I hear you on the radio. And as always I am gratified that all your lofty intentions, your academic performance and generosity of spirit never betray you. It is with real pleasure that I read in your letter that you are now going to sing Germont, Congratulations! I will be performing in a concert at the Leningrad Philharmonic conducted by Fedoseev on the Eighth of November. I would be very happy to see you. I cordially embrace you. Yours as always Sergei Lemeshev".

**The mezzo-soprano Olga Golovina (1904-1975)** studied singing with L. Zvyagina. She sang in the opera houses of Samara (1930-1933) and Sverdlovsk (1936-1937) and from 1937 was engaged as a soloist at the Maly Opera Theater. Among her operatic roles were Ljubasha, Delilah, Carmen, Lel, Lyubava, Marina Mnishek and Solokha. She had a rich voice with dramatic temperament. She performed the role of the Countess (sharing the role with Nadezhda Welter) and Pauline in Meyerhold's scandalous interpretation of "The Queen of Spades" at MALEGOT (1935). Amongst her recorded legacy are: Azucena in "Il Trovatore" (1950, cond. E. Grikurov), Martha in "Iolanta" (1955, cond. Grikurov), Frau Reich in "Die lustigen Weiber von Windsor" by O. Nicolai (montage, 1950, cond. N. Rabinovich), La Princesse de Grenade in Offenbach's operetta "Les brigands" (1948, cond. Grikurov), the 'fortune telling' scene from the opera "Carmen" (1939, cond. S. Brog), Azucena's tale, and the wedding couplets from the film of Dunayevsky's 'The Seekers of Happiness" (1938).

**Xenia Komissarova (1898-1979)** was a singer (mezzo-soprano) and manager. She had a repertoire of more than 60 roles. Including - Khivria, the Countess, Maddalena in "Rigoletto", Marcellina in "Le nozze di Figaro", Ursula in "The Bartered Bride" (A.N. Tolstoy and V. Rozhdestvensky edition), the sister-in-law in "May Night", etc. Her recordings include - Khivria and Popovich's scene from the opera "Sorochinsky Fair" (1939, with A. Kabanov, cond. S. Brog), the scene at the inn from "Boris Godunov" (1947, with Pavel Zhuravlenko, cond. E. Grikurov), and Grunya's song from the film "The Goalkeeper" (1937).

**Nikolai Tchesnokov (1898 - ?)**, who sings the role of Triquet in this performance, is well-known for his wonderful recordings of old Russian romances on shellac. He also recorded the role of the Clerk in Tchaikovsky's opera "Tcherevichki" (The Slippers) which he also played in the 1944 film of the opera. On the Maly stage he performed the roles of Lensky, Simpleton, Vašek in "The Bartered Bride", Bomelei in "The Tsar's Bride", Pantelei Melekhov in "Quiet Flows the Don" and the Announcer in "Colas Breugnon".

The bass **Valery Raikov**, who performs the role of the company commander, sang at the Maly Theatre from 1920. Amongst the most notable roles in his vast repertoire were Don Pasquale in Donizetti's opera, Doctor Bartolo in "Il barbiere di Siviglia" and "Le nozze di Figaro", Veit Pogner in the "Die Meistersinger von Nürnberg", Dapertutto in "Les contes d'Hoffmann", Gaspard in "Les cloches de Corneville", Dodon in the "Golden Cockerel", Kecal in "The Bartered Bride", Tcherevik in "Sorochinsky Fair", Frère Laurent in "Roméo et Juliette" and Zuniga in "Carmen". His recordings include Ketsal and Jenik's duet (with Boris Geft).

### Synopsis

**Act One. Scene I.** A summer's evening in the garden of the Larin estate. Upon hearing the youthful voices of her daughters - Tatyana and Olga - Larina and the nurse Filippyevna reminisce upon their youth. From a distance, they can hear the sound of a lyrical song. The peasants arrive to congratulate their mistress for the end of the harvest - bringing with them the symbolic gift of a sheaf of wheat decorated with coloured flowers and ribbons. The peasant's song attracts the attention of the girls - a wistful, dreamy Tatyana and carefree, playful Olga. They are visited by their neighbour Vladimir Lensky, a young poet madly in love with Olga, and his friend Onegin, an aristocratic young man recently arrived from St. Petersburg and already bored with living in the country. Tatyana is deeply affected by the meeting with Onegin, in whom she can see her future husband.

**Scene II.** Tatiana's bedroom. The girl is consumed by anxious thoughts. She is unable to sleep and asks the nurse to tell her about her youth. But this does not curb her excitement. She is absorbed with these new, unknown feelings and writes a letter to Onegin, confessing her love.

**Scene III.** In Larin's shaded garden the girls sing songs whilst gathering berries. Tatiana runs in excitedly. Onegin has arrived, and will be with them in a moment. Trembling, she expects a response to her confession. Onegin is polite and restrained. He is touched by Tatyana's sincerity, but is unable to reciprocate her love. Shocked and pained she listens to his lecture.

**Act Three. Scene I.** The nobility of St Petersburg are gathered at a big mansion in the capital. A glittering metropolitan ball is in full swing. Among the guests is Onegin, having returned from his travels. Neither travel nor leisure has lifted his feeling of boredom. Prince Gremin appears with his wife, and Onegin is astonished to recognize Tatyana. The Prince cordially tells him about his happy marriage to Tatyana and introduces Onegin to her. Only now does he understand the womanly perfection embodied in the young woman whom he had once so callously rejected. He is suddenly seized with love for Tatyana, and he decides to arrange a meeting with her.

**Scene II.** In a living room Tatyana excitedly reads Onegin's letter. She still loves him. Suddenly Onegin enters. In his words she hears his confession of love and repentance. Tatyana remembers their first meeting, when happiness was so possible and so close. But the past cannot be revisited; appealing to his honour and pride she asks him to leave her. Girlish illusions no longer hold any power over her. Onegin is left alone.
