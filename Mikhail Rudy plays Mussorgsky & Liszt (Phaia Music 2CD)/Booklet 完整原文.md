## English Liner Notes

### Modest Mussorgsky - Pictures at an Exhibition

The Pictures at an Exhibition is a suite of ten pieces, which evoke some of Hartmann's watercolors and drawings. The pictures are evoked, not described, in that the composer tried to translate into music the impressions and emotions he felt while walking through the gallery. Indeed the very lively theme by the different sections as it strigger was set together was given the title of "Promenade" by Mussorgsky.

The suite begins with the Promenade, which leads to Gnomus, the first picture, representing an ugly, misshapen creature, whose slender, crooked legs permit him to walk only with difficulty and in a grotesque manner. In the middle of the piece, however, the gnome makes an impressive and menacing appearance; the other tormented and tortuous like the gnome's gait. Then, in a quickly rising final flourish, Moussorgsky continues his Promenade, now melancholic and dreamy, toward Il Vecchio Castello. At the sound of this nostalgic cantilena a troubadour is singing a sweet, melancholy ballad to the accompaniment of his lute, whose rhythmic strummings are echoed in the piano low strings. This sad serenade, marked con gran dolore (with great pain) is a dream and the visitor continues his Promenade at a quick pace and hesitatingly stops in front of a picture representing the Tuileries at Paris: the Dispute of the Children after Play.

Then, without transition, another painting, Bydlo ("Cattle" in Polish), attracts Mussorgsky's attention. This heavy Polish cart with enormous wheels that is drawn by a team of oxen; it approaches in a vain lament, then returns, now lost in tinged with melancholy and sorrow: its theme is in a minor key. But with two exceptions, the Promenade, which is in a major key, reappears to link each of the succeeding pieces. This humorous, playful dance was inspired by one of Hartmann's costume sketches for the ballet Trilby; it is interrupted three times by a long high note that seems to suggest the crowing of the future cock.

The sixth picture is of two Jews from the Warsaw ghetto, Samuel Goldenberg and Schmuyle. The first is rich and fat, swollen by his self-importance; the second poor, thin, half-starved. The Promenade, which follows, acts as a sort of recapitulation, as though Mussorgsky wanted, in a kind of synthesis, to bring to a close, gossipy, squawking, and disputes. All this is very colorfully evoked by Mussorgsky, which makes for an extremely brilliant contrast to the next picture, Catacombs: Sepulchrum Romanum, which follows without transition and represents Hartmann visiting the Catacombs by the light of a lantern. The decor is suggested by a series of dark, powerful chords; then an Andante evokes Hartmann's slow walk along the rows of bones. A slightly modified version of the Promenade theme, extremely solemn, is used. On the score Mussorgsky wrote: "Cum mortuis in lingua mortua" ("With the dead in a dead language"), and then, in Russian: "The soul of my departed friend turns me to the skull and calls out to them, causing them to glow dimly from within": this picture ends with lighter harmonies.

Next we see the hut on Fowl's Legs, the strange cloak-shaped hut of the terrible witch Baba Yaga, the queen of the Russian fairytale. Here again the picture depicts not so much the witch herself (she is flying through the sky) whose central tricycle action evokes the weird atmosphere of the thick and disturbingly mysterious part of the forest where the hut is located. This picture ends with the diabolic scampering of the witch in her pursuit of a little frightened girl: the listener is taken along on her infernal gallop. These monumental doors inspired one of Mussorgsky's most grandiose pieces, with its large, imposing theme, which is at once heroic and religious, solemn and lyrical, which unfolds and develops majestically pealing bells. The work ends with the apotheosis of this majestically pealing bells.

### Franz Liszt

The unique and astounding sonata for piano of Liszt was preceded by five sonatas for piano solo, one sonata for piano and violin and one sonata for two pianos, a comparatively, small part of a total of over fourteen hundred works. And what is left of these youthful attempts which date almost all of his adolescence? The sonata for two pianos has been lost, the duo for piano and violin remains uncompleted and is several versions. As for the sonatas for piano solo, with their traditional tempo, only one can be found, and it is so different from the rest of Liszt's output and especially so, first and foremost, because of the place it occupies in the piano literature: it is its monolithism which, precisely, makes it the only one of its kind. It presents both pianists and musicologists with serious problems in terms of both its execution and its interpretation. It is, after all, just as much of a monolith as Beethoven's fourteenth Quartet, which by far exceeds its thirty-five minutes, but it is not built on a succession of tempi without any thematic reminders. Consequently some people have gone as far as to state that it was not a sonata at all!

In truth, this sonata in B minor, which is played in one go, like a Wagnerian act, requires more than a dozen different tempi. For it borrows from the sonata's thematic and formal elements which will be subject to variations, to fugal expositions, even to polyphonic superpositions like the Wagnerian leitmotifs. There will be cyclism of course, by force of circumstances, in this sonata and in fact, the profusion of tempi will lead, as in Wagner, to the suppression of "separate movements". One will discover there an abolition of all hierarchy between the themes and the time as between the characters and the scenes to which only the vocal success yields the preference of the audience. How, after that, can one not disorient a listener of sonatas, even if they are from the "last" Beethoven? How can one not put the most prepared interpreter in the awkward position? Now, let's not forget that in 1853, when Liszt completed this sonate - on February 2nd very exactly - Wagner was composing the Rhinegold, in other words was planting the first true milestone of the lyric reform.

The sonata is dedicated to Schumann: in return, obviously, for the dedication of the Fantaisie that only Robert could both understand and follow; but 1853 was to be both the last creative and lucid year of Schumann and it was only in 1854 that Liszt's sonata appeared at Härtel, when the beats of the Rhine rescued the unfortunate Schumann prey to the madness that had driven him to throw himself into the river.

Nuages Gris, dated from Weimar (1881). A quite the most atonal of these pages in spite of the continuous and ornamented pedal of B flat in the bass.

Prelude Funèbre. Dates from Budapest (1885). Antoine Golea quote highly observes that the fundamental of the modal scale on which this piece is built, is never heard.

(Ostern,insiter). Unknown date, but the andantes are all the more astounding. They contrast with the chromatic chords at the end, just like in the motet for men's choir and two organs, Ossa Arida.

Am Grabe Richard Wagners and Gondoles Lugubres. On February 13th, 1883 Wagner, suffering from erysipelas, was staying at the Palazzo Vendramin in Venice, about to leave for a gondola ride. On that day he was struck by a fatal crisis just as he was about to step into a gondola after having played on himself the opening scene of the Rhine Gold on the piano.

Bagatelle without Tonality. They again can seem very better than Antoine Golea who reminds us that "this is the piece where Liszt has gone furthest in terms of revolutionary innovations. No melodic lines, no succession of chords can be included in the sequence of major and minor keys. This work creates its own world of harmonies, its own rules.

The czarda is the national dance par excellence. At the end of his career, Liszt produced two. One of them macabre and this Czardas obstinée which turns around the pivot note of D sharp, authorizes false unresolved dissonant resolutions whose Bartók will have the secret, and expresses the desire of a nation which has not ceased through the centuries to assert itself despite all political and cultural invasions.

### Modest Moussorgsky - Tableaux d'une Exposition

C'est sous le coup de l'intense émotion éprouvée en visitant l'exposition posthume d'aquarelles et de dessins de son ami, l'architecte Victor Hartmann que Moussorgsky entreprend, le 12 juin de la même année, la composition des Tableaux. La mort d'Hartmann, survenue subitement le 23 juillet 1873, avait profondément affecté Moussorgsky. Malgré tout Hartmann devait perdre un ami se mêlait le remords de n'avoir pas toujours su lui manifester assez de sollicitude. Le maître et le compositeur s'étaient rencontrés plusieurs années auparavant et, au cours d'une période de grande activité créatrice, "Hartmann bouillonne comme bouillon-Moussorgsky" -écrit Stassov-. J'ai beaucoup appris de lui et beaucoup de bonheur à sa seule proximité et à ses paroles". Et le résultat fut tel que, fait rare, l'auteur lui-même s'en montra satisfait: un chef-d'œuvre de la littérature du piano était né.

Les Tableaux se présentent sous la forme d'une suite pour le piano d'une dizaine de pièces, évoquant chacune des aquarelles de Hartmann. Ce n'est à titre que lui-même a donné au thème très liant qui parcoure les diverses pièces, en évitant la monotonie d'un retour identique: celui de la Promenade.

### Franz Liszt

L'unique et monumentale sonate pour piano de Liszt (1852-53) fut précédée de cinq sonates pour piano seul, de deux sonates pour piano et violon et d'une sonate à deux pianos. Malgré bien pour un catalogue dépassant les quatorze cent opus! Et que reste-t-il de ces juvéniles essais qui remontent presque tous à l'adolescence? La plupart est perdue, le duo piano et violon est inachevé et subsiste en plusieurs versions. Quant aux sonates pour piano solo, aux temps bien traditionnels, on n'en peut recenser qu'une! Et qui est si différente du reste du catalogue de Liszt, avec d'un fort avant tant et surtout par la place qu'elle occupe dans la littérature pianistique: elle la doit à son monolithisme qui fait, juste-ment, son unicité. Aussi pose-t-elle aux pianistes comme aux musicologues de sérieux problèmes tant l'unité d'interprétation que d'exégèse. Elle n'est après tout ni plus ni moins monolithique que le quatuor de Beethoven, mais elle dépasse largement ses trente-cinq minutes, mais dix ne sont pas faite, elle, d'un enchaînement de tempi divers sans rappels thématiques. On en est allé jusqu'à prétendre que ce n'était une sonate à un seul mouvement!

A la vérité, cette sonate en si mineur, qui se joue d'un seul trait, comme un acte wagnérien, exige plus d'une dizaine de minutes de son orchestre. Car elle emprunte à la sonate des éléments thématiques et formels qui seront aussi sujets à variations, à expositions fuguées, voire à superpositions polyphoniques que les leit-motifs wagnériens. Il y aura du cyclisme bien sûr, de par la force des choses, dans cette sonate et de fait, le foisonnement de tempi qui aboutira, comme chez Wagner, à la suppression des "morceaux séparés". On y découvrira sur une abolition de toute hiérarchie entre les thèmes et le temps comme entre les personnages et les scènes auxquelles seule la réussite vocale la préférence de l'auditoire. Comment, après cela, ne pas dérouter un auditeur de sonates, fussent-elles du "dernier" Beethoven? Comment ne pas mettre dans l'embarras l'interprète le mieux préparé? Or, ne fusilions pas en 1853, quand Liszt achevait cette sonate -le 2 février très exactement -Wagner composait l'Or du Rhin, autrement dit plantait la première borne véritable de la réforme lyrique.

La sonate est dédiée à Schumann: en retour, bien évidemment, de la dédicace de la Fantaisie que seul Robert, pouvait à la fois comprendre et faire suite; mais 1853 devait être à la fois la dernière année créative et lucide de Schumann et c'est seulement en 1854 que la sonate de Liszt parut chez Härtel, quand les battements du Rhin repéchèrent le malheureux Schumann en proie à la folie qui l'avait poussé à se jeter dans le fleuve.

Les Nuages gris, datés de 1881 à Weimar constituent la page la plus atonale de toutes ces pièces. La date de composition de l'Unstri est inconnue. Des audaces stupéfiantes contrastent avec les accords parfaits de la fin.

Am Grabe Richard Wagners et les deux Gondoles lugubres sont inspirés par la journée du 13 février 1883: Wagner, souffrant d'érysipèle, séjournait au Palais Vendramin-Calergi de Venise, où il venait de mourir. Le 13 février, le cortège funèbre transporta alors qu'il s'apprêtait à monter en gondole après être allé jouer au piano la scène initiale de l'Or du Rhin. On ne saurait mieux dire qu'Antonio Golea a propos de la Bagatelle sans tonalité: "C'est dans cette pièce que Liszt est allé le plus loin dans le domaine des innovations révolutionnaires. Aucune ligne mélodique, aucun enchaînement d'accords ne peuvent être inclus dans le système de tonalités majeures ou mineures. Cette œuvre se ride dans un monde propre dont les seules lois sont d'écriture".

La czarda est la danse nationale par excellence. Au terme de sa carrière, Liszt en a produit deux. L'une d'elles macabre et cette Czardas obstinée qui tourne autour de la note pivot de la dièse, l'autorise de fausses résolutions dissonantes non résolues dont Bartók aura le secret, et exprime le désir tout d'une nation qui n'a pas cessé à travers les siècles de s'affirmer en dépit de toutes les invasions politiques et culturelles.
