RENATATEBALDI 4.10 George Hall 4.16 During the decades of the I95os and I96os, two sopranos in particular commanded the attention of international opera audiences, especially in the Italian repertoire. One of them was the Greek American Maria Callas, and the other her Italian contemporary, Renata Tebaldi. It would be possible to make much of the rivalry between the two, which was endlessly recycled by the media and by partisan fans -- though not without occasional interventions and ripostes from the two sopranos themselves. But in fact their repertoires, though containing some common, centric items, were as dissimilar as their voices and their artistic personalities. Both enjoyed outstanding careers and both left substantial recorded legacies that continue to be enjoyed and admired. And from a historical perspective both made a significant contribution to the world of opera of their time. 5.03 Renata Tebaldi was born in the Italian seaside town of Pesaro, in the Marche region of Italy, on I February I922. Her father was a cellist, but her parents had already separated when she was born and a later attempt at reconciliation proved short lived. She was essentially brought up by her mother, Giuseppina, whose death in I957 was a severe blow. Unlike Callas, Tebaldi otherwise succeeded in keeping her private life private, concentrating on her art and career. But the warmth felt for her by her 3.55 personality that apparently had a naturalness and a genuine warmth. Tebaldi played the piano as a child but was more interested in her voice. Her introduction to the formerly well known soprano Carmen Melis, herself a verismo specialist, led to studies at the Pesaro Conservatory. Here she developed a technique that would stand her in good stead over the succeeding decades - though, like many singers, she went through a couple of periods of vocal crisis later in her career that would cause her to pause and retrain her instrument. Melis arranged her debut in the relatively small role of Elena (Helen of Troy) in Boito's Mefistofele in Rovigo in I944, after which Tebaldi withdrew for a period of further study. Her breakthrough came with the re opening of La Scala, Milan - a victim of wartime bombing -in I946, a prestigious event conducted by Arturo Toscanini. After a successful audition Tebaldi was selected as a soloist in the Prayer from Rossini's Mose in Egitto in which she joined the veteran bass Tancredi Pasero and the well established tenor Giovanni Malipiero. The timing was perfect. As the Italian opera houses gradually got back into gear in the aftermath of the war, such a high profile appearance, to be followed by others at the same theatre, put Tebaldi into a prime position, along 19
with a handful of other singers, to slot into place as a member of a new and much heralded generation of singers. She started to sing widely in Italy in a broad repertory, some of which she soon relinquished (though the odd valuable trace of it remains on these discs). Wagner's Lohengrin and Die Meistersinger (at La Scala), as well as Tannhauser (at the San Carlo in Naples), were all operas she sang on stage, in Italian, in these early years. So were such diverse pieces as Handel's Giulio Cesare, Spontini's Fernand Cortez, Rossini's L'assedio di Corinto and Verdi's Giovanna d'Arco. These would all be given up as she steadily began to select and focus on her core repertoire. Unlike Callas's specialisation on the bel canto operas, which she approached in a unique manner, Tebaldi must have sensed that her individual strengths of line and tone were better deployed in the lyric repertory, and particularly in its subsection (or expansion) as a fully fledged lirico spinto soprano. Tebaldi's chosen field became, and remained, centred on Verdi, especially on the later, more complex roles of Leonora in La forza del destino, Aida, Desdemona and Alice Ford, plus the bulk of the Puccini roles - notably Manon Lescaut, Mimi, Tosca, Butterfly and later Minnie in La fanciulla del West. To these she added Ponchielli's vocally diverse (but still essentially - if not archetypally -lirico spinto) La Gioconda, and some of the more popular works of Puccini's lesser contemporaries, notably Catalani's La Wally, Maddalena in Giordano's Andrea Chenier and the title role in his Fedora, and - a personal favourite - the role of the French tragedienne in Cilea's Adriana Lecouvreur. Both vocally and temperamentally these suited Tebaldi very well, earning her a long standing position as a leading soprano at La Scala during the I95os. (Ironically, she eventually relinquished that stage to the burgeoning phenomenon of Callas at a point when her rival was not far away from the beginnings of her vocal decline.) Later she established herself at the Met in New York before another audience that expected and demanded the best. That she was so successful for so long at both these glamorous venues is a testament to her vocal and artistic command, and equally to her ability to engage with an audience. As with Callas at EMI, Tebaldi was also fortunate in her record company, Decca, which recorded the bulk of her stage roles plus a selection of other items, including, notably, a late collection of songs with Joan Sutherland's husband, Richard Bonynge, at the piano in a repertoire that he knew extremely well. After all, she had had an ample innings and could look back to significant successes and very few failures. Yet there was an essential simplicity and sincerity common, by all accounts, both to Tebaldi the woman and to Tebaldi the artist that found their way, again and again, to the hearts of the public. Au cours des années I95o et I96o, deux sopranos passionnerent plus particulierement les amateurs d'opéra de tous les pays, notamment dans le repertoire italien. L'une d'elles était la cantatrice greco américaine Maria Callas, et I'autre sa contemporaine italienne Renata Tebaldi. On pourrait en dire long sur la rivalité qui les opposa et fut constamment relancée par les medias et les plus farouches de leurs fans - non sans qu'il arrive aux deux sopranos d'intervenir ou de riposter elles memes -- mais en fait, leurs repertoires, meme s'ils présentaient certains traits communs, étaient aussi dissemblables que leurs voix et leurs personnalites artistiques. Toutes deux menerent des carrieres exceptionnelles et ont laissé derriere elles un legs discographique substantiel toujours apprécié et admiré aujourd'hui. Et d'un point de vue historique, toutes deux apporterent une contribution marquante a I'univers lyrique de leur époque. Renata Tebaldi naquit dans la ville cotiere italienne de Pesaro, dans la region des Marches, le Ier février I922. Son pere était violoncelliste, mais a sa naissance, ses parents étaient deja séparés, et une tentative ultérieure de réconciliation ulterieure fit long feu. Elle fut principalement élevée par sa mere, Giuseppina, dont la mort en I957 lui porta un coup terrible. Contrairement a la Callas, Renata Tebaldi parvint toutefois a préserver sa vie privee, se concentrant sur son art et sa carriere. Mais I'engouement ressenti pour elle par ses nombreux fans -- plus particulierement pendant ses années new yorkaises -- fut une réaction sincere face a une personnalite publique apparemment douée d'un naturel et d'une chaleur authentiques. Enfant, Renata Tebaldi jouait du piano, mais c'était surtout sa voix qui I'intéressait. Ayant rencontré la soprano Carmen Melis, spécialiste du verisme ayant connu son heure de gloire, elle entreprit d'étudier le chant au Conservatoire de Pesaro. Elle y développa une technique qui devait lui etre tres utile au cours des décennies suivantes - il lui arriva pourtant au cours de sa carriere, comme a beaucoup de chanteurs, de traverser une ou deux périodes de crise vocale, et elle dut s'interrompre et réeduquer son instrument. Wahrend der I95oer und 196oer Jahre zogen vornehmlich zwei Sopranistinnen die Aufmerksamkeit des internationalen Opernpublikums auf sich, vor allem im italienischen Repertoire. Die eine war Maria Callas (von griechisch amerikanischer Herkunft), die andere ihre italienische Zeitgenossin Renata Tebaldi. Es liefe sich nun viel aus der Rivalitat der beiden machen, die von den Medien und parteischen Fans endlos wiedergekaut wurde - allerdings nicht ohne gelegentliche Interventionen und Erwide rungen der beiden Sopranistinnen selbst. Doch tatsachlich war ihr jeweiliges Repertoire (wenn beide auch einige der gangigsten Sticke gemein hatten) so verschieden wie ihre Stimmen und Kinstlerper sonlichkeiten. Beide Sangerinnen erfreuten sich au Berordentlicher Karrieren, und beide hinterliefen ein bedeutendes Schallplattenvermachtnis, das man auch weiterhin genieft und bewundert. Februar I922 in der italienischen Hafenstadt Pesaro (Region Marken) geboren.
