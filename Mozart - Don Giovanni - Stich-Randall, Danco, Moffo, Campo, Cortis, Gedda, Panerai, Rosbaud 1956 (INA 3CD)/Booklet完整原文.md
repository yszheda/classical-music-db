## About INA "mémoire vive" Collection

L'Institut national de l'audiovisuel has, since 1992, been the publisher of a CD collection presenting works extracted from its radio and television archives. Today, with some 75 titles, the "INA, mémoire vive" collection offers musical works, mainly classical but also jazz and variety, as well as extracts from interviews or speeches that have marked their era.

Between traditional record production and making archives available online, these new releases benefit from the latest technologies of restoration and digital editing within the highly qualified services of INA. This corpus has now become a reference, regularly praised by the international press.

In 2006, INA opened its archives to the general public on the Internet. Several thousand television documents, regularly enriched, are accessible on the site www.ina.fr, which enjoys great success. This year, a large part of the site's new content will be dedicated to radio and music.

## Editor's Note

The first great hours of the Festival d'Aix-en-Provence, founded in 1948 by Gabriel Dussurget, were recorded by the national radio broadcasting and preserved by the services of INA. This document presents some technical defects despite the care in restoration that uses the latest innovations in the field. We wanted thus to preserve the essential of the magic of the open-air evening in the courtyard of the Archdiocese, where these soirées became and remain Aix legends.

Almost ten years after *Cosi fan tutte* (IMV024), which was already released on microgroove, we wanted to make available to the public, at an affordable price and special edition, this *Don Giovanni*. This perfect unissued recording, which, unless mistaken, has not been the subject of an approximate and sometimes incomplete pirate edition, as has happened with other Aix productions, gathers a team of exemplary singers, including three great names among the female voices: Teresa Stich-Randall, Suzanne Danco and Anna Moffo. At the podium (and at the keyboard for recitatives), the great Hans Rosbaud.

---

This recording, made on 12 July 1956 on magnetic tape in the courtyard of the Archdiocese of Aix-en-Provence, preserves exceptional vitality despite some defects in the original tape recording. Indeed, at times significant distortion was inscribed on the loud passages of the original tape from one of the two tape recorders used, particularly audible on CD 1 track 6 and on CD 2 tracks 12 and 19. These originals were restored at a sampling frequency of 88.2 kHz in 24 bits using cutting-edge techniques developed by INA, reading the original tape under the best possible conditions.

The 1950 and 1952 recordings added to CD 3 were made via telephone line between Aix-en-Provence and Paris*. Furthermore in 1952, the recording was inscribed on "Pyral" 78 RPM direct-cut discs. They are therefore marred by background noise and distortion due to saturation of the telephone circuits used at that time, but they provide incomparable testimony to the art of Léopold Simoneau.

Jean Francois Pontefract

* Frequency modulation was only introduced in France in 1954
