## OLIVIER MESSIAEN: MÉLODIES

Probably the greatest French composer of the twentieth century, Olivier Eugène Prosper Charles Messiaen was born on December 10th 1908 at Avignon in the Provence region and died, after a long career and a life that spanned almost the entire century, in Paris on April 28th 1992. Messiaen's music is deeply marked by three major influences: religion, ornithology, and Hindu rhythm. He sees himself as the son of the wonders of creation, life and the cosmos; a concept which links him through his strong ties with the catholic church not only to Christian beliefs but to the mystic elements of the greatest Eastern religions of the world. Because of this respect, he is one of the most complete and all-embracing of philosophical composer musicians.

Messiaen was fortunate enough to spend his childhood in the inspiring landscapes of the French Alps around Grenoble and to be inspired by the extra-musical stimulus of his mother's lyric poetry; she (Cécile Sauvage) wrote him in book of verse entitled "Le tombeau", which he claimed to have been a life-long influence on his work as a composer. In 1919, at a precociously early age he began his studies at the Paris Conservatoire. In 1930 he was appointed principal organist at La Trinité church, a post he kept for over 60 years. He also began studying theory under Maurice Emmanuel and composition under Pierre Dukas; best known for his "Sorcerer's Apprentice" and his post-Debussyian opera "Ariane et Barbe-bleue", the young Messiaen developed a deep love of birdsong, influenced by Jolivet, Varèse, Webern and Debussy himself; the great innovator in French music of the early part of the century. At the same time, Messiaen's musical harmonic devices (the Greek modes, Hindu rhythms combined with a detailed notation of birdsong which would be one of the most noticeable and outstanding characteristics of his works to all sides). In 1930, Messiaen married pianist and composer Claire Delbos and the two lived as a couple for the next thirty years. Not surprisingly, much of his last time in these early years was to be taken up in writing works for his own instrument, having already begun in 1928 with "Le Banquet céleste", a meditative organ piece. The period 1929-1935 saw the composition of eight works for organ; most as a spiritual composer – "Apparition de l'Eglise éternelle" (1932), "L'Ascension" – originally written for orchestra but transcribed later for organ (1933), "Les Corps glorieux" (1939). It is also from this period that his first great song cycle, the "Poèmes pour Mi" (1936, orchestrated 1937) as well as its slightly later successor the "Chants de terre et de ciel" (1938).

At this point, Europe was thrown into turmoil by the outbreak of the Second World War and Messiaen was sent to serve time in the German prisoner of war camp Stalag VIII-A in Görlitz, where in 1940 he composed one of his most moving and enduring works, the "Quartet for the End of Time". Based on texts from "Revelation", the eight sections use different combinations of the quartet's instruments – clarinet, violin, cello and piano. The Quartet is a deeply religious work, a kind of meditation on time and eternity, and the sort of religious simplicity which would influence more recent minimalist religious composers such as Arvo Pärt.

In 1942, Messiaen was appointed Professor of harmony at the Paris Conservatoire and in the final years of the War, he composed some of his best known piano works such as "Visions de l'Amen" for two pianos (1943) and the "Vingt Regards" (1944). The end of the war also saw the composition of his extrasymphonic symphony-guide "Harawi", a work that would remain unique and unclassifiable. It is perhaps his best known and popular orchestral work, the "Turangalîla" Symphony, a massive piece in ten movements for large orchestra and piano with ondes martenot (a rather strange electronic instrument enjoying something of a vogue at the time). The Symphony is in fact a "Tristan-Trilogy" begun with "Harawi" and concluded with the "Cinq Rechants" of 1948 and shows Messiaen at his most exuberant and most exotic. The 1950's saw the composition of works such as "Réveil des oiseaux" for piano and orchestra (1953), "Oiseaux exotiques" for piano and orchestra (1955-56) and "Chronochromie" for large orchestra (1960). In 1959, he began a huge project for solo piano "Catalogue d'oiseaux" in thirteen books. Although, he would never finally dispose of the bird songs, Messiaen was now ready to concentrate again on larger scale works such as the large-scale orchestral Greek-inflected liturgical drama "Saint François d'Assise", the largest of all his works. In 1966 the "Couleurs de la cité céleste" for piano, thirteen wind instruments and six percussionists and the massive "Méditations sur le mystère de la Sainte Trinité" for organ. Messiaen's music had now become so identifiable as its own genre – particularly in France, saw Messiaen's most massive work to date, the "Turangalîla" scored for piano, flute, clarinet, cello, vibraphone, ondes martenot, xylophone, large choir and a large orchestra. A monumental work in ten movements with a Hindu theme, the Symphony is essentially based on the experience of his experience of the Grand Canyon and his single opera "St François d'Assise". A series of pieces followed during the '80s and '90s but the aging composer yet another chance to portray the bird song as he loved in "Eclairs sur l'Au-Delà" sermon to the birds.

In contrast to the massive aspect of some of these pieces, Messiaen's later style also tended towards simplification and can be found in the opera is the final orchestral works. Despite that, across the long journey of his compositional lifetime, the music of Messiaen has a quality that makes it immediately recognisable and whilst some of the works must be taken on their own terms, a sense of patience and sympathetic listening reveals the greatness of the music and the inspirations behind it. Messiaen always claimed that song was an important part of his output, but apart from his 'Mélodie intime' of 1925 in fact there are only three major song cycles in his output, other than the very early three "mélodies" of 1930 at the beginning of his career and the Vocalise, the song cycles – all written for the wonderful voice of his first wife Claire Delbos whom he married in 1932 – are a song cycle 'Trois mélodies' that are the "Poèmes pour Mi" of 1936/7, an enigmatic title which refers to a pet name for his first wife, the composer Claire Delbos. These were composed at Petichet and are based on religious texts by the composer himself. The nine songs are a reflection on earthly love within the sacrament of marriage and are, as Messiaen put it, to be seen as being at the 'service of Catholic theology'. Much of the two book cycle is a reflective series of relatively simple poems describing these earthly relationships in Book One and their theological counterparts in Book Two. The cycle is extended and complemented by Messiaen's second grouping of songs under the title "Chants de terre et de ciel" where the initial religious link with wife is extended to include the ideas of fatherhood brought about by the birth of their son Pascal (the mother of Messiaen's son Pascal, referred to here specifically as "Bébé-Pilule" in the fourth song) and extends to encompass the Resurrection particularly in the final Easter-themed 'Résurrection'.

Whereas the first two cycles are based around religious ideas, "Harawi" takes its name from a Peruvian word suggesting a sort of Wagnerian 'love death' which leads to the idea of the 'Tristan Trilogy' mentioned above. The twelve poems here, again by the composer, are complicated surrealist texts, defining sublimation at times and even referring to bird song as in the reference to the 'Green Dove' which becomes the female beloved who is to die within the ideals of ecstatic love. Although the texts and the storyline are quite abstruse and hard to fathom, the music is in fact quite accessible and easy to follow, the inspirational Peruvian background transferred to that view of cosmic being which permeates so much of Messiaen's work. At times it almost seems that the accompaniment and the vocal lines are two separate and equal voices. The "Chants de terre et de ciel" symphony are based on this concept, with the songs ranging from the ecstatic dances of love to the dark episodes of the final song where the sleeping lovers are almost engulfed by the tragedy that has been played out through the love of the Green Dove and their tragic fate lover.

## HÅKON AUSTBØ - PIANO

In 1974 the Daily Telegraph, London, already characterized Håkon Austbø as the "possessor of towering talent worthy of international recognition". Since then, critics from the Carnegie Hall, New York, to the Concertgebouw, Amsterdam, have given his extraordinary art rave reviews. Due to his unusual sensibility and fine intuition of his repertoire, Håkon Austbø has won a coveted position in today's world of music.

Of Norwegian origin, Mr. Austbø performed extensively in his homeland before continuing his studies at the Paris Conservatoire and the Ecole Normale de Musique. In 1970 he was the first non-French to win the "Concours National de l'Union Française des Artistes Solistes" in Paris, and in 1971 he gained international attention when unanimously jury awarded him the first prize at the Olivier Messiaen Competition for Contemporary Music in Royan, France.

Håkon Austbø furthered his studies at the Julliard School, New York, at the Staatliche Hochschule für Musik, Munich, and in London. He won prize winner of the international Munich competition (in piano duo with Matsusaburo Suzuki, 1974), and as a member of the board of the UNESCO International Rostrum of Composers in Paris (1976), and as a member of the board of the UNESCO International Rostrum Bratislava (1975).

Presently based in the Netherlands, Mr. Austbø enjoys an extensive solo career throughout Europe, America and Asia, and his recordings for various labels have received international acclaim. In the CD field, his integral set of excerpts from Messiaen's Catalogue d'Oiseaux on Et'cetera, and his 10 Skryabin sonatas on SIMAX. The latter was acclaimed as a reference recording by Gramophone Magazine and received the Norwegian Grammy award in 1996.

Two years later Mr. Austbø received the same award for another SIMAX recording with the Norwegian cellist Truls Mørk, and again in 1995 for Messiaen's Vingt Regards sur l'Enfant-Jésus. This recording, part of a MIAOS project that won the Grand Prix du Disque from l'Académie Charles Cros, was also chosen by Classic CD as the best available version of the work. The most item to this project, the complete Catalogue d'Oiseaux coupled with Préludes pour piano, was awarded with another prize 1998 for the best solo recording. All 4 Préludes also at 4 Etudes de rythme have also been released.

His discography further includes a Schubert recital for Vanguard Classics, the Nørgaard Piano Concerto (with the Norwegian Radio Orchestra, conducted by Ole Kristian Ruud), Glinka's Variations for piano and orchestra for Brilliant Classics, and Bach and piano works by Erik Satie, Grieg (Lyric Pieces) and Brahms (Handel-variationer) for Brilliant Classics.

Mr. Austbø received the prize of the Norwegian critics in 1980 and was chosen "Performer of the Year" in Norway in 1992. He teaches piano major at the Amsterdam Conservatory and is moreover first vice president of the international Scriabin Society, Moscow. As a propagator of Skryabin's work, he is the initiator and artistic director of the LUCE-project that offers a unique realisation of the colour part in the symphonic poem Prometheus.

## INGRID KAPPELLE - SOPRANO

Ingrid Kappelle started her professional vocal training in 1982, with professor Mya Besselink at the Maastricht Conservatory of Music. She received, next to her completed piano studies, her Diploma Degree in Opera, Concert/Oratorio music, and an additional Distinction. She was a finalist in the Dutch Vocal Competition 's-Hertogenbosch (1988), and received the 2nd prize in the International Dranouter Competition (Netherlands) and the 3rd prize in the Britten Piers Soution (with Galina Vishnevskaya and Suzanne Danco). She participated in a number of masterclasses by Elisabeth Söderström and was been the famous mezzo-soprano Jard van Nes.

Ingrid Kappelle has a great love for this which she is invited internationally.

She sang main parts in "La Traviata", "Otello", "Aïda", "Faust", "Die Fledermaus" and "Tannhäuser", also so sang Oratorio repertory such as Bach's St. Matthew Passion, B minor Mass, Pergolesi's Stabat Mater, Dvorak's Stabat Mater, and Brahms' Requiem (Béthanie). Elsas and Paulus (Mendelssohn), and in 1992 she was, specially invited, by internationally renowned ensembles, for her unique interpretations of 20th century vocal music.

Besides the Messiaen project, together with Elena Fanoza (The opera "La vera Costanza" (Rossini) by Joseph Haydn and a live registration of the unknown oratorio "Agnes Dei" by Joseph Ryelandt were also recorded. Together with soprano Mieke Schwalk, Ingrid Kappelle performed recitals on the unknown Dutch female composer Bertha Wiardi: Wegener Koopman which resulted in a beautiful CD (Jatile label). Soon an guest performances took Ingrid Kappelle to Italy, Czech Republic, Germany, France and Austria. The Messiaen songs together with Messiaen specialist Håkon Austbø she performed, at the Messiaen Chamber Music festival in Oslo, Olivier Messiaen's complete songs for soprano & piano. She had recitals at Concerti where they performed two cycles by Karol Szymanowski in a concert of Russian poetry and piano works by Scriabin. In the season '95/'96 she was the main vocal guest at the Consequeence Festival Rotterdam and performed Sequence IV of Luciano Berio. With Critics and public were enchanted and completely lost in frenzi and praise in this 90 minutes scenic en vocal excess of passion and despair.

### HARAWI (1945)
**Chants d'amour et de mort**
**Poèmes et musique d'Olivier Messiaen écrit**
**à Petichet, Isère - du 15 juin au 15 septembre 1945**

**LA VILLE QUI DORMAIT**
La ville qui dormait,las.
Ma main sur ton coeur serré toi.
Le plein minuit le veut.
La violence double toi.
L'oeil immobile, sans répondre ton regard, moi.

**BONJOUR TOI, COLOMBE VERTE**
Bonjour toi, colombe verte,
Retour de ciel, perle limpide,
Départ de l'air,
Etoile enchantée, Ombre partagée.
Toi, de fleur, de fruit, de ciel et d'eau,
Oiseau des oiseaux.
Bonjour, l'eau.

**MONTAGNES**
Montagne haute rocher noir.
L'arctique étoile rayée noir.
Montagne, écoute le chant solitaire du vertige.
La pierre apparaît portée par ses mains libres.
Les rochers penchés sont les maisons où dorment vers le bas.
Gouffre lancés partout dans le vertige.
Mer sur noir.

**DOUNDOU TCHIL**
Doundou tchil
Piruatcha le voilà, ô mon aimé!
La danse des étoiles, doundou tchil,
Piruatcha le voilà, ô mon aimé,
Amour d'étoiles piruatcha, doundou tchil
Arc-en-ciel, eaux souffer, mon écho
Ton regard dans le mien,
Piruatcha le voilà, ô mon aimé,
Ciel de ciel et lune de miel, doundou tchil,
Iboungou, boungou,
Et toi mon aimée, mama nama,kuhipkas
Iboungou, boungou,
Ihoupara piruatcha,mama nama,mahkiphas.
Piruatcha le voilà, ô mon aimé!
ô danse des étoiles, doundou tchil
Piruatcha le voilà, ô mon aimé,
La ville qui dort,las, doundou tchil
Arc-en-ciel, mon souffer, mon écho!
ton regard mon souffer, mon souffer,
Piruatcha le voilà, ô mon aimé,
Mon ciel bleu, mon âme, doundou tchil.

**L'AMOUR DE PIROUTCHA**
Itoungoula, boungou, loungou,
Amour, ô mon amour,
Aimer la petite en tes bras verts,
Toujours l'aimer, le secret de mon coeur,
Parfois l'aimer, la perdre,
Couper-moi la main, doundou tchil,
Nuit souffles, nes souffles, bleu et or,
All! All! Danses nuages, nuées, nuaves,
amour, la mort!

**RÉPÉTITION PLANÉTAIRE**
Ré...pé...ti...tion...
Ré...pé...ti...tion...
Ma...pama,ma...pama nama fila,
[repeated]

**ADIEU**
Adieu toi, colombe verte, Adieu attristé.
Adieu toi, perle limpide, adieu attristé.
Toi de nuit, de fruit, de ciel, de jour,
Adieu l'amour.
Adieu toi, lumière rosevée, Pâffire à deux voix
Etale enchanteresse, Ombre partagée,
Dernière, ma main dans l'fruit de ciel, de jour,
Adieu l'amour.
Adieu mon ciel de terre,
Adieu toi, oiseau d'étoiles,
Adieu mon coeur de terre,
De fleur, de nuit, de fruit, de ciel, de jour.
Pauf' toujours!

**SYLLABES**
Colombe, colombe verte, Le chiffre cinq à lui,
Et lumières brisées. Très loin, tout bas, tout bas.
Où on vit de l'heure, Piruatcha mia
Où la sépulture du coeur, Piruatcha mia
Ou le murmure de l'eau, Piruatcha mia
mama nama fila, ma/papa, pampaphika
Afuli alli doundou tchil,
Mama nama, mama nama fila, tchil
[repeated]
Tchil, tchil,tchil,pampaphika ma, tchil, tchil
pampa nama fila, pampaphika,nama.
Tchil, tchil,tchil,pampaphika ma, doundou tchil tchil tchil
Etchevalier un cri noir!
Etchevalier un cri noir!
Oh! la mer de l'amour, de l'avant la terre à tout rompre,
Etche nla de temps, Ecalier, boumiant,
Boumiant, Doule tchil!
Tourbillon. Pénétrez mane en tourmant
Tourbillon, Pénétrez mane!
Tchil, tchil,tchil,pampaphika ma, doundou tchil tchil tchil!

**L'ESCALIER REDUIT, GESTES DU SOLEIL**
Du temps, du ciel, de l'eau, de la terre, de l'escalier du temps
Ma petite bouche la voit là, les tempes vertes, mauves,
L'escalier m'a fait du soleil,
Mon Dieu! Et d'ailleurs.
De l'eau, du temps, de ciel, de l'escalier de ciel
J'attends dans le vert, bruit, éclat d'étoiles,
C'est si simple d'être mort!
De l'eau, du temps, de ciel, de l'escalier du temps,
Ma petite bouche la voit là, les tempes vertes, mauves,
L'escalier m'a fait du soleil,
Ma petite bouche la voit là, les tempes vertes, mauves,
L'escalier m'a fait du soleil,
Comme le mort. L'oeil du temps,
Ciel de ciel, l'oeil de feu,
Mon Dieu! Et d'ailleurs.
Toi seul présent qui respire,
De feu, du temps, de l'oeil de feu,
L'escalier de ciel!

**AMOUR OISEAU D'ÉTOILE**
oiseau d'étoile, Ton oeil qui chante, Vers les étoiles,
Ta tête à l'envers sous le ciel
Ton oeil d'étoile, Chaînes tremblantes, Vers les étoiles
Ton oiseau conclut sa prière
Ta lumière, mauves,
Toutes les oiseaux des étoiles,
Avec tes quatre sens chante,
Dieu, silence augmente de ciel
Couleur du sang, de ciel.

**KATCHIKAITCHI LES ÉTOILES**
Katchikaïtchi les étoiles,telles les sauter,
Katchikaïtchi les étoiles, telles les danser,
Katchikaïtchi les étoiles, telles les sauter devant,
Katchikaïtchi les étoiles, telles les danser,
Les nébuleuses spirales traits de morts chevaux,
Les électrons, fourmis bleues, les nébuleuses,
Alpha du Centaure, Bételgeuse, Aldébaran,
Déjà! l'espace: arc-en-ciel depuis longtemps,
Rire levait l'aurore d'hier/au meurtre absent,
Où roule ma soie, mon chemin route dans le sang?
Toi, ici. Maint, main, Toi!
Où roule ma soie!

**DANS LE NOIR**
Dans le noir, colombe verte
Et lumières brisées
Je suis le noir, mon fruit de ciel, de jour,
Lointaine colombe verte
Qui bat l'air et s'effeuille!
Colombe: colombe verte, Le chiffre cinq à lui,
Et lumières brisées.
Très loin, tout bas, très bas, tout bas, très loin.
La ville qui dormait...
