## Martha
### by Friedrich von Flotow

Baron Friedrich von Flotow (1812-1883) was a prolific 19th-century composer who wrote a number of operas of which only *Alessandro Stradella* and *Martha* have survived. The latter, in fact, has been adjudged one of the most-performed operas of all time, which may come as a surprise to American audiences who hardly ever hear more than an excerpt or two from it; but in the German countries it is a perennial favorite. Opera lovers who have never seen or heard the complete work are familiar with the stirring tenor love song, "Ach, so fromm," often sung in Italian as "M'appari," and "Letzte Rose" ("The Last Rose of Summer"). Ironically, these two "greatest hits" from *Martha* are both recycles.

"Ach, so fromm" was lifted verbatim from Flotow's previous opera, *L'âme en peine*, and "Letzte Rose" is an arrangement of the Irish folk song whose original words were by Thomas Moore.

The opera had its premiere in Vienna on September 25, 1847 and within a year was being heard in Berlin, Paris and Prague. In 1849 it was first mounted in England, in the U.S. in 1852. It marked its 500th performance in Vienna in 1882, and though like so many works of its period it fell somewhat out of favor after World War I, it has retained a foothold at least in German theaters to the present day.

*Martha*, unlike so many German vocal works of its time, is not an operetta, not a *singspiel*, but a full-fledged Early Romantic comic opera, rather in the vein of Donizetti's comedies. There are no spoken parts: the opera is through-composed, and indeed was the first German comic opera to be so written. It is likely that this has contributed to *Martha*'s extra measure of international success, since audiences have been willingly sit through sung than spoken dialogue. But the chief source of its fame is surely its endless stream of charming, lively and beautiful melody.

## THE STORY

**Act I.** England in about 1710, during the reign of Queen Anne, one of whose maids of honor, Lady Harriet Durham, has grown bored with court life and the constant attentions of her foppish cousin, Sir Tristan Mickleford. As a diversion, she decides to attend Richmond Fair incognito, disguising herself as "Martha," her faithful maid Nancy as "Julia," and Sir Tristan as simple "John." At the fair, farmers seek servant girls to put under annual contracts. Among the eager employers are Plunkett and his foster brother, Lionel, the latter a young man of some mystery since his father on his deathbed gave him a ring with instructions to show it to Queen Anne if he ever should find himself in dire need. "Martha" and "Julia" merrily accept offers of work from the two young men, thinking to play a prank; but they are soon crying out in despair as they discover the law binds them to their contract and are led away to a farm in the country.

**Act II.** The two ladies decide their best chance of escape from bondage is to make royal nuisances of themselves, which they do in a series of comic incidents, pretending to be hopelessly ignorant and lazy. In the course of these hijinks, Plunkett takes a shine to "Julia" and Lionel becomes infatuated with "Martha." The ladies rather like the two men, but fear the Queen's wrath if they do not soon return to court. The opportunity is provided when Sir Tristan brings a carriage to rescue them in the dead of night.

**Act III.** The two agrarians, despondent over the loss of their ladies, betake themselves to the royal hunt for diversion. Plunkett sings in praise of porter ale, while Lionel muses on his hopeless love in "Ach, so fromm." Lionel is thrilled when "Martha" appears, though puzzled that she is dressed as a fine lady. For her part, Lady Harriet denies ever knowing Lionel and orders him arrested. Distraught, Lionel gives his ring to Plunkett and tells him to show it to the Queen.

**Act IV.** It turns out that Lionel is the son of the unjustly exiled Earl of Derby, whose inheritance has now been restored by good Queen Anne. Although Lady Harriet is at last ready to admit her attraction to Lionel, she has become half-mad with grief and rejects him along with wealth and title. Nancy and Plunkett, having become lovers, join Lady Harriet in devising a plan to bring Lionel back to reality. They construct an elaborate reproduction of the Richmond Fair, and as Lionel is led through it he comes upon Lady Harriet dressed again as the servingmaid "Martha." His reason returns and the young pair embrace, singing a final reprise of "The Last Rose of Summer."

## ABOUT THE COMPOSER

Friedrich Adolf Ferdinand, Freiherr von Flotow (1812-1883) was a German composer. He was born in Teutendorf, in Mecklenburg-Schwerin, and studied at the Paris Conservatoire. Although he was German, he spent much of his life in France and his early operas were greatly influenced by French opera, particularly that of Auber and Boieldieu.

Flotow wrote over 30 operas, but only *Martha* has remained in the regular repertoire. *Martha* was originally written as a ballet-pantomime, *Lady Harriette ou la Servante de Greenwich*, and was first performed at the Paris Opera in 1844. It was later adapted into the opera we know today, with a German libretto by Friedrich Wilhelm Riese.

Flotow's music is characterized by its melodic charm, light orchestration, and skillful use of folk songs. He was particularly adept at writing comic opera in the French tradition, and his works were popular throughout Europe during his lifetime.

## ABOUT THIS RECORDING

This 1944 recording of *Martha* represents an important historical document of German opera performance during the World War II era. The Berlin Staatsoper (State Opera) was one of Germany's leading opera houses, and this recording features many of the finest German singers of the period.

**Erna Berger** (1900-1990) was one of the most celebrated German coloratura sopranos of the 20th century. Known for her pure tone and agile technique, she excelled in roles by Mozart, Strauss, and in the German light opera repertoire.

**Peter Anders** (1908-1954) was considered one of the greatest German lyric tenors of his generation. His warm, expressive voice and natural acting ability made him a favorite in roles such as Lionel, Tamino, and the Italian opera repertoire.

**Josef Greindl** (1912-1993) was a distinguished German bass known for his rich tone and commanding stage presence. He had a long career at the Berlin Staatsoper and Bayreuth Festival.

**Johannes Schüler** (1894-1966) was a German conductor who served as music director at several major German opera houses, including the Berlin Staatsoper where he conducted regularly from the 1930s through the 1950s.
