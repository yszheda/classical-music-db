# BETWEEN TRADITION AND PROGRESS
## Music by Alessandro Scarlatti and Giovanni Battista Pergolesi

Alessandro Scarlatti was born in Palermo in 1660. Several of his brothers and sisters were musicians, and three of his sons – Pietro, Domenico and Giuseppe – made a name for themselves as composers. Alessandro arrived in Rome at a very early age; in 1678 he was already successful there as an opera composer, and became musical director at Queen Christina of Sweden. In 1684 he took up the same post at the vice-regal court in Naples, and during the next two decades he composed no fewer than 80 operas, thus laying the foundations of the city's reputation as "la capitale du monde musicale" in the 18th century. In 1702 he left his post, and from then onwards he spent most of his time in Rome. Together with Arcangelo Corelli, he was admitted to the Accademia Filarmonica, the leading musical circle in the city, which was devoted to the cultivation of literature, the arts and sciences. He remained its official composer and musical adviser until his death. He continued to train himself in musical theory and counterpoint, and wrote a cappella works alla Palestrina. In 1703 he was appointed assistant maestro di cappella at the church of Santa Maria Maggiore, becoming maestro there four years later. In 1708 Cardinal Grimaldi of his own Viceroy (who also engaged Handel) appointed him to the same position at the court in Naples again. But by this time Scarlatti was not turning out a whole series of works for everyday musical needs: instead, and as a replacement for Scarlatti's setting, which was judged old-fashioned.

There is scarcely any composer whose life and work, whose fame during his lifetime and legend in the decades after his death – he was granted no more than five years – has so totally concentrated the attention of later generations as a darling of the gods, and the remarkable success which his compositions began to enjoy even during his lifetime and very soon after his death aroused the interest of this musician, whose name was Alessandro Scarlatti, and who had died unknown outside his own circle: he was a European celebrity.

The title which we know about Pergolesi's life are due above all to the historian Francesco Florimo. We do, however, know that on 4 January 1710 Giovanni Battista Draghi was born at Jesi, a little town in the old Duchy of Ancona. Jesi still exists today under the name of "Pergola", after Pergolesi's home town of Pergolesi, the composer, and the nickname "Pergolesi" or Pergola, from which his name is derived, is a reference to his birthplace. The fact that the young Pergolesi studied at the Naples Conservatorio under such teachers as Francesco Durante and Gaetano Greco, and that he counted among his fellow-students such later famous musicians as Leonardo Leo and Nicola Jommelli, is well documented. After completing his studies – probably in 1729 – Pergolesi composed a great deal for the church, for private patrons and for various members of the aristocracy. In 1731 he was employed by the Prince of Stigliano as maestro di cappella, and in 1733 he was appointed to the same post at the court of the Viceroy of Naples, the Duke of Montemar. It was during this period that he composed his most famous works, including the opera "La serva padrona" and the "Stabat Mater". Pergolesi's health had always been fragile, and he suffered from a chronic illness which eventually proved fatal. He died on 16 March 1736, at the age of 26, in Pozzuoli, near Naples.

# ENTRE TRADITION ET PROGRÈS
## Musique d'Alessandro Scarlatti et de Giovanni Battista Pergolesi

Alessandro Scarlatti est né en 1660 à Palerme. Plusieurs de ses frères étaient également musiciens et trois de ses fils – Pietro, Domenico et Giuseppe – firent carrière en tant que compositeurs. Alessandro s'installa très tôt à Rome où, dès 1678, il connut ses premiers succès comme compositeur d'opéras et devint maître de chapelle de la reine Christine de Suède. En 1684, il fut nommé maître de chapelle à la cour vice-royale de Naples. Au cours des vingt années suivantes, il composa pas moins de 80 opéras, jetant ainsi les bases de la réputation de Naples en tant que «capitale du monde musical» au XVIIIe siècle. En 1702, il quitta ce poste et séjourna désormais la plupart du temps à Rome. Avec Arcangelo Corelli, il fut admis à l'Accademia Filarmonica, cercle musical le plus important de la ville, qui était consacré à la littérature, aux arts et aux sciences. Il en resta le compositeur officiel et le conseiller musical jusqu'à sa mort. Il continua à se former dans la théorie musicale et le contrepoint et écrivit des œuvres a cappella alla Palestrina. En 1703, il fut nommé assistant maître de chapelle à l'église Santa Maria Maggiore, devenant maître de chapelle quatre ans plus tard. En 1708, le cardinal Grimaldi, son propre vice-roi (qui engagea également Haendel) le nomma à nouveau à la cour de Naples. Mais à cette époque, Scarlatti ne composait plus toute une série d'œuvres pour les besoins musicaux quotidiens; il écrivait plutôt, en réponse à une commission nouvelle, des œuvres d'une plus grande responsabilité artistique. Il est mort à Naples en 1725.

# STABAT MATER SEQUENCE
## (Proper for the Feast of the Seven Sorrows of the Virgin Mary)

### Latin Text (Original)

**1.** Stabat Mater dolorosa
juxta Crucem lacrimosa,
dum pendebat Filius.

**2.** Cuius animam gementem,
contristatam et dolentem
pertransivit gladius.

**3.** O quam tristis et afflicta
fuit illa benedicta,
Mater Unigeniti!

**4.** Quae moerebat et dolebat,
pia Mater, dum videbat
nati poenas inclyti.

**5.** Quis est homo, qui non fleret,
Matrem Christi si videret
in tanto supplicio?

**6.** Quis non posset contristari
Christi Matrem contemplari
dolentem cum Filio?

**7.** Pro peccatis suae gentis
vidit Iesum in tormentis,
et flagellis subditum.

**8.** Vidit suum dulcem natum
morientem, desolatum,
dum emisit spiritum.

**9.** Eja, Mater, fons amoris,
me sentire vim doloris
fac, ut tecum lugeam.

**10.** Sancta Mater, istud agas,
crucifixi fige plagas
cordi meo validē.

**11.** Tui nati vulnerati,
tam dignati pro me pati,
poenas mecum divide.

**12.** Iuxta crucem tecum stare,
et me tibi sociare
in planctu desidero.

**13.** Virgo virginum praeclara,
mihi iam non sis amara,
fac me tecum plangere.

**14.** Fac, ut portem Christi mortem,
passionis fac consortem,
et plagas recolere.

**15.** Fac me plagis vulnerari,
fac me cruce inebriari
et cruore Filii.

**16.** Inflammatus et accensus
et per te, Virgo, defensus
in die iudicii.

**17.** Fac me cruce custodiri,
morte Christi praemuniri,
confoveri gratia.

**18.** Quando corpus morietur,
fac, ut animae donetur
paradisi gloria. Amen.

### English Translation

**1.** At the Cross her station keeping,
stood the mournful Mother weeping,
close to Jesus at the last.

**2.** Through her soul, of joy bereaved,
bowed with anguish, deeply grieved,
now a sword of pain hath passed.

**3.** Oh, that blessed one, grief-laden,
blessed Mother, blessed Maiden,
Mother of the all-holy One.

**4.** O that silent, ceaseless mourning,
O those dim eyes, never turning
from that wondrous, suffering Son.

**5.** Who on Christ's dear Mother gazing,
in her trouble so amazing,
born of woman, would not weep?

**6.** Who on Christ's dear Mother thinking,
such a cup of sorrow drinking,
would not share her sorrows deep?

**7.** For his people's sins, in anguish,
there she saw the Victim languish,
bleed in torments, bleed and die.

**8.** Saw the Lord's Anointed taken;
saw her Child in death forsaken,
heard His last expiring cry.

**9.** Eja, Mater, fons amoris,
me sentire vim doloris
fac, ut tecum lugeam.

**10.** Sancta Mater, istud agas,
crucifixi fige plagas
cordi meo validē.

**11.** Tui nati vulnerati,
tam dignati pro me pati,
poenas mecum divide.

**12.** Iuxta crucem tecum stare,
et me tibi sociare
in planctu desidero.

**13.** Virgo virginum praeclara,
mihi iam non sis amara,
fac me tecum plangere.

**14.** Fac, ut portem Christi mortem,
passionis fac consortem,
et plagas recolere.

**15.** Fac me plagis vulnerari,
fac me cruce inebriari
et cruore Filii.

**16.** Inflammatus et accensus,
Per te, virgo, sim defensus
In die iudicii.

**17.** Fac me cruce custodiri,
Morte Christi praemuniri,
Confoveri gratia.

**18.** Quando corpus morietur,
Fac, ut animae donetur
paradisi gloria. Amen.
