## Liner Notes - English (英文解说) - Pages 06-07

**PERGOLESI Stabat Mater**

The prodigious glory surrounding the legend of Pergolesi and the myth which sprang up throughout Europe after this composer of genius who died at the frightfully early age of 26, was a complex phenomenon that was in fact in equivalent only to the death of Mozart at 35.

Born at Jesi in the Marches, Pergolesi was sent at the age of twelve to Naples where, at the Conservatorio dei Poveri di Gesù Cristo, he was a pupil of Gaetano Greco, Leonardo Vinci and Francesco Durante. In 1732 he became maestro di cappella to Prince Ferdinando Colonna Stigliano, viceroy to the Viceroy of Naples. Two years later, when Charles de Bourbon ascended the Neapolitan throne, Pergolesi entered the court's service. Following the success of *La Serva padrona* and in 1735 was appointed assistant organist in the Neapolitan royal chapel. Following in the footsteps of the two Neapolitan masters who were the greatest in the Kingdom: Porpora and Leo, Pergolesi composed for the theater at an astonishingly early age and with a mastery which at first appeared that of Metastasio. L'Olimpiade was premiered at Rome in 1735 and opened the path to a European career appreciated in Naples, his comedy *Le Finte Viaggiatrici* was staged in 1732 and was triumphantly received, as were his comic intermezzi *La serva padrona* (1733). His opera *Il Flaminio*, was performed in the autumn of 1735. At this period the young composer's health was already in decline, and early the following year he retired to the Franciscan monastery at Pozzuoli, not far from Naples. According to Marchese Villasari, it was there that Pergolesi wrote his celebrated *Salve Regina* and a set of Requiem. His sublime *Stabat Mater*, He died of tuberculosis at Pozzuoli on 16 March 1736. Pergolesi left four cantatas, a set of 30 fugues and three masses to be sung at the Conservatorio, several of the greatest Sinfonias for written on the death of the celebrated Neapolitan writer for the Duke of Maddaloni, chamber works which are lost.

In Counter-Reformation Italy there was certainly no better opportunity to compose than in Naples. In 1733 alone Pergolesi wrote two sacred dramas for Naples, *La conversione di San Guglielmo*, *Dario* and *L'Olimpiade* and a *San Guglielmo*, *Dario* and *L'Olimpiade*, a comic opera *Lo frate 'nnamorato*, and a *Salve Regina* in C major. Pergolesi also wrote two masses of the Saint that in F major, commissioned for the Chapel of Maddaloni. Pergolesi performed in sumptuous realization at the Church of San Lorenzo in Lucca, at Rome, in 1735, as well as other religious works, most importantly an impressive set of motets inspired by Pergolesi by the Archbishop of Naples to celebrate San Gennaro, recently chosen as protector of the city against epidemics.

The *Salve Regina* is the best known of the four Marian hymns, which Pergolesi set to music twice in this famous text, the first in A minor and the second in F minor, both for soprano (always sung in churches by a castrato) and for strings and continuo.

The second setting was then transcribed for alto voice in transposition in F minor, in which Pergolesi lived here. The daring harmonies, the chromatism and dissonance together form a rich polyphonic fabric in which the melodic energy is concentrated in the voice part and the bass line. This is a piece of great intensity and inner feeling.

The *Stabat Mater* is constructed on a grander scale, its proportions are broader, it is more declamatory and dramatic. It was commissioned by the Confraternity of Maddaloni, shortly before Pergolesi's departure for Pozzuoli. This work was intended to replace Alessandro Scarlatti's setting which had served as a model for all; the solo soprano and alto, in recitativi (in Santa Maria delle Grazie di Pace), a church which became an aristocratic patron had a certain charge and where he had endured a foundation for the performance of religious works every year on that Sunday in September.

Pergolesi's *Stabat Mater* was performed widely throughout Europe as a unique after his death. It had the distinction above all thanks to a revival of a large number of manuscript copies, as well as numerous editions, translations, reworkings and adaptations. No less than 25 copies have a version of it, that manuscript of which was discovered only in 1961 in the Berlin Staatsbibliothek.

The *Stabat Mater* was performed in London as late as 1749 by the lady company of the Mantuan brothers, and in 1801 Giuseppe Verdi, probably inspired, rendered version of his own, with added parts for a wind instruments.

The text tells of different kinds of vocal textures in a varied sequence that embraces both contrapuntal, fugal ideas and homophonic, chordal style. The vocal parts provide the soloists with opportunities to ornament; they demonstrate their powers of expression. There is an alternation - even a mixture - of the traditional solo, choral and instrumental characteristics of the world of secular music. At last in the last quarter of the eighteenth century the property of different compositional styles in the *Stabat Mater* and in the *Salve Regina* were to be found among Italian music historians and theorists such as Padre Martini; these writers tried to demonstrate that such music was in essence a mixture of styles, the *Stabat Mater* and *Le Finte Viaggiatrici* both, of course, we know that such borrowings and adaptations of music from the secular to the sacred sphere and vice versa were common among Italian composers who worked at the same time in opera houses, churches, and theatres. Pergolesi's rapid and profuse works to commissions from the same aristocratic patrons, for performance before essentially the same public.

Pergolesi's *Stabat Mater* was heard for the first time in France at the Concert Spirituel. Six or seven singers of Naples sang the *Stabat Mater* during a stay in 1750. This performance took place in 1733, a few months after its composition. The work aroused considerable interest and was to remain in the program of the Concert for some twenty years. The *Stabat Mater* was performed a few times every year during this period, often in parallel or fragmentary form, or in dubious adaptations, which the melodic energy is concentrated in the voice part and the bass line. ## Liner Notes - French (法文解说) - Pages 11

**Par le jeune homme aurait composé son *Salve Regina* et, comme un Requiem, son sublime *Stabat Mater*. Souffrant de phtisie, Giovanni Battista Pergolesi s'éteignait à Pozzuoli, le 16 mars 1736.**

Pergolesi laissa quatre cantates, des solfèges et quelques messes à chanter au Conservatoire.

Sinfonia et-quatre mouvements, pour violoncelle et basse continue, écrite sans doute pour son mécène, le duc de Maddaloni, dont la virtuosité au violoncelle était fort appréciée.

Dans l'Italie de la Contre-Réforme, les occasions de composer ne pouvaient faire défaut au musicien de talent qu'était Pergolesi. Dans la seule année de 1733, Pergolesi composa pour Naples deux drames sacrés, *La conversione di San Guglielmo*, *Dario* et *L'Olimpiade*, un opéra bouffe, *Lo frate 'nnamorato*, et une *Salve Regina* en ut majeure. Pergolesi composa également pour la chapelle de Maddaloni, plusieurs messes et motets. La plus célèbre de ces hymnes à la gloire de la Vierge, Pergolesi la mit en musique deux fois dans ce texte fameux, la première en la mineur et la seconde en fa mineur, toutes deux pour soprano (toujours interprété, à l'église, par un castrat) cordes et continuo.

La seconde fut ensuite transcrite pour voix d'alto, dans la tonalité de fa mineur. C'est la version enregistrée par Il Seminario musicale. Les harmonies très audacieuses, le chromatisme et les dissonances y forment une riche structure polyphonique, où la voix et la basse continue concentrent l'énergie mélodique. C'est une pièce d'une grande intensité et d'un lyrisme profond.

Le *Stabat Mater*, lui, se déploie sur une plus vaste échelle, plus décoratif aussi, plus déclamatoire et plus dramatisé aussi, il devait donner à la chapelle de Maddaloni. Pergolesi venait de se fixer à Maddaloni. Cette composition était destinée à remplacer le *Stabat Mater* d'Alessandro Scarlatti qui, depuis le début du XVIIe siècle, servait de modèle à toutes les œuvres de ce genre.

Commandée pour l'église Santa Maria delle Grazie di Pace, une église qui possédait une chapelle votive et où il fallait exécuter des œuvres religieuses chaque deuxième dimanche de septembre.

Le *Stabat Mater* de Pergolesi connut une large et rapide diffusion partout en Europe après la mort du musicien. La gloire posthume du compositeur napolitain tient sans doute avant tout à cette œuvre. Sa partition a fait l'objet d'innombrables copies, éditions, traductions, réécritures et adaptations. Johann Sebastian Bach en fit un pastiche (l'allemand *Tilge, Höchster, meine Sünden*, BWV 1083), dont le manuscrit autographe ne fut découvert qu'en 1961 à la bibliothèque d'État de Berlin.

Le *Stabat Mater* était exécuté au Danemark vers 1745 par la compagnie des comédiens italiens de Mangini. À Paris, au Concert Spirituel, Pergolesi eut encore une nouvelle consécration, avec des instruments à vent.

Le texte se compose de douze strophes qui font se succéder les deux vocaux, tantôt contrapuntiques et fugés, tantôt homophones et accordaux. Les parties solistes, elles, laissent libre cours à l'effusion lyrique des chanteurs. Alternance ou union du stile antico, voulu par l'Église et du stile moderno, propre au monde de la musique profane. Dans les trente dernières années du XVIIIe siècle, le *Stabat Mater* de Pergolesi suscita encore de nombreuses polémiques parmi les théoriciens et les historiens de la musique (le Père Martini, l'abbé Antonio Eximeno, Vincenzo Manfredini et Saverio Mattei) s'efforçaient de montrer que, dans certains versets de cette partition religieuse, Pergolesi avait réemployé des thèmes profanes empruntés à *La serva padrona* et au *Flaminio*. En fait, ces adaptations de la musique profane vers le sacré, et vice versa, étaient une pratique courante chez les compositeurs italiens qui travaillaient à la fois pour les théâtres et pour les églises, créant des œuvres profanes et des pièces religieuses commanditées par les mêmes mécènes aristocrates et interprétées devant le même public.

Le *Stabat Mater* de Pergolesi fut entendu pour la première fois en France en 1747 par une troupe de chanteurs italiens qui venaient de faire le bonheur du public à Paris. Pierre-Charles de l'Isle, directeur de l'Académie royale de musique, demanda à Pergolesi un *Stabat Mater* pour la chapelle de Versailles. L'œuvre fut créée en grande pompe le 11 avril 1749, avec un effectif vocal et instrumental considérable. Le succès fut immense. L'œuvre fut reprise pendant vingt ans, chaque année, au Concert Spirituel.

**Sylvie Marny**
