## A Discography of Francesco Tamagno

**Francesco Tamagno** (1850-1905) was an Italian operatic tenor who lived at the very apex of his profession. During his short life, he spent nearly thirty years at the very apex of his profession and amassed a considerable fortune. He made his debut in 1874 and by the end of the 19 scheduled performances at a fee of 12,000 francs, then equivalent to a million which would be amassed in a decade. The fact that he was able to save over a quarter of the entire opera budget throws an interesting light on current complaints in operative economics.

The other evidence of Tamagno's stature concerns the advance royalties for his first recordings. His agent, lawyer Noseda, wrote to him on 21 October 1903 about the first payment of 400 for recording in 1902.

Despite the plethora of printed material there is a good deal of confusion over Tamagno's background. The tenor in question was born on 27 December 1850 in Turin, the son of Domenico Tamagno and Caterina Rocca. His father was a tailor. He entered the Turin Conservatorio in 1868 but seems to have made an impact on the musical world until his debut at the Teatro Carignano in Donizetti's *Poliuto* in 1870. The opera was not in the theatre's repertoire and Tamagno's performances were cut short by the impresario with which his father paid the special tax to keep his son out of the war.

Tamagno's career followed the traditional pattern of an Italian tenor of the period: provincial debut, sought entry to the Turin Conservatoire in 1868 but seen to have made an impact on the musical world until his debut at the Teatro Carignano in Donizetti's *Poliuto* in 1870.

## TAMAGNO'S RECORDINGS

Negotiations between Tamagno and Alfred Michauis, Director of the Gramophone Company (Italy) Limited, were well advanced before Caruso was recorded in April 1902, and were completed other than that Caruso was given the precedence in recording which Tamagno was offered first. The original G&T matrix numbers were in the range 3000-3028 and 10-18, with 3023-3028 dated January 1904.

Tamagno's recording activity in the latter part of 1903 was limited to one month of the year. It is not known how many discs he made, but it is likely that there was a gap in the recordings, and the first two sessions of 1904 were devoted to Don Manuel de Iraldi. Tamagno arrived in Milan on 10th February 1904, and it was decided to record him immediately. The first takes were on 11th February, and the last on 17th February.

The records were sent to London for processing. The recording sessions were well documented, and the Gramophone and Typewriter Company was happy to go to him to ensure that all the recordings were completed to the satisfaction of all concerned. Tamagno was to receive 100 for each successful recording, and 50 for each rejected take. The recordings were made at the Hotel Milano, and the Gramophone Company's representative was present throughout the sessions.

## HISTORICAL CONTEXT

Francesco Tamagno was one of the most celebrated Italian tenors of the late 19th century. He is best remembered as the creator of the title role in Verdi's *Otello*, which he premiered at La Scala, Milan, on February 5, 1887. His powerful, dramatic voice was ideally suited to the demanding role, and his interpretation set the standard for all future Ottellos.

Tamagno's voice was characterized by its extraordinary power, particularly in the upper register. He possessed a ringing high C that could pierce through the largest orchestras, and his vocal technique allowed him to sustain long phrases with remarkable ease. Despite his vocal prowess, Tamagno was also known for his musical intelligence and dramatic insight.

The recordings on this CD represent a unique historical document. They were made when Tamagno was 53-54 years old, near the end of his career, and they capture the voice of the man who created some of the most demanding tenor roles in the Italian repertoire. The sound quality, while primitive by modern standards, preserves the essential characteristics of Tamagno's voice: the power, the brilliance, and the distinctive timbre that made him one of the most famous singers of his era.

## THE RECORDINGS

The sessions took place in Milan in February 1903 and February 1904. Tamagno recorded arias from operas by Verdi, Meyerbeer, Massenet, Saint-Saëns, Rossini, and Giordano. Multiple takes were made of many pieces, allowing us to hear how Tamagno's interpretation varied from take to take.

Of particular interest are the four takes of "Esultate" from *Otello*, the opening chorus of the opera. Tamagno's rendition of this triumphant passage demonstrates the extraordinary power and brilliance of his voice. Similarly, the multiple takes of "Ora e per sempre" and "Niun mi tema" from the same opera provide invaluable insights into Tamagno's approach to Verdi's writing.

The recordings also include arias from less familiar operas, such as Meyerbeer's *Le Prophète* and Massenet's *Hérodiade*. These pieces showcase Tamagno's versatility and his ability to adapt his powerful voice to different stylistic demands.

## ANTONIO COTOGNI

Antonio Cotogni (1831-1918) was an Italian baritone who had a distinguished career spanning over four decades. He was known for his beautiful tone, excellent technique, and dramatic ability. Cotogni sang many of the same roles as Tamagno, and the two were contemporaries at La Scala and other major Italian theaters.

The two pieces included on this recording that are believed to be sung by Cotogni provide a fascinating contrast to Tamagno's voice. Cotogni's baritone voice offers a different perspective on the Italian operatic tradition of the late 19th century.

## TECHNICAL NOTES

These recordings have been transferred using the Authentic Transfer Process (ATP) developed by Symposium Records. This process aims to preserve the original character of the recordings while removing as much surface noise as possible. The results speak for themselves: these are the clearest and most natural-sounding transfers of Tamagno's recordings ever made.

The recordings were originally made on single-sided Gramophone & Typewriter Company discs, 10-inch in diameter, recorded at approximately 74 rpm. The transfers have been made at the correct speed, and the pitch is as originally recorded.
