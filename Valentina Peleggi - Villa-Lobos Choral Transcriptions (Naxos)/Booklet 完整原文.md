## 封底

Choral music holds a central position in Villa-Lobos's catalogue of works, but among these famous pieces is a series of little-known transcriptions for a cappella choir taken from the standard classical repertoire. They were intended for a teachers' chorus and for use in schools, and through astonishing alchemy they achieve a true 'orchestration' of largely piano originals, adding a fresh new repertoire for vocal ensembles. This album also includes the first ever recording of Villa-Lobos's complete set of a cappella transcriptions from Bach's *The Well-Tempered Clavier*.

## 内页说明

### About This Series

The series The Music of Brazil is part of the project Concerts, developed by the Brazilian Ministry of Foreign Affairs in order to disseminate Brazilian composers dating back to the 18th century. The recordings are being carried out by the Orquestra Sinfônica do Estado de São Paulo, the Camerata Antiqua de Curitiba and the São Paulo Symphony Orchestra. Further recordings of chamber and vocal music will gradually be added to this collection.

The works were selected according to their historical importance for Brazilian music and the existence of recordings. Most of the works recorded for the series have never had recordings available outside Brazil; many others will have their world premiere recordings. Important not only as a project in the preparation of new editions and the recording of works to be recorded, many of which, despite their relevance, have only been available in the composer's manuscript. This work will be carried out by the Brazilian Academy of Music and by musicologists working together with the orchestras.

### Heitor Villa-Lobos (1887-1959)
### Choral Transcriptions

#### Orchestrating for Voices

Choral music holds a central position in Villa-Lobos's vast catalogue of works. Among the landmarks of this repertoire are his three oratorios, his choral symphonies and his many choral transcriptions, including the well-known *Bachianas Brasileiras No. 9* (1931), originally written for a choir of female voices and a percussion group.

The booklet's attractive cover is occupied by a set of less well-known transcriptions for a cappella choir that he made between 1932 and 1935: a collection of sixteen choral pieces by Mendelssohn, Handel, Haydn, Mozart, Beethoven, Schubert, Schumann, Chopin, Massenet and Rachmaninov, but also Bach and Fugues (that is the Well-Tempered Clavier, and it is curious to observe that it includes some of the same works from which the composer later drew inspiration for his own *Bachianas Brasileiras*).

These transcriptions were originally intended for the choir of the Órfãos dos Professores (Orphans of Teachers) school in Rio de Janeiro, which provided an impressive set of often virtuosic folkloric arrangements, and which later served as a model for the São Paulo Symphony Choir's educational project.

In 1932, Villa-Lobos was invited by Anísio Teixeira (a leading Brazilian figure of that generation in the fields of education and culture, to whom this CD is dedicated) to create a curriculum of music education in the public school system of the city of Rio de Janeiro. The composer seized the opportunity to put into practice a task that called for a radical rethink of the learning system itself. His first concern was to train teachers and children – he initially recruited 400 children from the favelas, who sang in a choir of four mixed voices; he then conducted choirs of 1,000, 3,000 and even 10,000 voices, with the support of 200 teachers. Alongside the works of Kodály and Orff, Villa-Lobos transcribed pieces for vocal ensembles that were intended for the 'humbler' task of creating new audiences for music, but also for training choristers and teachers. This album is the first recording of the complete set of Villa-Lobos's a cappella transcriptions of Preludes (three) and Fugues (four) from *The Well-Tempered Clavier*, and it is curious to observe that it includes some of the same works from which the composer later drew inspiration for his own *Bachianas Brasileiras*.

In order to meet this challenge, Villa-Lobos carried out persistent research and available data of a virtuoso choir. Taking into consideration the fact that these were to be used in schools, the composer decided to intensify research and develop revision of the scores, so as to move these transcriptions closer to the original, and – while remaining close to the spirit of the original texts – to render an 'orchestration' for vocal ensembles (when orchestrating for voices).

It is the sheer musical control of no category of voices that allows these transcriptions to be so convincing: a mixed choir that could be described as 'orchestral'. Da *Orquestração do Brasil* (The Orchestration of Brazil) is the title of a documentary about the São Paulo Symphony Choir, and it refers to the unique sound of this ensemble, which is capable of achieving an impressive range of timbres and dynamics.

These sixteen transcriptions are now published in a single volume for the first time, and this recording is the first to present them complete. They are a testament to Villa-Lobos's genius as an arranger and his deep understanding of the human voice.

#### The Repertoire

**Mendelssohn's *Lieder ohne Worte*, Book 2, Op. 30, No. 9 in E major (1830-32)**, also known as 'Venetian Gondola Song', is one of the composer's most famous piano pieces. Villa-Lobos's transcription captures the gentle rocking motion of the gondola and the melancholy beauty of the Venetian lagoon.

**Bach's *Prelude and Fugue No. 8 in E flat minor/D sharp minor*, BWV 853** from *The Well-Tempered Clavier*, Book 1, is one of the most profound and emotionally charged pieces in the collection. The Prelude's lamenting melody is beautifully rendered by the choir's sopranos and altos, while the Fugue's intricate counterpoint is masterfully distributed among the four voice parts.

**Schumann's *Träumerei* (Dreaming) from *Kinderszenen*, Op. 15, No. 7 (1838)** is one of the most beloved piano miniatures in the repertoire. Villa-Lobos's choral version preserves the dreamy, nostalgic quality of the original while adding a new dimension of vocal warmth.

**Bach's *Fugue No. 1 in C major*, BWV 846** is the opening fugue from *The Well-Tempered Clavier*, Book 1. Its clear, straightforward structure makes it an ideal introduction to fugue writing, and Villa-Lobos's transcription highlights the purity of Bach's counterpoint.

**Schubert's *Ständchen* (Serenade)**, one of the *Schwanengesang* songs (D. 957, No. 4, 1828), is based on a poem by Ludwig Rellstab. The song's melancholy beauty is perfectly suited to choral treatment, and Villa-Lobos's transcription captures the longing and tenderness of the original.

**Bach's *Fugue No. 21 in B flat major*, BWV 866** from *The Well-Tempered Clavier*, Book 1, is a joyful, dance-like fugue with a lively subject. Villa-Lobos's transcription brings out the rhythmic vitality and harmonic richness of the original.

**Chopin's *Waltz No. 7 in C sharp minor*, Op. 64, No. 2 (1846-47)** is one of the composer's most popular waltzes. Villa-Lobos's choral version captures the grace and elegance of the original while adding a new dimension of vocal color.

**Bach's *Prelude No. 22 in B flat minor*, BWV 867** from *The Well-Tempered Clavier*, Book 1, is a deeply expressive piece with a singing melody over a pulsing accompaniment. Villa-Lobos's transcription brings out the lyrical beauty of the original.

**Rachmaninov's *Prélude in C sharp minor*, Op. 3, No. 2 (1892)** is one of the composer's most famous piano pieces. Villa-Lobos's choral version captures the dramatic intensity and emotional depth of the original.

**Bach's *Prelude No. 14 in F sharp minor*, BWV 883** from *The Well-Tempered Clavier*, Book 2, is a contemplative piece with a beautiful melodic line. Villa-Lobos's transcription preserves the introspective quality of the original.

**Massenet's *Élégie* from *Cinq pièces caractéristiques*, Op. 25 (1886)** is a melancholy piece that showcases the expressive capabilities of the choir. Villa-Lobos's transcription captures the sorrowful beauty of the original.

**Bach's *Fugue No. 5 in D major*, BWV 874** from *The Well-Tempered Clavier*, Book 2, is a brilliant, virtuosic fugue with a lively subject. Villa-Lobos's transcription brings out the technical brilliance and harmonic sophistication of the original.

**Beethoven's *Adagio cantabile* from the *Piano Sonata No. 8 in C minor*, Op. 13 'Pathétique' (1797-98)** is one of the composer's most beloved slow movements. Villa-Lobos's choral version preserves the singing quality and emotional depth of the original.

**Bach's *The Well-Tempered Clavier*, Book 1, BWV 846-869 and Book 2, BWV 870-893** are among the most important collections of keyboard music ever written. Villa-Lobos's transcriptions of selected preludes and fugues from these collections demonstrate his mastery of choral writing and his deep understanding of Bach's music.

**Bachianas Brasileiras No. 9 (1931)** is the final work in the series of nine *Bachianas Brasileiras*, and it is the only one written for choir alone (originally for female voices and percussion, but here performed in the version for mixed choir a cappella). The work consists of two movements: a Prelude (Bach's introduction) and a Fugue (Brazilian). The Prelude is a beautiful, contemplative piece that showcases the choir's ability to create a rich, orchestral sound. The Fugue is a brilliant, virtuosic piece that demonstrates the choir's technical prowess and the composer's mastery of counterpoint.

### São Paulo Symphony Choir (Osesp Choir)

Founded in 1994 and now recognized as a benchmark for vocal music in Brazil, the São Paulo Symphony Choir's repertoire spans a variety of musical periods, with a particular emphasis on the 20th and 21st centuries and works by Brazilian composers. It has recorded several albums for São Paulo Digital, and the Elatus Fine and Naxos labels. Between 1994 and 2013, the Osesp Choir was directed by Naum Alves de Souza. From 2011 until 2016, Valentina Peleggi was its chief conductor, with William Gomes serving as chorus master – a post he continues to hold. In January 2020, the Choir performed at the World Economic Forum in Davos. Switzerland, under the baton of Marin Alsop, honorary conductor of the São Paulo Symphony Orchestra.

---

### Valentina Peleggi

Italian conductor Valentina Peleggi has a deep connection to Brazil. Principal conductor of Osesp Choir between 2011 and 2016 and conductor in residence for the São Paulo Symphony Orchestra during 2017 and 2018, Peleggi was awarded Conductor of the Year 2016 by the São Paulo Association of Art Critics, and voted Young Talent of 2017 by *Revista Concerto* magazine. She is currently guest music director of the Theatro São Pedro, and music director of the Richmond Symphony, and was an IBD-Musicae Fellow at English National Opera from 2019 to 2020. In 2015 she won the Taki Concordia Conducting Fellowship, and in 2014 the conducting prize at the Campos do Jordão International Winter Festival, Brazil. Peleggi is an honorary Associate of the Royal Academy of Music.
