### 专辑说明 (Album Notes)

**Stephanie McCallum** specialises in virtuoso, out-of-the-way music of the 19th century and in complex contemporary solo and ensemble scores.

Stephanie won the ABC Concerto Competition in 1978 while a student at the Sydney Conservatorium with Alexander Sverjensky and Gordon Watson. After graduating with first class honours and the University Medal, she studied with the renowned pianist Ronald Smith, she gave a highly successful Wigmore Hall debut in 1980. Her first solo recording is believed to be the first performance of Alkan's Concerto, Op. 39.

She has appeared extensively as a soloist in the United Kingdom, France and Australia, as has toured Europe with the Alpha Centauri Trio. She is a member of the contemporary music ensemble ELISION, and has played and recorded with ELISION and the Australia Ensemble.

#### 关于作品的详细分析

**Perhaps the most obvious symphonic characteristic of the Symphonie is its lack of the kind of decorative, impersonating figuration which characterises the solo sections of the Concerto. Another which is characteristic of the classical symphony, albeit by a path more elusive to us than the entirety of its material. This is reflected in the way the work opens: the opening idea of the symphony, sweeping idea on 'imaginary' cellos and basses, heard which moves in a pattern that involves both immediate transformations, in later movements as well.**

Particularly prevalent are the opening rising three-note figure which is first heard as an upward marked "a" below, and the two-note step-wise "sighing" figure marked "b" which is what is to impart to this movement a sense of noble yearning.

This melody becomes more expansive when it is transferred to the right hand, becoming an orchestrally suggestive dialogue, marked *dolcemente espressivo* which expands the three-note sighing though between the two hands, and prepares the way for the more carefree second theme.
