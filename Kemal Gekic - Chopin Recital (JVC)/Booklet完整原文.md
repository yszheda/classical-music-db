## Introduction (Japanese original text translated to English)

Kemal Gekic was 22 years old when this was recorded in 1989, and in 1990 when this was released he was 23. This was his first visit to Japan. In April 1989 he competed in the [Chopin Competition in Warsaw], and many had high expectations of him, he was voted very popular by the audience, selling out on the same day, and the judges were impressed by his powerful playing. He ended up finishing in third place.

Watching this competition was very interesting. When I confessed I thought the first prize should have gone to someone else with a quieter, more Western style of playing, and the second place would have gone to another, I'm often asked what I think. But no one expected that after a few months when we had the chance to record him here in Japan, that his playing would make such an impression. His playing at the competition was supported overwhelmingly by the audience, the voting was extremely popular, and it sold out immediately. He impressed the judges with his strength of will.

He plays with freely bold settings of tone color, a strong rhythmic pulse, and after two evenings of playing, you can see his brilliant scholarship. This was his first recital tour in Japan, starting October 20, 1989 at Tokyo Bunka Kaikan Small Hall, and then around the country. He approaches each work with study, draws out his own conception, and in this recital his playing has shed his old skin, advancing step by step. He steps out to meet a new self through dialogue, gradually his confident expression takes hold, he has a uniquely rushing pace, his conception of the pieces has been further polished, he doesn't react to others, he advances forward with strong will keeping his conviction deep in his heart.

In the first half there is willpower, a sense of conviction. In the second half, you can't call his playing just an excellent student's reading, he has extraordinary expressive power. You don't know what will come next, he has the strong fascination of an unknown that draws you into the recital. At this period of his life he discovers new sides of composers, reads through the score again, this time, what kind of man is this? He brings it out, his personality comes through in his interpretation, his playing is full of character, he actively participates in the music, at the bottom of the music there is a strong cry, a weeping expression, that's his special characteristic. He throws himself into the score with his lyrical nature, deeply studies the image in each section, I've said this before. The greatest reason is that his playing lets you feel all four emotions joy anger sorrow happiness very deeply. His depth of sorrow, like a flash of lightning, the dynamic expression makes these beloved works come alive again in our time. He is very great.

He was 28 this year (1990), he already holds an assistant professor position at his alma mater the Academy of Music. This young star still eagerly explores the path.

## Work Notes

### Grande Polonaise Brillante preceded by Andante Spianato in E-flat major, Op. 22

Chopin composed this work at his country villa in 1843, completed the next year 1844. At the beginning of this work, there are dissonant chords, he had just received news of his father's death, his heart was not calm. His health was worsening, his ideas became more varied, but the youthful brilliant writing still flows through the entire work on a large scale. The Andante section was originally written in 1830 for one of his pupil the Countess Woyna. In 1834 he added the introduction section, and dedicated to her. It was first performed in 1835. The opening is 4/4, the first theme has an impressive descending figure, the second theme is in D-flat major with an idyllic color and an elegant melody. At the end there is a short polonaise with rich national character. The form has many technical challenges.

### Ballade No. 1 in G minor, Op. 23

This is 3/4 in G minor, clear three-part form. Composed in 1831, completed in 1835. It began during a short stay in Bavaria. The form has a fast theme A, and after a glance the middle section B brings a pastoral flavor. The introduction sets the story beginning, and then moves to the reprise of A.

### Polonaise No. 6 in A-flat major "Heroique", Op. 53 "Heroic"

This is 4/4 in A-flat major. A narrative poem. After the first and second themes it welcomes the faster trio section. It's a polonaise character. In the middle section you can see key changes clearly. Three-part form. This is a mature work. Composed by Chopin in 1842. It opens with a strong theme in the long introduction. There is a patriotic feeling of majesty, the characteristic is the impressive rhythm carved by the left hand. The chords successive strikes are excellent.

### Piano Sonata No. 3 in B minor, Op. 58

This is Chopin's late representative work. Completed 1844, five years after the second sonata.
