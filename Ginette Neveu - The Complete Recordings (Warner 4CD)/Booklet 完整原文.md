## GINETTE NEVEU - THE COMPLETE RECORDINGS

### English Liner Notes by Tully Potter (2019)

---

**GINETTE NEVEU**

Performing artists who die young often acquire a halo of posthumous fame. In the case of Ginette Neveu, killed aged 30 in a plane crash on 19 October 1949, that halo has been especially bright, as her career was at its flood tide. She seemed to symbolise the recovery of classical music after the war's height; youthful admiration was replacing heroes of the interwar years whose company she had already joined, such as Heifetz, Elman and Menuhin.

Music ran in her family: her great-uncle was the organist Charles-Marie Widor, her father was an amateur luthier, her mother taught piano, and her elder brother Jean-Paul, known as Jean, a pianist. Born in Paris on 11 August 1919, she began violin lessons at five with her mother, transferred to Lise Trullard and at seven, made her debut with the Concerts Colonne and Gabriel Pierné, playing Bruch's G minor, then studying with George Enescu, in 1928 Neveu won first prize at the École Supérieure de Musique, as well as the City of Paris Prix d'honneur. In 1930 she entered Isidor Busch's Conservatoire class and nine months later took a first prize, aged 11, prompting comparisons with Henri Wieniawski. In the 1931 Vienna International Competition on the same occasion as David Oistrakh and Henryk Szeryng, Neveu was impressed that he offered a full scholarship. Neveu could not take it up for two years but then studied with him for four years in Berlin and Belgium and continued to consult him thereafter.

In 1935, 16-year-old Neveu beat 26-year-old David Oistrakh to first place in the Henryk Wieniawski Competition in Warsaw, creating a fission with Ravel's Tzigane. Even the French juror, Gaubilard, told Oistrakh he had been the victim of anti-Semitism, but the Ukrainian himself was most magnanimous. Neveu toured Germany twice and visited the Soviet Union. In 1937 came her New York recital debut at Town Hall on 12 March: she played Strauss's Sonata, Bach's Chaconne, the Hartley Sonata, Paganini's 5th Caprice, Wieniawski's Polonaise and Ravel's Tzigane, the latter two on her second New York visit later that year. In 1938 she played the Brahms Concerto and was impressed by the offer of a full scholarship.

The war and the German Occupation held her back and she refused to tour Germany, despite lucrative offers. In occupied Paris, she played Beethoven's Concerto for the first time on 4 May 1941, with Paul Paray conducting. In 1942 she began her recitals with brother Jean as Neveu Nat duo. An important event was the 1943 première of Poulenc's Sonata, written in memory of Federico García Lorca and edited by Neveu. Poulenc did not get on well with stringed-instrumentalists – he dumped his second attempt at a quartet after one playing, leaving it reburned by the Quatuor Galvet in 1946 – and felt that "despite some technical innovations by Ginette Neveu, the [Sonata] did not amount to much". Nevertheless, some violinists have succeeded with it and Neveu carried on performing it and Poulenc tried it out before an audience of friends and gave the first public performance on 21 June at the Salle Gaveau, in aid of the war prisoners' wives and artists. They collaborated in at least one more artistic performance during the war. In 1949 the composer revised the finale and played it in Paris with Neveu as soloist, if typically for the time, the pianist was not named.

### French Text - «Ginette Neveu» par Hugues Moutschen (2019)

Les interprètes morts jeunes récoltent souvent, après leur décès, une célébrité d'ordre tout particulier. Dans le cas de Ginette Neveu, disparue à trente ans, le 28 octobre 1949, dans un accident d'avion, le chagrin causé par son immense disparition s'est fait d'autant plus cruel que sa carrière était encore dans sa phase d'expansion. Elle semblait incarner le renouveau de la musique classique après la terrible conflit mondial : avec son visage juvénile et lumineux elle succédait aux monstres sacrés de l'entre-deux-guerres, au niveau desquels d'ores et déjà hissée, tant son talent était immense.

Ginette Neveu était issue d'une famille profondément musicienne. Son grand-oncle n'était autre que l'organiste Charles-Marie Widor, et si son père pratiquait le violon en amateur, sa mère, elle était une violoniste confirmée, tandis que son frère aîné Jean-Paul, plus connu sous le nom de Jean, était pianiste. Né à Paris le 11 août 1919, elle reçut à l'âge de cinq ans ses leçons premières de violon de sa mère, puis étudia avec Lise Trullard et, à sept ans, fit son entrée au Conservatoire de Paris, dans la classe d'Isidor Busch. Elle y obtint un premier prix au bout de neuf mois, à onze ans à peine, ce qui permit de la comparer à Henri Wieniawski. En 1931, au Concours Wieniawski qui se déroulait à Varsovie, elle remporta le premier prix devant David Oistrakh et Henryk Szeryng.

Cette édition remasterisée en 24 bit - 96 kHz de l'intégrale des enregistrements HMV et Electrola de Ginette Neveu a nécessité une recherche des meilleures sources, c'est-à-dire celles qui présentaient le meilleur compromis entre l'état de conservation et la fort préservation de l'enregistrement original. ### Biography Summary

**Ginette Neveu (1919-1949)**

The French violinist Ginette Neveu was just 30 when her plane crashed in the Azores in October 1949. She had studied with George Enescu and Carl Flesch, and as The Observer wrote in 1945, "Her playing was superbly vigorous and passionate, and made the impression that its great qualities, such as eloquent phrasing and an apparently limitless range and variety of tone, came from the only true source – an identity with the music and with her instrument."
