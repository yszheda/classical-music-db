## About Aureliano Pertile and Toscanini

Aureliano Pertile (1887-1952) was one of the greatest Italian tenors of the 20th century. This second volume of the Pertile Edition focuses on his recordings made under the baton of the legendary conductor Arturo Toscanini between 1928 and 1932.

These electrical recordings represent Pertile at the height of his artistic powers. The collaboration with Toscanini, one of the most demanding conductors of the era, brought out the finest qualities in Pertile's singing - his remarkable breath control, dramatic intensity, and musical intelligence.

The repertoire spans the Italian operatic tradition from Donizetti's bel canto to Puccini's verismo, with excursions into Wagner (sung in Italian) and French opera. These recordings document a golden age of Italian singing and preserve the art of one of Italy's most celebrated tenors in repertoire that showcases his unique vocal and dramatic gifts.

The electrical recording process, introduced in 1925, allowed for a much wider frequency range and dynamic range than the earlier acoustic process. These recordings therefore offer a more complete picture of Pertile's voice and artistry than the acoustic recordings of the early 1920s.

## About the Repertoire

This album presents 17 arias from Pertile's Toscanini years:

**Donizetti**: From "L'elisir d'amore" and "La Favorita," showcasing the bel canto tradition.

**Bellini**: "I Puritani" - one of the great bel canto tenor roles.

**Flotow**: "Marta" - the famous "M'appari tutt'amor" in Italian translation.

**Verdi**: A substantial Verdi selection including Luisa Miller, Il Trovatore, La forza del destino (with baritone Benvenuto Franci), and two selections from Otello (including the duet with Franci).

**Wagner**: Lohengrin's farewell, sung in Italian as was customary in Italian opera houses of the period.

**Bizet**: The Flower Song from "Carmen" in Italian.

**Boito**: From "Mefistofele" - a work closely associated with Toscanini.

**Leoncavallo**: "Vesti la giubba" from "Pagliacci."

**Puccini**: Two arias from "Manon Lescaut" and the famous "E lucevan le stelle" from "Tosca."
