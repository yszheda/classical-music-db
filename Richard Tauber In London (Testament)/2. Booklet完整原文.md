## 内页第4-5页 传记文章

# Richard Tauber in LONDON
## THE ABBEY ROAD RECORDINGS, 1933-1939

IT IS perhaps not surprising that Richard Tauber, who was born in Linz an der Donau on 16th May 1891, should have enjoyed such a successful career as a singer, both in the theatre and in the concert hall. His mother Elisabeth Denemy, born in Vienna in 1847, was a soubrette of some repute and admired throughout Austria, although she died a sad death in Salzburg. Tauber's grandfather, and there still remains a family business in Vienna today. He had thirteen daughters and one son, Tauber's father, who was also born in Vienna, in 1861. He was of Jewish descent and was a highly respected actor of international repute. Like many parents in the performing arts, they wanted their son to train as a doctor or lawyer, but the young Richard had other ideas, being determined to become a singer. He was sent to Das Hoch'schen Konservatorium in Frankfurt am Main where his musical studies included conducting, a skill that he was to use to considerable acclaim later in his life. His success was such that, in 1911, he was introduced to the famous singing teacher Professor Carl Beines, who agreed to take him on as a pupil. Two years later Tauber sang to his father, who was by then Director of two theatres in Chemnitz. His father was so impressed that he arranged for his son to be engaged for performances of Mozart's *Die Zauberflöte*. These were a personal triumph for Tauber and a five year contract with the Dresden State Opera, and then contracts with the Berlin and Vienna State Operas, soon followed. This was the beginning of a career that was to take Tauber all over the world, performing an enormously varied repertoire with charm and panache, and winning a tremendous following wherever he sang.

In 1931 he made a highly successful visit to the United States of America and six years later - and once again in 1939 - followed this with a tour of forty cities in America and Canada, including a concert in the Carnegie Hall and a performance before the President, Franklin D. Roosevelt. Tauber considered this performance one of the pinnacles of his career.

1933 saw the year when Tauber was to leave Germany; he had been beaten up at the hands of the Nazis and had fled the country forever that very night. He had taken with him his friend Mary Lossef, a most beautiful, dramatic soprano, who was to become a lifelong companion to Tauber. In 1936 and 1938, Tauber made visits to Italy singing at La Scala and all the major cities. It was while Tauber was in Italy that he heard news of the invasion of Austria by the German army. He never saw his mother again and was never to return to his beloved homeland. Although in 1938 he was declared stateless, he applied for and was granted, in 1939, British citizenship. He made his first visit to Britain in 1932, singing in the Royal Albert Hall, but, for him, one of the high points in his career was his meeting with King George V whose cherished words were: *"You do not sing with your voice, you sing with your heart."*

During the Second World War he toured Britain, with the London Philharmonic Orchestra, as a conductor. Malcolm Sargent was conducting elsewhere and Tauber was engaged to conduct in his place. The results of the many concerts he gave were, it was agreed, of an exceptionally high standard. Indeed, Sir Thomas Beecham compared him with the finest conductors in the world at that time.

On the 27th September 1947, at the Royal Opera House, Covent Garden, Tauber's life came, as it were, full circle. His first operatic performance was in Mozart's *Die Zauberflöte*; that night, he gave what was to be his final live operatic performance, as Don Ottavio in Mozart's *Don Giovanni*. One week later he had an operation to remove a lung after cancer had been diagnosed. It was then found that malignant tumours had spread to the other lung. On 7th January 1948, Tauber was rushed to the London Clinic, Harley Street; he died in the early hours of 8th January.

The very great success of his career is due in some measure to the versatility which he instilled into his musical life. He was as at home in the operas of Mozart as he was in the operettas of Franz Lehár. He not only made many hundreds of stage and concert appearances, but also made an enormous number of recordings, as well as broadcasting all over the world. His prowess on the conductor's podium has already been mentioned and is perhaps better known than his list of individual and highly successful compositions which extends to some 150. One should not forget also the films that he made: there were nine in all, of which the four most successful were made in this country. The remainder were made by Tauber's own company in Germany; unfortunately, his cousin, Max Tauber, swindled Richard out of most of the profits from these films, and Tauber was forced to declare heavy taxes and losses to none other than Hermann Goering. However, the success of this branch of his career can be measured by the fact that to-day the Richard Tauber Foundation sells about ten videos of his films per week.

But it is for his very personal performances of the lighter side of the repertoire for which he is arguably best known today and this issue brings together some of the highlights of the most cherished recordings made by Tauber in this field, as well as some popular operatic arias in which he excelled. They were all made between the years of 1933 to 1939 when Tauber was at his peak. During this period he was making some of his most memorable appearances on stage: 1934 in *Giuditta* at the Vienna State Opera; La Scala in 1936 and in 1938. His commitment to this repertory is evident in every note and the expressive warmth of his singing comes across as strongly today as it did when first recorded.

*from notes by Dr. Dirk van der Meyden*

# A note from Keith Hardwick...

It is a pleasure to be asked by Testament to carry out the transfers from 78s for their CD re-issues of historic records. In doing these, I still have the same objective which had guided my work in the past - **"the music, all the music, and nothing but the music"**. This goal is, alas, virtually unattainable, but one can attempt to eliminate as much of the "non-music" as possible without cutting out any of the musical frequencies. As in the past, I am able to use a wide range of stylus sizes, electronic "de-clickers" and sophisticated play-back equipment - but no acoustical/mechanical playback, no added swimming-bath echo; just all the musical sounds which were captured in the ancient grooves.

It is a pleasure, too, to be able to choose Testament's historic repertoire. We aim to give the music lover the chance to hear the best recordings of the past in the best possible sound.

K. Hardwick

## Richard Tauber Foundation

**10 Victoria Road, Mill Hill, London NW7 4SB**
Telephone: 081-959 2645

Chairman: Dr. Dirk van der Meyden, Dipl. Ing.

Richard Tauber, the world famous tenor, was born in Linz an der Donau in Austria on 16th May 1891 and died in London on 8th January 1948. He is regarded as perhaps one of the most outstanding singers the world has ever known. His impact on opera was enormous: he gave unrivalled performances in over eighty operas. Likewise, his contribution to operetta was unparalleled, performing with brilliance in many works, including all those of his favourite, Franz Lehár. He was also a fine interpreter of Lieder, starred in musical films, conducted and composed, all with equal aplomb. The pleasure that Richard Tauber gave to millions of people the world over is immeasurable.

The aims of the Richard Tauber Foundation are:

- To create a permanent home, open to the public, for the display of memorabilia from his life, which are being obtained by the Foundation.
- Tauber himself was extremely generous and contributed to people in need. It is therefore the intention of the Foundation to award scholarships to those young opera singers who wish to follow in the path of Richard Tauber's career.
- Bearing in mind the distressing circumstances in which Tauber spent his last months, to give grants to those singers of opera and operetta who, through illness, are unable to continue with their career.
- To commemorate his contribution to the world by the erecting of a statue.

Please send any donation to the address above; your contribution will be acknowledged. Details of Annual and Life Membership are available from the Foundation.

*The aims of the Foundation can only be achieved with your help.*
