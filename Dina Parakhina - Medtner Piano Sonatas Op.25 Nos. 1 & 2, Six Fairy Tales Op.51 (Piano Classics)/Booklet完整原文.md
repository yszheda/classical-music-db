# Nikolai Medtner (1880-1951)
by Graham Sutherland

When one thinks of Russian composers who left their homeland after the 1917 Revolution and settled in the West, the names of Rachmaninov, Prokofiev and Stravinsky immediately come to mind. However, there is one other major figure who made the journey, and for many years his music was seriously neglected. That composer was Nikolai Medtner, and although he composed a number of works in different genres, it is generally accepted that his greatest achievements are to be found in his piano music. The two big sonatas Opp. 25 and the Six Fairy Tales Op. 51 here are fine examples of his mature genius.

Medtner was born in Moscow in 1880, and studied at the Moscow Conservatory with Sergei Taneyev, one of the most respected teachers of the time. After graduating he quickly built up a reputation as both composer and pianist, but the October Revolution changed everything, and in 1921 he left Russia, never to return. After a number of years living in Germany and France, he finally settled in London in 1936, where he remained until his death in 1951. During his lifetime, Medtner enjoyed the support of a number of important champions, most notably the pianist Sviatoslav Richter, who did much to promote his music. However, it is only in recent years that his work has begun to be more widely appreciated, with an increasing number of recordings and performances appearing on the concert platform.

What marks Medtner out from other Russian composers of his generation is the absolute individuality of his musical language. He was never attracted by the avant-garde trends that influenced so many of his contemporaries, and remained true to the Romantic tradition throughout his creative life. His music is characterized by rich contrapuntal writing, complex harmonic structures, and a deep emotional seriousness that can sometimes verge on the austere. At the same time, however, he was capable of writing melodies of great beauty and lyricism, and the best of his works have an intellectual and expressive depth that rewards repeated listening.

The two Piano Sonatas Opp. 25 were composed between 1923 and 1927, when Medtner was already in his forties and at the height of his powers. They represent the culmination of his achievement in the field of piano sonata writing, and each has its own distinctive character. The First Sonata in F minor is subtitled the "Sonata-Ballade", and as this title suggests it has a strong dramatic and narrative quality. The work is tightly constructed around a few basic motifs that are developed throughout the three movements with considerable contrapuntal ingenuity. The opening movement is full of energy and conflict, with a powerful main subject that makes its presence felt from the very outset. The slow movement is more lyrical and expressive, while the finale returns to the dramatic mood of the opening, building up to a powerful and conciliatory conclusion.

The Second Sonata in E minor is known as the "Night Wind", and this title gives a good indication of its character. It is a larger-scale work than its predecessor, in four movements rather than three, and it has a more turbulent and restless quality throughout. The opening movement begins with a theme of great lyricism, but the music quickly becomes more dramatic, with passionate outbursts contrasting with more reflective passages. The scherzo is quicksilver and light-footed, contrasting with the gentle, flowing lines of the Andante piacevole that follows. The finale is the most tempestuous movement of all, marked Presto con fuoco, and it builds up to a climax of great power and intensity before the quiet, enigmatic close.

The *Six Fairy Tales* Op. 51 date from 1935, some eight years after the Opp. 25 sonatas, and they show Medtner exploring a more intimate and lyrical vein. As the title suggests, each piece has a descriptive title that evokes a particular mood or scene, and Medtner responds to each one with music that perfectly captures the character of the subject. They range from the light, airy textures of "The Little Cloud" and the witty scherzando of "The Jester" to the wintry stillness of "The Snowman", the romantic lyricism of "The Princess and the Wild Rose", the skittering energy of "The Goblin", and the calm resignation of "The Last Blossom". They are all masterly miniatures, perfectly crafted and full of inventive piano writing, and they make an ideal coupling for the two larger sonatas.

Medtner found an early and enthusiastic champion in the great Russian pianist Sviatoslav Richter, who famously said that Medtner was "one of the greatest composers of the twentieth century", and that "his music is a treasure that must be preserved". It is particularly fitting that on this CD we hear another Russian pianist, Dina Parakhina, who has established a considerable reputation as a distinguished interpreter of Medtner's piano music. Her performances combine technical command of the highest order with a deep understanding of Medtner's musical language, and she proves here once again that this music is every bit as vital and relevant today as it was when it was first composed. We are convinced that this release will do much to further the growing appreciation of this most individual and important of Russian composers.

# Dina Parakhina

Dina Parakhina studied at the Moscow Central Music School and the Moscow Tchaikovsky Conservatoire with Professor Lev Naumov, the legendary pedagogue whose students included many of the most distinguished Russian pianists of the last fifty years. After graduating she joined the faculty of the Moscow Conservatoire, and also gave numerous concerts throughout Russia and Eastern Europe. In 1992 she moved to the United Kingdom, where she has since built up a successful career as both performer and teacher.

She currently teaches at the Royal Northern College of Music in Manchester and at the Royal Academy of Music in London, and gives regular masterclasses both in the UK and abroad. She has performed at many major festivals and concert venues throughout Europe, the United States and the Far East, and has recorded a number of highly acclaimed CDs of music by Medtner, Rachmaninov, Scriabin and others for the Piano Classics label.
