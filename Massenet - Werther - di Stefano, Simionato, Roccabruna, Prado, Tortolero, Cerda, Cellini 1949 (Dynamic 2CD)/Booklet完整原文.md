## Introduction (Original Italian text translated)

After the second world war, when the world's most important theaters began reopening, in 1946 Giuseppe di Stefano debuted in Reggio Emilia in Massenet's *Manon*, and quickly EMI began a collaboration that would last many years, producing a series of memorable recordings with Maria Callas (including *Ballo in maschera*, *Cavalleria rusticana*, *Lucia di Lammermoor*, *Pagliacci*, *Rigoletto*).

This recording documents one of the most memorable evenings in the opera history of the 20th century: the young Giuseppe di Stefano in a work that seemed written especially for him, Massenet's *Werther* (sung in Italian, as was customary in almost all non-French-speaking countries).

The Sicilian tenor, in the full splendor of his wonderful vocal means, in a particularly inspired performance, he is capable of moving us, enthusing us on every note. He does not just interpret Werther: he is Werther.

Giulietta Simionato (born in Forlì in 1910), on the contrary, already had a long career behind her, even though she would reach success only after World War II, when she became one of the main stars of La Scala. The role of Charlotte was one of her favorites, and Giuseppe di Stefano has Werther, Carlotta is one of his most inspired and incandescent interpretations, documented by two studio recordings. The first, 78rpm, was made for Columbia in Mexico City; the second, LP, by Decca in the 1950s.

Next to him, Giulietta Simionato in great shape, excellent Fausto del Prado as Albert and Eugenia Roccabruna as Sofia; everything supported by inspired and fiery conducting of Renato Cellini, one of those conductors who back then were called just "honest craftsmen", and today who would have much to teach to many of today's transient "star" conductors.

At just twenty-eight years of age (he was born in Catania on July 24, 1921), Giuseppe di Stefano had already quickly climbed the stages of a career that promised extraordinary results. The present recording documents one of the most memorable evenings in 20th-century opera: the legendary performance that the young di Stefano gave of a work that seemed written just for him.

(Translated by Danile Pilarz)
