**ABOUT THE ARTISTS**

It is unusual in the world of chamber music for the members of an ensemble to remain unchanged. The Griller String Quartet, comprised of the same musicians from its inception in 1928 until its demise in 1961, was renowned for its highly polished ensemble playing, wide expressive range, and superior musicianship. The members were first violinist and leader Sidney Griller (b. 1911); second violinist Jack O'Brien (b. 1906), violist Philip Burton (b. 1907-1943) and violist Colin Hampton (b. 1911). Together they formed one of the world's greatest chamber music ensembles.

They started playing together while studying with the famous violist, Lionel Tertis, at the Royal Academy of Music in London. Following their first concert in London in 1928, they received enthusiastic encouragement from Ralph Vaughan Williams and Dame Myra Hess, and by 1932 they were recognized as the foremost British string quartet. Their first European tour was in 1930, a New York debut on February 2, 1938, was acclaimed by the critics of the New York Times as the most important and successful debut recital of the year. In 1949 they began full tenure at the University of California, Berkeley, as personnel and teaching-quarters.
