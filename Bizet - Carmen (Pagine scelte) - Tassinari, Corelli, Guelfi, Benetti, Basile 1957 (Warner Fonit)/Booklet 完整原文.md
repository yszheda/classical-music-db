All'interno della ricchissima discografia della Carmen si realizzò negli anni Cinquanta una selezione che la Cetra realizzò negli anni della ripresa di questa opera in Italia. Fu la volta di una Carmen con la protagonista assoluta, la grande voce di Pia Tassinari, e un giovane Franco Corelli nel ruolo di Don José. Questa registrazione del 1957 rimane una delle più belle testimonianze dell'opera italiana del dopoguerra.

In Italian Carmen performances from Piedmont to Rossini, the tradition was always very present. The production of this selection represents an important milestone in the discography of Carmen.

Within the plentiful recordings of Carmen there is this selection that was made by Cetra in the fifties, during the revival of this opera in Italy. This is the turn of a Carmen with the absolute protagonist, the great voice of Pia Tassinari, and a young Franco Corelli in the role of Don José. This 1957 recording remains one of the most beautiful testimonies of post-war Italian opera.

## Inside Booklet - Pages 5-6 (Notes by Giancarlo Landini)

Dopo il debutto a Spoleto nel Conte di Luna al Teatro dei primi di questo secolo, Pia Tassinari (1903-1995) si rivela una delle più grandi voci femminili del Novecento italiano. La sua Carmen, incisa per la Cetra nel 1957, è una delle sue interpretazioni più celebri. La Tassinari, moglie di Franco Corelli, possedeva una voce scura, verista e di grande gusto, un tipo di voce che il Belcanto-Rinascimento e i suoi sostenitori hanno condannato a morte, nella vana speranza di scoprire ancora una volta la chiara voce del baritono romantico. Cantante di istinto, dotata di uno strumento vocale di considerevoli dimensioni e forza, affronta facilmente la parte che richiede un registro acuto e pronto. Onora facilmente la famosa frase con una violenza virile che cancella l'ironia francese della scrittura di Bizet, ma le conferisce la grandiosità ostentata di un torero che risponde al gusto dell'epoca. Le tre famose cantanti, alle quali dobbiamo aggiungere la Micaëla di Margherita Benetti, un elemento degno delle scene liriche degli anni Cinquanta, sono dirette da Arturo Basile,

Within the plentiful recordings of Carmen there is certainly worthy this selection that Cetra realised in the Fifties, and published with the code LPC 55020. What is a reissuing recording that is indispensable to all the melomaniacs: that the voice of more than one reason to be attractive: on the one hand, the singing of Pia Tassinari, one of the greatest Italian singers of the post-war period, the most famous Carmen of the years around 1950, and on the other Franco Corelli. The tenor of Ancona, on his debut in 1951 in Spoleto in the role of the Duke in Rigoletto, was immediately acclaimed as one of the greatest tenors of the century.

Firstly, this selection bears witness to Pia Tassinari's Carmen that may be said to be the best of all the great mezzo Flemo Tassinari. Born in Forlì in 1903. At the age of 26 she began her career singing the part of Turiddu in Cavalleria rusticana at the Theatre of Lucca. At the end of 1930s, in the wake of a successful experience in South America, where she had sung with the tenor Gigli, she began to be considered as one of the greatest singers of the period. She was also the teacher of Franco Corelli, who was to become her husband. The Corelli on Bizet's heroine, of course, the role of Don José, the young soldier who lets himself be taken in by the gypsy's charms, is one of the finest in his repertoire. The recording also shows the great art of Margherita Benetti as Micaëla, and of Gian Giacomo Guelfi as Escamillo.

**Giancarlo Landini**

## Inside Booklet - Pages 7-8

**Pia Tassinari** (Foto - Photo: Cetra, Torino 1940 ca.)

born in Siracusa in 1914, a veteran of the RAI orchestras, and arts consultant to Cetra. The latter proves his sturdy professionalism and knowledge of the lyrical stage and its needs, and it is not only a matter of praising the very effective reading, but also the intelligence set in the elegance of the instrumental part (the diffuse sounds of the choruses in the Habanera, and the play of the clarinets, the oboes and the bassoons in the Seguidilla, are more than enough in this sense), and with which he links the Italian, verist, interpreting of Carmen, to the requirements of a more modern listening audience.

The overall result is a significant picture of the Italian interpreting taste of the Fifties, that still now offer moments and reasons of interest.
