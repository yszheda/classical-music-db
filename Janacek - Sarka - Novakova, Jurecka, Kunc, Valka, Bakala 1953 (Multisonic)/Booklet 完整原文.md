## Libretto Synopsis

### Act I

The maiden Šárka rebels against the tyranny of Přemysl and his men. She is captured and bound to a tree as punishment for leading the maidens' revolt. Ctirad, a warrior in Přemysl's court, discovers her and is immediately captivated by her beauty. Šárka plays on his sympathy, claiming she was punished for opposing the maidens' rebellion. Ctirad frees her and falls deeply in love.

### Act II

Ctirad is completely under Šárka's spell. She manipulates his emotions, pretending affection while planning revenge. In a famous scene, she serves him drugged wine at a banquet, and while Ctirad and his warriors sleep, the maidens attack and massacre them. Šárka's vengeance against the male oppressors is fulfilled through this brutal act.

### Act III

The aftermath of the massacre. Šárka reflects on the violence and the cost of vengeance. The opera concludes with the tragic consequences of the conflict between the sexes, as portrayed in this heroic tale from Czech mythology.

## Programme Notes by John Tyrrell

Those who know only later operas of the Czech composer Leoš Janáček (1854-1928) will be surprised by the very different world conjured up by his first opera. Its characteristically Slavonic scale seems to place it within a literary and musical context that seems to link relatively closely with Smetana's masterpieces. Writing about the opera in 1924, Janáček's biographer Vladimír Helfert declared:

"Janáček's Šárka is closest in its character and form to Smetana's libretto and has evidently grown from it. (...) At the time it was written Janáček's 'Šárka' was the most perfect and stylistically the purest Czech tragic opera of its time apart from the work of Smetana."

The subject matter from Czech mythic history was popular with Czech authors, artists and musicians in the nineteenth century. The third section of Smetana's orchestral cycle "My Country" is called "Šárka" (1880), as was Fibich's best known opera (1897). The famous Janáček melodrama had already been written decades before Fibich's opera and was obviously indispensable for the same action. It was written by the leading Czech symbolist poet Julius Zeyer (1841-1901) at the time of honouring his father, impressing with five opera texts behind him. For the libretto Zeyer seems to have adapted, though perhaps part ("Větrník") of the five-part verse epic "Vyšehrad", the second part of the trilogy he undertook in his youth. But Zeyer's "Šárka" was rejected by Dvořák – perhaps he was looking for something more international for the foreign audiences he was attracting in numerous musical works. Furthermore the libretto, which at least until the final act strongly appeared in ensembles, was not of the kind with Dvořák's existing operatic partners. Janáček's "Šárka", which also found its way to the Prague opera office – quickly fruitlessly – was between January and February 1887 in the now theatrical magazine "Česká Thalia".

It was there that Janáček read it. Although opera had not featured much in his life so far, Janáček had been showing interest in the genre ever since Blou opera exclusively Czech theatre in 1883. Janáček received a manuscript of the new production in the theatre magazine "Hudební listy" he founded the next year. This Zeyer's "Šárka" came into his hands at an opportune moment, and he was so keenly hoping to do something for his career that he did not wait to obtain Fibich's three, he had come to compose.

The action is based on the legend of Šárka, a warrior maiden who leads a revolt of women against men after the death of the Amazon queen Libuše. The story comes from ancient Czech mythology and tells of the maidens' war that followed Libuše's death.

In the autumn of 1887, therefore, the twenty-three-year-old Janáček began composing the opera. By August Šárka was complete and on 28 October 1888 it was performed in a concert version at the Besední dům in Brno. The first staged performance took place at the National Theatre in Brno on 23 November 1925, with Ludmila Kvapilová in the title role.

Janáček revised the opera several times throughout his life. The final version we know today dates from 1924-1925.

## Über die Oper (Deutsche Fassung)

Die Handlung basiert auf der Legende von Šárka, einer Kriegerin, die nach dem Tod der Amazonenkönigin Libuše einen Aufstand der Frauen gegen die Männer anführt. Die Geschichte stammt aus der alttschechischen Mythologie und erzählt vom Mädchenkrieg, der nach Libušes Tod folgte.

Im Herbst 1887 begann der dreiundzwanzigjährige Janáček mit der Komposition der Oper. Im August war Šárka vollendet und am 28. Oktober 1888 wurde sie in einer Konzertversion im Besední dům in Brünn aufgeführt. Die erste szenische Aufführung fand am 23. November 1925 im Nationaltheater Brünn statt, mit Ludmila Kvapilová in der Titelrolle.

Janáček überarbeitete die Oper mehrmals während seines Lebens. Die endgültige Fassung, die wir heute kennen, stammt von 1924-1925.

## O Opeře (Česká Verze)

Břetislav Bakala (1897-1958) byl jedním z nejdůležitějších a nejvlivnějších osobností českého hudebního života první poloviny 20. století. Byl žákem a spolupracovníkem Leoše Janáčka, zakladatelem a dlouholetým dirigentem Brněnského rozhlasového orchestru. Bakala byl významným propagátorem Janáčkovy hudby a pořídil mnoho důležitých nahrávek české hudby.

Studoval skladbu u Janáčka a byl jeho blízkým spolupracovníkem. V roce 1923 založil Brněnský rozhlasový orchestr, který řídil až do své smrti v roce 1958. Bakala byl obzvláště známý svými interpretacemi Janáčkových oper a sborových děl.

Tato nahrávka, pořízená v roce 1953, je světovou premiérovou nahrávkou Šárky a představuje důležitý historický dokument české hudební kultury.

## About the Composer

**Leoš Janáček (1854-1928)** was one of the most important Czech composers. His operas include Jenůfa, Káťa Kabanová, The Cunning Little Vixen, The Makropulos Case, and From the House of the Dead. Šárka was his early opera, written in 1887 and revised several times until its final version in 1924-1925.

---

## About the Conductor

**Břetislav Bakala (1897-1958)** was one of the most important and influential figures in Czech musical life in the first half of the twentieth century. A pupil and close associate of Leoš Janáček, he was the founder and long-time conductor of the Brno Radio Symphony Orchestra. Bakala was a champion of Janáček's music and made many important recordings of Czech music.

He studied composition with Janáček and was his close collaborator. In 1923 he founded the Brno Radio Symphony Orchestra, which he conducted until his death in 1958. Bakala was particularly known for his interpretations of Janáček's operas and choral works.

This recording, made in 1953, is the world premiere recording of Šárka and represents an important historical document of Czech musical culture.

---

## Historical Note

This recording was made at the Ostrava Studio of Czechoslovak Radio from 28 to 29 October 1953. It is the world premiere recording of Janáček's Šárka and has been released as part of the Prague Opera Collection by Multisonic in 1992.

The opera was not performed in its complete form until 1925, nearly 40 years after it was written. This 1953 recording therefore represents a crucial document in the history of Czech opera.
