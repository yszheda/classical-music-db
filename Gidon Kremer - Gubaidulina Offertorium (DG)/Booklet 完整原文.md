## SOFIA GUBAIDULINA:
## OFFERTORIUM (CONCERTO POUR VIOLON)
## HOMMAGE À T. S. ELIOT

### (German Text)

In Jahre 1931 in Tschistopol an der Wolga geboren, erhielt Sofia Gubaidulina ihre Ausbildung als Komponistin bei Shebalin, Chentov und im Moskauer Konservatorium. 1974 wurde sie Mitglied der Union der Sowjetischen Komponisten. Ihre Musik wurde in der ganzen Welt aufgeführt.

Die Komponistin Sofia Gubaidulina ist eine originäre und individuelle, aber auch eine sehr persönliche Künstlerin. Sie hat eine einzigartige musikalische Sprache entwickelt, die sich durch spirituelle Tiefe und technische Meisterschaft auszeichnet.

### (English Text by Steven Ledbetter)

Sofia Gubaidulina has emerged in a short time as one of the most original and powerful Russian composers of the late 20th century. Born in 1931, she grew up in a small town on the Volga River, began piano lessons at the age of five and started composing early in order to be permitted to continue her studies to play from finger exercises. She began serious study of composition in Kazan as a seventeen-year-old. Her principal work took place at the Moscow Conservatory from 1954, where she studied with Nikolai Rakov and Vissarion Shebalin and earned her postgraduate degree in 1963. Already marked as an up-and-comer, Shostakovich encouraged her to "follow your own path," the final lasting inspiration in the words of Gubaidulina's teachers, a member of the committee that evaluated her final examination: "I want you to follow your own path, even if I don't like it." Moscow, supporting herself as a composer of film music while creating a number of works for a wide range of vocal or instrumental combinations.

"I find in film music wonderful opportunities for experiments, for my most, as it were, powerful experiments. It's not only that I can use a huge orchestra for applied music, but it's so... The instruments suddenly become like characters in some kind of drama... become like characters in some kind of drama."

Gubaidulina's basic conception was that "the theme would offer itself as a sacrifice." It is heard, at first, almost complete – only its last note. D, has been retained for use more examples.

The solo violin enters on the next-to-last pitch, echoing the horn, playing its own material (but still using two notes F and E, forming a semitone). The lack of the final note opens the door to the possibility that the entire theme will be heard again in a brooding way before taking the entire orchestra as the basis for an expansive cadenza.

Gidon Kremer asked Sofia Gubaidulina to write a concerto for him and she responded with a work that was commissioned by the Boston Symphony Orchestra. The commission specified that the scoring for the work could include a soprano soloist. Though the work was first performed by Gidon Kremer and the Boston Symphony in 1980, after the composer read T. S. Eliot's Four Quartets, which she found "shattering." The musical material of the poetic cycle is the main source of this work.

### (Italian Text by Detlef Gojowy)

Nata nel 1931 a Čistopol', sul Volga, Sofia Gubaidulina si diplomò al Conservatorio di Mosca nel 1963. Nikolaj Rakov e Vissarion Šebalin furono i suoi maestri di composizione. Nel 1974 entrò a far parte dell'Unione dei Compositori Sovietici. La sua musica è stata eseguita in tutto il mondo.

La personalità creativa di Sofia Gubaidulina è una delle più originali e indipendenti nell'ambito della musica contemporanea. Il suo linguaggio musicale si distingue per la profondità spirituale e la maestria tecnica.
