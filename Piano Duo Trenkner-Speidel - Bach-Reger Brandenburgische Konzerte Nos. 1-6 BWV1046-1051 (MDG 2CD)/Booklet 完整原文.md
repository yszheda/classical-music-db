## MDG - Our Sound Ideal

All MDG recordings are produced in the natural acoustics of chosen concert halls. It goes without saying that our philosophy refrains from any sort of sound-modifying manipulation with reverberation filters, etc.

We aim at genuine reproduction with precise depth gradation, original dynamics and natural tone colors. It is to us that the work acquires its musically appropriate spatial dimension and that an artistic rendition can attain to the greatest possible naturalness and vividness.

We now propose from our product lines seven new sound worlds. We have tried out many possibilities, experimented with different hall acoustics, microphone positions and all conceivable technical equipment - not to lose ourselves in mere technical details, but to capture the sound which comes closest to the ideal of naturalness. During the editing process we have also kept our goal firmly in mind: maximum clarity and transparency, a natural flow of the music, precise articulation and a lively dynamic range.

## Arrangement for Pianoforte Duet by Max Reger
### Dr. Susanne Popp

Max Reger (1873-1916) was regarded by his contemporaries as a master of variation and a representative of the extreme avant-garde. And yet he probably invested the greater part of his time in composing musical pastiche than any other turn-of-the-century artist. Reger not only picked up on traditional forms and compositional techniques and filled them out with his own highly personal, modern language. Reger the performer, editor, and arranger in fact owes an important part of his short career to the works of his predecessors, from Bach by way of Schubert, Schumann and Brahms to Röntgen and Wolf. It was of the conviction that true progress has preserved what is good. His precise knowledge of the masterpieces of the tradition, Bach arrangements figure importantly in his work not only because of their astonishing numbers but also because of their continuous demand. Reger has about 150 Bach arrangements to his credit, most of them are piano works, and most of these orchestral works (flute organ, piano, and orchestral versions) were published between 1898 and 1915. With the arrangements the composer ranges throughout his life's career, so that the plan mapped out to his own advantage marks his compositional phases from marked by a great variety of arrangements types, from editions for practical use to the freest transcriptions, from music for private performance to virtuosic concert pieces. He was only a nineteen-year-old student at the Weiden Conservatory and had just completed his first chamber compositions when he came forward with what was for the time a daring arrangement of works. One of his most original and unusual arrangements, the Orchestral Suite in G minor based on five movements from the English Suites and Partitas, was published only a few years before his death.

Reger's motivation for his lifelong occupation with Bach's music is multifaceted. Both he had great admiration and reverence for the "Cantor" of St. Thomas as the source of musical creativity and divine art (letter of May 11, 1895, to Busoni), but this was not the only reason why he kept on returning to this model and adapted himself to it. A.C. Howells sums up the attitude of the late 19th century and end of all music as a sort of perfect mono. During a period of upheaval he hoped that the Bachian spirit and work as a sort of miracle cure against the threatening disintegration of art and culture. He shared this hope with many of his contemporaries, who, from very different motives, were calling for a "return to Bach." A survey conducted by the journal Die Musik among composers and practicing musicians in 1905 is instructive for this purposes. The featured question was, what does Johann Sebastian Bach for me and what does his music mean for our time?

"Power + miraculousness + and health" are the constants linked to the Bach phenomenon, which Reger too sees as solid in the surf, a "fountain of health," and as "New Testament" of Reger's answer contains the answer to all questions about the organist's self. Bach is for the beginning and end of all music and the "Alpha and Omega" rests on him! What sets Bach marks pardon me, it supposed to mean - for our times in which we are still in the dark, not only for all those composers and musicians who, with all their faults, have understood Wagner" but also for all those composers who, with all their faults, have understood Wagner.

Reger's answer might suggest that Bach was the ideal of music for him and all others, but this was not at all the case. Reger recognizes in Bach's works dark periods of crisis and depression, suggest that Bach had a therapeutic effect for him, whether as a composer or as a player. His creative fantasy or as an inspiring example of the solution of formal and compositional problems. For him the arrangement always involved a learning process, and at the same time a source for new material and appropriated its form in order to be able to make technical studies of his own compositions as well. As a controversial innovator in order to be able to make technical studies of his own compositions as well.

The suggestion to undertake Bach transcriptions came from Henri Hinrichsen, a Leipzig music publisher. Reger had promised Hinrichsen that he would make arrangements of orchestral works by Bach. The suggestion to undertake these transcriptions came from Henri Hinrichsen, who owned the C. F. Peters Publishing Company in Leipzig. In a letter of July 1, 1904, he asked Reger whether he might want to make transcriptions of the six Brandenburg Concertos for piano four hands. "Would you be interested in arranging the six Brandenburg Concertos by Bach for the piano duet? I have an extraordinarily good edition of these works, and I think they would sell very well." Reger's reply was enthusiastic: "I will be very glad to undertake this work. It is a project that I have been thinking about for a long time."

## Jean Sebastian Bach: Les Concertos brandebourgeois BWV 1046-1051 dans un arrangement pour piano à quatre mains par Max Reger

Considéré par ses contemporains comme un révolutionnaire et un représentant de l'avant-garde radicale, Max Reger (1873-1916) est certainement l'un des compositeurs de son époque qui s'est le plus intéressé au passé musical. Non content de reprendre les formes et les techniques de composition traditionnelles en les renouvelant de façon extrêmement personnelle et originale, Reger interprète, éditeur et arrangeur, a consacré une part très importante de sa carrière - trop courte hélas! - à l'étude et à l'interprétation des œuvres de ses prédécesseurs, de Bach à Wagner et Wolf, en passant par Schubert, Schumann et Brahms.

Car il était intimement persuadé que le véritable progrès ne pouvait s'épanouir qu'en se fondant sur une assimilation parfaite des chefs-d'œuvre du passé. Sa connaissance approfondie des œuvres de Bach jouèrent à cette occasion un rôle prépondérant. Il ne s'agit pas seulement de révéler l'intérêt qu'il a toujours porté à la musique pour piano et orchestre, mais aussi à celle pour piano seul.

## Evelinde Trenkner

Evelinde Trenkner, the daughter of the composer and conductor Werner Trenkner (1902-81), was born in Weimar and studied with Walter Gieseking and Wilhelm Kempff. In 1969 she later called her "some of the most gifted young pianists" and a "former child prodigy who has matured into a musical personality in her own right." Critics have singled out "her very personal and unusual powers of spontaneous communication with audiences." She has given concerts in the most important music centers of all the continents, radio recordings in Germany and numerous TV-productions in the United States and Germany; in recent years chamber music performances with Natalia Gutman, Oleg Kagan, Yuri Bashmet, Zakhar Bron, Maxim Vengerov, and others.

Evelinde Trenkner is a professor at the Academy of Music in Lübeck, the artistic director of the Robert Schumann Music Festival, and the president of the Clara and Robert Schumann Foundation. Her festival features works from the epoch between 1870 and 1950, and especially promotes the works of the Schwencken brothers and the late nineteenth and early twentieth century. It is designed for very gifted young artists, particularly pianists.

## Sontraud Speidel

Sontraud Speidel was born in Karlsruhe and studied with Vronne Isenklöper, Branco Musikulin, Stefan Askenase, and Geza Anda. Competition prizes: Bach Prize in Washington, D.C. (only German entrant), Jackson International Competition, Cincinnati, and Ettore Pozzoli Prize in Italy. Concert tours: European countries (including the former Soviet Union), USA, Canada, Mexico, Japan, and Brazil. Her repertoire includes the so-called "master composers of the nineteenth century such as Czerny, Hummel, Farrenc, Henselt, Rheinberger, and Sterkel, and the music of contemporary composers of all six parts by Johann Nepomuk David. Sontraud Speidel is a professor at the Academy of Music in Karlsruhe, director of the "International Keyboard Academy of the California State University system."
