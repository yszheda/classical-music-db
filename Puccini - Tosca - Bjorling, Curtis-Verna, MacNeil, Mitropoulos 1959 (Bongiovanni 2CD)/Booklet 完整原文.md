## Liner Notes

After an unpleasant incident that took place at the Metropolitan, where I was the conductor, I had the opportunity to hear Jussi Björling in two performances: in *Cavalleria rusticana* and *Tosca*. In the former, his performance was good and he sang with a beautiful voice; in the latter, his singing was much better. This is to say that Jussi Björling had in him something of the great tragedian.

During the intermission scene in *Tosca*, the action moves from one act to another, and Jussi Björling sang with a sense of tragedy and a sense of the theater that was remarkable. He had the ability to project the character's inner turmoil and his voice was always beautiful, always supported by excellent breath control.

The Met's Toscas during those years were good, and Mary Curtis-Verna was no exception. She had a good voice and sang with feeling. Cornell MacNeil was an excellent Scarpia, with a voice of great beauty and a commanding stage presence.

Dimitri Mitropoulos conducted with his usual precision, verve and romantic inspiration which is so characteristic of him. He led the orchestra and the chorus with a firm hand and a clear vision of the work.

This recording is a valuable document of a great artist in one of his finest roles. Jussi Björling's Cavaradossi is a model of style and good taste, and his singing is always beautiful, always expressive, always moving.
