# Plácido Domingo: A unique phenomenon (English) - Page 10-11

It is impossible not to be fascinated by Plácido Domingo. He is a tenor and a conductor, a singing actor and an opera house administrator, a man who has made it his business to discover his successors and who, above all, is inquisitive and self-critical.

*"I know I sing a lot," Domingo once said in an interview. "but this is my best time, I can now give my best. Each evening I give my all, when it's over, it's over. When it's over, what more can I ask? longer accepts the singer Domingo."*

This interview appeared in February 1973. Today, almost thirty-five years later, Domingo was born in Madrid on 21 January 1941 but grew up in Mexico and continues to be active as a singer, conductor, and intendant. He is director of the Washington Opera and the Los Angeles Opera; he conducts in opera houses and records in studio and on concert platforms; and he still sings the tenor roles that interest him within his vocal means.

The first of the ninety principal roles that Plácido Domingo has sung onstage were *Danilo* and *Rosillon* in Lehár's *The Merry Widow*, while the first mi- nor operatic role that he recalls was Count Boris in Rigoletto, which he sang in September 1959. By the autumn of 1962 Domingo and his young wife had moved to Mexico to take up an engagement in Tel Aviv. In the summer of 1965 they moved to New York, where Domingo was now under contract to the New York City Opera.

It was here that Rolf Liebermann heard him and invited him to sing at the Hamburg State Opera, where Liebermann was currently the intendant. "In January 1967 I sang *Cavaradossi* there," recalls Domingo, "then my first Don Carlos to Vienna in the following May. I can still remember the first time when I had to sing everything for the first time in Vienna."
