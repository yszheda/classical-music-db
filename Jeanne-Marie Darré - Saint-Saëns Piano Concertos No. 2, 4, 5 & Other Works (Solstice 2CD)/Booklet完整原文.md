## Page 1 (scans/HIPPO_20241216_0001.jpg) - Contents / Biography of Darré

### Jeanne-Marie Darré

Jeanne-Marie Darré was born in Paris in 1905. She entered the Paris Conservatoire at the age of nine, and studied with Isidor Philipp and Louis Diémer. She gave her first concert at the age of eleven, with the Concerts Lamoureux Orchestra, and won her first prize at the Conservatoire at fourteen. She quickly established an international career, performing throughout Europe, the Soviet Union, and North and South America. She was particularly admired for her performances of Saint-Saëns, Fauré, Franck, Debussy, Ravel and Chopin. She taught at the Paris Conservatoire from 1958 to 1975, counting among her students many distinguished French pianists including Jean-Yves Thibaudet. She died in 1989 in Paris.

This recording of the complete Saint-Saëns piano concertos was one of her last projects. She had first performed and recorded them fifty years earlier, in the 1950s, for EMI, and those recordings have long been legendary. This new recording, made when she was ninety-seven years old (!), is a fitting testament to her lifelong love of this music.

## Page 2 (scans/HIPPO_20241216_0002.jpg) - Biography continued

In spite of her age when this recording was made, her technique remains astonishing, and her musical insight is as deep as ever. She still had the ability to make this music sound completely fresh and new, with a spontaneity that many younger pianists cannot match.

Jean-Claude Casadesus, conductor of the Orchestre National de Lille, has long been an admirer of Jeanne-Marie Darré, and it was his initiative to make this recording. The result is a historic document: a great pianist returning to music she has loved her entire life, and leaving us with a final testament.

## Page 3 (scans/HIPPO_20241216_0003.jpg) - Liner Notes: Saint-Saëns and his Piano Concertos by Jean-Philippe Moussion

### Camille Saint-Saëns and his piano concertos

Camille Saint-Saëns was born in Paris in 1835 and died in Algiers in 1921. A child prodigy, he gave his first concert at the age of ten, and by the end of his teens he had already composed his first two piano concertos and numerous other works. He was a man of extraordinary gifts: not only was he a great composer, he was also an outstanding organist, a gifted pianist, a talented painter and poet, and wrote important works of music criticism. He was one of the founders of the Société Nationale de Musique, and was a great influence on the younger generation of French composers including Debussy, Ravel, Fauré, and many others.

Saint-Saëns began his series of five piano concertos in 1858, when he was just twenty-three, and completed the fifth and last in 1896, nearly forty years later. The five concertos span almost his entire creative life, and each one is completely different from the others, showing the evolution of his style over half a century. They are all brilliantly written for the piano, and they deserve their place among the great concertos of the French repertoire.

## Page 4 (scans/HIPPO_20241216_0004.jpg) - Piano Concerto No. 2 in G minor, Op. 22

### Piano Concerto No. 2 in G minor, Op. 22 (1868)

The second piano concerto was composed in 1868, when Saint-Saëns was thirty-three. It is the most popular of the five concertos, and it's easy to understand why. From the opening bars of the Andante sostenuto, with its dramatic piano entry, through the famous scherzo-like middle movement, to the brilliant finale, it is full of memorable melodies and brilliant pianistic writing. The concerto was premiered by the composer himself at the piano, with Anton Rubinstein conducting, and it has been a favorite with pianists and audiences ever since.

The three movements:
1.  **Andante sostenuto – Allegro scherzando**: The slow and dramatic opening gives way to a lively scherzando section, with the piano flowing effortlessly from one theme to another.
2.  **Andante**: The beautiful slow movement is full of Saint-Saëns' characteristic melodic grace and elegant harmony.
3.  **Finale – Presto**: The brilliant presto finale brings the concerto to a sparkling conclusion.

## Page 5 (scans/HIPPO_20241216_0005.jpg) - Piano Concerto No. 4 in C minor, Op. 44

### Piano Concerto No. 4 in C minor, Op. 44 (1875)

The fourth concerto, composed seven years after the second, is a much more ambitious work structurally. It is in two main movements, each divided into two sections, giving it a four-movement structure overall: Allegro moderato – Andante, then Scherzo – Finale. This experimental structure shows Saint-Saëns experimenting with classical forms, and it creates a very satisfying dramatic arc. The writing is more ambitious harmonically, and the thematic material is more developed than in the earlier concertos. It has always been a favorite with more adventurous pianists, and it shows Saint-Saëns the innovator at work.

## Page 6 (scans/HIPPO_20241216_0006.jpg) - Piano Concerto No. 5 in F major "Egyptian", Op. 103

### Piano Concerto No. 5 in F major "Egyptian", Op. 103 (1896)

The fifth and final piano concerto was composed twenty-one years after the fourth, during a trip to Egypt, where Saint-Saëns loved to spend his winters. It is often called the "Egyptian" concerto because of the tropical melodies and rhythms that Saint-Saëns incorporated into the work, inspired by his time on the Nile. It is a sunnier, more relaxed work than the fourth concerto, full of beautiful decorative writing and memorable themes. The slow movement evokes the quiet of an evening by the Nile, with its gentle undulating rhythms, and the finale brings the concerto to a brilliant and cheerful conclusion. It is a fitting conclusion to Saint-Saëns' series of piano concertos, the work of a mature master looking back on his life and career with serenity.

The four movements:
1.  **Allegro maestoso**
2.  **Andante – Adagio**
3.  **Scherzo vivace**
4.  **Finale – Allegro non troppo**

## Page 7 (scans/HIPPO_20241216_0007.jpg) - 6 Études de concert (1877)

### 6 Études de concert (1877)

After completing the fourth concerto, Saint-Saëns published his six concert études in 1877. These are not just technical studies, they are full-scale character pieces, each one with its own distinct personality. They show Saint-Saëns' mastery of piano writing at its peak, and each one explores a different side of his musical personality.

1.  **No. 1 Presto d'après le "Dies Irae"**: A brilliant and dramatic opening study based on the ancient Gregorian chant.
2.  **No. 2 Andante sostenuto**: A beautiful and lyrical slow piece, full of elegant harmony.
3.  **No. 3 Scherzo diabolico**: A fiery and brilliant scherzo, one of the most difficult pieces in the repertoire.
4.  **No. 4 Moderato alla marcia**: A stately march-like study.
5.  **No. 5 Lento**: A quiet and introspective slow piece.
6.  **No. 6 En forme de tarantelle**: A brilliant tarantella to bring the set to a sparkling conclusion.
