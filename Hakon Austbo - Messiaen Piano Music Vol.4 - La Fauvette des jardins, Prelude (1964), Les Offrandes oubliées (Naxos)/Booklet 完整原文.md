### Notes on the Music

**Olivier Messiaen**

Olivier Messiaen is among the most influential figures in the music of the twentieth century. As first alarming and shocking audiences, he later won an unassailable position, respected at home in France and admired for his achievements internationally. Messiaen is a composer of intensely personal, emotional and inspired by a deep Catholic philosophy. He entered the Paris Conservatoire at 13 and two years later entered the organ class of Marcel Dupré. Maurice Emmanuel and Paul Dukas. In 1931 he was appointed organist at La Trinité and held this position until his death, particularly in the Fauré-Durpé tradition. Commissions came from the French government and in 1931 he was appointed organist at La Trinité in Paris, a position he was to hold for the next 40 years. He was to become the most influential French composer in the second half of the 20th century.

In 1940, as a prisoner-of-war in Silesia, he wrote his most famous work, the *Quatuor pour la fin du Temps* (*Quartet for the End of Time*), returning, on his release in 1942, to the Conservatoire, where he taught harmony, and then, from 1947, music analysis. He had a strong influence on the following years through teaching and composing. There followed works such as *Visions de l'Amen* and *Vingt regards sur l'enfant Jésus*.

As a composer his attention was now turned to the organ. He had written, particularly in 1930, in a number of styles, and in 1935 he wrote his first work for organ. **Les offrandes oubliées**

The orchestral *Les offrandes oubliées* was written in a burst of inspiration in a matter of days, Messiaen later suggesting that it was perhaps an opera. The orchestral work was first performed the following year. It is in the form of a triptych, with the outer sections fast and the central section slow. It is finally the offer of salvation through the Eucharist which gives it its structure. The work opens with a passage marked *Pesant* (heavy) which introduces the theme of the Cross, followed by a section marked *Moins vif* (less lively) with a theme of sin, sorrowful, deeply sad. The melody suddenly turns into a joyful dance, marked *Très vif, allègre* (very lively, cheerful). The work concludes with a return to the opening material.

**Fantaisie Burlesque**

*Fantaisie burlesque* was written in 1932 and is dedicated to Madame Yvonne Loriod, de la Kreule. It is a work of great virtuosity, demonstrating Messiaen's interest in bird-song and his fascination with rhythm. The piece is characterized by its playful and whimsical nature, with sudden changes in tempo and dynamics.

**Pièce pour le tombeau de Paul Dukas**

*Pièce pour le tombeau de Paul Dukas* was written in 1935 as a tribute to Messiaen's teacher Paul Dukas, who died in that year. The piece is a contemplative and mournful work, reflecting Messiaen's deep respect for his mentor. It features a slow, meditative tempo and rich harmonic language.

**Rondeau**

The *Rondeau* of 1943 belongs to a period in the life of Messiaen when he was writing for the organ. He had returned to the Conservatoire as a student of harmony and analysis. The piece is based on a medieval rondeau form, with a recurring theme that returns in various guises throughout the work.

**Prélude (1964)**

The *Prélude* of 1964 was first published in a collection of organ pieces compiled by the American organist and composer Jean Langlais. This is the world première recording of the piece, which was transcribed for piano by Messiaen himself. The piece is a short but intense work, showcasing Messiaen's mature harmonic language.

**La fauvette des jardins**

*La fauvette des jardins* (The Garden Warbler) was composed in 1970 and is one of Messiaen's most important late piano works. Like the earlier *Catalogue d'oiseaux*, it is based on bird-song, but here the focus is on a single species, the garden warbler. The work is in three movements, each depicting different aspects of the bird's habitat and song. The first movement presents the warbler in its garden setting, surrounded by other birds. The second movement depicts the bird by a green and violet lake, with reflections in the water. The third movement shows the warbler over undulating water, with the sound of water lapping against the shore.

The Garden Warbler sings and sings again. A new contrast: the flight of the Black Kite and the sudden calm of the great lake. The Kite climbs and descends, describing great spirals in the sky, while the circles of its flight inscribe in the turns of its tail helping the movement of its wings, until it finally comes to rest in a tree. The Garden Warbler begins again, and warmth. There are the most beautiful hours of summer: the monotone of the Great Screech Owl on the moor surface, the golden light and the golden song of the Blackbird. All the song birds are heard in shades of blue: peacock blue, azure, sapphire. The silence is only broken by the Chaffinches.

### About the Artist

**Håkon Austbø**

Of Norwegian origin, the pianist Håkon Austbø performed extensively in his own country before continuing his studies at the Paris Conservatoire and Ecole Normale de Musique. In 1970 he won the Concours National de la Guilde Française des Artistes Solistes and in 1971 drew international attention with the unanimous award of first prize at the Olivier Messiaen Competition for Contemporary Music in Royan. He furthered his studies at the New York Juilliard School, the Munich Staatliche Hochschule für Musik and in London. He won awards in Munich in 1974 and at the Paris Ravel Competition in 1975 and in the same year as member of the Trio du Nord at the UNESCO Rostrum in Bratislava. Håkon Austbø is at present based in the Netherlands and enjoys an active concert career throughout Europe and in North America.
