## LEHÁR & TAUBER

It was once said that Richard Tauber sang *You are my Heart's Delight* 5000 times; that is as it may, forty years on the song remains synonymous with Tauber, and the records of this great artist show that even his original 78 rpm discs are still collected. No other excerpts from operettas and no tenor in particular has ever enjoyed such an enormous success.

The reasons for this are not difficult to understand. Tauber had a very thorough musical education, which gave the same preparation and attention to a popular operetta song as to an operatic aria; he was an extremely careful artist, but was never criticized by the musical press for "washing his voice on operetta and second rate material". Such comments would have made no difference to his public who loved him.

Richard Tauber was born in Linz on 16th May 1891 at a small inn known locally as "Zum Neustadt". His father was a Catholic mother and a Jewish father. Although the latter had hopes for Richard to become a conductor, the boy had a burning ambition to sing and, following a period of study at Base Conservatoire, made his professional debut in 1911 in *Tannhäuser* at the Neustadt Theatre in Chemnitz. This led to a two-year contract with the Dresden Opera, followed by engagements at many European centres.

The meeting with Franz Lehar in 1923 was of course a turning point in the careers of both men. Lehar, born in Komarom, Hungary, on 30th April 1870, the son of a regimental bandmaster, had achieved early major successes with *The Merry Widow*, *The Count of Luxembourg*, and *Gypsy Love* in the early years of the 20th century, but between 1912 and the meeting with Tauber he had experienced only moderate success with succeeding operettas. In 1923, *Frasquita* opened at the Theater an der Wien, the production ran into difficulties and Tauber was asked to take over the lead. In spite of being under contract to the Staatsoper, he agreed to sing the part as soon as a convenient agreement could be reached. The celebrated tenor enormously. His co-star was the Czech soprano Jarmila Novotna, a beautiful girl with a beautiful voice, and success seemed inevitable; problems galore were round the corner, however.

Since the work was based on an Italian subject, Lehar thought that Jarmila would appear in this dedication, but the Italian dictator banned the score with a warning stating that no officer of his army would run away with a married woman!

Before the premiere, the Directors of the Staatsoper had foreseen all about the idea of a Lehar operetta staged in sacred portals. Furthermore, Lehar had made his orchestration so heavy that many soloists found his music too well. The work was only a moderate success at the time, in spite of Tauber and Novotna and the splendid music.

*Giuditta* was Lehar's last work for the stage. It has never been produced professionally in Britain, although BBC Radio during the 1960s presented a complete studio performance, conducted by Vilem Tausky.

## The Land of Smiles

The serenade from this operetta enjoyed a tremendous success, and gave Lehar the inspiration he needed.

There was the voice that he had been searching for - the voice that was to inspire such melodies as *O Maiden, my Maiden*, *Girls were made for love and kiss*, and *You are my Heart's Delight*, that was to conquer the world, that was to sing the world, *You are my Heart's Delight*.

*The Land of Smiles* was in fact a reworking of an earlier unsuccessful operetta entitled *The Yellow Jacket*. It was produced in Vienna in 1925. With a revamped libretto, this was remodelled as the Metropolitan Opera in Berlin on 10th October 1929, with Tauber as Sou-Chong and Vera Schwarz as Lisa. It was a great success, and the plot is a relatively simple one.

In 1910 Lisa, the beautiful daughter of a Vienna factory owner, is engaged to Gustl, one of her father's staff. It is introduced to the Chinese diplomat, Prince Sou-Chong. On an embassy ball in Vienna, and the two fall madly in love. Soon however the Prince is recalled to China to take up the Governorship of a Province. Lisa is prepared to give up everything to be with him.

On their arrival in Peking, Sou is informed that in order to fulfil his obligations, and in accordance with traditional Chinese custom, he must marry a Chinese princess of his rank and standing. He must therefore select from the three available selected Manchu maidens as soon as possible.

Lisa is shattered, for we welcome in China has not been a warm one. None of the Prince's family, with the exception of Sou's sister Mi, is prepared to accept Lisa; indeed, the Prince's uncle is very angry with Mi because she had shown some kindness to Lisa.

Disillusioned, Lisa decides to return to Vienna. Sou is very unhappy, but he realises the impossibility of the situation and reluctantly agrees to let her go. The operetta ends on this sad note.

The plot and circumstances surrounding the operetta are similar. Lehar had long wanted to compose a more serious work for Tauber, one more worthy of some Vienna State Opera rather than the smaller theatres where the majority of his previous works had been staged.

## Giuditta

Within a few years political events were to take a sinister turn. Tauber left Austria, soon to settle in London, take British citizenship, and become the most popular tenor of his time.

Other celebrated operetta composers (notably Kalmán and Oscar Straus) as well as many famous performing musicians, especially those with Jewish background, were leaving their homelands to settle in the United States during the 1930s. Lehar, faced with the problem that his wife was Jewish, decided to stay on, saying that he was too old to uproot himself and emigrate to a foreign land.

He may have believed that because Hitler admired many of his operettas, particularly *The Merry Widow*, for which Lehar composed a new overture dedicated to Hitler, that no harm would come to him or his wife. But - the same, in spite of having lived alone at the outset, he always lived with the fear of his wife being taken from him.

During the war years there were a number of revivals of Lehár operettas, and the composer made some recordings. Towards the end of the war, he decided to settle in Switzerland.

In 1946 Tauber was on a visit to Switzerland, and a reunion was arranged for the two old friends. A broadcast on Radio Beromünster was arranged, in which Lehar conducted the Radio Orchestra, and Tauber sang the arias for which he was especially famous - *You are my Heart's Delight* - sung from cover.

Both of these great musicians died in 1948. Tauber first, of cancer, on 8th January, and Lehar on 24th October. The loss to the world of music was felt for a short space of time was disastrous.

Lehár was one of the last great composers of Viennese operetta in the tradition of Johann Strauss Jnr., Suppé, Millöcker, etc., having the same gift of melody and mastery of orchestration which were as highly thought of as those of Elgar or Vaughan Williams.

Tauber, the great "Tenor of the operetta", was mourned world-wide. He left an enormous series of recordings on the Odeon and Parlophone labels, and ranks as one of the most prolific recording artists of all time.
