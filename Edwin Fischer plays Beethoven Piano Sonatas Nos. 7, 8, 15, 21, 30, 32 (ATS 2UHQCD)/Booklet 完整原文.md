## Liner Notes by Riccardo Risaliti
*(first appearance for ARKADIA)*

Fischer (Swiss, take note) had begun with Bach and Mozart, before the "damnation," and then made the Classic-Romantic German culture his own - faithful to Classicism as discipline, swept up by Romanticism by temperament. "In the work of art," Novalis had written, "Chaos must shine through the scheme of order." There is no need to point out that such a performer (they used to say: Bach in his fingers, Mozart in his heart, and Beethoven in his brain) was naturally the perfect interpreter of Beethoven, from whom he simply and naturally, and with great formal sense, grasped the plasticity, the force and the sensibility, the tenderness, and the sense of human tragedy. His choice was not an aesthetic position, nor was it intellectual, or even opportunistic - after D'Albert and Lamond, Schnabel was the only point of reference in the Beethoven camp. Fischer was one of those interpreters who searched for the truth of every psychological moment in Beethoven. To him, the single movements of a sonata like the Op. 10, n. 3, were variously "pianistic animation," "sense of freedom," "humourism," as he aptly writes of the Largo in effect. This is music that tends towards joy, despite the dark forebodings of the Largo e mesto. And the Op. 28? It is another sunny moment, consolatory, in which a pointed sense of piano colour creates a naturalistic sonority that anticipates the supernatural pianissimi of the Op. 111.

"Temperament," to this ideal, He has to. "Beethoven," he writes in his book, "appeals to the chorus of boys," to the will, to the structural needs of the listener." As a result, "by way of strict mental discipline, he arrives at structure, certainty, and clarity. Eliminating, abbreviating, simplifying, ennobling... his true moral greatness is in knowing how to give things up, to dominate his instinct." And Fischer understood Beethoven's moral greatness as did few others, which all of which his performances testify, from the pathos of the "Pathétique" Sonata to the brilliant playing of the "Waldstein" to the luminous grace of the Major Sonata, Op. 109.

In the years immediately following the Second World War, one of the most fascinating literary interpretations of Beethoven's Sonata, Op. 111, was certainly that which can be read in the eighth chapter of Thomas Mann's Doctor Faustus, that scholarly, imaginative, extrovert analysis-performance which takes place in front of the leading intellects, headed by Professor Kretzschmar. On the purely musical side, however, the most fascinating - as well as the most famous - was that of Edwin Fischer, as can be ascertained in this performance, which followed the publication of Mann's novel by a few years. It's an interpretation which, like Kretzschmar's before it, passes over the "Worldly" weight of the initial movement with fluent ease to put the emphasis on the profoundly human content of the Arietta. The idea of the arietta, a theme "destined to undergo adventures and vicissitudes for which in its idyllic character it seems at first apparently born." Thus, Kretzschmar-Mann. Fischer, like Thomas Mann, was also an interpreter capable of rendering with the most convincing luminosity the highest point of German Classic sonata and also the apocalyptic visions of the collapse of a civilization with the most cynical clarity (Brahms' Second Concerto): Fischer as Faust.

Edwin Fischer was born into the magnificent generation of the 1880s. Swiss, as were Cortot and Rudolf Ganz (who were their separate ways, however, one towards Paris, the other towards America), the young Edwin grew up and had his first success in Berlin as the student of Liszt's students: the great D'Albert and Martin Krause, whose school also produced Arrau. More than an interpreter at the piano, he was also a conductor (Bach!), and a tireless keyholder and a teacher (his aim, according to Brendel, was "to lift the student above himself") Fischer made extracts, bits of his courses and his musical ideas in the Musikalische Betrachtungen and in his notes on the Beethoven sonatas - descriptive, psychological, atmospheric analyses of Beethoven's art, made at various times and in various states of mind, and centered on images, comparisons, adjectives. The most evocative characteristic of Fischer's manner, of his attitude towards the music, has been defined by Brendel as Zaubermacht innerer Friede (the magic power of inner peace) and explained with the dichotomy kindlich (childish) and meisterlich (masterly), present in Fischer's own personality. A certain childishness arises out of the naturalness, the spontaneity, the joy of playing and continually discovering, out of the sense of wonder and discovery; his sense of the measure, of the balance, of the qualities, the proportioning of emotion, the control of sound, the equilibrium and architecture. Fischer was not what is meant today by a refined artist: in affectation, the hedonism for its own sake, the music loses substance. Truth, the sense of the music, of the message, of the mission, this was the basis of all his research. Truth, and expressiveness: Fischer's art is especially expressive; it emerges from a particular sonority which is completely personal. A Romantic Classicist by way of temperament and education, like Goethe, a good son of German Neoclassicism of the end of the 19th century. He understands the romantic importance of sound, which he uses to filter the classic ideal through a romantic Weltanschauung. He subordinates his innate generosity, his "Temperament," to this ideal.

## エドウィン・フィッシャー／ベートーヴェン
### リカルド・リザルティ 執筆 (初出：ARKADIA 社)

ベートーヴェンは、論理的な感覚と、意志と、聴き手の構造的な欲求に訴えかけていくと彼は著書の中で書いている。その結果、厳しい精神修養によって、彼は構造の一律性、有機的発展、法と普遍性とを到達し、高揚化へと到達する。こうして、彼の精神的な偉大さは、物事をあるがまま、本能を支配する方法を知っていることである。それは、彼のすべての演奏が証明している。

（悲怆）ソナタの悲哀、《ワルトシュタイン》の輝かしい演奏、《長調ソナタ》作品 109 の光彩輝く優美さなど、この両者でかつて典型と言えよう。

第二次世界大戦直後、ベートーヴェンのソナタ作品 111 に対する最も魅惑的な文学的解釈のひとつは、トーマス・マンの『ファウストス博士』第 8 章に収められている。ケルツシュマー教授の演奏会における分析的で、哲学的で想像力に富んだ、外向的分析パフォーマンスで読むことができるものであることは確かである。しかし、純粋に音楽の側面で、この作品を最も魅力的に解釈したのは、エドウィン・フィッシャーであった。この解釈は、それ以前のマンのデビューと同年に、最初の奏者の「音楽的な重さ」を流畅に乗り越え、アリエッタの魔法のように変奏する劇的な重点を置いたもので、「その牧歌的な様式においては、明らかにされはしないが、ひたむきな冒険と波乱を経験する運命の主題」なのだ。こうして、マンは分析的な視点から彼を位置づけ、彼の名を歴史に刻みつけたのである。

フィッシャーは、1880 年代の音楽家が華やかなりし時代に生まれた。コルトーやドルフ・ガンツと同じスイス人でしたが、一人はパリへ、もう一人はアメリカへと別々の道を歩んだ。若きエドウィンはベルリンで育ち、リストの弟子である偉大なダルベールや、クラウゼを師として受け、マティン・クラウスが主宰した最初のキャリアを始めたのである。フィッシャーは、ピアノを弾くだけでなく、指揮者（バッハやモーツァルト）を鍵盤で弾きながら）、教授（弟子のブレンデルによれば、彼の目的は「生徒を自分より上に引き上げること」）でもあった。フィッシャーは、彼の散文や音楽的アイデアの断片を『ムジカリーシェ・ Betrachten』ベートーヴェンのソナタのノーツに残している。フィッシャーの芸術に対する態度の最も明白な特徴は、ブレンデルによって Zaubermacht innerer Friede（内なる平和の魔力）と定義され、フィッシャー自身の性格にある Kindlich（子供っぽさ）と Meisterlich（達観した）という二項対立で説明されている。ある種の子供っぽさは、自然さ、自発性、演奏する喜び、絶えず発見する喜び、驚きや感動から生じるものである。ある種の量の達成は、支配的な質感、感情のバランス、音のコントロール、平衡感覚、建築に見られるものである。フィッシャーは、今日の洗練された芸術家ではない。人気が、それ自身のための快楽主義によって、音楽の本質を見失う。真実、そして表現力。フィッシャーの芸術は、特に表現力が、特に彼の演奏様式の基礎であった。真実、そして表現力。フィッシャーの芸術は、特に表現力が、彼の演奏様式の基礎であった。
