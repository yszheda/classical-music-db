### IGOR NIKONOVICH

Igor Nikonovich was born in St. Petersburg (then Leningrad) in 1958. He studied at the Leningrad Conservatory with Pavel Serebryakov and later with Dmitri Bashkirov at the Moscow Conservatory. He won prizes at the Schubert Competition in Dortmund and the Maria Canals Competition in Barcelona, and has performed widely throughout Russia and Europe. He is currently based in St. Petersburg, where he teaches at the St. Petersburg Conservatory. He has specialized in Russian piano music, particularly Scriabin, and his recordings have been acclaimed for their penetrating insight into this distinctive repertoire.

### ALEXANDER SCRIABIN (1872-1915)

Alexander Scriabin was one of the most revolutionary and distinctive Russian composers of the early twentieth century. His piano music explores a completely unique harmonic and melodic language, moving from the richly romantic early works through the increasingly chromatic and mystical works of his later period, to the edge of atonality in his final compositions. This album collects a selection of rare early works and fragmentary pieces that are not often recorded, showing the development of Scriabin's unique musical language from early in his career.

### Rare Scriabin: The Early Works and Unfinished Fragments

This collection brings together a selection of rare piano works by Alexander Scriabin, many of them early works from the 1880s and 1890s, some of them unfinished fragments that were published later. These pieces show the young Scriabin developing his unique harmonic and melodic voice, experimenting with the language that would come to define his later music. We can already hear the harmonic adventurousness, the love of rich sonorities, the mystical atmosphere that would characterize his later music. Even these early pieces show the distinctive Scriabin sound world that makes his music so unique and immediately recognizable.

The major work on this recording is the *Sonata-Fantasy* in G-sharp minor Op. 19, one of Scriabin's most intriguing works from his middle period, composed in 1912-13 when he was moving towards his late mystical style. It's a single-movement sonata that explores a very personal harmonic and expressive world, and it's rarely performed and recorded. This recording by Igor Nikonovich presents these rare works in performances that capture their unique character and atmosphere.

### Notes on the Composer

Alexander Scriabin was born in Moscow in 1872. He studied at the Moscow Conservatory with Sergei Taneyev and Anton Arensky, and quickly established a reputation as a composer and pianist with a very personal style. His early works are clearly rooted in the romantic tradition of Chopin and Liszt, but he increasingly developed his own unique harmonic language, moving towards increasing chromaticism and mysticism, eventually moving close to atonality in his last works before his early death in 1915 at the age of 43. His music has always had a devoted following among pianists and listeners who are drawn to its unique combination of harmonic innovation, poetic intensity and mystical vision.

Igor Nikonovich is one of the most prominent contemporary Russian interpreters of Scriabin, and he brings a deep understanding and insight to this music, which allows the unique character of these rare pieces to emerge.
