## HEITOR VILLA-LOBOS (1887-1959)

Heitor Villa-Lobos was born in Rio de Janeiro, just one year before the "Golden Law" abolished slavery. This reform was the most significant single social change Brazil had experienced. It led to an enormous cultural development and prosperity for the country, which is reflected in Villa-Lobos' life and work. He remains one of the most colourful figures in twentieth-century music and is truly the Brazilian composer, who put his country on the international map, transforming the musical life of his homeland.

Brazilian culture, founded on a mix of Portuguese, African and Amerindian traditions, fascinated him. His numerous trips to Brazil's more inaccessible regions, to the north-eastern states and the Amazon interior brought him firsthand acquaintance with the musical heritage of the rural areas.

The primitive, often joyful ambience of the native dance traditions are present in intricate cross rhythms and ostinato patterns. Together with lyrical cantilenas, reminding the tradition of the "modinha", a slow minuet-mood song form, originating from Portugal, we can sense the Latin American passionate approach to life in all its intensity.

Latin American folk music is a result of native inspiration though it is also a consequence of European culture, in particular from France and Spain. H.Villa-Lobos had an intuitive approach to music which drove him through lots of dry and stuffy Conservatory training far from his mind. "One foot in the Academy and you are changed for the worst" he used to say. In fact he was practically a self-taught musician. Beside private studies with his father, which was a highly cultivated amateur, he learned some valuable lessons by Darius Milhaud who visited Brazil in 1918. During his stay in Paris (1923-1930) H.Villa-Lobos acquainted himself with the music of Debussy, Ravel and the group of French composers, called "Les Six", who shared the aesthetic ideals of Eric Satie and Jean Cocteau. The result of H.Villa-Lobos' private studies was an enormous production of compositions of European music, but with a strong Brazilian accent.

In Europe H.Villa-Lobos is best known for his compositions for guitar and his "choros" for various vocal and instrumental combinations, especially "Bachiana Brasileira" nr.5 for soprano and eight celli. In addition he wrote music for a wide range of different instruments, symphonies and operas. This CD presents a selection of his most appreciated works for piano solo.

## CICLO BRASILEIRO / BRAZILIAN CYCLE

The suite Ciclo Brasileiro was composed in 1936 and its four movements belong to H.Villa-Lobos masterpieces for piano.

In **Impressões Seresteiras** H. Villa-Lobos invites us to the same region in Northern Brazil, telling about the impressions of a minstrel. The different parts of this composition are bound together by a charming little waltz. The brilliant **Dansa do Indio Branco** alludes to Brazil's history and cultural diversity.

At the beginning one hears the drums of the Indians in the jungle. While the dance rhythm is constant, a choral of the Portuguese colonists arises: the Dance of the White Indian. **O Plantio do Caboclo** describes peoples hard life in the barren landscape of North East Brazil. While the right hands ostinato illustrates an inconsolable monotony and loneliness increases the cantilena in the left hand from serenity to a passionate climax just to return back to calmness.

A fête, a festival on the countryside is the subject of **Festa no Sertão**. Here we find an atmosphere of intense joy of life, expressed by Brazilian native dance-rhythms and ostinato patterns. The elegiac middle part creates a strong contrast. This movement you will find at the end of this CD (track 16).

## AS TRÊS MARIAS / THE THREE MARYS

Like in A Prole do Bebê, H.Villa-Lobos shows in **As Três Marias** that he is a master of smaller forms. These three little pieces, composed in 1939, are written on a Brazilian children's story:

"Once there were three little girls, the "Three Marys of Earth", who romped and played in the countryside of Brazil. They were always cheerful and the best of friends. Smilingly they travelled all the paths of life together.

That this trinity might serve as a perpetual symbol of the union of humanity, fate has preserved them as eternal stars in the heavens to illuminate the path for the other children of Earth." (H.Villa-Lobos)

## ALMA BRASILEIRA THE BRAZILIAN SOUL, CHOROS, NO.5

With his choros H.Villa-Lobos created a new form of musical composition in which the synthesis of different Brazilian modalities was its main feature. The character of the music is often rhapsodic and improvising. In **Alma Brasileira**, written in 1925, we can sense the extremes in Brazilian temperament. The basically mysterious doleful character gets interrupted by a sudden part of energetic violence.

---

## A PROLE DO BEBÊ / THE BABY'S FAMILY-SUITE NO.1

Between 1918 and 1921 H.Villa-Lobos wrote two suites for piano, entitled **A Prole do Bebê** (The Baby's Family). Brazilian nursery melodies are used in the first suite, describing with an amazing empathy a child's collection of dolls. The musical language has an impressionistic flavour and is influenced by Cl.Debussy, D.Milhaud and M.Ravel. This first suite has become H.Villa-Lobos' best known piano composition, even if it was no success when his friend Artur Rubinstein played this music for the first time at Rio de Janeiro in 1922. People wanted some "real" Brazilian music...

## GISELA HERB

Gisela Herb was born and grew up in Munich, Germany. Since 1982 she has lived in Stavanger, Norway, where among other things she teaches at Stavanger College, School of Arts Education. Her debut concert took place in Oslo, where she was described as "an exceptional piano talent".

Gisela Herb has been soloist with orchestras from Norway, Germany, Russia and Brazil, she has performed on European and Latin-American radio and television and has given recitals in Scandinavia, Europe, Russia and Latin-America. In 2001 she started playing piano-duo with Naoko Subayama Aarnio from Helsinki. During The International Piano-Duo Festival in Genova in 2002 they performed among others Olivier Messiaen's masterpiece Visions de l'Amen ("a very special presentation, holy and with profundity, with high standard concerning precision and the quality of sound").

In recent years Gisela Herb has concentrated on the piano music from Latin-America. Her first solo CD (1996) was dedicated to the Argentinian composer Alberto Ginastera. "Gisela Herb's talent, temperament and intuition let her deeply captivate the work of A.Ginastera" (Buenos Aires). Thanks to various grants from the Norwegian Government and the local Rogaland county council she continued her study trips to Latin America. As a result she presents now a new solo-CD from this continent, with piano music from Brazil's famous composer Heitor Villa-Lobos.
