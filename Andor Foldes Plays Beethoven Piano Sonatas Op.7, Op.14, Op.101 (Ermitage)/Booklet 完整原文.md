## ANDOR FOLDES PLAYS BEETHOVEN PIANO SONATAS
### LUDWIG VAN BEETHOVEN (1770-1827)

---

## Sonata op. 14 n.° 1
**Allegro molto e sempre delicato**
**Allegretto; marcia alla marcia del 2. tempo**
**Allegretto (Il. il Coda)**

After Beethoven composed his three first piano sonatas for the first time (Op. 7), he returned to the more intimate tone of the first two sonatas. These two Opus 14 sonatas are in their development almost entirely dependent on the sonata form; they are the least pianistic, almost orchestral in conception. It is characteristic of the first variation of this form that Beethoven presents it almost exclusively in the development of the innovation - drive: the theme of the first theme, then the theme of the second, and finally the coda (Kunst). What follows is a construction that has nothing surprising in its formal procedure: 14-16 bars of variation for the first theme and 16 for the second.

It is not part of Beethoven's characteristics in the first 14 of his 32 piano sonatas. The two sonatas Op. 14 have something of chamber music character; they are not so much solo works but rather like a string quartet in which all voices participate equally. This is especially true of the first sonata, which is often compared to a string quartet. The second theme in particular shows this character: it is a true dialogue between the voices.

The minuet-trio of the second movement is almost entirely derived from the first theme. The trio, which is in the parallel key, is a true masterpiece of counterpoint. The finale is a rondo-sonata form with a coda that brings back the first theme.

**Sonata op. 14 n.° 2**
**Allegro**
**Andante**
**Scherzo: Assai allegro**

The second sonata of Op. 14 is in G major. The first movement is in sonata form. The second movement is a set of variations in C major (the subdominant). The third movement is a scherzo in G major.

The first movement is characterized by its light, graceful character. The second movement is a set of variations on a simple, song-like theme. The variations become increasingly elaborate, with figuration and ornamentation. The third movement is a lively scherzo with a contrasting trio section.

Both sonatas were published in 1799 by Mollo & Co. in Vienna. They were dedicated to Baroness Josefa von Braun, one of Beethoven's most important patrons in Vienna.

## Sonata op. 7
**Allegro molto e con brio**
**Largo con gran espressione**
**Allegro**
**Rondo (Poco allegretto e grazioso)**

The Sonata Op. 7 is one of Beethoven's longest piano sonatas. It was composed in 1796-1797 and published in 1797 by Artaria in Vienna. It was dedicated to Countess Babette von Keglevich, a piano student of Beethoven.

The first movement is in sonata form with a slow introduction. The second movement is a lyrical Largo in A flat major. The third movement is a Menuetto with a Trio. The finale is a Rondo in sonata form.

This sonata shows Beethoven's mastery of the classical form while also pointing toward his more personal style. The work is characterized by its emotional depth and technical demands.

## Sonata op. 101
**Allegretto, ma non troppo**
**Vivace alla marcia**
**Adagio, ma non troppo, con affetto**
**Allegro**

The Sonata Op. 101 in A major was composed in 1816 and published in 1817. It is the first of Beethoven's "late" piano sonatas. It was dedicated to Baroness Dorothea von Ertmann, one of Beethoven's most talented pupils.

The first movement is a lyrical, intimate Allegretto. The second movement is a march-like Vivace. The third movement is a short, contemplative Adagio that leads directly into the finale. The finale is a fugue-like Allegro that demonstrates Beethoven's contrapuntal mastery.

This sonata marks the beginning of Beethoven's late period, characterized by increased complexity, introspection, and formal innovation.

## ANDOR FOLDES

The pianist Andor Foldes (1913-1992), one of the most respected artists of his generation, was celebrated in the musical centers of the world: Paris, London, Brussels, Amsterdam, Buenos Aires, Zurich, Barcelona, Buenos Aires, Tokyo. Born in Budapest, he began piano studies at the age of eight, giving a concert with the Mozarteum Orchestra in Salzburg at thirteen. He studied with Ernst von Dohnányi at the Franz Liszt Academy in Budapest. After winning first prize at the International Franz Liszt Piano Competition, he began an international career.

After the success of his American debut in New York, he decided to settle in the United States. He gave his first post-war European tour in 1949. His recordings of Bartók's complete piano works won the Grand Prix du Disque. He has published a book "Wege zum Klavier" (Paths to the Piano).

Since 1961, he has lived in Herrliberg near Zurich with his wife, the writer Lili Foldes.
