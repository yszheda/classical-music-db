## Berg: „Lulu"

Der Entschluß, Frank Wedekinds *Lulu-Tragödie* zu vertonen, steht seinem Ursprung nach in merkwürdiger Analogie zur Wahl des Wedekind-Fragments *Bacchae* Male war es eine Schauspielaufführung, die den Stoff dem Künstler greifbar vor Augen stellte. Der Einbruch des von Karl Kraus am 29. Juni 1905 in Wien veranstalteten Uraufführung der *Büchse der Pandora* wurde noch verstärkt durch die Rede, mit der Kraus die Premiere einleitete.

Während aber beim *Wozzeck* die Schauspielvorführung unmittelbar die Arbeit an der Texteinrichtung und Vertonung auslöste, vergingen bei *Lulu* mehr als zwanzig Jahre, in welchen Berg sich mit den verschiedenenartigen Opernstoffen befaßte.

Nach der Komposition des Werkes wurde er eine Zeitlang am stärksten von Gerhard Hauptmanns Geschichte *Und Pippa tanzt!* angezogen, bis er sich im Frühjahr 1928, bestärkt durch Freudenserst, endgültig für die Wedekindsche Tragödie entschied. Mit der Einrichtung des Textes war Berg bis zum Frühjahr 1929 fertig, nach kurzer Unterbrechung durch die Komposition der *Weinarie* arbeitete er fast ununterbrochen an der *Lulu*, die er im April 1934 (Particell) vollendete; die Instrumentation beschäftigte ihn dann bis zu seinem Tode.

## Die Handlung

**COMPACT DISC 1**

Im **Prolog** führt der Tierbändiger in der Arena seine Bestien vor, das „wahre, das sanfte, wildumwitterte" Geschöpf durch das menschliche Genre …

**Erster Akt**

Lulu, einst Trinkerjungen Jugend Dr. Schöns gewesen, ist von ihm an einen alternden Medizinalrat verheiratet worden. Als sie der Maler, der sie leidenschaftlich bestürmt, in ihrem Boudoir überrascht, wird sie vom Medizinalrat überrascht. Er bricht, vom Schlag getroffen, zusammen. Lulu wird die Frau des Malers, der vom Koleriker zum Geschäftsmann avanciert und, von Dr. Schön finanziert, eine glänzende Karriere beginnt. Der Arzt, den Lulu zu zähmen, indem er ihr ein Leben in Luxus ermöglicht. Doch Lulu weigert sich, ihn frei zu geben, und zwingt ihn, sie von seiner Frau scheiden zu lassen. Als der Maler durch Dr. Schöns Enthüllungen in der Vergangenheit seiner Frau erfährt, begeht er Selbstmord. Der Maler tot, Lulu aber bleibt. Sie heiratet den Dr. Schön.

**COMPACT DISC 2**

Lulu feiert in Gegenwart Alwas, des Sohnes von Dr. Schön, einen Triumph als Tänzerin. Auch Alwa gerät in Bann. Tumult im Theater: Lulu spielt einen Ohnmachtsanfall vor, der sich in Begleitung seiner Braut im Zuschauerraum entdeckt.

Dr. Schön sitzt in der Garderobe und Lulu zwingt ihn, einen Abschiedsbrief an seine Braut zu schreiben, andernfalls würde sie ihn mit dem Prinzen aus Afrika für immer verschwinden.

**Zweiter Akt**

Dr. Schön ist verheiratet. Er ist der Mann des Jahres, den sie wirklich liebt. Merkwürdige Gestalten tauchen in ihrem Salon zu. Leben: Der Bettler Schigolch, der sich als Lulus Vater ausgibt, der Tierbändiger Rodrigo, ein Gymnasiast und Alwa. Lulu, von all diesen Männern fasziniert. Dr. Schön vermag dieses Leben kaum noch zu ertragen, hat aber die Kraft nicht, sich aus ihm zu befreien. Als er erkennt, daß auch sein Sohn Alwa in Lulu verliebt ist, drückt er Lulu eine Pistole in die Hand, damit sie sich töte. Erst verständnislos, dann entsetzt, nimmt sie den Revolver. Mit der Waffe in der Hand, zehn Jahren Gefängnis verurteilt, erkrankt sie an Cholera. In ihrer unseligen Liebe zu Dr. Schön hat sie ihren eigenen Ruin und ihre Befreiung: In der Krankenbaracke tauschen sie mit Gräfin Geschwitz die Kleider. Schigolchs kommt Lulu aus dem Gefängnis zurück. Entsetzt über ihr Aussehen stürmt der Tierbändiger davon, nachdem er seinen Plan, Lulu zu seiner Partnerin im Zirkus zu machen, gescheitert sieht. Alwa, Lulu jetzt leidenschaftlich verfallen, flieht mit ihr ins Ausland.

**Dritter Akt**

Verfolgt von Erpressern und Denunzianten, mußte Lulu von Paris nach London fliehen. In einer armseligen Dachkammer haust sie mit Alwa und Schigolch. Um sich und die beiden Männer durchzubringen, ist Lulu zum Straßenmädchen geworden. Jack der Ripper endet ihr Leben. Vergebens sucht die Gräfin Geschwitz, die ihr nach London gefolgt war, sie zu retten. Auch sie fällt dem Lustmörder zum Opfer. So verlöscht der „glühende Feuerball Lulu, der alles, was mit ihm in Berührung kommt, versengt, nachdem alles Leben um ihn erloschen oder erlöschend dahindämmert".

## Anmerkung

Alban Berg konnte die Instrumentation der Oper nicht vollenden. Nachdem er das Particell des ganzen Werkes niedergeschrieben hatte, hat er die beiden ersten Akte komplett und 268 Takte der ersten Szene des dritten Aktes fortlaufend instrumentiert. Das Fragment der ersten Szene des dritten Aktes ist in dieser Wiedergabe der Oper nicht enthalten. Als Musik des dritten Aktes erklingen diejenigen Teile, die Alban Berg selbst in die *Lulu-Suite* aufgenommen und zu diesem Zweck vorweg instrumentiert hatte. Der Schluß der *Lulu-Suite* stimmt mit dem Schluß des dritten Aktes überein. Die gesprochenen Texte wurden von Günther Rennert aus Wedekinds *Büchse der Pandora* ausgewählt, formuliert und melodramatisch in den Ablauf der Musik eingefügt.

## Berg: "Lulu"

There is a curious analogy between the way Alban Berg decided to set to music Frank Wedekind's tragedy *Lulu* and how he chose *Wozzeck*: each time it was a theater performance that opened the composer's eyes to the works. Moreover, the impression made by the world premiere of *Die Büchse der Pandora* (*Pandora's Box*) in Vienna on 29 May 1905 was heightened by the speech with which Karl Kraus, the organizer of the performance, introduced the premiere.

But whereas the performance of *Wozzeck* immediately triggered Berg's adaptation of the text and its musical setting, with *Lulu* more than twenty years passed in which Berg pondered over a great variety of operatic subjects, only after completing *Wozzeck*, he felt attracted, at least for a certain amount of time, above all to Gerhard Hauptmann's play *Und Pippa tanzt!* It wasn't until the spring of 1928 that Berg finally decided on Wedekind's tragedy, a decision made easier by the advice of his friends. Berg finished arranging the text the following spring, and after a short break to compose the aria *Der Wein*, he worked almost uninterruptedly on *Lulu*, completing the short score in April 1934. He spent the remaining months of his life working on the instrumentation.

Since Berg agreed entirely with the view of the *Lulu* tragedy expounded by Karl Kraus in his speech, let us quote a few sentences: "A soul from the afterlife rubbing the sleep from its eyes." A poet had lover written on a totally unmaterialistic, artistic formation of feminine beauty, holds *Lulu's* hand. In this man an angel, on the other hand, are the key to this maze of femininity, to this labyrinth in which many a man has lost his life and his soul. The Mistress of Love has gathered all the animal types of men around her; so that she can serve her by taking what she has given. The poet is her husband, the man who expresses it in words. Then, once he is completely intoxicated by this sweet union of contrasting parts, once he feels fulfilled, he will find the words, in the last act of *Die Büchse der Pandora*, which are uttered from the depths of her soul: "Here, in front of this painting, I am winning back my reflection. At last I can understand my undoing. Everything we went through becomes so natural, so obvious, and so deep. Without ever having the least bourgeois attitude before these flesh and blood riddles, these large, innocent children's eyes, this beautiful exuberant body – may he cast the first stone upon it!"

Spoken in front of the portrait of the woman who destroyed everything since she was destroyed by everyone, these words encompass the world of the author Frank Wedekind." Berg, while arranging Wedekind's text into an opera libretto, limited his work on it to a totally unmaterialistic, artistic formation of feminine beauty, holds *Lulu's* hand. In this man an angel, on the other hand, are the key to this maze of femininity, to this labyrinth in which many a man has lost his life and his soul. The Mistress of Love has gathered all the animal types of men around her; so that she can serve her by taking what she has given. The poet is her husband, the man who expresses it in words. Then, once he is completely intoxicated by this sweet union of contrasting parts, once he feels fulfilled, he will find the words, in the last act of *Die Büchse der Pandora*, which are uttered from the depths of her soul: "Here, in front of this painting, I am winning back my reflection. At last I can understand my undoing. Everything we went through becomes so natural, so obvious, and so deep. Without ever having the least bourgeois attitude before these flesh and blood riddles, these large, innocent children's eyes, this beautiful exuberant body – may he cast the first stone upon it!"

It should also be mentioned that Berg uses the twelve-tone technique here in a highly specific way, as in the earlier *Lyric Suite* and the aria *Der Wein*. In addition to exercising his absolute musical function, it was suited above all to the particular needs of the music dramaturgic. Berg thus succeeded in relating the entire "leitmotivic" and "leit-harmonic" structure of the opera to one single musical form, just as the author had placed one sole human being at the center of the drama. Everything else is "produced" from it and must be seen in a functional context with it. In closing, let us quote a comment made by Berg in January 1935 during the first performance of the *Lulu Symphony* in Prague, since Berg placed great value on it as a singularly stringent formulation of his own views in the evocation of the mythical essence of the *Lulu* character: "*Lulu* is a heroine of supra-dimensional power in feeling and suffering, destroying everything around her which succumbs to her enchantment, a piece of nature beyond good and evil, and thus to be deciphered only by the music as a self-contained cosmos in a context beyond all concepts. How this glowing fireball which sears everything that comes in contact with it finally dies, after all life around it has died or is in the process of dying…"

This stimulated the psychologist in the musician to deal with the surreal sphere in which dreamlike images scary past life figures rushing towards their death, since only by dying will one last reflection of a great innocent demon shine upon them. After his *Wozzeck*, now it is Alban Berg's *Lulu* that will lead an eternal life. She will with an easier time than poor Wozzeck, for he was, along with Don Juan and Faust, a one of those characters who have been walking around us since time immemorial and whom the poet does not first have to give shape to, but simply have to find.

**WILLI REICH, 1968**

## Synopsis

**COMPACT DISC 1**

In the **Prologue**, the animal tamer presents his beasts in the arena: "the true, wild, beautiful animal, tamed by the human spirit" …

**Act One**

Lulu has married her earliest youth, has to an elderly professor of medicine. While Lulu is having her portrait painted, the artist succeeds to his lust and pursues Lulu in her ardor. When her husband surprises them in flagranti, he is so shocked that he collapses and dies. Lulu then marries the painter, who does not suspect that she is being financed by her paramour. Dr. Schön hopes to free himself from Lulu by providing her a life of luxury. Lulu, however, refuses to give him his freedom, especially now that she knows the artist to make a socially respectable marriage. When the artist is told about Lulu's past by Dr. Schön, he commits suicide. – Lulu is in the dressing-room of the theater, preparing herself for her dance number in the presence of Alwa. Dr. Schön's son. Alwa has also fallen under her spell. There is a tumult in the theater: Lulu feigns a heart attack when she sees Dr. Schön in the company of his fiancée in the auditorium. Dr. Schön rushes into the dressing-room and Lulu forces him to write a letter of renunciation to his fiancée. She threatens to leave him for good by marrying an African prince.

**COMPACT DISC 2**

**Act Two**

Lulu has married Dr. Schön, the man she really loves. They entertains a number of strange people in her home: the beggar Schigolch, who claims to be Lulu's father, the animal tamer Rodrigo, a gymnasium pupil and Alwa. Lulu fascinates all of them. Dr. Schön can no longer stand this life, but lacks the strength to liberate himself from it. When he realizes that his own son Alwa is also in love with Lulu, he presses a revolver into Lulu's hand so that she can kill herself. After some hesitation Lulu finally shoots Dr. Schön. Sentenced to ten years in prison, Lulu falls ill with cholera. In her doomed love for Lulu, Countess Geschwitz sacrifices herself and obtains Lulu's freedom by smuggling herself into the sick bay under Lulu's name and exchanging clothing with her. Lulu emerges from the prison at Schigolch's aim. Horrified at the way she looks, the animal tamer realizes that he cannot use her as his partner in the circus and flees. Alwa, now totally bewitched by Lulu, leaves the country with her.

**Act Three**

Pursued by blackmailers and informers, Lulu has been forced to flee from Paris to London. She now lives with Alwa and Schigolch in a wretched attic. In order to support herself and the two men, she is forced to become a prostitute. When Lulu is attacked by Jack the Ripper, Countess Geschwitz, who has followed her to London, tries in vain to save her, but also falls prey to the sex killer. Thus the "glowing fireball Lulu which sears everything that comes in contact with it, after all life around it has died or is in the process of dying".

## "My God, she's singing everything that's there!"
### Anneliese Rothenberger speaks about her Lulu

Anneliese Rothenberger, Lulu was undoubtedly one of the greatest challenges of her career, and of the most fascinating. She totally clashed with her image of "charm and grace". – That's precisely the image I was trying to counterbalance! As a singer, you're quickly stereotyped. For example, with Günther Rennert I was almost always supposed to sing little hussies. When the opera in Hamburg, he just couldn't conceive me as the Parisian cocotte. But when I finally started being cast in lyrical roles, I suddenly found myself at the other end of the spectrum: the good, the pure, embodiment of nobility, goodness, benevolence. In this respect, Lulu was an attempt to once again to this trend. Lulu "sweetness". I wanted to show another side of myself.

The very side which Rennert had seen years before. – True, he had offered me the Lulu in 1957. However, I must have been able to carry it off then; I would have been overreaching myself. When Rolf Reinelt called me ten years later, I also began by refusing. But then came one of his typical answers: "It was certainly right for you to have said 'no' back then; but if you don't say 'yes' now, you'll be making a mistake." So I accepted, all the more readily since Rennert was directing, and buried myself in the score. I worked on that role nearly half a year, five hours a day too little. Hilja Plinzkey and Evelyn Lear took a long time to work out.

What do you feel when you hear your recording of *Lulu* today? – It's been a long time since I listened to it. I was absolutely amazed at how well it sounds. With respect to voice technique too, I really don't know how I ever learned all that in such a short time. With all these notes that just come out of nowhere! And then the staging, I have never sang *Lulu* for the first time with the orchestra. The coach had always given me my notes, so I knew the piece for the first orchestral rehearsal. I was totally at sea and was ready to throw in the towel. Fortunately, that situation improved from week to week. In the end, I still didn't know whether all the notes were right. But Anja Silja once said to my husband: "My God, she's singing everything that's there!" That is, it could never do with Konstanze and Pamina. Aida is a Lulu type. – Yes indeed. My husband once said to me: "You'll be just fine as long as you sing Donizetti and Mozart and Strauss." And besides, Alban Berg was thinking of a singer like Lili Claes for Lulu – a classical coloratura soprano, not a dramatic or "character" singer. That makes the part all the more believable. Lulu isn't supposed to sound like *Wozzeck's* Marie. She should be like a compelling character." – I noticed this amazement in the audience – and the enthusiasm of the musicians. Of course, I also was flattened. After the third performance I took the role out of my repertoire. At the World's Fair and then to the Metropolitan Opera. There too it was a big success. In 1968, however, I only sang two more times. It is so demanding that you really shouldn't sing anything else on the side. I even scheduled to sing Konstanze at the Munich Opera Festival in 1967, it went well, but it could have gone wrong. Finally, Rennert was convinced that I should specialize in parts like Lulu. "There are women who are just like Konstanze and Pamina. But a Lulu you have – few can do."
